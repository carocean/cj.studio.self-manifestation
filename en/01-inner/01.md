# Lighting

What are the things within the world like?  
If they were not things for an observer,  
by what could they be known?

A realm without observer—  
how would its being stand?

Bright or dark?  
Wind or not? Cold or hot? Sound or not?

If one asks thus,  
then faintly an observer is already present—  
and the saying contradicts itself.

A realm in which there is no observer within—  
name it the Mute.

The Mute: seeming to exist yet without sound,  
seeming to move yet without sign.

Its being is not knowing;  
its transforming is not recognizing.

As if it had a beginning yet knew not its birth;  
as if it had growth yet knew not its flourishing;  
as if it had an end yet knew not its fate.

Silent it abides—  
sound and Information do not pass.

Suppose within the Mute-Realm  
one observer is cast.

Dimly—sight comes into use.  
Faintly—hearing comes into use.  
Softly—touch comes into use.  
Subtly—taste comes into use.

Though the uses of this observer are unclear,  
the surrounding character already appears—

like candlelight shining around.

The observer, by awareness lighting the surrounding,  
makes its character appear.

This is called Lighting.

Therefore God said:

“Let there be light,”  
and there was light.

God did not make things appear by light itself,  
but by creating the observer that makes them appear.

Without an observer to light them—  
bright for whom?

Things do not exist by name,  
nor by boundary,  
nor by color,  
nor by change.

What makes them exist is this:

things excite one another,  
and the observer senses them.

Sensing—image.  
Intent images it.

This is called Imagery.

Beyond Imagery—  
this is called Reality.

When wind blows,  
the hollows answer with sound.

Following the hollow and measuring the tone,  
I know the wind blows.

A flute is blown  
and the holes answer with notes.

Though the blowing force is the same,  
the holes differ and the sound differs.

Thus one breath again blows upon the hollows of observers—  
the breath is one,  
yet sounding in another, the sound differs.

Therefore two observers gazing upon one thing  
form different Imagery.

Within nature, things excite one another,  
and the observer among them records them as character.

From this Information arises.

Information is the trace of things upon the observer—  
the observer’s Imagery of them.

Thus character and name arise.

If there were no observer among them,  
though things still excite and trace one another,  
there would be no Information,  
no character,  
no name.

Thus they return to the Mute.

Gazing upon one place,  
people name its color “red”.

Such is the use of language.

If a speechless infant gazes upon it,  
what then is that color like?

Though no name is used,  
there already seems a character there.

Imagery arises within.

This image has no name,  
yet stands clear and lucid—

this not that,  
different and unlike,  
hard to describe,  
yet forming its own form.

Not only humans are thus.  
Is it not also so for the mosquito?

Seeing yellow, it flees far away.  
Without Imagery of yellow, how could this be?

Thus the observer gazes upon things  
and measures character.

Imagery arises.

It is formed privately  
and cannot be given to another.

Others cannot peer into it—  
not because it lacks form,  
but because communication proceeds by shared pointing.

Each names his own Imagery.  
Common understanding arises from language,  
and Imagery is taken to be the same.

Light shines upon the field.

Candlelight is not moonlight;  
moonlight is not sunlight.

Thus at night things appear dim,  
yet by day they appear mirror-clear.

Is not the observer’s lighting also so?

Lower observer—candlelight.  
Middle observer—moonlight.  
Higher observer—sunlight.

Yet human—what light?

Hearing reaches only a thousand meters;  
sight only several miles;  
smell scarcely a foot.

Seeing tables and chairs yet not atoms.

Tasting meat and savor yet not molecules.

Gazing upon the river of stars  
yet not knowing that a higher observer  
might take it as a single stone.

How then can human lighting be called clear?

The insect observes directly outward.

Birds and beasts observe outward  
and observe the observing.

Yet human observes  
the observing of observing of observing.

Observing upon observing seems without end,  
yet it reaches what I call my observing.

I once reflected upon the observing of my observing.  
Faintly it seemed there was an observing behind it.

Whether the observing of divine beings is also thus  
I do not know.

This is called Intent-Transformation.

The deeper the transformation of intent,  
the more penetrating the lighting,  
the clearer the sensing.

CJ once asked himself:

“I?”

At that moment a numb electric sensation arose,  
a net-like gathering at the heart.

From the heart along the neck upward  
Imagery surged forth—  
wordless yet clear,  
hard to describe.

Then again he asked:

“I I I?”

At once beneath the skin of the whole body  
a burning numbness arose,  
as if currents moved within.

Imagery opened and reached clarity.  
The heart answered:

“Oh—I understand.”

Thus it is known:

Observing upon observing may have no end,  
self upon self may have no limit—

yet the verification of “I”  
is obtained directly in Imagery,  
straight and immediate.

Heaven has set it so—  
how marvelous.

Without saying my mind is here—yet it is here.  
Without saying my body is here—yet it is here.

Directly imaging it,  
its sensing is alive.

From this it is seen:

Language is founded upon Imagery.

Language is like the tongue of Imagery,  
sounding outward to reach common understanding.

Thus Imagery stands before language.

Language is the observing of observing—  
called higher Imagery.

The meaning of words becomes clear only within Imagery,  
felt inside the observer,  
and then known.

Thus speech is not required of the observer,  
nor is it the measure of the observer’s height or depth.

The beginner driver speaks much yet cannot drive.  
The skilled driver moves naturally,  
following the character.

Imagery arises—clarity arises.  
Imagery merges—communication flows.

The highest imagery is wordless,  
yet connects the myriad things.

Magnet:

Ancient people took it as stone.  
Ants take it as mountain.  
Floating microbes take it as heaven.

None know magnetism.

Yet magnet-bacteria image it.

Thus they see what higher beings cannot see—  
the Reality they observe is as of another world.

If even so,

what of an observer whose body were an aggregate of neutrinos?

To it ordinary things would be as nothing.  
What it senses—gravitational field as substance.  
What it sees—weak interaction as form.

And what of a being whose body is light?

Alas!

What the world is like—  
how could I know it fully?

How do I know that the observer within my brain  
does not ask the same question  
of the world within the brain?

Yet I take myself to be outside  
and do not feel that I am inside.

Strange indeed.

Reality manifests within,  
yet is taken as outside.  
How wondrous the illusion.

Every observer manifests its observing within itself,  
yet cannot manifest it to another.

If it must manifest to another,  
it becomes the other’s observing,  
becomes the other’s “I”.

Thus Self-Manifestation  
manifests only to itself.

Therefore it is called the Self-Manifesting Being.

Its nature refers to itself—  
thus it is named Self-Manifestation.

Because its nature refers to itself,  
the Observer Paradox arises.

Things excite things  
and are taken as Signal.

The observer excites its observing  
and takes it as Information.

The observer excites the observing  
of the observer of observing  
and takes it as Information.

Thus endlessly—  
no final observer is reached.

In other words:

Within a collection, if A manifests to B,  
within B there must be an observing a₁ of A,  
and an observer B₁ making a₁ manifest.

If a₁ manifests to B₁,  
within B₁ there must be an observing a₂ of a₁,  
and an observer B₂ making a₂ manifest.

Thus without end.

If reasoned only by the observer paradox,  
the Self-Manifesting Being could not exist  
and nothing could appear.

Yet I know I have spoken this argument.

Knowing this,  
I know the argument appears to me.

Therefore I know with certainty  
that a Self-Manifesting Being exists.

How can this contradict reason  
yet hold in fact?

The Self-Manifestation of the Self-Manifesting Being  
is Lighting.

Not like a mirror receiving light.

Lighting gathers intent,  
acts and fixes it,  
thus obtaining Imagery.

Imagery—  
the domain of signals which Intent ordains.

Signal transmits from before to after.  
Intent ordains from after toward before.

Thus manifestation appears within the present field.

Therefore it is called Self-Manifestation—  
manifestation to itself.

All life has desire.  
Desire is driven by intent.

Life gathers intent, acts, and gazes.  
Thus Imagery necessarily arises,  
and environment becomes manifest.

Therefore all life is a Self-Manifesting Being.

Because all are born of nature,  
CJ calls life Primordial Being.

Human-made devices until now:

The radio does not know the sound within it.  
The television does not know the image within it.  
The computer does not know the numbers within it.  
AI does not know what it does.

These cannot form Imagery.

They are only used by humans—  
substitutes for force,  
substitutes for intelligence.

Alas—  
still trapped in the primordial generation  
and not knowing how to step beyond it.

Then what is the next generation?

When the first Self-Manifestation Circuit is born,  
the Age of Self-Manifestation arrives with thunder.
