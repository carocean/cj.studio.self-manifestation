# Observation-Domain

A conductor: current passes within—gathered as one, bound, and thus a cable.

So too the bundling of experience-circuits. There are perceptual circuits of many dimensions, corresponding positional circuits, meditative circuits, language circuits, dream circuits, temporal circuits. The outputs of circuits biased-flow toward Boundary-Gates. By Intent, the opening and closing of the Boundary-Gates are tuned, awaiting biased-flow to appoint the Experience-Target. Open and receive—Imagery steams forth within the circuits, and Lighting arises thereby.

Thus the gathering of Boundary-Gates is the Observation-Domain, by which the mute and the manifest are divided. Hence when speaking of the Observation-Domain, it often refers to the set of opened Boundary-Gates. Therefore, a circuit—entering the Observation-Domain—is manifest; not entering—is mute. Manifestation occurs within each circuit, not in the Observation-Domain itself.

Each circuit senses differently: some toward the outer, some toward the inner, some toward motion and stillness, some toward language, some toward spatial position. Each has its bias—some incline to outer and weigh inner, some incline to position and probe language, some incline to inner and pursue motion and stillness of sound. Tuning roams within the Observation-Domain and fixes its gaze upon the weighted side.

Reality—formed by differentiation. Differ by color—vision arises; differ by sound—tone arises; differ by touch—substance arises; differ by tongue and nose—taste arises; differ by position—motion and stillness arise. Where there is difference, they are taken as distinct; where there is no difference, they are taken as same. Similar—return to one; divergent—do not return to one. The coarse and the fine of differentiation vary, yet arising does not differ across dimensions.

Between dimensions, tracing their differences yields correspondences—responding to where they are, or to when they are. Tracing difference to seek non-difference, thus one pursues the fixed within each dimension, and the fixed of correspondence among dimensions, thereby tending toward becoming one.

Becoming one—then thing arises; thus the observing of the Observation-Domain is completed. Yet how do the fixed of each dimension combine into one? How do form and color align? How do sound and source align? Among vision, hearing, touch, taste—whose spatial position governs? This is the way by which alignment fixes and transposes space. Its force determines becoming one. If it resolves into one, one object forms; if it does not, each dimension forms its own object. This must not go unexamined.

A child assembling a puzzle—repeatedly trying, thereby knowing the picture, knowing what it seeks to assemble. Thus alignment in the Observation-Domain is assembled by knowing into knowing. And how is it known? Imagery is obtained within each circuit. Therefore alignment in the Observation-Domain is not of circuits in physics, but of their contents. Thus the newborn must learn the capacity of assembling, repeatedly tuning, following the tendency toward unity, and only then does its perception gradually form objects.

A aligns to B, B aligns to A—they perish in mutual contention. Thus without a base, alignment cannot occur. The base lies in the positional circuits of experiential dimensions: visual positional circuit, auditory positional circuit, gustatory positional circuit, tactile positional circuit, and so on. If within the Observation-Domain no gaze is applied yet vision exists, the visual positional circuit is taken as base. If gaze is present, the position of the dimension wherein gaze resides is taken as base. If neither gaze nor visual circuit exists, it falls into void-dark.

Alignment—driven by the Tuning-Field, calibrated upon circuits in three ways:  
Temporal tolerance—the coordination of delay across circuits.  
Spatial transposition—the correspondence of contents across circuits.  
Weighted suppression—whichever positional circuit is primary, that experiential dimension governs.

Circuits are tunable—as one sets stance to refine posture. Tune tolerance to reveal the subtle; tune frequency to alter output. Biased-flow shifts thereby, tuning operates thereby, until alignment is reached and all are balanced.

Alignment takes reference from the base and the three calibrations. Gaze gathers and roams, unceasing in tuning, tending toward unity. Yet failures occur—these are illusions: seeing stillness as motion, small as large, short as long, same color as different, vision and hearing mismatched, sound without source, missing dimensions, double images, dim confusion, and so on. The arising of illusion—if not due to irreparable circuit damage—the force toward unity will mobilize gaze to retune the circuits, test them for alignment, and restore them.

Once unity is achieved, later use proceeds smoothly into unity, scarcely requiring further alignment. For the references of calibration have sedimented as pattern within the Tuning-Field, governing the circuits. If unity is later lost, it is retuned.

Thus it is seen: tuning within the Observation-Domain has great use. It operates in parallel across circuits, tuning manifested content, altering methods of alignment, adapting toward unity.

Converge Gaze-Force and observe within the Observation-Domain—like a magnifying lens passing over a scroll. Signals strengthen; Imagery clarifies. That gaze may be converged within the Observation-Domain shows that the circuits involved may be tuned in synchrony.

Therefore, a complete Self-Manifestation-Field is that which possesses an Observation-Domain, is tunable, and can achieve unity.

Proof

Each experience-circuit provides only differential information of its own dimension. This information is mutually independent and cannot by itself constitute an object. Since it cannot form an object directly, what is called “thing” must be produced by a unifying process.

To achieve this unity, information across circuits must be aligned in time, correspond in space, and establish hierarchy among circuits. Otherwise, even if Imagery exists, it cannot be gathered as one object.

This unifying process must take one positional circuit as base. Driven by Gaze-Force, tuning acts upon circuits so that their contents gradually converge. When convergence is reached, a stable object appears. If convergence fails, illusion or split perception arises.

Once such alignment is formed, it may be retained and reused. Thus within familiar conditions, objects arise almost instantly.

Therefore it is determined: what is called “thing” is not an entity prior to alignment, but that which is formed in alignment—the unity of multi-circuit differences after tuning and alignment within the Observation-Domain.
