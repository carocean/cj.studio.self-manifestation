# Coloring

Sour, sweet, bitter, pungent, salty—Imagery.  
Rancid, gamey, fishy, stale, foul—also Imagery; from these, like and dislike divide.

Friend, kin, companion, child, respect—Imagery of relation.  
Enemy, foe, bandit, traitor—also Imagery of relation; from these, closeness and distance are set apart.

Man has like and dislike—thus closeness and distance divide.  
Beast has like and dislike—thus approach and avoidance follow.

Like and dislike attach to Imagery—thus preference.  
Preference—liking this, disliking that.  
From this, disposition arises.

Emotion—what is added upon observing.  
Now added here, now added there.  
Seeing a scene—emotion arises; seeing a thing—one recalls a person.  
Within it, like and dislike arise—preference forms, disposition stands.

Thus emotion arises—and like and dislike issue.  
Attached to Imagery, clinging upon it, like dye upon cloth—thus Coloring.

Like and dislike—tendency, bias.  
From bias—action: now approaching, now avoiding.  
This is approach and avoidance; this is the use of Coloring.

Since emotion is that which is felt, what is felt manifests—thus Imagery.  
Thus it has its own Self-Manifestation circuit—called the Coloring-Circuit.

The Coloring-Circuit does not act alone; it operates with other circuits.  
It generates Coloring-Imagery in the Imagery-Field, and colors the Imagery of other circuits.  
Liking and aversion, closeness and avoidance, tension and safety—these are lines of emotion, degrees of tendency.  
Like and dislike stand at two ends—thus the differentiation of the Coloring Experience-Field.

From it issues biased-flow—reaching the Experience-Target, entering the Boundary-Gate.  
This is the ever-open gate of the Observation-Domain.  
It colors reality with emotion, strengthens tendency toward decision, and tunes the direction toward which Intent proceeds.

Thus bias-driven speech and action arise.

Thinking of living beings is limited; thus they rely on Coloring, adding tendency to what they enact.  
The rabbit sees the wolf—flees: fear.  
The bee sees the flower—draws near: liking.  
Thus approach and avoidance are achieved.

An infant sees filth—knows not its foulness, plays with it and does not avoid.  
Grown, it covers its nose.  
Thus it is known: Coloring-Imagery arises gradually thereafter.  
If there are inborn likes and dislikes, they are inherited from the parent.

Music, film, and story—hundreds of feelings turn within their unfolding.  
Emotion sediments in patterns within the Coloring-Field.  
Reuse deepens; closeness fades, distance grows.  
Thus the musician is easily moved.

Therefore CJ says: the Coloring-Circuit is a system of tendency-states.  
For low-intelligence beings to live, it must not be lost.  
If man were to lose it, though he know ten thousand strategies, he cannot choose one.
