On Self-Manifestation · Outer Chapter 3 v1.5

# On Mathematics

To observe is to have number, whereby division becomes possible;  
for number to possess form, whereby indication becomes possible;  
for form to become calculable, whereby classification becomes possible;  
for calculation to become verifiable, whereby comparison becomes possible.

What may be indicated is already divided; what may be compared is already classed. Thus number is born of what is already divided and already classed, and thereby is born of what is already formed.

Dwelling within the already-formed and employing number, nothing is inconvenient, nothing impossible, nothing unattainable.

Absent this “already-formed,” what could number act upon? As for the not-yet-formed, upon what could it be used?

If division be finite, then it may accord to its division; if division be infinite, then it must be forced into one. Therefore, what may be counted, indicated, classed, and compared finds its efficacy in the already-formed, forming within reality, forming within the Observation realm.

Thus number, reality, and its Observation realm are one mother with three sons. The mother comes forth only after divisibility becomes possible; therefore the three sons are born under the same constraints.

Consensus saith:

“Axioms, mathematics, and knowledge each arise within the various Observation realms, and cannot be wholly identical across all realms.”

Thus mathematics cannot cross Observation realms and remain universally operative.

Before division, prior to formation — where could number be used?

Division is differentiation: the distinctions before and behind, left and right, among signals (pulses). Such distinctions must enter structure ere they may be spoken of. Within structure, trace-response arises.

Within the domains of the Sedimentation-Field, what may be distinguished extends into detail; what may not be distinguished returns into its primal class. Thus are born what may be counted, compared, and indicated.

Some signal-sources (the origins of signals) lie exceedingly near. Their crossings become too entangled for the field to differentiate; thus their distinctions collapse into a single domain, whose boundaries grow nearly uniform. This is called the lower limit of differentiation. Thereupon the boundaries of the domain gradually strengthen, and compression is formed.

Moreover, when signals differ greatly across dimensions, they divide into great domains according to those dimensions; these are called dimensional domains. Signals likewise divide by dimensions and enter the field.

Signals of the same dimension must have kindred sources; therefore the domains they form must be near-neighbors. Some overlap, some intersect, and few fail to intersect. Thus one knows them necessarily to be children of dimensional domains.

The traces of water upon earth have depth and shallowness; the traces of signals upon the field likewise have depth and shallowness. The traces of water upon earth possess their domains; the traces of signals upon the field likewise possess their domains.

If repeatedly struck, their depth increases, for overlapping domains bring forth depth. If struck but little, their shallowness increases, for sparse overlap brings forth shallowness. Much overlap yields depth; sparse overlap yields shallowness — how much more so isolation without overlap?

If struck once yet never revisited, the trace-response gradually fades; this is called forgetting. Such is determined by the qualitative nature of the field.

Forgetting and pulses interact within the field; their traces may diffuse or seep. When the Sedimentation-Degree may deepen, it diffuses; when it reaches the bottom, it seeps.

The function of tuning is to converge the paths traversed by pulses, separate the boundary between the active and the inactive, remove the noise at the margins, and render the domains distinct. Thus the boundaries of things become divided, and the boundary itself possesses its minimum threshold.

First, signal s1 enters along boundary e1 (segment), striking forth domain f1. Thereafter s2 likewise enters along e1, striking forth domain f2. If f1 and f2 intersect at domain fa, then fa necessarily indicates the commonality of s1 and s2. This is induction fallen into the field. Such logic long hangs within thought, yet in the end may descend into structure.

s1 contains non-fa, s2 likewise contains non-fa, and yet both contain fa. Thus differentiation becomes threefold because the field can contain and distinguish three domains.

This is called the field’s differential distinguishability.

The number of domains per unit volume that may be differentially distinguished is called Resolution.

Because the field’s four primitives — sequence, frequency, intensity, and density — differ, Resolution is not uniformly distributed throughout the field; therefore its average value is taken.

Thereafter s3 arrives and strikes, and s4 follows thereafter. The striking does not cease, yet the field possesses physical limits. Domains must possess interstitial gaps for misalignment. When no further gaps for misalignment remain, the upper limit of differentiation is reached. Therefore this upper limit is called the capacity of the field.

Field capacity is the product of field volume and its Resolution.

The capacity per unit volume of the field is called Capacity-Rate.

Capacity-Rate and Resolution are one under two names.

When speaking of differential distinguishability, one speaks of Resolution.

When speaking of the measure of the abundance and scarcity of domains within volume, one speaks of Capacity-Rate.

Therefore capacity is likewise equal to the product of Capacity-Rate and field volume.

The ratio between the number of domains already in use within the field and its capacity is called Capacity Utilization.

Beyond its capacity, lateral branches of things can no longer be incorporated, nor can novelty be explored. One thereby becomes fixed upon what one already holds. Thus it is said: as humans age, what they cling to may harden almost like stone.

On the Sedimentation-Field once defined capacity as the ratio of Information-count to field volume.

Information is reckoned relative to that wherein it resides.

This is as though scattering grain and counting chickens thereby; as though comparing reflections against their frame.

Its measure must necessarily fall into disorder.

Therefore this chapter defines capacity through volume and domain-count.

Capacity thereby becomes measurable.

For both abide upon the physical layer.

Thus after v1.2, On the Sedimentation-Field adopts the definition of capacity established in this chapter.

If overlapping domains can deepen no further, then those domains can divide no further. Their inability to re-enter differentiation is called Domain Saturation.

Saturation gives rise to seepage.

This is called Edge-Seepage — seepage itself.

The Information within Edge-Seepage closely correlates with the Information of the domain-core.

To proceed from core toward edge is called deduction.

To proceed from edge toward core is called induction.

Thus when Edge-Seepage arises, fine classes are born.

Edge-Seepage links one after another; core and edge alternate in mutual intersection.

Take this as core, and its circumference becomes Edge-Seepage.

Take this as Edge-Seepage, and its center becomes core.

Core and edge spread across the field in adjoining continuity, bordering one another, such that no class may exist alone, and nothing lacks its finer forms.

Their overlap is as mountains.

Descending from their edges, one beholds imagery with core and outer mass.

Therefore one may shift e and pursue its outer mass.

Pursuing that outer mass, one may obtain the details of imagery.

This is the structural cause of deduction.

Following the overlaps of domains, circling and pursuing neighboring domains, there may be no end.

Therefore by tracing imagery and exploring its near-correlations, there likewise may be no end.

This is the structural cause of associative comparison.

Thus because the field possesses differentiation, the Information-Realm thereby born may be counted, indicated, classed, and compared.

Therefore no Self-Manifesting Entity lacks its mathematics.

Trace-response forms gullies, whose shape resembles lines; by terminology these are called Gully-Lines.

Gullies connect and form ravines, whose shape resembles lines possessing ends; by terminology these are called Terminal-Ravines.

Gully-Lines and Terminal-Ravines possess depth and shallowness, and thereby form a graph possessing depth.

Therefore the Sedimentation-Field is a graph possessing depth.

This is called the Field-Graph, and its depth is called Sedimentation-Degree.

What is called depth:

Many flows gather into a Terminal-Ravine and may emerge or may not emerge, as with the capacity and pulses of the neuronal axon hillock; whether they emerge bears strength or weakness.

A Gully-Line possesses flow-pressure, and may emerge or may not emerge, as with the capacity and pulses of axonal fibers; whether they emerge likewise bears strength or weakness.

Flow passes through its Gully-Line and thereafter enters the Terminal-Ravine, and may emerge or may not emerge, as with the capacity and pulses of neuronal synapses; whether they emerge likewise bears strength or weakness.

Their emergence may bear strength or weakness, or may bear equal strength.

If equal in strength, material may fill its gullies and ravines, making passage more frequent and emergence easier.

As by transmitters.

As by ion pumps.

As by inner and outer pressure.

Joining the depth of the Terminal-Ravine unto its Gully-Line, and supposing Gully-Lines possess shallowness and depth, emergence or non-emergence, therefore the name Gully-Line is commonly employed as abbreviation for the whole thereof.

Yet though frequent passage makes emergence easier, contrariwise it grows ever more obstructed.

Sparse passage causes Sedimentation-Degree to grow shallow, as footprints gradually fade upon the shore.

Bundles of Gully-Lines are called Line-Bundles.

Some terminate within the Line-Bundle.

Some pass beyond it without terminating.

Thus a Line-Bundle need not be orderly.

Take any segment of a Line-Bundle, and it becomes a Domain.

Such is the structure of Domain.

Because Gully-Lines possess thresholds of ingress and egress, therefore Line-Bundles likewise possess them, and Domains likewise possess them.

Flow may emerge from a Line-Bundle, or may fail to emerge therefrom, because threshold exists.

Therefore if Edge-Seepage does not cross the threshold of the Line-Bundle at the boundary of a certain Domain, Edge-Seepage of that Domain does not arise.

If it does not cross the threshold of a Line-Bundle within, it likewise cannot diffuse.

Every Gully-Line possesses its threshold.

Their values are unequal.

Even within the same Gully-Line, threshold-values may likewise differ.

The deeper the Sedimentation-Degree, the smaller its threshold-value; contrariwise, the greater the threshold-value becomes.

Consider the foregoing discourse:

the Sedimentation-Degree of fa is deeper than that of s1 and s2.

If s3 enters from e1, it must first fill fa, and only thereafter seep into the two sides.

Such is the nature of flowing water.

Therefore what is deep first receives the flow, and commonality first emerges.

This is why the observer first apprehends the whole, and only thereafter enters detail.

Because what is deep first receives the flow, signals favor the paths long familiar.

These are the familiar paths, the familiar domains.

Our new learning is but the deepening of old recognition.

Therefore the Sedimentation-Degree of new learning is the sum of old and new together.

Moreover, because of the striking of signals, what is deep emerges first; thus new learning first emerges, as though standing atop, functioning as a stack.

Because new learning accumulates upon old recognition, it is called more familiar than old recognition.

Therefore the deep may be called the familiar.

Hence the common saying:

“the familiar emerges first”

means precisely that the deep emerges first.

Sedimentation-Degree itself may be tuned.

Tune its depth, and old recognition may emerge forth.

Thus one may desire not semantic meaning, but merely to hear sound itself.

One must necessarily apply Fixation-Force beneath semantics, shallowing its depth, as though lifting from the bottom and gradually raising it upward layer by layer.

Thereby old recognition may emerge layer by layer.

This is the wondrous use whereby Sedimentation-Degree transforms old and new.

Thus one knows:

the nature of sedimentation may unify (compress), but cannot overwrite (delete old recognition); it may only forget.

s5 bends through this and that, then through the that beyond that, thereby forming route r.

That route is a Domain of bundled lines, turning through this Domain, that Domain, and domains beyond domains, continuing its course.

Thereafter s6 likewise follows this route; thereafter s7, s8, until even s100.

Thus the route gradually deepens, and a law t is gradually obtained within route r.

Therefore t is called the law of s5 through s100.

Hence one knows:

route is the presence of law,

and law is the name of route.

If s1000 traverses route r and desires to depart toward route z, then s1000 must possess law t.

Therefore route r may be obtained as the common law of all signals traversing it.

If s1001 traverses route r yet does not depart toward route m, but alone departs toward route z, this is called principle.

At this time route r and route z shall form new route rz, and new law shall furthermore emerge.

Whenever signals traverse it, its Sedimentation-Degree increases; when they do not, it decreases.

How comes its diminishing through sparse traversal?

There must herein be wind and sun that fade the traces.

This necessarily derives from the qualitative nature of the field.

The speed whereby shallowness changes must be measurable; therefore this measure is called Field-Quality.

Field-Quality determines the speed of forgetting.

Without it, the field would easily saturate.

Saturation would swiftly lose distinguishability.

Therefore Field-Quality is a necessary property of the field.

Thus one knows:

the Sedimentation-Field may be graphed,

and may be mathematized.

Therefore cj shall open a separate chapter within the Formal Chapters to establish a new mathematics,

naming it:

Field Mathematics.

And one knows:

mathematics within the Observation realm cannot mathematize generation,

yet may mathematize the field wherein it resides —

fortunate indeed!

Yet as for Field Mathematics,

cj has never seen its like within the present age.

Moreover,

there is the saying:

“Without measures and rulers, square-inches are hard to form.”

Since the future realization of Self-Manifestation Field engineering requires first the sharpening of its instruments,

effort shall be exhausted to establish it.

Field Mathematics is the art whereby generative structures bring forth Information.

By establishing its principles,

wielding its arts,

and measuring its structures,

one may know the operation of the Information-Realm within the field,

and the mutual transformations of material states.

This is the way of the arising and extinguishing of reality,

and it cannot but be deeply studied.
