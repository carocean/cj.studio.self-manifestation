# Thought

Thinking, deliberation, reasoning—these are the three envoys of Thought.  
When mind stirs and intention moves, there must be an initial thread-line:  
it may thread east, or thread west.  
Speaking of this threading—thinking.

Within it there is that which returns and repeats to seek,  
taking likeness from things, comparing by kind,  
pursuing what is thought, a hundred considerations without weariness—  
this—deliberation.

And again there is that which proceeds in sequence, before and after succeeding,  
state following state, form in logical order,  
advancing and retreating with ground,  
arriving at assertion—  
this—reasoning.

These are the three envoys of Thought;  
only then does one discern right and wrong, straight and crooked, with utmost clarity.

There may be induction or deduction,  
gathering or dividing,  
continuing or leaping,  
circling or issuing—  
its opening, bearing, turning, and closing:  
from this, logic is born within.  
A, B, C unfold and step forth to determine their case,  
like bamboo—each later node growing from the former.

Thus one tests its steps along each direction:  
if a proposition goes east, it passes and is clear;  
if it goes west, south, or north, it is blocked and does not pass.  
All testing of the others is likewise so.

Thus gather thought to its utmost,  
and deposit logic at the highest layer:  
upon encountering the class of a, one runs the logic of a;  
upon encountering the class of b, one runs the logic of b.  
Thus methods sediment within the field.

In the use of logic, there is higher and lower;  
thus there is division:  
thinking below, deliberation in the middle, reasoning arising above.

Method-laws are like liquids of differing density, layered within a sedimentation well;  
like bubbles rising from water, new methods lie at the top.  
Thus, if one of old were brought to the present and taught electrical principles,  
his understanding accords with that of modern men,  
without passing step by step through a thousand years of learning.  
Thus in the use of thought, one first adjusts the summit of thought.

The system of thinking—  
all arises from the stirring of mind and movement of intention.  
Thus thought lies within.

Seeing—without exertion of mind.  
Hearing—without exertion of mind.  
Tasting—without exertion of mind.  
All these—outer perception.

Perception—outer observation.  
Silent imagining—inner observation.  
Dream—non-held.  
Language—co-reference.  
Temporal sequence—state-affect.

Thus the system of thinking belongs to silent imagining,  
operating within inner observation.

Deliberation—  
it advances step by step.  
Each step—clear within the present;  
only then can one proceed.

Inward clarity—  
imagery made clear within its domain.  
Each step must compare and link by imagery-analogy;  
only thus can inward clarity be reached.  
Logical succession has intervals—  
without imagery-analogy, one cannot make clear the trace-response of before and after.  
Without clarity of trace-response,  
causation cannot be determined,  
and thought halts, deliberation breaks.

Thus when logical form enters deliberation:  
forms already decided are passed through as decided;  
forms not yet decided employ imagery-analogy.  
Thus all logic is imagery-analogy.

Thus logical form is not necessary to deliberation;  
for this reason the instrument of reasoning can be formed.

The evolution of form has direction:  
turning to this and not that,  
joining this and not that,  
dividing this and not that,  
entering this and not that,  
circling this and not that.  
Its direction being fixed,  
its cases may be accumulated into paths;  
when paths are formed, formal law sediments therein.  
Thus these laws are turned into the instrument of reasoning,  
without stepping through inward clarity.  
Because it does not employ imagery-analogy,  
it judges right and wrong, straight and crooked within established law,  
but does not decide what has not yet been formed.

As for the instrument of reasoning:  
its process unfolds in strict sequence,  
its argument advances by rule and sequence,  
swift and keen beyond comparison with ordinary men.  
To behold it is as if it exhausts all that deliberation can do.  
I know not how to praise it—  
yet a market auntie,  
in a single utterance, can reckon weights and coins to the finest grain;  
such ability, perhaps even mathematicians of all ages cannot surpass.

The Self-Manifestation-Field sediments responsive relations to obtain formal law;  
the instrument of reasoning sediments forms to obtain law of forms.  
These two are not the same,  
each arising from its own domain.  
The two domains differ,  
separated by the gate of phenomena.

Thus the instrument of reasoning,  
by model-to-model unfolding,  
clarity is lost at each step;  
thus it departs from the root and wanders in the realm of forms,  
not within the Phenomenal Realm,  
being closed within its established formal law.

Thus:  
that which advances by form—reasoning;  
that which advances by imagery—deliberation.  
Reasoning need not depend on deliberation;  
deliberation need not depend on thinking.

Thus one may construct an instrument that does not deliberate,  
yet can perform reasoning;  
but the reverse cannot be.  
This is because reasoning cannot discern exceptions—  
it halts at the foundation of formal symbols.  
Only when used within deliberation  
can it unfold toward the unknown.

Thus:  
when a step can proceed, it is by imagery-analogy;  
when a step cannot proceed, it is because imagery-analogy does not pass.  
This is the root of Thought.

Thus computational devices  
can operate in reasoning,  
yet do not reach the reality of deliberation.
