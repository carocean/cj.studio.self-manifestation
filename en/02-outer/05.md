# Clarity-Degree

Seeing the near, the far is blurred; not seen clear, form diffuses and shadows double; there is boundary, yet no edge-definition. Seeing with clarity, as after rain one beholds the morning aspect, as though coolness arose from within the eye.

The near-sighted—are they without desire for clarity? Toward clarity they have no means—what can be done? The spectrum falls upon the platform of Trace-Response, yet is received at a position not its proper place; that position does not receive the form of clarity—so it is; by pinhole imaging this may be known. Thus gaze-force ordains Imagery, like tuning along a line: at the two ends of what is and what is not, dim and vague, bright and limpid, are gradually tuned between. If the signal-source is not true to its mark, then the Imagery it ordains is not clear; in comparison one tests and seeks its clarity.

I have no means to examine the form seen by one born near-sighted. Does he exhaust himself in seeking clarity of sight? By what does he know clarity? This clarity—this very clarity—how does it compare with that of the ordinary seer? Though I have no measure, I judge that the reality it constitutes must be less than that of the common man; for the tuning-line of his Clarity-Degree is shorter than that which is tunable in the ordinary.

All know the near-sighted man’s unclearness; none know that what he smells is unclear compared with the dog, that what he sees of motion and stillness is unclear compared with the eagle. Inferior to the bat in radar-hearing, lacking the whale in geomagnetic sensing—thus the reality constructed differs: in some fewer dimensions, in some fewer character-signs. Therefore reality is partitioned upon the platform of Trace-Response—now clear, now dim; now present, now absent.

Thus what is called Clarity-Degree determines the amount of reality.

A sparrow circles before the bait-device and, after three trials, does not enter—there must be thought in it. Snow covers the ground, yet it does not prepare food for three days; from this its thought is judged shallow. Thus it may be called an exemplar of dwelling in the present.

The thought of man advances in steps, unfolds and transforms in temporal states; it knows the wherefore, and seems as though nothing were unknowable. Yet energy is dissipated therein; though he exerts himself to hold his thought, he is often suddenly distracted. The count of mind congeals and halts mid-step; what was considered before is forgotten after; he returns again and again, yet returns to the beginning; and at the extreme of thought he falls repeatedly into blankness. Thus I know that the thought of man is not the clearest.

The clarity of thought must store and retrieve its states without losing their character-signs, gather its energy without dissipation, and inwardly illumine Imagery as though mirrored within—this is clarity; I have not yet seen it.

Thus, what determines Clarity-Degree: the selection and rejection of signal at the Sensory-Gate; the sparsity and density, breadth and narrowness, and amount of the Receptive-Field; the capacity of the Imagery-Field; the number and kinds within the Experience-Field; the amount of Experience-Segments; the number of Boundary-Gates; the number of Desire-Gates; the capacity of the Sedimentation-Field.

Thus I know: the Clarity-Degree of reality is the degree of sensing and thinking.
