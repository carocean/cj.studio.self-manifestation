# Electromagnetism

Things, by Causal-Response, transform one another, turning without cease; by Causal-Response traces arise among them, none can halt them; states shift and turn, exhausting yet without end. This saying—once spoken—is firm indeed. Yet by what is it known? By what is knowledge reached? Though without an observer it cannot be known, yet without a conductor it cannot be reached. The conductor bears the character-sign of Causal-Response, and again responds to the observer—can it hold to its proper measure?

Now the turning of physics, transformations arise upon it: below, fire drives; above, vapor rises; each has its boundary and return. Thus upper and lower are set as boundary: the lower is the boundary of physics; the upper is the Information-Realm. Fire drives it; water carries it; the computer contains it. The upper takes the lower as its lodging; the lower takes the upper as its vessel. Thus above and below move in mutual motion, the world forms, and manifestness arises.

Therefore Information does not stand alone; it must be in its vessel. Its motion is nothing but the vessel’s transformation in Causal-Response. Sometimes it takes the Information-Realm below as vessel, standing above its transformations; sometimes it takes the lower of the lower Information-Realm as vessel, standing above the transformations of transformation. Descending without exhaustion below, or reaching an end, it returns at last to its host. Thus there are vessels of layered transformation. What is called layered transformation is that the lower drives change in the upper; therefore boundaries are set as layers.

Thus, to transmit transformation and yet still indicate its source, to drive each layer without disorder—one must examine the material of the vessel: measure the speed it may conduct, the character-signs of Causal-Response it may carry, what aspect of things it may respond to, what aspect of the host it must not respond to, what may be controlled, what may not, and gauge the host’s capacity in amount—thereby constructing the Information-Realm and manifest reality. This way of selecting material cannot but be exacting, cannot but be deeply considered.

Having spoken of transformation, next is succession. In succession, three in sequence make current; that which can be current is called conductor. Thus in selecting materials and choosing kinds, one must examine their conductivity, testing whether they accord with speed, aspect, control, and capacity—then take them for use. Select that which can be determined to keep currents of the same source the same, currents of different sources different, and that later currents do not lose the character of their Causal-Response source—this may be taken.

Looking among surrounding materials, many can carry the character-signs of Causal-Response and also conduct. Wind carries the character-signs of taste and sound; water carries those of soil-quality and thermal change. Yet though they may give rise to an Information-Realm to nurture the ten thousand beings, at our scale they are not swift, their aspects misaligned, not easily controlled, and small in capacity—thus they are not used.

Raise the head and cast the gaze: light spreads across the four fields. It responds to the ten thousand things, altering its spectrum; according to how things respond to light, it carries their character-signs and travels through space at light-speed. This is Heaven’s bestowal upon the present age! Its distinction is unlike all other things of the world. Therefore it is often taken as the material dividing the Information-Realm and the Host-Realm, and cannot but be carefully examined.

That which belongs to light is electromagnetism. Thus that light can carry is that electromagnetism can carry; that light can float in the upper realm is that electromagnetism can float in the upper realm; that light is distinct in the world is that electromagnetism is distinct. Therefore, to choose the material that separates the two realms, it must be examined in electromagnetism.

Electromagnetism not only carries the character-signs of Causal-Response; when mediated by circuits it conducts, when leaping among ions it becomes current. From this there are computers; from this there are biological neural networks. Its traces within relays form two states—either zero or one; its traces within synapses—either halt or pass. Thus pulses and fields construct Information, formed by traces. When current returns again, Information of Causal-Response is born; the motion of Information again responds with traces. Thus Information and host answer one another, mutually driving their states, transforming without cease.

Yet tracing from the Information-Realm back to traces, one must trace to its host in the lower layer. Observing Information within the realm, it may be that it responds with traces below, or that it is the response issued by pivots below. The deeper one probes, the more it cannot be obtained in its substance—this is the difficulty.

As for traces of sedimented fields responding to current, among materials present in the world, none are seen as fitting as electromagnetism. The fitting of current and field is like water and soil blending: the field receives current as tidal flats receive waves, as brain regions receive impulses. Conversely, current can also strike and shape its traces.

Ionic conduction—compared to circuits—is not swift, yet it can proceed in pulses, thereby increasing capacity. Both are not as swift as non-mediated electromagnetism; yet it is unknown where such electromagnetism leaves its traces. If it has traces, then again there must be a medium—thus contradicting the prior assumption.

Thus in choosing field and current, one must weigh their power of trace-response, taking that which is most fitting. That which carries the ten thousand beings through life and death—how could it be taken without utmost care and deep deliberation?
