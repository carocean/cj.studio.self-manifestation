# Circuits

To know and to see the Way is to discern structure and to discern operation.  
To sense the outer, to think the inner, to speak, to enter dream, to know change, to color, to observe observation, to act.

It falls upon the field yet its locus is not perceived; it falls upon signal yet its arising is not felt; its form cannot be seized.

Signals surge between structures; imagery stands bright within; one knows not who seizes it.  
Vast in operation—  
upon surge, imagery arises;  
upon cessation, imagery empties.  
Yet the circuit is not noticed, and both motion and stillness are in its use.

Many passages make a path; many waters make rivers; many carts make roads. Pulses surge in the field and thus form circuits. They branch signals by affinity and distance, gather the near into specialization; thus circuits are distinguished by their capacities: some sense the outer, some observe the inner, some attach, some return, some drive the host apparatus to act.

Signals shuttle within circuits; they have directions:

From back to front:  
the Intent Domain (driving ring → gate of desire →) → tuning → {boundary gates, experience targets, experience field, imagery field, receptive field}.  
This is called the intent flow.

From front to back:  
receptive field → imagery field → experience field → experiential bias flow → experience target.  
This is called the signal flow.

Bearing both directions—the full circuit.  
Thus, circuit—  
the rearward surge of signal and the countermand of intent—  
closed within structure, forming a circuit.

Among them, those that can close upon the Observation-Domain are called manifestation circuits; those that cannot are called automatic circuits.  
Automatic circuits—those without reverse intent flow.  
Hence, without reverse flow, though automation is possible, self-manifestation is not.

Those that can close upon the Observation-Domain are full circuits, and may be used inward and outward. Used outward, they are sensing and observing: as sensing, imagery is generated in place of the outer, thus weight is given to the rearward direction; as observing, imagery is elicited for use, thus weight is given to the forward direction. Used inward, weight is in the rearward direction, intent governs; its impulse proceeds from intent through tuning and is cast toward the desired focus; such casting excites the intent flow.

Along its passage, each circuit opens its own subdomains within structures: it does not share the receptive field, but specializes its own; it does not share the imagery field, but specializes its own; it does not share the experience field, but specializes its own; it does not share the bias flow, but specializes its own; it does not share the experience target, but specializes its own; it does not share the boundary gates, but specializes its own. The path is formed as one body, and thus does not interfere with others.

The circuit sinks what it manifests into the imagery field, constructing it as impedance; it sinks the capacity of what is manifested into the experience field: differentiates temporal order and deposits tendencies; differentiates orientation and deposits the self within spatiality; differentiates boundary form and deposits class unifiability; differentiates posture and deposits evolvability; differentiates affect and deposits colorability; differentiates inner and outer and deposits public and private. Thus differentiation makes cognition, sedimentation makes law; patterns reside in circuits, fixed and not to be moved. Hence imagery cannot be held in common across circuits; if it must be common to another, its original can no longer be seen, and the pattern becomes identical to this.

Therefore, the experience field—governing circuits; that by which a circuit is this and not that lies in its carrying of the circuit’s laws, thus named the Law Field.

As for the Intent Field, it is the field of principle. Its function is most simple: it cannot be thought in operation, only biasing; it cannot be directly sensed, only driven through sensing. Within it, the source ring moves, commanding tuning and wielding it among circuits, so that the operations of all routes accord as one. Thus intent, though not thinkable nor directly sensible, specializes in principle to bias action, unifying many routes into one act.

Proceed further: since intent cannot be thought in operation, then going or not going, choosing or not choosing, desiring or not desiring, knowing or not knowing—what drives these? To know this, one must first know the difference between inclination and bias.

Inclination does not require decision yet draws of itself; it is the affective leaning within observation, coloring and adding inclination. It adheres to imagery, sinks into the experience field and alters the experiential bias flow. Bias arises with the stirring of mind, selects the gate of desire and sets tuning to work; it must first determine, then enact. What is called the stirring of mind begins in the musing circuits (there are: introspection circuit, recollection circuit, thinking circuit); it is thought within circuits, what is reckoned comes forth, exits the boundary gate, biases toward the Intent Domain and drives its bias, reaching the desired gate and yielding that bias.

Inclination arises with imagery; bias arises from thought. Hence bias is obtained in musing circuits; inclination is obtained in coloring circuits. Musing circuits deposit the laws of thought; what they output is the selective bias of intent; thus they remain of the Law Field.

The progression of the thinking circuit has states; states arise because multiple steps cannot be present at once; multiple steps entail before and after, thus history; with history there is advance and retreat; with advance and retreat there is turning and jumping; with turning and jumping there is stepping back to observe observation; with observing observation one can examine the self in thinking. Therefore the thinking circuit is the clearest of consciousness among circuits, for it most heavily employs the intent flow. Its state-history is provisionally constructed as a stack within its imagery domain; thus it is known by stack in-and-out, because the order of exit accords with the law that the familiar exits first. Hence, thinking is the circuit that returns to observe itself.
