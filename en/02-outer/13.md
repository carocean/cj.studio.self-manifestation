# Life and Death

Vast the cosmos — Causal-Response co-transforms without bound; its Trace-Response, in the observer, returns to one: called “Presence,” called “this,” called “that,” called “thing.”

Its transformations do not cease; one passes and again turns in Causal-Response and becomes in the cosmos. As to the observer, it loses its fixity; and that which is transformed re-enters the observer and is called “The Other.” Thus the original one is dead, and The Other is born.

Thus the affair of life and death is but a naming of Causal-Response; it is the axis of Trace-Response in the observer, thus named in two: life and death. Therefore, without an observer, there is no axis; there is only Trace-Response, and no use of the names life and death.

Hence, without an observer, life and death are not to be spoken of.

In the dim: Trace-Response gives rise to the Sedimentation-Field; sedimentation gives rise to Laws; Laws give rise to Circuits; Circuits give rise to Drive; Drive contends with Laws. In the mute: its coming is without clarity; the structure undergoes Contraction (see Inner Chapters, Observation Gate structures) to oppose it. That which is opposed is called “Presence,” that which is received back is called “I.” Therefore, the “Observation of Observation” says: “Presence and I arise mutually; I and Presence come forth together.” This is what is meant.

Thus Presence and I are born of contention; contention is born of Contraction. Contraction gives rise to Presence, and in turn gives rise to I; Contraction gives rise to I, and in turn gives rise to Presence. Therefore, without Presence, no I; without I, no Presence.

I and Presence are born in the course of Contraction, as flame ignites gas within a sealed cylinder. That I does not continue: one Contraction, one I arises. That I is said to continue: I can only verify I within the course of Contraction; thus as I appears, I is ever there.

Further, I is verified within Contraction, and cannot be verified in the course of the Contraction of The Other; therefore, two I’s do not verify one another, each bound within its Contraction.

As for the form of ghosts arising in the mind, it is my private domain; and to move my private domain into The Other, it must pass through Contraction and then arise mutually with The Other, becoming The Other’s private domain, becoming The Other’s I-as-private, thus formed as The Other’s I. Therefore, to take the persistence of the private as proof that I continues is like adding water to grasp a shadow.

Thus it is: if all my private domain be transferred to The Other, and further my Contraction be transferred to The Other, The Other’s Contraction gives rise to The Other-I; then all my private domain belongs to The Other-I. How would The Other-I know that this private belongs to my private? I dwell in my private domain; it dwells in its private domain. I and it do not communicate. By this I know that I cannot be copied.

Therefore, when two I’s meet, they cannot make one I; they must address as you and I.

If one presses to the extreme: one appearance, one cessation. At cessation, I cannot verify that I had appeared; at appearance, I cannot verify the emptiness at cessation. Thus one does not know that one appearance and one cessation are the dying and birthing of I; one does not know that I does not in fact continue.

Then, at cessation, if the lamp be changed—extinguishing the original Contraction mechanism, raising a new Contraction structure, and then wholly replacing the private—this is deemed the continuation of I; yet it is the arising of The Other-I, and the original I stops updating.

When the original I stops updating, its Contraction is no longer in use, no longer frequently produces the power of Contraction; this is called death.

Between appearance and cessation, to replace I with a new arising, to discard the old private and take up the new—relative to the old, this is forcedly called liberation.

Therefore, the way of liberation is to grasp it between appearance and cessation.

cj says: Life and death are the great affair of man, standing within the frequency of appearance and cessation. At cessation, do not take it as already perished; at appearance, do not take it as just born. Observing Presence, one takes I to be enduring; relying on the private, one takes I to be lasting; grieving and rejoicing over rise and fall, wealth and poverty, crying out over the pain of loss—like viewing a mirror within a mirror, one performs error therein, and does not know that I and I are entirely the same.

Entirely the same: relative to the opposing Presence, I arises. Awake! What does I bear, and where does I return!
