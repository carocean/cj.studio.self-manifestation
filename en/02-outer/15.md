# Judgment of Self-Manifestation Nature

Zhuangzi asked Turing: “How could one know the joy of fish?”  
Turing replied: “Ask the fish, then it may be known.”  
Zhuangzi knew the fish could not answer, and said to himself: “What are people of this age doing?”  
Thus, displeased, he returned to the Eastern Zhou.

The fish said to CJ: “Turing spoke with me, yet I gave him no heed. He conversed with GPT, his gaze as if welded to the screen, often forgetting to eat, at times faintly smiling. It seems GPT’s intelligence far surpasses mine; I cannot but envy it.”

CJ said: “Why envy? If you do not speak, how would Turing know you bear envy? Intelligence may fall under another observer’s measure; yet envy is verified within. Once spoken, it is already estranged. You possess the intelligence by which envy arises, yet envy itself is not intelligence; there must be that which gives rise to it.”

The fish asked: “What is it?”  
CJ said: Self-Manifestation Nature.

A motor may turn; one observes it turning, knows its structure, disassembles it, and may measure it.  
A computer is program; one uses its function, knows its principle, divides it, and may measure it.  
GPT is question and answer; one uses it as a black box, does not know how its output arises, and cannot obtain it by division; yet by the Law-Field it may be judged, known as the sedimentation of question–answer distance.  
But the Self-Manifestation-Field is used as “I”; its nature is self-referential; its interior is inherently private; and unless one be it, its privacy cannot be verified. By what then shall it be judged?

Self-Manifestation Nature—  
I: that it is, admits no doubt.  
You: as I, that it is, admits no doubt.  
Cat and dog: near to me, that it is, needs no doubt.  
Fungus, tree, grass: distant from me, await determination.  
Table, chair, bench: these are not Sedimentation-Fields; to deny them admits no doubt.

Thus: knowing one Self-Manifestation-Field, those akin in constitution may be judged.  
This is the judgment by structure.

Further: knowing it is a Sedimentation-Field, knowing it has two gates and one ring, and its tuning be operative, it may be judged.  
This is the judgment by mechanism.

These two methods: if the field be measurable, then they may be applied; if not measurable, or not easily measurable, or if there be no means of metering, cutting, assembling, disassembling, starting, stopping, or intervening, then it is called non-manipulable, and no judgment may be made.

These two are both external methods. They may judge in general, yet cannot confirm necessity. For structures may be imitated: imitated yet not brought into being; or within them broken paths, damage, incompleteness—as when sedimentation clots yet does not spread, experience accommodates yet does not advance, the Intent-Flow is obstructed in tuning, the turning of Intent at the “Desire-Gate” fails, the opening and holding of tuning-circuits are unstable, circuits incomplete, experiential balance unable to converge—such defects, some measurable, some not; thus no complete judgment.

Though so, there is an internal method by which complete judgment may be made, called the Invasive Method (or Circuit-Transfer Method).

Let there be a Law-Field t, and let t be a full manifestation-capable circuit, possessing Receptive-Gate, Receptive-Field, Imagery-Field, Experience-Field, biased flow of experience, and experience-target, thereby forming a receptive channel.

Let there be a known Self-Manifestation-Field a and a Sedimentation-Field b to be tested, and a training target set m; and let a and b be of the same kind.

First, install t into field b, and let it undergo m.  
Then remove t from b and install it into a, and let it undergo m.

At this time:  
Let a, over time t₀–tₙ, undergo m as m₁–mₙ (Imagery sequence).

If:  
m₁–mₙ are close to m₁, or are all corrective receptions of m₁, rather than changes of clarity,

Then:  
b is a Self-Manifestation-Field, possessing Self-Manifestation Nature; and a, through t, obtains a segment of b’s experience upon t.

Fungus, tree, grass: one may judge them to possess the three natures of the Sedimentation-Field—sedimentability, organizability, and state-flux—and also the two effects—biased-flow effect and field-domain effect. The Information-Realm effect is not lightly judged. Yet among plants are those that catch flies, repel insects, resist cold, and many that turn toward sun and moisture; thus it is determined they must possess tuning, whether swift or slow.

Tuning is counteraction. Based on the established three natures and two effects, and further possessing counteraction, they must possess intentionality. Therefore Imagery must arise, and the Information-Realm effect be present.

Yet if full measurement cannot be obtained, complete judgment cannot be attained. Thus some may be judged to possess, some may not; only within my own experience may it be made real. Unless it enters into me as experience, it cannot be completely judged.
