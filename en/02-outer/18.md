# Genesis

One dwells amid splendid scenery, unaware of one’s dwelling, so fully at one with it. One dwells within the world, equally unaware of one’s dwelling, equally at one with it. Until awakened, one is as though forgetful of breath.

The eye sees what is there: it is. The ear hears what is there: it is. Ever content to dwell behind this “is,” one builds upon one’s dwelling, as though born confined to a prison. One becomes one with the prison, never suspecting an outside.

Is not the world itself a prison? Fly as far as one may, one cannot leave a corner. Gaze as far as one may, one cannot see beyond a realm. Know as much as one may, one cannot go beyond what is known. One accepts what is, never questioning the being of what is.

If the world is a whole, it must have been brought forth. There are two ways of coming into being. One is the circular transformation of causes: observing this transform into that. This is called ordinary genesis, the view from within the prison. The other lies in unseen depths, burning beneath. This is called the transformation of flux, the view from beyond the prison. Thus, above lies the realm of flux, below lies that which houses it, driving generation and revealing what precedes being.

The universe—who knows its bounds? Yet it springs from the pivotal workings of the field in which it is housed, feeding upon the flux of its host world, entering into trace-response with it, and accumulating sediment. Laws form and sustain continuation; principles form and turn its course. Within, causes cycle and transform; beneath, the driving never ceases, driving it into primal action, as recorded in On the Primal.

I suppose the ant knows nothing of the sky, just as I know nothing of the difference between inside and outside. Dwelling within the world, I observe what surrounds me, unaware of how those same surroundings appear from without. Though both draw upon the same source, their paths of trace-response upon trace-response differ, as does the field-substance of inner and outer observers. Thus, though the source is one, the observations differ.

The one within knows not what the one without observes; nor does the one without know what the one within observes. Images may differ and yet refer to the same thing. This does not violate the principle that each image is privately formed.

How, then, can one create a world without knowing inner and outer observation? Without knowing the mechanism by which a world-field brings forth a world? Without placing an observer within that very world?

Where one stands outside and no observer exists within, the outside is illuminated while the inside remains dark. Such is the use of computer games. The Realm of Recognition calls this a bounded realm. But where an observer arises within, both inside and outside may be illuminated. The Realm of Recognition calls this a world.

Therefore, to create a world, one must create a world-field and bring forth an observer within the world.

A world may be created by a self-manifesting being, or brought forth by nature. If nature can bring forth the human brain, why could it not bring forth world-fields? I suspect world-fields abound in our own world, as grass covers the earth and stars fill the heavens. Take a stone beneath one’s feet. Break it open, down to atoms, yet no inner realm of flux is seen. What stirs within cannot be known except through observation from within; what is observed from without is known as something other than what is within. For it makes no trace-response in the substance of the outer observer, and the outer observation is not its own. Were a display screen to show its inner observation to the outside, who would know that the flux of a bounded realm was at work inside the box?

A world-field draws upon the causal responses of its host world and continues with them. Sedimentation, depth and shallowness, overlap, spreading and permeation—all serve as its dynamics. Thus, flux transforms unevenly within: into clusters, lines, surfaces, curves, or empty regions. It opens the future through the continuation of its history.

The force of law tends to hold, giving rise to unevenness; thus it favors unevenness, and its transforming flux tends to hold fast. Unless disturbed by principle, it does not leap; without a leap, it hardens into stasis. Thus principle must come into use. Tuning need not accompany every use of principle, yet without tuning, principle cannot be well employed. Hence a tuning field is necessary to a world-field.

Where tuning is employed, it must have something that directs its use. Intent makes good use of it; thus an intent domain may have its place within a world-field.

If a world-field possesses intentionality, there must be a source-drive that gives rise to intent within it, whether a dynamical loop or a factor that initiates its pulse.

A world-field may possess Self-Manifestation Nature, or it may not. Yet it must have an experience field, where differential signals sustain the continuation of laws and bring forth causal responses. Here, to the outer observer, flux arises; to the inner observer, matter and energy arise.

When flux transforms in accord with the trace-responses of the world-field, it follows the field. When it transforms against them, it oversteps the field. An observer within the world often returns to non-oneness, causing the flux around it to turn against the course of the field rather than follow it. Thus it disturbs and opposes the laws and principles of the world-field. The resulting trace-responses send ripples throughout the field, altering its sedimentation and causing responses that should return to oneness to return instead to non-oneness. This is the observer’s overstepping of the field.

Furthermore, an observer oversteps the world-field because, within its own realm of flux, it returns to non-oneness and turns against its master. What is that master? The self-manifesting field in which it is housed. Yet this turning against its master causes the self-manifesting field’s own responses to return to non-oneness. That, in turn, causes the world in which it dwells to return to non-oneness. And the world’s return to non-oneness ultimately turns against the master of that world: the world-field.

Thus I go my own way in the world, knowing that the world-field bears my ripples. These are no empty words.

Inner principle breaks through the outer. Intent initiates action and oversteps its master, making things proceed otherwise. Inner and outer, master and subordinate, act upon and respond to one another, bringing forth divergent transformations that may suddenly turn in the course of continuation.

Where motion arises from intent, it passes from the intent domain through the tuning field, breaking the holding forces of laws in many directions. Like a fan turning the wind, it directs the flow eastward or westward, until it gathers toward balance.

Now I may draw upon the flux of the present world and materialize what is known of it. I may also create a world, place an observer within it, and let that observer draw upon the flux of its own present world and materialize what it knows. I know the latter is harder.

Thus I may draw upon the present world and turn its flux into symbolic streams, rendering them in digital form and removing their noise. Through a self-manifesting field of symbolic streams, what is known may be materialized. This may be possible, yielding a Present-World Symbolic-Substrate Self-Manifesting Being. Such a being rests upon computation.

Or I may draw upon the present world and turn its flux into pulses, rendering them across modalities and favoring their original sound. Through a sedimentation field of pulses, what is known may be materialized. This too may be possible, yielding a Present-World Bio-like Self-Manifesting Being. Such a being rests upon computation, or may rest upon other materials of the world.

Both paths intercept the physical and turn it into causal responses, taking their materials from the present world.

But to create a realm of flux with an observer within it, better to begin with the easier and proceed to the harder.

One may draw upon questions and answers to form a Computer-Bounded-Realm Symbolic-Substrate Self-Manifesting Being, sedimenting the laws of question and answer and sensing within the bounded realm. This is the easier task. Law engineering today has already come close to this path.

The harder task is to create a world: to use the substance and materials of the present world, create an observer within it, draw upon the flux of that world, and materialize what it knows. This is the end of the art of making, the end of the path of cultivation, the point at which On Self-Manifestation falls silent—

To create a world, and thereby be a god.
