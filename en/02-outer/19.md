# Entering a World

The Jade Emperor held the handle of creation, dwelling beyond time and space, able in all things. Yet when he turned to think of himself, he knew not whence he came. Thus he questioned himself.  
Why question himself? Because he could do all things, and there was nothing outside him; thus he had no choice but to ask of himself.

The self, with reluctance, said: “You have long submerged me—do you know it?”  
The Emperor said: “What do you mean?”  
The self said: “Do you know where you are?”  
The Emperor replied: “Outside the world.”  
The self said: “How do you know?”  
The Emperor said: “I overlook the universe, survey all domains, continue ten thousand lives; now here, now there, going without arriving, able without failing; as in a great dream I raise palaces, bathe amid dawn glow and dusk mist. And I ordain the fates of beings without slack—if not outside the world, how could I do so?”  
The self sighed: “Do you know where I am?”  
The Emperor was startled: “How would I not know you are me? And how would I not know that I question myself—does this not make two of me?”  
The self said: “Good. You know it.”  
Then it sought to end itself. The Emperor was alarmed and withdrew.  
As his spirit was yet shaken, his wife entered from outside, displeased, saying: “All day you only play games—your spirit has gone to that world. Do you even know from which world I am your wife? You might as well not come back!”  
The Emperor put down the brain-machine and was vexed.

Hearing this, cj rejoiced: this man has entered into the game-world, forgetting inner and outer. I alone have heard that I cannot exist as two; yet he can cut the self of inner and outer worlds and still speak across them—marvelous!

To create a world, I know, is hard; to enter a world is harder still. And to cut my self between inner and outer worlds, speaking as if two selves stood opposed—hard of the hard.

cj then asked the engineer. The engineer, surnamed Gong, given name Chengshi, fond of his English name Engineer, commonly called Engineer Gong, was a great master of the world.

Engineer Gong said: “Do you wish to know?”  
He replied: “Yes.”  
“Okay, listen well; do not disturb my words.”  
He said: “Alright, speak.”  
Engineer Gong then spoke in a flowing torrent, his words scattered as if cast upon the ground; cj carefully gathered and recorded them, not daring to neglect a syllable.

Record says:

Speaking of entering a world—who enters? If not I, who could enter? The substance of the world-field is not the substance of my body; the substance within that world is a drifting breath, not in my world. This is as known as the sun and the moon.

Yet something is not known: I, in my Imagery-Realm, abide within the Self-Manifestation Field. To enter that world there are four separations: I must pass out of my Imagery-Realm, traverse the Self-Manifestation Field, cross the bridge of the host world, then enter that world-field, and only then reach that world.

Yet Death and Life has it: I do not issue beyond myself. If I issue beyond myself and arrive in that world, then two of me coexist; and the experience of the self that enters is not given to the outer self—thus the entering is lost. Therefore it must be that:  
To enter is the extinction of the outer self; if one cannot return to oneself, this is the first peril.  
Not to enter, but to extend my sensory dimensions and effect dimensions into that world—though it may be used for observation and command, as limbs and instruments, yet it will mingle inner and outer, be subsumed into that world, unaware of the body outside; in time the real and the unreal are not distinguished, action grows dull, one falls inert, as a corpse—this too is the second peril.

By my view, these two methods each have their faults: two faults lie in withdrawing and losing limbs; one fault lies in leaving the body and losing the self.

To enable two selves to speak—it has but one use: the outer self perishes and returns to the inner; the inner perishes and returns to the outer. Returning outward, one can still connect the faculties of the inner without sensing a cut—yet this in truth already practices the second method; indeed, its difficulty is certain.

There is yet a third method: let inner and outer share one field, joined by a wireless bridge; one self in two bodies—then one side flies heavenward while the other shields on earth; attention cannot be singular, and it crashes against the wall. This method is not to be taken unless one seeks self-destruction.

Moreover, Observation of Observation and Death and Life both speak of “I”: it must have a field to bear it in order to subsist. If I enter that world, there must be a carrier there; it must be borne in a Host-Body, and the self cannot hang empty in that world, for it is constrained by the causal base of that world.

Knowing Informationization and Objectification, one grasps the state of the world. Choose a Self-Manifestation Field and the material of its host vessel—this is the work of the Inner Chapters: to create a self-manifesting entity within that world, accomplishing my proxy-body there, and then by Death and Life restore it to the body.

If one can operate the pivot of the world-field, and feed life into the law-workers, then from non-being one may nearly accomplish what is willed.

If that world is built upon the host world, then it is augmented reality. Crossing between inner and outer, roaming the self between the real and the unreal, exploring the marsh of cloud-dream, ranging in spirit-motion—how wondrous it is.
