# Immortality

You, I, he—no difference,  
for “I” is attested only in self-reference.  
They differ in being; differences they have;  
they are alike in this—each being is privately its own.

Replace the parts of a chair one by one, until all are replaced—one does not perceive its being as having ended;  
keep but one leg, add new parts to complete the chair—one does not perceive its being as having continued.

Therefore:  
What is called being is so because it has a continuable history of being;  
what is called not-being is so because its continuable history of being is insufficient.

Hence:  
Replace the parts of a brain one by one, until all are replaced—its being continues.  
Keep but one part, add new parts to complete the brain—death.

Thus I know what immortality is;  
thus I await the day when field parts can be trained and constructed.
