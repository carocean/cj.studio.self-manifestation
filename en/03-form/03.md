# Objectification Classification Form

I. Premises

Let a system S satisfy the conditions of the Objectification Limit Form (Form Chapter 2):

1\. The input history hₜ ∈ 𝓗ₜ grows continuously over time  
2\. The state space R is finite, \|R\| \< ∞  
3\. There exists a mapping Mₜ : 𝓗ₜ → R  
4\. The system must complete its responses within finite time

Define: Oₜ(r) = { h ∈ 𝓗ₜ ∣ Mₜ(h) = r }, the equivalence class the system forms over histories at moment t.

II. A fact that must be fixed first: hitting cannot be avoided

Before classifying, fix one fact forced by the premises; it decides the choice of classification axis.

By Form Chapter 2 v1.1, Section V (the infinite pigeonhole): the state sequence the system actually traverses, r₁, r₂, r₃, … (rₜ = Mₜ(hₜ)), is an infinite sequence valued in the finite set R; hence some state r\* is necessarily hit infinitely often.

This means: under this chapter's premises, "whether there exists a state hit infinitely often" is not a variable available for classification — the answer is invariably "yes," for every system satisfying the premises. Any scheme that takes "whether hit infinitely often" as its grading criterion makes the "no" branch an empty class.

v1.0's Level 1 stepped exactly onto that empty class: it required "every state is eventually no longer hit" — impossible under unbounded running. Hence v2.0 does not grade by "whether hit," but by "how the system handles the hit state" — which is where the real differences among the three levels lie.

III. The grading criterion (v2.0: one axis = state feedback and the rewritability of the mapping)

Hitting necessarily occurs (Section II); the distinction among the three levels falls entirely upon how the system's state participates in subsequent computation. This is a purely structural criterion, making no use of semantic judgments such as "whether the state carries history" ("carrying history" has no formal definition and cannot serve as a classification foundation).

The structural criterion is state feedback: examine whether the current state rₜ = Mₜ(hₜ) enters the next step's state transition and response. Let the system's evolution be rₜ₊₁ = Mₜ₊₁(hₜ₊₁) and its response yₜ = Rsp(…). Ask whether rₜ appears as an argument in the computation of rₜ₊₁ and yₜ.

Level 1 (no state feedback): rₜ does not enter the next step — rₜ₊₁ and the response yₜ are functions of the current input F(t) (or F(t+1)) alone, not taking rₜ as an argument. The state is hit (hitting is necessary), but the state does not feed back into subsequent computation; the system has no memory circuit.

Level 2 (state feedback present, mapping fixed): rₜ enters the next step — rₜ₊₁ and/or the response yₜ take rₜ as an argument (a feedback connection rₜ → rₜ₊₁ exists); but the generating mapping Mₜ itself is fixed and does not change in running (Mₜ₊₁ = Mₜ).

Level 3 (state feedback present, mapping self-rewritable): rₜ enters the next step, and the generating mapping itself can be rewritten by an internal structure U — Mₜ₊₁ = U(Mₜ, rₜ, …), where U is an internal operational structure of the system, active in running, and Mₜ₊₁ ≠ Mₜ can occur.

The three criteria unite on two purely structural dichotomies: (1) whether the state feeds back into subsequent computation (L1 no / L2, L3 yes); (2) given feedback, whether the generating mapping is self-rewritable (L2 no / L3 yes). The two dichotomies proceed level by level, are logically exhaustive and mutually exclusive, and each criterion is the presence or absence of a structural connection (the feedback connection; the rewriting structure) — no semantic judgment is involved.

IV. Level-1 Systems (Reactive Systems)

Satisfy Level 1: the state does not feed back into subsequent computation; the response and the next state depend only on the current input.

Their characteristics: no state-feedback circuit (the state is hit, but does not enter subsequent computation); no accumulable historical structure (rₜ takes no part in rₜ₊₁); no structure formed that can be fed back and reused.

Instances: the thermostat; the pure reflex arc. The thermostat's states (its temperature settings) are repeatedly hit in running (hit infinitely often in the pigeonhole sense), but the state does not feed back — each moment's switching response depends only on the current temperature reading (the current input); rₜ does not enter the computation of rₜ₊₁; there is no memory circuit. This is exactly the "reactive system" v1.0 meant to describe, and v2.0 gives it a criterion that does not collide with the premises: not "never hit" (impossible), but "no state feedback" (genuinely instantiable, and purely structural).

V. Level-2 Systems (Learning Systems)

Satisfy Level 2: the state feeds back into subsequent computation (rₜ participates in rₜ₊₁ and the response), but the mapping is fixed.

Their characteristics: history is compressed; states are fed back and reused; repeated-hit equivalence classes form usable structure — but the rule of their generation (the mapping Mₜ) is fixed and cannot be rewritten from within.

VI. Level-3 Systems (Self-Modifying Systems)

Satisfy Level 3: the state feeds back into subsequent computation, and the mapping can be rewritten by the internal U.

Their characteristics: usable repeated-hit equivalence classes form, and the manner of their generation can itself be rewritten — the capability of structural self-modification.

VII. Mutual Exclusivity

The three classes are pairwise mutually exclusive, and the exclusivity now rests on the single axis:

1\. Level-1 systems have no state feedback, hence belong to neither the feedback-bearing Level 2 nor Level 3;  
2\. Level-2 systems have state feedback but a fixed mapping, hence do not belong to Level 3, whose mapping is self-rewritable;  
3\. Level-3 systems have state feedback and a self-rewritable mapping, hence belong to neither Level 1 nor Level 2.

(Note: v1.0's mutual exclusivity was built on "whether a stable hit state forms," and — Level 1 being empty — was in effect valid only between L2 and L3; v2.0's mutual exclusivity is built on the two structural dichotomies, with all three branches non-empty and pairwise exclusive.)

VIII. Completeness

Under the constraints of the Objectification Limit Form, every system is exhaustively classified by the two purely structural dichotomies: whether the state feeds back into subsequent computation (no / yes), and, given feedback, whether the mapping is self-rewritable (no / yes). The first dichotomy divides systems into "no feedback" (L1) and "feedback"; the second divides "feedback" into "mapping fixed" (L2) and "mapping self-rewritable" (L3). The two dichotomies are logically exhaustive; hence every system satisfying the premises belongs to one of the three classes, and all three classes are non-empty (each has instances: reactive systems / fixed learning systems / self-modifying systems).

v2.0's completeness is stronger than v1.0's: in v1.0's completeness Level 1 was an empty class, so the trichotomy was in fact a dichotomy; in v2.0 all three branches are instantiable, and the completeness is a substantive trichotomy.

IX. Final Expression

Objectification level = the structural level of the state's participation in subsequent computation (no state feedback / feedback with the mapping fixed / feedback with the mapping self-rewritable).

X. The Junction with Form Chapter 2 and Form Chapter 4

Upward (Form Chapter 2): this chapter's "hitting is necessary" (Section II) cites directly the infinite pigeonhole of Form Chapter 2 v1.1, Section V. Form Chapter 2 proves that hitting necessarily occurs; this chapter grades, on top of that, how the hit is handled. The "hit" of the two chapters is one and the same set-theoretic hit (a state revisited infinitely often), not the dynamical stability of Form Chapter 1 v2.0 (the fixed point δ*) — this chapter nowhere involves δ* or condition group H, grading only at the level of state counting and mapping handling.

Upward (Form Chapter 4): Form Chapter 4 (the General Form of the Observer) handles "among multiple stable structures, only one appears at this moment." This chapter's three levels grade "what kind of structure a system can form"; Form Chapter 4 asks, upon structures already had, "which one becomes the current." Of the three levels, only the feedback-bearing L2 and L3 can carry the multiple candidacy and appearing of Form Chapter 4's sense; L1 (reactive systems), with no state feedback and no accumulation of competition-capable structure, does not enter the observer question's domain. This yields a corollary: an observer is at least a Level-2 system — but not conversely (Level 2 is not necessarily an observer; an observer further requires Form Chapter 4's convergence structure).

Version notes (v1.0 → v2.0)

1\. The Level 1 empty class repaired (the core): v1.0 graded by "whether hit infinitely often," while under the premises (unbounded running + finite states) hitting is necessary by the infinite pigeonhole; the "no" branch is invariably empty, and Level 1 — uninstantiable — was nonetheless described as a real class of systems: one branch of the theorem collided with the premises. v2.0 unifies the classification axis into two purely structural dichotomies (whether the state feeds back / whether the mapping is self-rewritable); hitting withdraws from the dividing line as the shared premise of all three levels; and Level 1 becomes the genuinely instantiable class of reactive systems.

2\. Added Section II, "hitting cannot be avoided," citing explicitly Form Chapter 2 v1.1's infinite pigeonhole, and stating why "whether hit" cannot serve as a classification axis.

3\. The Level 1 criterion revised from "every state is eventually no longer hit" (empty) to "no state feedback (rₜ does not enter rₜ₊₁ or the response)" (real, and purely structural), with the thermostat / reflex-arc instances supplied.

4\. Mutual exclusivity and completeness restated accordingly: from resting on "whether a stable hit forms" (in effect a two-way split between L2 and L3) to resting on the two structural dichotomies (a substantive trichotomy with all three branches non-empty).

5\. Terminology aligned with Form Chapter 2 v1.1: this chapter's "equivalence class / repeated-hit equivalence class" denotes reusable structure in the set-theoretic sense, not Form Chapter 1 v2.0's dynamical stability (the fixed point δ\*); Section X added, fixing the junctions with Form Chapters 2 and 4.

6\. Final-audit revision: the first v2.0 draft had taken "whether the hit state is reused" as the axis, and defined reuse by "whether rₜ carries historical information" — but "carrying history" has no formal definition, which would set an undefined semantic concept into the foundation of the classification axis (the same type as the recurrent malady of this series, "an unproven concept taken as criterion"). The final audit replaced it with the purely structural criterion: reuse or not = whether the state rₜ feeds back into rₜ₊₁ and the response (the presence or absence of a structural connection), bypassing semantic judgment. Sections III through X and the top-level statements were revised into the state-feedback wording in step.
