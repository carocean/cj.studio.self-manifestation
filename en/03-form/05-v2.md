# Observer Limit Form

Master Proposition

The General Form of the Observer has proven:

CurrentFact ⇒ F ∧ M ∧ \[Composite ⇒ I\].

Where:

F is member constitution;

I is the jointly-being-one of a composite current;

M is object-side identity bearing across a non-zero time window. This Form no longer proves these three duties.

This Form examines only:

To where can CurrentFact still be reduced?

This Form proves:

Along the three proven directions of responsibility — member constitution, composite jointly-being-one, and object-side identity — the presently proven reduction boundaries of CurrentFact stop respectively at F, I, and M.

Candidate facts cannot substitute for member constitution; juxtaposed facts cannot substitute for composite jointly-being-one; static sameness cannot substitute for object-side identity; determinate or random outcomes alone cannot substitute for the responsibilities they have not realized.

1\. The Reduction Criterion

Let W be a kind of reduction structure.

Call W a pure reduction if and only if:

1\. W gives only candidates, outcomes, juxtaposition, stability, or the manner of outcome generation;

2\. the formal output of W does not directly contain the responsibility under test;

3\. W does not take the responsibility under test as a defining condition.

This Form uses:

W ⇏ X to mean:

within the working domain of this Form, there exists a permitted structure in which W holds while X does not.

Therefore:

W ⇏ X means W cannot alone guarantee X; it does not mean W and X exclude each other. The unified countermodel criterion is:

~~~text
if there exist permitted structures X₁, X₂ such that:

W(X₁) = W(X₂), while:
~~~

R(X₁) ≠ R(X₂), where R ∈ {F, I, M}, then:

W ⇏ R.

If:

W ⇒ CurrentFact, then by the General Form of the Observer necessarily:

W ⇒ F ∧ M ∧ \[Composite ⇒ I\].

Hence any reduction structure that cannot guarantee any one of these necessary responsibilities cannot alone constitute CurrentFact.

2\. Candidate Reduction

Candidates are the antecedent objects the General Form of the Observer itself established. Let:

C\_t^\* = {c : Comp\_t(c) = 1} be the candidate completion set at moment t.

Candidate activity, candidate completion, candidate maintenance, candidate decay, and candidate switching all give candidate-layer facts only.

For F: there can exist two permitted structures with the same candidate activity and completion sets.

In structure A, a further non-arbitrary relation within the system determines which candidates constitute the current H, hence:

F(A) = 1.

In structure B, the same candidate set is only listed as a roster by an external observer, and no fact within the system determines its membership boundary, hence: F(B) = 0.

And yet:

candidate facts(A) = candidate facts(B).

Therefore:

candidate facts ⇏ F.

For I: fix:

F = 1; Composite = 1.

There can exist two permitted structures with the same candidate membership, activity, completion, and maintenance facts, but in one structure the members jointly become one current, while in the other the members merely stand in juxtaposition.

Therefore:

candidate facts ∧ F ∧ Composite ⇏ I.

For M: fix:

F = 1; Composite ⇒ I.

There can exist two permitted structures with the same moment-by-moment candidate state trajectories, but one structure is sustainedly borne by one and the same object-side current, while the other merely regenerates isomorphic states at each moment. Therefore:

candidate facts ∧ F ∧ \[Composite ⇒ I\] ⇏ M.

Hence:

candidate facts ⇏ CurrentFact.

Even where candidate dynamics reach any permitted state, candidate-layer facts alone still cannot cross the structural boundary of the current.

3\. Determinate Reduction

Let:

D\_cand = f(Γ\_t) mean that candidate outcomes are produced from the explicit state by a determinate mapping. Define:

Det\_cand^−F as the determinate reduction that only determinately produces candidate or completion outcomes without prescribing the member constitution of the current.

Define:

Det^−I as the determinate reduction that may have completed member constitution but does not prescribe composite jointly-being-one.

Define:

Det^−M as the determinate reduction that may have completed member constitution and, in the composite case, jointly-being-one, but does not prescribe object-side identity bearing. A determinate mapping may output only candidate or completion sets, or it may directly complete in-system member assignment.

If it outputs only candidate or completion sets, then:

Det\_cand^−F ⇏ F.

If it has non-arbitrarily settled the current membership within the system, then F can hold; but determinacy itself still cannot entail composite jointly-being-one.

Fix:

F = 1; Composite = 1.

There can exist two permitted realizations with the same determinate member output, but in one realization the composite members jointly become one current, while in the other the members are merely listed side by side. Therefore:

Det^−I ∧ F ∧ Composite ⇏ I.

Fix further:

F = 1; Composite ⇒ I.

There can exist two permitted realizations with the same moment-by-moment determinate output, but one realization is sustainedly borne by one and the same object-side current, while the other only reproduces the same output repeatedly. Therefore:

Det^−M ∧ F ∧ \[Composite ⇒ I\] ⇏ M.

Hence:

determinate outcome rules alone ⇏ CurrentFact.

A determinate mechanism can realize a current, but determinacy itself is not the current.

4\. Random Reduction

Let:

D\_cand ∼ P(·\|Γ\_t) mean that candidate outcomes are produced by a probabilistic process.

Define:

Rand\_cand^−F as the random reduction that only gives a candidate outcome distribution or a single candidate outcome without prescribing the member constitution of the current.

Define:

Rand^−I as the random reduction that may have completed member settlement but does not prescribe composite jointly-being-one.

Define:

Rand^−M as the random reduction that may have completed member settlement and, in the composite case, jointly-being-one, but does not prescribe object-side identity bearing. A random process may give only a candidate outcome distribution, or it may actually settle the current membership within the system.

If it gives only a candidate outcome distribution or a single candidate outcome, then: Rand\_cand^−F ⇏ F.

If the random process has non-arbitrarily completed member settlement within the system, then F can hold; but randomness itself still cannot entail composite jointly-being-one.

Fix:

F = 1; Composite = 1.

There can exist two permitted realizations with the same random membership outcome, but in one realization the composite members jointly become one current, while in the other the members merely stand in juxtaposition. Therefore:

Rand^−I ∧ F ∧ Composite ⇏ I.

Fix further:

F = 1; Composite ⇒ I.

There can exist two permitted realizations with the same moment-by-moment outcome distribution or the same sampling sequence, but one realization is sustainedly borne by one and the same object-side current, while the other only rebuilds the same state moment by moment. Therefore:

Rand^−M ∧ F ∧ \[Composite ⇒ I\] ⇏ M.

Hence:

random outcome rules alone ⇏ CurrentFact.

A random mechanism can realize a current, but randomness itself is not the current.

5\. Juxtaposition Reduction

The juxtaposed facts of this Form mean only that several constituents exist at once, act at once, complete at once, or persist at once; system-side member assignment is not included.

For F: there can exist two permitted structures with entirely the same juxtaposed facts.

In structure A, a further non-arbitrary relation within the system determines which juxtaposed constituents constitute the current, hence:

F(A) = 1.

In structure B, the many merely exist at once, and no fact within the system determines their membership boundary, hence:

F(B) = 0.

And yet:

juxtaposed facts(A) = juxtaposed facts(B).

Therefore:

juxtaposed facts ⇏ F.

The core gap of juxtaposition reduction falls at I.

Fix:

F = 1; Composite = 1.

There can exist two permitted structures with the same member roster and juxtaposed states, but in one structure the members jointly become one current, while in the other the members are merely many-existing-together. Therefore:

juxtaposed facts ∧ F ∧ Composite ⇏ I.

Fix further:

F = 1; Composite ⇒ I.

There can exist two permitted structures with the same cross-time juxtaposed states, but in one structure the composite current is sustainedly borne by one and the same object-side structure, while in the other the several members merely persist severally or are rebuilt moment by moment.

Therefore:

juxtaposed facts ∧ F ∧ \[Composite ⇒ I\] ⇏ M.

Hence:

juxtaposed facts ⇏ CurrentFact.

Appearing together is not being one together.

6\. Static Reduction

Let a neutral state carrier X satisfy, within a non-zero time window I\_t: ObsState(X\_τ) = ObsState(X\_τ′) for all τ, τ′ ∈ I\_t.

This means only that the observable state values or structural descriptions at different temporal positions are the same.

For F: there can exist two permitted structures with the same static observable states.

In structure A, a further non-arbitrary relation within the system determines which constituents constitute the current, hence:

F(A) = 1.

In structure B, though the observable states are the same, the so-called membership range is given only by external description, and no fact within the system determines its membership boundary, hence:

F(B) = 0.

And yet:

static facts(A) = static facts(B).

Therefore:

static facts ⇏ F.

For I: fix:

F = 1; Composite = 1.

There can exist two permitted structures with the same static member states, but in one structure the composite members jointly become one current, while in the other the members are merely juxtaposed and stable. Therefore:

static facts ∧ F ∧ Composite ⇏ I.

For M: fix:

F = 1; Composite ⇒ I.

There can exist two permitted structures with entirely the same observable state sequences, but one structure is sustainedly borne by one and the same object-side current, while the other merely regenerates isomorphic states at each moment. Therefore:

static facts ∧ F ∧ \[Composite ⇒ I\] ⇏ M.

Hence:

static facts ⇏ CurrentFact.

Sameness is not being the same one.

7\. The Responsibility-Absorption Theorem

Theorem. Whatever mechanism can form CurrentFact, by the General Form of the Observer,

must guarantee:

F ∧ M ∧ \[Composite ⇒ I\].

This is an unconditional logical conclusion.

If one further inherits the realizational precondition that the necessary responsibilities must be actually borne by the system's internal structure, then any successful mechanism also necessarily realizes these responsibilities in some role-equivalent way.

Therefore:

if a so-called weaker mechanism cannot guarantee any one of these responsibilities, it cannot constitute CurrentFact; and if a so-called weaker mechanism can constitute CurrentFact, it is no longer a reduction of CurrentFact, but merely another realization of F, I, M.

QED.

8\. The Observer Reduction-Limit Theorem

Theorem. Let W be any pure reduction structure.

If:

W ⇏ F, or:

W ∧ F ∧ Composite ⇏ I, or:

W ∧ F ∧ \[Composite ⇒ I\] ⇏ M, then:

W ⇏ CurrentFact.

Therefore:

along the three responsibility directions tested in this Form, the presently proven reduction boundaries of CurrentFact stop respectively at:

F; Composite ⇒ I; M.

Candidate facts cannot substitute for member constitution; juxtaposed facts cannot substitute for composite jointly-being-one; static sameness cannot substitute for object-side identity. Determinate or random outcomes alone cannot substitute for the responsibilities they have not realized.

This theorem does not declare that F, I, M have exhausted all necessary duties of CurrentFact, nor that they constitute the unique formal expression. This theorem proves only:

every responsibility the General Form of the Observer has forced out cannot be substituted by the weaker facts above.

QED.

9\. Breach Modes

Any of the following holding breaches the corresponding limit conclusion:

1\. candidate activity or candidate completion, without guaranteeing F, still suffices to determine the membership range of CurrentFact;

2\. with F = 1 and Composite = 1, juxtaposed activity or juxtaposed completion, without guaranteeing I, still suffices to make the composite members jointly one current;

3\. with F = 1 and Composite ⇒ I, static stability, state similarity, or repeated generation, without guaranteeing M, still suffices to make the earlier and later states one and the same maintained current;

4\. some purely determinate, purely random, candidate, juxtaposed, or static structure, unable to guarantee any one of the necessary responsibilities F, I, M, still strictly constitutes CurrentFact.

11\. Closing

The General Form of the Observer has proven:

a maintained current requires at least member constitution, composite jointly-being-one, and object-side identity bearing.

This Form presses on downward along the direction of reduction.

Candidates can give only candidate facts;

juxtaposition can give only many-existing-together;

the static can give only sameness of state.

Determinacy and randomness prescribe only the manner of outcome generation; unless their concrete mechanisms otherwise realize the corresponding responsibilities, CurrentFact cannot be derived from determinacy or randomness themselves.

Any mechanism truly able to form CurrentFact necessarily guarantees the necessary responsibilities the General Form has forced out; under the realizational precondition, it also necessarily bears these responsibilities actually through the system's internal structure.

Therefore:

along the three proven responsibility directions of member constitution, composite jointly-being-one, and object-side identity, CurrentFact cannot be further reduced to candidate, outcome, juxtaposed, or static facts.
