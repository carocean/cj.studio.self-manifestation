# Objectification-Current Limit Form

This chapter attempts a general proposition:

Upon the base where Objectification is already established, any system capable of forming and maintaining the unique current necessarily realizes six structures — discrimination, orientation source, orientation entry, driving, differential action, and convergence — and satisfies the order constraint that discrimination precedes convergence. This chapter gives the step-by-step chain of derivation for that universal necessity.

The division of labor with the adjacent Forms:

The Observer Group forces out, by forcing-out argumentation, the necessary direction of the three duties at the observer-current layer, and lists unique occupancy as the pool-level current single-value principle (a constitutive commitment, unproven); the Observer Limit Form checks by reduction-limit that the observer mechanism is irreducible (the four reduction proposals as diagnostic instances of the universal theorem). This chapter sets out from the Objectification base and gives the step-by-step derivation of the six structures' necessity under the task condition — this chapter no longer calls itself "the derivation-chain version of Form Chapter 4's general theorem (ii)" (that general theorem no longer exists; its universal-necessity section has no successor), but holds a derivation chain of its own at the objectification-current layer: starting from the task condition "the system must form and maintain unique occupancy under multiple candidacy and the determinacy gap," each indispensable structure is derived in turn. The three bear one another up: the Observer Group gives "it exists and is unique" (both existence and uniqueness resting on the commitment of the pool-level current single-value principle), the Observer Limit Form gives "it has no other way," and this chapter gives "why it must be composed of exactly these parts."

Note on the scope taken up: this chapter derives from the point where the candidate set 𝒞ₜ is already formed, and so derives only the six items T, R, P, D, L, K; the necessity of candidate exposure C and candidate domain-restriction B lies at the formation of the candidate set — C generates candidates from stable structures, input compression, and inner unfolding (the candidate-running definitions of the Observer Mathematical Form), and B is the qualification gate of the Observation-Domain (Form Chapter 22): stable structures must pass the qualification domain before entering competition. Objectification gives the can-be; the Observation-Domain gives the can-enter-appearing; what this chapter derives is the generative structure of the "is-here" after admission.

One sentence:

Stability answers "what can be there"; this chapter answers "from can-be to is-here, which parts the middle must contain."

I. Premise Inheritance

The Objectification Limit Form has proven:

Mₜ : 𝓗ₜ → R

Under finite state constraints and continuous input, history necessarily undergoes compression, truncation, and reuse, forming the set of stable structures R.

The observer question is built upon this: when among the stable structures there exist multiple candidates for the current, it must be explained how "at this moment, this one" comes to stand.

Let the candidate set be 𝒞ₜ ⊆ R, with \|𝒞ₜ\| ≥ 2. The candidates have already been admitted through the qualification domain (Form Chapter 22: the two gates Cand and Adm); this chapter does not re-derive that step.

II. Basic Setup

At moment t the system satisfies:

1\. The candidate set 𝒞ₜ is composed of stable structures, and \|𝒞ₜ\| ≥ 2;

2\. The task condition: under sustained continuation pressure (the Observer Group's first premise: occupancy must serve continuation), the system forms and maintains the maintained unique occupancy, denoted c*ₜ, Occₜ(c*ₜ) = 1 (occupancy includes persistence; candidate completion must be sustained through the completion window, Observer Mathematical Form, Definition 45). The existence and uniqueness of this condition are not posited in this chapter — existence and uniqueness rest on the pool-level current single-value principle (a constitutive commitment, unproven, open to empirical breach), while the joint oneness of the constituents is given unconditionally by duty I of the General Form of the Observer. Should the commitment be breached, the task condition is rewritten, but the derivation chain itself is unaffected, since the derivation depends only on the content of the task condition and not on the grade of its upstream. This chapter takes them as the task condition and derives the structures necessary to accomplish the task, listing uniqueness as an axiom in no form whatsoever;

3\. The current explicit conditions are Γₜ;

4\. The determinacy gap: there exists no mapping f : Γₜ → 𝒞ₜ such that c\*ₜ = f(Γₜ);

5\. The system must form and maintain the current occupancy within finite time.

III. Form of the Problem

Under the conditions above, the system must generate from 𝒞ₜ a c*ₜ ∈ 𝒞ₜ satisfying Occₜ(c*ₜ) = 1, and hold that occupancy relation over a finite time interval.

What this chapter must prove: the structure-set realizing this task is forced out by the task itself — with any one item missing, the task cannot be accomplished.

IV. The Division from the Exclusion Arguments

The exclusion of the four reduction proposals — deterministic mapping, stochastic process, parallel activation, and static structure — has been completed by Form Chapter 5 (universal necessity load-bearing; the four classes demoted to diagnostic instances with missing-item locations attached); this chapter does not repeat it. Only the three citations directly relevant to this chapter's derivation are retained:

First, deterministic mapping is not available: this is setup condition 4 (the determinacy gap), which is also the premise side of Form Chapter 5's Exclusion Path One (R lacks independence).

Second, stochastic process is not available: random drawing has neither sustained bias nor convergence maintenance; within any Δt \> 0 there is no guarantee of Occ\_τ(c\*ₜ) = 1, failing the persistence definition of occupancy (Form Chapter 5, Exclusion Path Two; D and K lacking). A distinction must be kept: what is excluded here is the mechanism whose device is drawing; the case of noise-seeding locked in by feedback possesses D and K, does not belong to this class, and its exclusion is given separately at Step Two of the derivation chain (it violates the continuation premise).

Third, simultaneous parallelism is impossible: this is not an exclusion of this chapter's own, but an inherited upstream clause — within one tuning-resource pool the maintained occupancies number at most one, which is now the pool-level current single-value principle (a constitutive commitment, unproven). It must be recorded truthfully that simultaneous double occupancy is not excluded dynamically: the Observer Mathematical Form, Theorem 8, has proven that a retention state of double completion can exist, so the former Lemma 3's "parameter gate and dynamical gate, doubly secured" no longer holds; the impossibility of juxtaposition is now borne by the commitment, and should the commitment be breached this clause falls with it. At no step does this chapter invoke uniqueness itself to exclude its contrary; the exclusions are borne wholly by the upstream clauses and the task condition — hence no circle.

The unavailability of static structure itself needs no exclusion argument; one sentence states it: stability says only that a structure "can be there," not that it "is currently there"; when \|𝒞ₜ\| ≥ 2, stability contributes nothing to "why this structure becomes the current" (Form Chapter 5, Exclusion Path Four: the entire generative chain lacking).

V. The Chain of Derivation

What follows is the body of this chapter: from the task condition, the necessity of the six structures is derived step by step. Each step has the same form — suppose the structure missing, and prove the task unaccomplishable.

Step One: the necessity of discrimination (T)

If the candidate set 𝒞ₜ remains of equal standing throughout the system's evolution, only three outcomes can appear: parallel coexistence, random rotation, or sustained drift. Parallel coexistence is excluded by the inherited theorem (Section IV, third citation); random rotation fails the persistence of occupancy; sustained drift lets no candidate reach and stay at occupancy. None of the three accomplishes the task.

Therefore: equal standing must be broken; denote the relation that bears the breaking by ≺, and the structure realizing ≺ is the discrimination structure T. The source of ≺ is itself under a double constraint — see the next two steps: it can be neither a function of Γₜ nor noise.

Step Two: the necessity of the orientation source (R)

One side: if the relation ≺ is given wholly by Γₜ and its functional extensions, there exists an equivalent mapping f : Γₜ → 𝒞ₜ with c\*ₜ = f(Γₜ) — contradicting setup condition 4.

The other side: if ≺ is given by noise alone — note that noise-breaking is not dynamically impossible: random fluctuation, amplified and locked by positive feedback, can indeed form and maintain an occupancy. The ground of its exclusion lies not in maintenance but in the other half of the task condition: the winner selected by noise bears no relation to the system's state and continuation; such occupancy has no action coverage (the action-coverage criterion of the Observer Adjudication Form's criterion grid) and constitutes the cheap-observer form excluded by cheap-realization exclusion (Observer Realization Form, Section 4) — contradicting the sustained-continuation premise of setup condition 2.

Therefore: ≺ is neither a function of Γₜ nor noise; discrimination must contain a source that introduces, beyond the current explicit conditions, a new partial order coupled to continuation. That component is the orientation source R (its independence requirement now stands in the Strengthened Form of Current Generation: R is listed in the candidate placement table, resting on the supplementary proof of its H5).

Step Three: the necessity of orientation entry (P)

If the orientation source takes no part in the candidates' comparison process, the relation among candidates remains wholly decided by Γₜ and the candidate structures; the system falls back to the deterministic-mapping case — contradicting setup condition 4.

Therefore: orientation must enter the domain of candidate competition. The structure bearing this entry is the orientation-entry structure P.

Step Four: the necessity of driving (D)

If orientation acts only instantaneously, the candidates' relative advantage cannot be pushed forward continuously; there exists τ \> t with Occ\_τ(c\*ₜ) ≠ 1 — contradicting the persistence condition of occupancy.

Therefore: orientation's action must persist over a finite time interval. The structure bearing this persistence is the driving structure D.

Step Five: the necessity of differential action (L)

If orientation acts wholly alike upon all candidates, the relative relations among candidates do not change; equal standing is not broken, falling back to the impossible case of Step One.

Therefore: orientation must exert differential action upon the candidates. The structure bearing this differential allotment is the scope-of-action limitation structure L.

Step Six: the necessity of convergence (K)

Even if some candidate prevails in the competition, advantage itself is not occupancy: occupancy is the maintained completion-state (z = 1 and sustained through the completion window, Observer Mathematical Form, Definition 45). If there is no structure that pushes advantage to balance and fixes it as the current, there exists τ \> t with Occ\_τ(c\*ₜ) ≠ 1.

Therefore: there must exist within the system the convergence structure K, which pushes the outcome of advantage forward and fixes it as the current.

Step Seven: the order constraint (T ≺ K)

If convergence preceded discrimination, then: either all candidates are fixed simultaneously — contradicting the inherited theorem (at most one); or some candidates are presupposed prior — and that priority is itself discrimination, the order remaining discrimination-first.

Therefore: T ≺ K.

VI. Main Conclusion

Where the Objectification Limit Form is established, and:

𝒞ₜ ⊆ R, \|𝒞ₜ\| ≥ 2 (candidates admitted through the Observation-Domain)  
the task condition: forming and maintaining the maintained unique occupancy (its holding resting on the pool-level current single-value principle, a constitutive commitment; the joint oneness of its constituents given by duty I of the General Form of the Observer)  
¬∃ f : Γₜ → 𝒞ₜ such that c\*ₜ = f(Γₜ)

then the system necessarily satisfies:

there exists a discrimination structure T  
there exists an orientation source R  
there exists an orientation-entry structure P  
there exists a driving structure D  
there exists a scope-of-action limitation structure L  
there exists a convergence structure K

with T ≺ K.

These structures bear, respectively, the functions of candidate differentiation, orientation introduction, action entry, sustained driving, difference formation, and outcome fixation; their joint working composes the generative path from candidate set to unique current. Together with C and B at the candidates' formation (the note on scope), this is the eight-word functional group listed in the candidate placement table of the Strengthened Form of Current Generation (of which C, B, K, and D are settled, while T, L, R, and P rest on the supplementary proof of its H5) — this chapter has supplied, at the objectification-current layer, the derivation chain for six of them, and for that universal necessity.

VII. Minimal Expression

Under Objectification established: Mₜ : 𝓗ₜ → R.

If 𝒞ₜ ⊆ R, \|𝒞ₜ\| ≥ 2, the task condition holds, and ¬∃ f : Γₜ → 𝒞ₜ, then there exists the observer generative mechanism Φₜ (one and the same mechanism as Form Chapter 5's Φₜ; its full characterization in Form Chapters 4 and 5), such that:

Φₜ(𝒞ₜ) = c\*ₜ

VIII. Final Conclusion

The Objectification Limit Form gives the necessary formation of stable structures; the Observation-Domain gives the admission boundary of stable structures; this chapter and the Observer Group give, at their respective layers: after admission, the generation of the unique current is necessarily accomplished by the six structures working jointly — this chapter treating the necessity of structures at the objectification-current layer, the Observer Group the necessity of duties at the observer-current layer, the two differing in layer and supporting each other.

Therefore:

stable structures give the "can-be,"  
the Observation-Domain gives the "can-enter-appearing,"  
observer-structure generates the "is-here."
