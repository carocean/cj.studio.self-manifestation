# Dissolution Form of Mis-Externalization

This chapter attempts a general proposition:

The pair of source labels "inner / outer" holds no ontic status in reality — they are not a prior partition of the real, but the output of the attribution mechanism. What is dissolved is the reality of the partition, not the reality of the world.

The division of labor with the Mis-Externalization Forms:

Form Chapter 7 proves that "the outside" can be constructed; Form Chapter 8 proves that "the outside" cannot be read; this chapter proves that "outer" and "inner," as a pair of partitions, hold no a-priori status — the three chapters together complete the characterization of Mis-Externalization: construction, unreadability, dissolution of status.

The scope of the dissolution must be drawn in advance, lest it overstep. This chapter dissolves one thing, preserves one thing, and passes no verdict on one thing:

Dissolved: the ontic status of the label pair lᵢₙ / lₒᵤₜ — they are no partition of the real subsisting prior to the attribution mechanism.

Preserved: the fact of the generative path. The structure sₜ was indeed generated by some real path; that fact exists; the system merely has no access to it (the source gap is non-adjudicability at the epistemic layer, not an absence of fact at the ontic layer — Form Chapter 7, counter-example 1). The dissolution does not touch this.

Not adjudicated: the existence or non-existence of the external domain Ω. Form Chapter 8's type-layer argument asserts only that Ω is not an object available to the system; whether it exists, this system passes no verdict. This chapter's dissolution is fully compatible with that argument: what is dissolved is the claim "the outer label corresponds to some available real domain," not Ω itself.

One sentence:

"Outer" is not a place discovered but a label generated; "inner" is not a foundation but the other value of the same two-valued label structure.

⁂

1\. Basic Setup

Let the system's current structure at moment t be sₜ ∈ S.

Anchoring: sₜ is the converged imagery maintained in the occupied state at that moment (that it is a single convergence form is given by duty I of the General Form of the Observer; for the formal definition of occupancy see the Observer Mathematical Form, Definition 45); this chapter's dissolution of the "inner / outer" labels has as its working range the attributions of the appearing layer (Form Chapter 22).

Its source interpretation set is Ʃₜ ⊆ P, satisfying:

\|Ʃₜ\| ≥ 2, with the source gap holding (Form Chapter 7, Definition 1)

And: Ʃₜ is generated or delimited wholly within E(sₜ); no interpretation-source set independent of structure is admitted.

Let there exist the attribution mechanism Ψₜ, with output Ψₜ(sₜ) = lₜ, its complete functional form Ψₜ(sₜ) = Kₛ(sₜ, Λₛ(Ʃₜ, X)), where Ʃₜ ⊆ E(sₜ) and Δₜ(Ʃₜ) ≠ ∅ (Form Chapter 8, Section 10).

Here lₜ ∈ L = {lᵢₙ, lₒᵤₜ}.

Ψₜ still stands, in this chapter, as the label-generating structure. Whether its output represents a real partition of reality is exactly this chapter's thesis — this chapter will argue that it represents only the marking result generated within structure (conclusion at Section 13; before the argument is complete, nothing is presupposed).

2\. The Problem Restated

Under \|Ʃₜ\| ≥ 2 with the source gap holding, source non-adjudicability obtains.

The General and Limit Forms have proven: "of external origin" is not read; it is the attribution result generated and maintained by the Mis-Externalization structure (Form Chapter 7, general theorem; Form Chapter 8, main theorem).

The question now: if "outer" is only a generated label, is the "inner" set against it still a real partition? Further: does the partition this pair of labels constitutes possess a-priori ontic status at all?

3\. The Generatedness of the Labels

By definition: lₜ = Ψₜ(sₜ).

Hence: "inner / outer" is no property the structure itself carries, but an output after the structure has run.

Therefore: lₜ belongs neither to sₜ itself nor to the presupposed content of Ʃₜ; it belongs only to the output of Ψₜ.

That is: "inner" and "outer" are both generated labels — not things lying there in advance, waiting to be discovered.

4\. The Lock of the Two-Valued Structure

Define: outer = 1, inner = 0, and stipulate:

lᵢₙ ≔ ¬lₒᵤₜ

Hence: inner and outer are not two independently existing items, but the two values of one two-valued structure.

That is: L = {0, 1} is only the output space of Ψₜ, not a partition of the real.

This section is exactly the two-value lock cited by Form Chapter 7, Section 3 (the logical layer of the uniqueness inheritance) and by Form Chapter 8, Exclusion Path Four (the logical-layer exclusion): once lᵢₙ ≔ ¬lₒᵤₜ holds, the same structure holding both labels is l = ¬l, a logical contradiction — parallel attribution is excluded right here, no added constraint required.

5\. The Dependence of the Opposition

Since lₜ is generated by Ψₜ, and Ʃₜ is wholly internal, the opposed pair "inner / outer" appears only after Ψₜ has run.

Therefore: it is not that "inner / outer" exists first and structures are then sorted into one side; it is that Ψₜ exists first, and only then the two labels "inner / outer."

That is: inner-and-outer depends on Ψₜ; Ψₜ does not depend on inner-and-outer.

6\. Exclusion Path One: the A-Priori Inner–Outer Partition

Suppose: reality is of itself divided into "inner" and "outer," every structure natively belonging to one side, and the system's attribution made accordingly — then no generating mechanism Ψₜ would be needed.

The supposition is excluded — but what is excluded must be stated precisely, lest the epistemic and ontic layers be confused.

What is excluded is the functional existence of the a-priori partition: if an a-priori partition existed and the system could attribute by it, attribution would be readable directly from structure or conditions — contradicting the source gap (Form Chapter 8, Exclusion Path Two), and contradicting the impossibility of reading (Form Chapter 8, Exclusion Path One). Hence, whatever reality "in itself" may be, an a-priori partition cannot be what the system's attribution rests on; the system's inner–outer labels do not come from it.

Further, within the system, the entire assignable content of the concept "the inner–outer partition" is the generation and use of the labels — beyond the label operations, the partition has no residual ontic content within the system to point to. The system-internal "inner–outer partition" is thereby gathered wholly into the label layer.

One natural rejoinder must be blocked in advance: the system does contain real structural boundaries — the pool-boundary (individuation, Mathematical Form of Observer Individuation, Section 6: the layered pool-track definitions and the pre-identification protocol), the Observation-Domain boundary (admission and reachability, Form Chapter 22), the Recognition-Realm boundary (the closure of discriminability) — these are real; is "outer" not simply the tracking of events that cross them? It is not, on three grounds. First, these boundaries partition the qualification and identity of structures (who counts as one observer; what can appear; what can be discriminated), not the provenance of contents — their criteria (the Sep condition; the three gates; the indiscriminability closure) do not overlap with the label's conditions of generation (gap plus bias). Second, were the source label readable off some real boundary's crossing-detection, attribution would be deterministic mapping, already excluded by Form Chapter 8, Exclusion Path Two — the very existence of the gap means no boundary detection available within the system can single-valuedly deliver the attribution. Third, the label can run contrary to any boundary fact (a wholly internally generated structure labeled outer; a translated product that crossed the boundary labeled inner) — this very defeasibility proves the label is no reading of any real boundary. Real boundaries exist; but the source label reduces to none of them — it is constructed exactly where boundary detection gives out.

7\. Exclusion Path Two: "Outer" Corresponding to an Available Real External

Suppose: the label "outer" corresponds to some real external domain the system can access or verify — then attribution would be a reading of that domain.

But all the system can obtain are sₜ and Ʃₜ, and Ʃₜ is wholly internal; direct reading of a real external domain is impossible at the level of type (Form Chapter 8, Exclusion Path One: f(Ω) is undefined), and whatever crosses the boundary through translation is already internal structure, not the external itself.

Therefore: no external source domain exists that can be called upon or verified; the label "outer" corresponds to no available real domain.

Note the conclusion's exact boundary: what is denied is the correspondence between the label and an available real domain — not the existence of Ω. Whether Ω exists, the system cannot know, and this system passes no verdict — precisely the point of compatibility with Form Chapter 8's type-layer argument: that argument concludes from "Ω is not an available object," never from "Ω does not exist."

8\. Exclusion Path Three: "Inner" as the Real Substrate

Suppose: "inner" is an independently real foundational region — then it must be logically independent of "outer."

But lᵢₙ ≔ ¬lₒᵤₜ, and "outer" is an output label of Ψₜ.

Therefore: "inner" is likewise only a label; within this Form's two-valued structure it is fixed by the negation of "outer," and holds no foundational status independent of that label structure. To take "inner" as substrate is to take a label value as substrate — and the entire content of a label value lies within the label structure.

9\. The Closure of the Structure

From the foregoing: Ʃₜ is internal; lₜ is generated by Ψₜ. There exists, therefore, the closed chain:

sₜ → Ʃₜ → lₜ

What is closed must be stated precisely: this is the closure of the source-adjudication chain — in the passage from sₜ to lₜ, there is no direct input from Ω; all the materials the adjudication uses (the interpretation set, the bias, the determination functions) are internal structures.

This is not the closure of the causal chain: the system receives input as ever; input crosses the boundary through candidate-translation (Form Chapter 22); the translated products take part in subsequent generation and attribution — but the translated product is internal structure, and by the time it enters the adjudication chain it carries no authentication mark of "from outside" (otherwise attribution could be read, contradicting Form Chapter 8).

Therefore: "inner / outer" is generated wholly within the structure — at the adjudication layer; and this in no way means the system has no causal exchange with its environment.

10\. Mis-Externalization Re-Delimited

In the General Form, Mis-Externalization was described as "taking a structure to be outside." In this Form the word "mis-" can be given its two readings — one dissolved, one preserved:

First, "mis-" relative to the a-priori partition — dissolved. "Misjudgment" presupposes that the label has a-priori truth conditions (reality natively inner and outer; the judgment can be right or wrong). By Section 6, no such a-priori partition can serve as ground; under this reading there is no judging-right or judging-wrong at all, and the "mis-" comes to nothing.

Second, "mis-" relative to the path fact — preserved, but confined to an epistemic-layer description. The real generative path of structure sₜ exists (Form Chapter 7, counter-example 1); when the attribution label disagrees with that path fact (an internally generated path labeled outer), the disagreement holds as a relational fact; but the system itself has no way to check it, for checking would require reading exactly the path the gap conceals.

The exact characterization of Mis-Externalization is therefore: the system generates a label where checking is impossible, and re-interprets the label as a source property. The "mis-" is no deviation from an a-priori truth; it is this: the relation between the attribution and the inaccessible path fact can never be settled from within the system.

What actually happens is: label generation + re-interpretation of the label — not a (mistaken) reading of the real source.

11\. The Dissolution of the Opposition

By the points above, "inner / outer":

is not a-priori (Section 6);  
corresponds to no available real domain (Section 7);  
constitutes no independent foundational partition (Section 8);  
and its generation is closed throughout within the adjudication chain (Section 9).

It is only: a pair of label values output by Ψₜ.

Therefore the opposition inner-versus-outer can be dissolved: the pair no longer exists as a structure of reality, only as a structure of marking.

12\. The Delimitation of Real Status

The conclusion here is: "inner / outer" holds no ontic status in reality.

This does not deny that, in experience, the inner–outer distinction occurs as ever — clear, stable, not to be cancelled at will — for attribution is a functional structure that continuation requires (Form Chapter 7, Section 3: the attribution demand), the label is maintained by convergence (Kₛ), and its firmness is precisely the Mis-Externalization structure running as it should.

This Form's conclusion states only: the distinction comes from structural generation, not from a presupposed partition of reality itself. What the dissolution cancels is the ontic reading of the partition, not its functional existence — the reading dissolved, the inner–outer distinction goes on being used; it is simply no longer taken for a seam of the real.

13\. Main Theorem

When the following conditions hold:

sₜ is a maintained occupancy (that it is a single convergence form given by duty I of the General Form of the Observer; its existence resting on the commitment of the pool-level current single-value principle), and the attribution demand holds (Form Chapter 7, Section 3)  
\|Ʃₜ\| ≥ 2 ∧ Δₜ(Ʃₜ) ≠ ∅, with Ʃₜ wholly internal  
Ψₜ is the attribution mechanism (Form Chapter 8, main theorem: Ψₜ ≅ Externalization(sₜ))  
no available external source domain exists (Form Chapter 8, Exclusion Path One)

then:

(i) "inner / outer" constitutes no a-priori partition of the real; it is only the output label space of Ψₜ (Sections 3 through 11);

(ii) the dissolution does not reach the fact of the generative path (the path fact exists at the ontic layer; its inaccessibility holds at the epistemic layer), and passes no verdict on the existence of Ω (the scope delimitation of the general proposition);

(iii) therefore the "externality" on which Mis-Externalization rests holds no ultimate status — yet as a functional structure it holds as ever, and is necessary as ever (Form Chapters 7 and 8).

14\. Minimal Expression

sₜ —Ψₜ→ lₜ (Ψₜ containing within it Ʃₜ, X, and the constraints of E and Δₜ)

where: lₜ ∈ Output(Ψₜ), lₜ ∉ the structure of reality itself.

15\. Closing

The label "outer" points to no attainable far shore; "inner" is not a foundation, but the other value of the same two-valued label structure.

There is no a-priori inner; there is no a-priori outer.

The so-called "mis-" is no departure from the real; it is the re-interpretation, where checking is impossible, of a label already generated.

Once the generating mechanism is seen through, the inner–outer division is taken back into the label layer, and the name "Mis-Externalization" dissolves with it — the reality of the world, the fact of the path, the function of attribution: all wholly unharmed.

Form Chapter 7 proves that "the outside" can be constructed; Form Chapter 8 proves that "the outside" cannot be read; this chapter proves that "the outside" need not be believed — it was only ever a label.
