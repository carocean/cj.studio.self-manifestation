# General Form of Existence

General Proposition of this Chapter:

Once it is admitted that structures within a system can be continuously generated, it must at the same time be admitted: generation is not equal to existence.

That is:

If the system can continuously generate structures, why do these structures not all become existence, but only a part are retained, able to participate subsequently, and able to maintain their standing?

In this chapter, through successive inquiry, the proof is established and the general form of existence is derived.

The aim is:

To obtain the minimal formal model of existence-structure, together with its proof, counterproof, and breakdown paths.

We first present the result.

Minimal General Form of Existence

Exist = (G, U, S, E, Δ, X, K)

Under the joint conditions of generative expansion, temporal continuation, and sustainability constraint, any system capable of forming and maintaining an existence-set must simultaneously possess: generative expansion, elimination of the unsustainable, stability determination, cost structure, threshold formation, escape blocking, and existence locking.

Therefore, existence is not generation itself, but the stable subset compressed out of generation by the system.

One sentence

Generation gives what can come forth; existence locks what can remain.

Abstract

The General Form of Generation has already shown that under continuous input, pattern reuse, and internal unfolding, structures are continuously generated within the system. The question thus shifts from “whether structures exist” to: since structures can be continuously generated, why do not all structures become existence?

This paper proposes: existence is not an automatic result of generation, but a result selected under sustainability constraints when generation persists and structures may differentiate. First, “generation ≠ existence” is elevated from empirical observation to structural constraint; then it is shown that once a system exhibits unfolding, reuse, and temporal continuation, generated structures necessarily exceed existing ones; further, if the system requires structures that can sustainably participate, it must filter generated results, eliminating those that are unsustainable.

The core conclusion is: existence is not the continuation of generation, but the stable subset locked during generation; this subset must be structurally closed and positively separated in cost from its ground state.

Introduction

In everyday consideration, the question is not only “whether structures exist,” but also “which structures truly stand.”

A structure may be generated yet not retained; a pattern may appear momentarily yet not become part of the system; a result may form transiently yet not enter subsequent operation.

This leads to a fundamental question:

Why do generated structures not all become existence?

A common assumption holds: once generated, it already exists. This paper advances a stricter position:

Generation and existence are not equivalent.

Thus the question is no longer:

Can the system generate structures?

but:

Among generated structures, why are only some retained?

Argument Flow

We proceed by successive inquiry.

I,

First question:

Can “generation = existence” hold?

If the system does not distinguish “instantaneous generation” from “sustainable retention,” then the concept of existence collapses structurally. For if all that has appeared is called existence, then existence degenerates into historical trace, no longer distinguishing structures that can truly be invoked, participate, and be maintained in the present.

Thus, if the concept of existence is to stand, it must be admitted:

Existence must possess persistence.

Definition 1: Existence

Existence is not “having once appeared,” but “after generation, remaining callable, able to participate, or capable of affecting subsequent structure within the system.”

If a structure disperses immediately after generation, it does not belong to the existence-set.

II,

Given that existence is not the automatic result of generation:

Why does generation produce more than existence?

If the system is a single-channel, single-path deterministic mapping, then each step generates only one structure; no selection problem arises.

Thus the problem of existence belongs to systems where:

Multiple structures can be generated within the same or continuous time.

Definition 2: Generative expansion

Generative expansion is the capacity of a system to produce multiple structural outcomes within a given interval.

III,

Is generative expansion an additional assumption?

No.

From the General Form of Generation:

Whenever there exist path differentiation, pattern reuse, and historical feedback, multiple generation outcomes arise inevitably.

Thus:

The domain of generation exceeds the domain of existence.

IV,

Given that generation exceeds existence:

Why are not all structures retained?

If all structures were sustainable, no selection would occur, and no stable hierarchy would form.

Thus:

Not all generated structures are sustainable.

This yields:

Elimination of the unsustainable.

Definition 3: Elimination of the unsustainable

Elimination of the unsustainable is the natural removal of generated results that cannot be maintained or cannot participate in subsequent structural operation.

V,

How does elimination arise?

If elimination were arbitrary, the system would fluctuate without stability.

Thus there must exist a mechanism that causes structures to differentiate into the sustainable and the unsustainable.

This yields:

Stability determination.

Definition 4: Stability determination

Stability determination is any mechanism that causes structures to differentiate into the sustainable and the unsustainable.

VI,

Given such differentiation:

On what basis does it occur?

If all structures are equal in maintenance, no differentiation arises.

Thus:

There must exist differences in sustainability.

This yields:

Cost structure.

Definition 5: Cost

Cost is the minimal condition required to maintain a structure.

Cost is not an added quantity, but the minimal formalization of sustainability difference.

VII,

Given cost differences:

Does there exist a boundary below which structures cannot persist?

If so:

A threshold arises.

Definition 6: Threshold

A threshold is not a prior boundary, but the boundary that emerges from convergence of sustainability differences within the system.

VIII,

Is threshold alone sufficient?

If there exists a sequence of structures whose cost approaches the minimal cost while remaining sustainable, then the existence-set is not separated.

Thus:

Such sequences must not exist.

This yields:

Escape blocking.

Definition 7: Escape blocking

Escape blocking means that no sequence of sustainable structures exists whose cost approaches that of the minimal structure.

If escape is not blocked, threshold does not form a structural boundary.

IX,

Once escape is blocked:

The existence-set must be structurally closed and positively separated in cost from the ground state.

That is:

∃ Δₛ \> 0 such that  
𝓔 = {s₀} ∪ { s ∣ E\[s\] ≥ Δₛ }

This is not selection, but structural necessity.

This yields:

Existence locking.

Definition 8: Existence locking

Existence locking is the detachment of structures from the generative flow, no longer counted as events, but as structural units, entering the domain of standing, upon which all subsequent participation, operation, and attribution depend.

X,

Thus the structure of existence is derived.

Under generative expansion, sustainability constraint, and escape blocking, the system must possess:

Generative expansion,  
Elimination of the unsustainable,  
Stability determination,  
Cost structure,  
Threshold formation,  
Escape blocking,  
Existence locking.

Without any one of them, existence cannot be formed.

XI,

Thus the General Form of Existence is written:

Exist = (G, U, S, E, Δ, X, K)

and:

The domain of generation exceeds that of existence;  
Threshold holds if and only if escape is blocked;  
The existence-set is a stable closed subset within the generative flow, positively separated from the ground state.

XII,

General Theorem:

Under generative expansion, sustainability constraint, and escape blocking, any system capable of forming and maintaining an existence-set must possess G, U, S, E, Δ, X, K.

This set is minimal and complete.

Proof:

Without G, no generation;  
Without U, no selection;  
Without S, no differentiation;  
Without E, no difference;  
Without Δ, no boundary;  
Without X, no separation;  
Without K, no existence-domain.

Q.E.D.

XIII,

Breakdown Argument

Single-path system: no generative expansion  
Infinite-sustain system: no elimination  
No-difference system: no cost structure  
No-escape-block system: a sequence approaching the ground state exists  
Instant system: no existence

All fail to produce existence structure.

XIV,

System Position

Generation answers “what may be generated”  
Existence answers “what may be retained”  
The present answers “which among the retained stands now”

XV,

Final Compression

Generation gives what can come forth  
Existence locks what can remain  
The present determines what stands

Without generation, nothing can come forth  
Without existence, nothing can remain  
Without the present, nothing stands
