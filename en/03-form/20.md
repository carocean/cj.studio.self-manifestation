# General Formula of Sedimentation Dynamics

This chapter argues a central thesis:

Accepting the fact that “history influences the future,” and requiring this influence to produce stable continuation within a bounded field, implies: within a system, not only does change occur, but there exists a structure through which differences left by the past can bias subsequent paths.

That is:

If a field can undergo repeated actions, accumulate distinct Trace-Effects, and continue receiving subsequent Pulses—why does it not remain permanently uniform, permanently chaotic, permanently structureless? Why does it instead produce Sedimentation-Degree differences, Familiar Routes, Closures, Dominant Continuation Structures, and trans-temporal stable continuation?

This chapter pursues these questions through successive interrogation and derives the General Formula of Sedimentation Dynamics.

The goal is:

To obtain the paradigm model of sedimentation-dynamic structure (its minimality not yet fully proved), and to provide: positive and negative proofs, distinctions among the listed concepts (incompressibility), a conditional account of minimal completeness, breakdown paths, and a final total compression formula.

The result is stated first.

General Formula of Sedimentation Dynamics

Sedimentation-Dynamics = (I, T, H, D, B, F, C, U, K, S)

Under the simultaneous conditions of repeated action, historical retention, finite competition, the presence of dispersal, and subsequent bias, any system capable of forming and sustaining a sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structure necessarily possesses:

Field Entry,  
Trace-Effect Generation,  
Historical Retention,  
Differential Accumulation,  
Continuation Bias,  
Familiar-Route Locking,  
Closure Return,  
Convergence Compression,  
Dispersal Resistance,  
Continuation Binding.

Therefore:

Sedimentation structure is not an externally imposed rule.

It is: the stable result that forms when history persistently biases future paths, under the pressures of finite competition and dispersal.

In one line

History biases the future.  
Finite competition compresses the future.

Abstract

Field Mathematics has established how Trace-Effects form Groove-Lines, Familiar Routes, Co-Nuclei, and Closures. Domain Mathematics has established how localized stable continuation forms Domains, Lock, and Sync. Co-Nucleus Mathematics has established how prolonged Domain overlap produces Co-Nuclei that form conceptual layers and generalization structures.

Yet the question presses further:

If sedimentation structure is to form and be sustained, what must it possess?

This chapter proposes:

If a field satisfies:

\- actions can leave Trace-Effects;  
\- history cannot completely reverse;  
\- subsequent continuation is biased by history;  
\- the field cannot flow uniformly without limit;  
\- all structures are subject to dispersal;

and a Dominant Continuation Structure borne by sedimentation differences (hereafter a sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structure) forms and is sustained in it, then this structure necessarily possesses:

Sedimentation-Degree differences,  
Familiar Routes,  
Closures,  
Dominant Continuation Structures,  
and trans-temporal stable continuation.

This chapter does not assert that a field satisfying the five conditions necessarily generates such a structure (note to Section 12).

The core conclusions of this chapter are:

Without history, there is no sedimentation.  
Without Sedimentation-Degree differences, there is no Continuation Bias.  
Without Continuation Bias, there is no Familiar-Route Locking.  
Without Familiar-Route Locking, Closure Return cannot be defined.  
Without finite competition, Dominant Continuation Structures cannot be defined.  
Without Dispersal Resistance, Dominant Continuation Structures cannot be sustained.  
Without Continuation Binding, trans-temporal stable continuation cannot be established.

Therefore:

The General Formula of Sedimentation Dynamics is not an empirical description.

It is:

under this chapter’s definitions, the necessary structure of a sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structure.

Introduction

What we ordinarily observe is already-formed structure.

Groove-Lines are already there.  
Familiar Routes are already there.  
Domains are already there.  
Co-Nuclei are already there.  
Laws appear to already be there.

But the real question is not:

Where is structure?

But rather:

Why does structure appear at all?

If a field completely restores itself after every action, there is no history to speak of.

If history exists but does not alter subsequent paths, history has no dynamic significance.

If subsequent paths are influenced, but all paths remain perpetually equipotential, Sedimentation-Degree differences cannot form.

If differences form but do not take part in subsequent continuation, Familiar Routes cannot be locked.

If Familiar Routes exist but cannot form mutual-return relations, Closures cannot be established.

If Closures can flow without limit in parallel, Dominant Continuation Structures cannot be structurally defined.

If Dominant Continuation Structures cannot resist dispersal, stable continuation cannot be sustained.

If Dominant Continuation Structures cannot preferentially continue themselves, trans-temporal stable continuation cannot be established.

Therefore, this chapter does not begin from “structure already exists.” It begins from:

Why the field is no longer undifferentiated.

Argument Flow

I.

The first and most basic question:

Is sedimentation dynamics present in every system that undergoes change?

No.

If a system completely restores itself after every action, change occurs but no sedimentation takes place.

Therefore:

Actions must enter the field.

Definition 1: Field Entry

I: Field Entry

Field Entry is: a given action entering the field and participating in field-state change.

I requires only: that the action enters the field. It does not guarantee that a stable differential is left behind.

II.

Given that field entry exists, ask:

Does entry necessarily leave an effective differential?

Not necessarily.

If an action merely passes through the field without leaving any local change, the field still does not form sedimentation.

Therefore: Trace-Effect Generation becomes necessary.

Definition 2: Trace-Effect Generation

T: Trace-Effect Generation

A Trace-Effect is: the effective change left by an action within the field.

T requires not only that an action enters the field, but also: that the action leaves a persistent local differential.

Without Trace-Effects, history cannot be structurally established.

III.

Given that Trace-Effects exist, ask:

Do Trace-Effects necessarily constitute history?

Not necessarily.

If a Trace-Effect immediately and completely reverses after forming, the past cannot participate in the future.

Therefore: Historical Retention becomes necessary.

Definition 3: Historical Retention

H: Historical Retention

Historical Retention is not complete preservation of the past. It is: the field cannot completely restore itself to the state prior to Trace-Effect entry.

As long as the past leaves an irreversible differential, history is established.

IV.

Given that history exists, ask:

Does history necessarily produce structural differentials?

Not necessarily.

If all history is retained uniformly throughout the field, then: history exists, but no differential continuation tendency forms.

Therefore: Differential Accumulation becomes necessary.

Definition 4: Differential Accumulation

D: Differential Accumulation

Differential Accumulation is: history leaving unequal Sedimentation-Degrees among different Structural Positions, Groove-Lines, or paths.

Sedimentation-Degree is the degree of sedimentation depth, borne by the hosting mechanism (Field Chapter 1, Definition 9).

H requires only that history exists. D requires: that history is non-uniform.

Differential Accumulation is not the name of a memory, nor an intensity of effect; it is a difference of sedimentation states. The usual effects of Sedimentation-Degree are not written into this definition; they are given as directional relations: among comparable structures of the same kind, the deeper the Sedimentation-Degree, the lower the threshold (Field Chapter 1, Equation 3a); under identical comparison conditions, the deeper receives flow no later than the shallower, and, when a further strict condition holds, the deeper receives flow first (Field Chapter 1, Law 5 and Theorem 17; Outer Chapter 3: “the deeper receives the flow first”).

D asserts only that the difference exists, not that the difference has altered subsequent continuation; the latter is B.

V.

Given that Sedimentation-Degree differences exist, ask:

Do they necessarily participate in subsequent continuation?

Not necessarily.

Outer Chapter 3 states that “the deeper receives the flow first,” and this law has its comparison conditions: the same entry, a shared entry flow pressure, unobstructed channels, no difference of selective bias or tuning, and comparable structures of the same kind (C₌ of Field Chapter 1, Equation 4). When these conditions are absent, a Sedimentation-Degree difference may leave subsequent progression unchanged. For example, if the deeper path is blocked (Inner Chapter 15: “meeting obstruction, it goes around”), flow takes only the shallower path: the difference exists, yet subsequent progression is the same as if the difference were leveled. For this comparison, the sedimentation difference has no dynamic effect.

Therefore: for sedimentation differences to participate in future generation, a further term is required: Continuation Bias.

Definition 5: Continuation Bias

B: Continuation Bias

Continuation Bias is: subsequent continuation depending on the difference of Sedimentation-Degrees: in a declared comparison task, when the Sedimentation-Degrees of the compared objects are leveled to a declared common level, with everything else unchanged, the relative progression of subsequent continuation changes.

That is: sedimentation differences have altered future paths. “The deeper receives the flow first” (Outer Chapter 3) is its typical case.

B does not require a probabilistic expression: the reading may be the first-opened set, the order of precedence, or shares.

B is not all Biased Flow. Biased Flow has several contextual uses in the Inner and Outer Chapters (earlier traces guiding later flow, differential output of the experiential field, intention selecting or changing the direction of flow, coloring changing experiential biased flow; see Inner Chapters 15, 7, 4 and Outer Chapter 9), and selective bias, tuning, and coloring can also change the direction of flow. B takes only the kind borne by sedimentation differences. The formal definition is in Appendix 1, Section 5.

VI.

Given that Continuation Bias exists, ask:

Does bias necessarily produce stable paths?

Not necessarily.

If a sedimentation difference alters progression in only one comparison and does not recur afterward, the system has only transient tendencies and forms no frequently traveled path.

Therefore: Familiar-Route Locking becomes necessary.

Definition 6: Familiar-Route Locking

F: Familiar-Route Locking

Familiar-Route Locking is: a Familiar Route persistently sustaining Continuation Bias within a declared window: it is a Familiar Route; in each declared sub-segment of the window, later flow goes to this route alone first, and this sole precedence depends on its sedimentation difference: once the sedimentation differences are leveled, this route no longer comes first alone.

A Familiar Route is a route that is commonly familiar (Outer Chapter 3: “because the deeper receives the flow first, signals travel easily and it is commonly familiar; this is what is called the commonly familiar route, the commonly familiar domain”), determined according to Field Chapter 1, Definition 13: it has existing deep sedimentation and has a frequent-travel record within the declared window. “Familiar” is the name for the deeper (Outer Chapter 3: “the deeper may be called familiar”). Receiving flow first is how a Familiar Route manifests, not its definition.

Re-traversal produces a deepening effect; when deepening outweighs shallowing and the bottom limit has not been reached, the route becomes deeper and its threshold lower (Field Chapter 1, Equations 2 and 3a; for paths, premised on the path threshold rule of Appendix 1, Section 1; on a plateau the threshold may stay unchanged).

Familiar-Route Locking is close to, but distinct from, Structural Bias in Field Chapter 1 (Definition 22): Structural Bias does not require a Familiar Route and is defined by a differential threshold and actual biased continuation over a long window; F requires a Familiar Route and is defined by the route’s own bias (B\_m^q in Appendix 1, Section 5) holding persistently in every sub-segment.

VII.

Given that Familiar Routes exist, ask:

Do they automatically constitute stable structure?

Not necessarily.

If Familiar Routes cannot form mutual-return relations, structure can only slide in one direction and cannot be reused.

Therefore: Closure Return becomes necessary.

Definition 7: Closure Return

C: Closure Return

Closure Return is: multiple locked Familiar Route segments forming a reusable mutual-return continuation structure.

A Closure is not a rule. A Closure is: the stable mutual-return relation formed among Familiar Routes.

Note: Closures in Field Chapter 1, Equation 16, do not in general require Familiar Routes (“Familiar Routes may participate in Closures; Familiar Routes do not require Closures, nor do Closures require Familiar Routes”). C in this chapter refers specifically to Closures formed by locked Familiar Routes, a subclass of the Closures of Field Chapter 1; F ≺ C in the dependency chain holds only for this subclass.

VIII.

Given that multiple Closures exist, ask:

Can multiple Closures be sustained in parallel without limit?

No.

If all Closures flow in parallel without limit, there is no uniquely preferred continuation. Without a uniquely preferred continuation, Dominant Continuation Structures cannot be defined.

Therefore: Convergence Compression becomes necessary.

Definition 8: Convergence Compression

U: Convergence Compression

Convergence Compression is not spatial limitation. It is: the system being able to stably sustain only a finite total of effective continuation at any given moment.

Therefore: some Closures must suppress others. Some paths are reinforced, some are suppressed, some are abandoned. Thus: locally unique preferred continuation forms.

Note (v1.2): a Dominant Continuation Structure in this chapter is one formed by Convergence Compression among Closure Returns (C, i.e., Closures of locked Familiar Routes); it is called a sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structure. Wherever this chapter says “Dominant Continuation Structure,” this kind is meant.

IX.

Given that a Dominant Continuation Structure exists, ask:

Is it necessarily stable?

Not necessarily.

Without Dispersal Resistance, any sedimentation structure continuously shallows. If continuous shallowing occurs, the Dominant Continuation Structure cannot sustain its structural differential. If the structural differential cannot be sustained, the Dominant Continuation Structure cannot stably continue itself.

Therefore: Dispersal Resistance becomes necessary.

Definition 9: Dispersal Resistance

K: Dispersal Resistance

Dispersal Resistance is not absolute invariance. It is: the structure remaining reusable under shallowing, disturbance, competition, and flushing.

K does not form structure. K: resists structural loss.

K in this chapter acts on Closure Return (C): the Closure keeps its mutual return within the window, and its irreplaceable carrying paths do not recede below the reusability floor (formal criterion in Appendix 1, Section 9).

Note: K (Dispersal Resistance) and U (Convergence Compression) both depend on C (Closure Return) having been established; they stand in a parallel rather than dependent relation—the formation of Convergence Compression is not a prerequisite for Dispersal Resistance, nor vice versa. Both jointly support the establishment of S (Continuation Binding). See the dependency chain in Section 11.

X.

Given that a stable Dominant Continuation Structure exists, ask:

Does it automatically produce trans-temporal stable continuation?

Not necessarily.

If the Dominant Continuation Structure cannot preferentially continue itself, the system may have local dominance but may still scatter anew at every moment.

Therefore: Continuation Binding becomes necessary.

Definition 10: Continuation Binding

S: Continuation Binding

Continuation Binding is not the Dominant Continuation Structure itself. It is: the structure that causes the Dominant Continuation Structure to preferentially continue itself rather than scatter anew at every moment.

Therefore: trans-temporal stable continuation is not static existence. It is: the result of the Dominant Continuation Structure continuously continuing itself.

Note: S (Continuation Binding) functionally corresponds closely to Lock in Field Chapter 2 (the persistent historical bias imposed upon present convergence). Lock is the continued structural constraint that historical accumulation exerts upon present convergence at the Domain level, causing Domains to preferentially recur along old Familiar Routes. S is the structural condition for Dominant Continuation Structures to preferentially continue themselves at the level of the general formula. Both are expressions of the same dynamic principle at different levels: S is the general-formula-level abstraction of Lock; Lock is the Domain-level concrete realization of S.

XI.

At this point, the General Formula of Sedimentation Dynamics has been derived step by step.

Without Field Entry, the field undergoes no change.  
Without Trace-Effect Generation, change leaves no effective differential.  
Without Historical Retention, the past cannot influence the future.  
Without Differential Accumulation, history produces no depth differentials.  
Without Continuation Bias, sedimentation differences do not participate in future generation.  
Without Familiar-Route Locking, bias does not persist into a route within the window.  
Without Closure Return, Familiar Routes cannot form reusable structures.  
Without Convergence Compression, Closures flow in parallel without limit and Dominant Continuation Structures cannot be defined.  
Without Dispersal Resistance, structural differentials cannot be sustained over long durations.  
Without Continuation Binding, the Dominant Continuation Structure cannot stably continue itself.

Therefore, the General Formula of Sedimentation Dynamics is formally written as:

Sedimentation-Dynamics = (I, T, H, D, B, F, C, U, K, S)

where:  
I: Field Entry  
T: Trace-Effect Generation  
H: Historical Retention  
D: Differential Accumulation  
B: Continuation Bias  
F: Familiar-Route Locking  
C: Closure Return  
U: Convergence Compression  
K: Dispersal Resistance  
S: Continuation Binding

The dependency relations are:

I ≺ T  
T ≺ H  
H ≺ D  
D ≺ B  
B ≺ F  
F ≺ C  
C ≺ U  
C ≺ K  
(U ∧ K) ≺ S

where: “≺” denotes strict unidirectional structural necessity: A ≺ B means “B being established requires A to already be established,” and the converse does not hold (A being established does not entail B). “≺” does not denote temporal ordering; it denotes structural necessity.

Note: v1.0 wrote U ≺ K (Convergence Compression as a prerequisite for Dispersal Resistance), but this direction was incorrect—the establishment of Dispersal Resistance does not require Convergence Compression as a prior condition. Both depend on Closure Return (C) and stand in parallel, jointly supporting Continuation Binding (S). This version revises to: C ≺ U, C ≺ K, (U ∧ K) ≺ S.

Note (v1.2): the necessity directions of the three links D ≺ B, B ≺ F, and F ≺ C are given respectively by Proposition 1 of Appendix 1 (B ⇒ D), Definition 6 (Familiar-Route Locking contains Continuation Bias), and Definition 7 (C is formed by locked Familiar Routes); witnesses that the converse directions fail are given in Section 13.

XII.

The master theorem can now be stated:

Under the simultaneous conditions of repeated action, historical retention, finite competition, the presence of dispersal, and subsequent bias, any system that is capable of forming and sustaining a sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structure necessarily possesses: I, T, H, D, B, F, C, U, K, S.

Note: The conclusion of this theorem depends on the premise “capable of forming and sustaining a Dominant Continuation Structure.” Satisfying the five basic conditions is necessary but not sufficient—if a system satisfies the five conditions but for some internal reason is persistently unable to form a stable Dominant Continuation Structure, the conclusion does not apply.

Note (v1.2): this theorem concerns sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structures (note to Definition 8): they are defined over Closure Return (U and K both presuppose C); C is formed by locked Familiar Routes; F contains B; and B implies D. The theorem is therefore a conditional structural conclusion for this class: the necessity of D, B, F, and C is given by definitions and propositions, and the necessity of the other terms is to be understood according to this chapter’s definition of each. This chapter does not prove that stable dominant structures in general require F and C; non-sedimentary sources such as selective bias, tuning, and coloring can also produce dominance and lie outside this theorem.

Any subset or replacement structure that cannot simultaneously realize: Field Entry, Trace-Effect Generation, Historical Retention, Differential Accumulation, Continuation Bias, Familiar-Route Locking, Closure Return, Convergence Compression, Dispersal Resistance, and Continuation Binding is insufficient to constitute sedimentation-dynamic structure.

Proof:

Without I, no effective action enters the field.  
Without T, change leaves no Trace-Effects.  
Without H, the past does not influence the future.  
Without D, history produces no depth differentials.  
Without B, sedimentation differences do not participate in future generation.  
Without F, bias does not persist into a route within the window.  
Without C, Familiar Routes cannot form mutual-return reuse structures.  
Without U, Closures flow in parallel without limit; Dominant Continuation Structures cannot be defined.  
Without K, structural differentials cannot be sustained over long durations.  
Without S, the Dominant Continuation Structure cannot stably continue itself.

Furthermore, if the dependency relations are as given in Section 11, then each later term presupposes all earlier terms.

Therefore: I ≺ T ≺ H ≺ D ≺ B ≺ F ≺ C ≺ (U ∧ K) ≺ S is necessary. ∎

XIII.

Incompressibility Proof

I cannot be merged into T. Because: Field Entry requires only that an action enters the field, while Trace-Effect Generation requires the action to leave an effective differential. There exist actions that pass through the field without leaving Trace-Effects. Therefore: I ≠ T.

T cannot be merged into H. Because: Trace-Effect Generation requires only that an effective change is left, while Historical Retention requires that the change cannot completely reverse. There exist Trace-Effects that immediately and completely reverse without leaving history. Therefore: T ≠ H.

H cannot be merged into D. Because: Historical Retention requires only that the past has not completely reversed, while Differential Accumulation requires that different Structural Positions form non-uniform differentials. There exist cases where history is retained uniformly across the entire field with no structural differential. Therefore: H ≠ D.

D cannot be merged into B. Because: Differential Accumulation requires only that a Sedimentation-Degree difference exists, while Continuation Bias requires that this difference alters the relative progression of subsequent continuation. There exist cases where a Sedimentation-Degree difference has formed but does not alter subsequent progression: the deeper path is blocked (Inner Chapter 15: “meeting obstruction, it goes around”), and flow takes only the shallower path; when the two Sedimentation-Degrees are leveled to any legal common level, the deeper path remains blocked and subsequent progression is unchanged (the blocked example in Appendix 1, Section 5). This case arises because a comparison condition of “the deeper receives the flow first” is absent (the channel is blocked); it does not conflict with that law. Therefore: D ≠ B.

B cannot be merged into F. Because: Continuation Bias requires only that a sedimentation difference alters subsequent progression in one comparison, while Familiar-Route Locking requires that the route is a Familiar Route and that the bias holds persistently in every sub-segment of the window. There exist cases where, in a single input, the deeper receives flow first (B holds), after which input is interrupted and there is no frequent-travel record within the window: B holds while Familiar-Route Locking does not (Appendix 1, Section 6). Therefore: B ≠ F.

F cannot be merged into C. Because: Familiar-Route Locking requires only that one Familiar Route persistently sustains bias, while Closures require mutual return among locked Familiar Routes. There exist unidirectional locked Familiar Routes without Closure structures. Therefore: F ≠ C.

C cannot be merged into U. Because: Closure Return requires only stable mutual-return formation, while Convergence Compression requires that among multiple Closures a uniquely preferred continuation forms. There exist cases of multiple stable Closures flowing in parallel without a Dominant Continuation Structure. Therefore: C ≠ U.

C cannot be merged into K. Because: Closure Return requires only stable mutual-return formation, while Dispersal Resistance requires that the dominant structure resists shallowing. There exist cases where Closures form but the structure quickly disperses. Therefore: C ≠ K.

U cannot be merged into K. Because: Convergence Compression requires only that locally unique preferred continuation forms, while Dispersal Resistance requires that the dominant structure resists shallowing. There exist cases of transient Dominant Continuation Structures without long-term stable structures. Therefore: U ≠ K.

K cannot be merged into S. Because: Dispersal Resistance requires only that structure does not quickly disperse, while Continuation Binding requires that the Dominant Continuation Structure preferentially continues itself. There exist cases of stable but perpetually flow-switching structures. Therefore: K ≠ S.

XIV.

Minimal Completeness (Conditional Account)

This section does not prove that “no independent structural term beyond these exists”; it gives only a conditional proposition:

For any proposed new structural term X: if its function can only enter existing historical structure, alter existing differential structure, adjust existing bias structure, participate in existing Dominant Continuation Structure, or reinforce existing continuation structure, then X’s function is necessarily reducible to one or a combination of (I, T, H, D, B, F, C, U, K, S).

Therefore: an X meeting this condition does not constitute a new independent structural term. Whether every relevant new term meets this condition is not proved in this chapter.

Note (v1.2): non-sedimentary sources of Biased Flow, such as selective bias, tuning, and coloring, if they participate in dominant continuation, fall under “participate in existing Dominant Continuation Structure” above; they are not merged into B.

Hence: (I, T, H, D, B, F, C, U, K, S) is the set of necessary structures this chapter establishes for sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structures; Section 13 gives distinctions among the listed concepts (adjacent or related terms are not identical and do not imply each other in reverse), and Section 11 gives conditional necessary dependencies; the minimality of the whole structure (that each of the ten terms cannot be realized, replaced, or derived by the others) is not yet fully proved, and its completeness is not proved either.

XV.

Breakdown Arguments

Counterexample 1: Fully restoring field. If the field completely restores after every action, there is no Historical Retention. Sedimentation dynamics cannot form.

Counterexample 2: Trace-Effect-free field. If actions leave no effective change, there is no Trace-Effect Generation. Sedimentation dynamics cannot form.

Counterexample 3: Uniform history field. If history is retained but produces no differential, there is no Differential Accumulation. Sedimentation structure cannot form.

Counterexample 4: Bias-free field. If Sedimentation-Degree differences exist but, because a comparison condition is absent (for example, the deeper path is blocked), do not alter subsequent progression, the sedimentation difference is dynamically inert and Continuation Bias does not hold.

Counterexample 5: Random bias field. If bias holds only occasionally and does not persist in every sub-segment of the window, Familiar-Route Locking does not hold.

Counterexample 6: Unidirectional Familiar-Route field. If paths are familiar but cannot form mutual-return reuse, Closures cannot be defined.

Counterexample 7: Unlimited parallel-flow field. If all Closures can flow in parallel without limit, Dominant Continuation Structures cannot be defined.

Counterexample 8: Absolute-Accumulation field. If all structures only accumulate without dispersal, the system loses effective differentials.

Counterexample 9: Unsustainable field. If structure cannot resist dispersal, Dominant Continuation Structures cannot be sustained.

Counterexample 10: Momentary-dominance field. If the Dominant Continuation Structure cannot preferentially continue itself, trans-temporal stable continuation cannot be established.

XVI.

Final Total Compression Formula

History-Bias under Finite-Competition ⇒ Structure

That is: Structure is the stable result formed when historical bias operates under finite competition and dispersal pressure.

Note: the formula above is a philosophical compression expressing the chapter’s overall view; it is not a formal proposition, and its “⇒” is not used as a sufficient condition.

The formal conclusion of this chapter is written:

Structure\_sed ⇒ B ⇒ ∂Future/∂History ≠ 0

where Structure\_sed denotes that a sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structure holds. In this chapter ∂Future/∂History ≠ 0 is a dependence notation denoting counterfactual dependence: when the sedimentation state left by history is changed, the future reading changes accordingly. It does not assert that a partial derivative exists in the ordinary sense of calculus, and no differential operations may be based on it.

That is: if a sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structure holds, Continuation Bias B must already hold (dependency chain); if B holds, leveling the sedimentation differences changes the future reading, i.e., the future changes with the sedimentation state (historical retention), and in this sense ∂Future/∂History ≠ 0.

Neither step is reversible: ∂Future/∂History ≠ 0 does not imply B, since it can also hold through changes in the common level, selective bias, tuning, or coloring; B does not imply Structure\_sed (witnesses such as B ≠ F in Section 13).

Appendix: General Expression of Sedimentation Dynamics

1\. Basic Domain and Notation

Let the field be: 𝒮  
Let the field state at time t be: 𝒮(t)  
Let the set of all actions entering the field be: ℐ(t) = {i₁,i₂,…}  
Define the continuation space: 𝒫(t)  
Define the effective continuation path set: 𝒬(t) ⊆ 𝒫(t)  
Define the path continuation probability: Pr(q,t), where q ∈ 𝒬(t) (a reading of continuation shares; this appendix does not require all Biased Flow to be expressed probabilistically)  
Define the path Sedimentation-Degree: Depth(q,t) : 𝒬(t) → ℝ⁺. The working domain M must declare its model: (a) path-level model: Depth(q,t) is a state variable of M; (b) aggregation model: Depth(q,t) is given from the Sedimentation-Degrees of the Structural Positions traversed by the path by a declared aggregation (e.g., minimum or weighted sum), non-decreasing in each position’s Sedimentation-Degree  
Define the path threshold rule: M must declare how the opening condition of path q is given by its sedimentation state (e.g., θ(q,t) = g\_q(Depth(q,t))). The path threshold rule has a weak and a strict grade. Weak: g\_q is non-increasing in Depth and admits plateaus, so different depths may have the same threshold. Strict: g\_q is strictly decreasing. For cross-path comparison of thresholds, each path being monotone on its own is not enough: the two paths must share the same threshold mechanism (g\_a = g\_b = g) and satisfy the comparison conditions, or the required threshold order must be declared and verified directly. Under the same mechanism, weak monotonicity yields only “the deeper has a threshold no higher than the shallower,” θ(a) ≤ θ(b); the strict order θ(a) \< θ(b) requires the same strictly decreasing mechanism, or direct declaration and verification. With plateaus or different mechanisms, no strict threshold order or strict precedence is inferred from the depth difference alone. Example: Depth(a) = 2, Depth(b) = 1, θ\_a = 10/Depth(a) = 5, θ\_b = 1/Depth(b) = 1; both mechanisms are strictly decreasing, yet the deeper a has the higher threshold. Moreover, the path threshold rule cannot be derived from monotone aggregation: if a serial channel opens according to its shallowest point, two paths with local Sedimentation-Degrees (100, 1) and (2, 2) have sums larger and smaller respectively, yet the former has the higher passage bottleneck  
Define the structural differential function: δ : 𝒮 × 𝒮 → ℝ (a differential function between two field states; it shares its symbol with, but differs in meaning from, the Sedimentation-Degree δ(x) of Field Chapter 1; in this appendix Sedimentation-Degree is always written Depth)  
Define the field’s finite continuation capacity (share bound): Λ(t) ∈ ℝ⁺, satisfying: ∑Pr(q,t) ≤ Λ(t), where q ∈ 𝒬(t), each path counted once

That is: the sum of continuation shares within the field is subject to a finite competition constraint. A bounded sum of shares does not imply that the number of sustained structures is finite; the question of number is handled by resource accounting (Section 8).

1\. Field Entry and Trace-Effect Expression

Define Field Entry: I : ℐ(t) → 𝒮(t), denoting: an action enters the field.

Define the Trace-Effect Generation operator: T : (𝒮(t),iₖ) → 𝒮′(t), satisfying: δ(𝒮(t),𝒮′(t)) \> 0. Then: a Trace-Effect is established.

If: δ(𝒮(t),𝒮′(t)) = 0, then: the action passes through the field without leaving a Trace-Effect.

Therefore: I ≠ T.

1\. Historical Retention Expression

Define the Historical Retention condition: ∃Δt \> 0 such that δ(𝒮(t),𝒮(t+Δt)) \> 0. Then: history is established.

If: ∀Δt \> 0, δ(𝒮(t),𝒮(t+Δt)) = 0, then: the field completely restores. Therefore: no Historical Retention.

Define the Historical Retention operator: H : 𝒮(t) → 𝒮(t+Δ), satisfying: H(𝒮(t)) ≠ 𝒮(t).

1\. Differential Accumulation Expression

Define the Sedimentation-Degree difference: D(qᵢ,qⱼ,t) = \|Depth(qᵢ,t) − Depth(qⱼ,t)\|

If: ∀qᵢ,qⱼ ∈ 𝒬(t), D(qᵢ,qⱼ,t) = 0, then: history exists but produces no structural differential. Therefore: no Differential Accumulation.

Define the Differential Accumulation condition: ∃qᵢ,qⱼ ∈ 𝒬(t) such that D(qᵢ,qⱼ,t) \> 0.

1\. Continuation Bias Expression

Define a comparison task T: declare a working domain M, comparison objects K ⊆ 𝒬(t) (at least two paths), an input protocol P, a time window W, a relative reading Y (e.g., the first-opened set, the order of precedence, shares), and a common-level function m.

Legality conditions for m: (1) declared in advance, not chosen after reading the results; (2) idempotent: m(c,…,c) = c; (3) intermediate: min\_K Depth ≤ m(Depth\_K) ≤ max\_K Depth; (4) symmetric: independent of the ordering of the members of K. Common choices are the minimum, the arithmetic mean, and the maximum.

Define the leveling intervention I\_m: set the Sedimentation-Degree of every member of K to c\* = m(Depth\_K). Quantities that M declares to be determined by Sedimentation-Degree (such as thresholds) are recomputed by M’s rules; states independent of Sedimentation-Degree (channel blockage, tuning, selective bias, coloring, input protocol P) remain unchanged. Y reads only relative progression within K, not absolute times or absolute flow.

Realizability of the intervention: in the path-level model (a), set each member’s Depth directly to c*. In the aggregation model (b), M must declare a write-back rule: a setting of position-level Sedimentation-Degrees such that every member of K has aggregated value c*, Structural Positions not belonging to the paths of K remain unchanged, and the result is unique; moreover, when every member’s Depth already equals c\*, the write-back is the identity. When paths in K share Structural Positions, this requirement may have no solution (example: Depth(q₁) = x, Depth(q₂) = y, Depth(q₃) = x + y, with x = 1, y = 2; the arithmetic mean is 2, requiring x = y = x + y = 2, which has no solution). When no realizable and unique write-back exists, I\_m is undefined.

Define the Continuation Bias condition: B\_m(T) is true if and only if I\_m is defined and Y(I\_m(M),P,W) ≠ Y(M,P,W). Then: Continuation Bias is established. When I\_m is undefined, B\_m(T) is recorded as “undefined,” not judged false.

A report of B must state the m used; where a conclusion is required not to depend on m, the results of checking each member of a declared family of m must be reported. When the paths have different threshold mechanisms, the truth value of B\_m may vary with m.

Define the member precedence reading: by a declared rule, derive from Y the precedence status of q, Lead\_q(Y) ∈ {sole-first, tied-first, not-first}. For example, with the first-opened set as Y: q is sole-first if it is the only first-opened path, tied-first if it opens in the first batch together with other paths, and not-first if it is not in the first batch; with shares as Y, determine by the largest share.

Define member bias: B\_m^q(T) is true if and only if I\_m is defined and Lead\_q(Y(I\_m(M),P,W)) ≠ Lead\_q(Y(M,P,W)). That is: after the sedimentation differences are leveled, q’s precedence status changes.

Since Lead\_q is a function of Y, B\_m^q(T) true implies B\_m(T) true. The converse fails: B\_m may arise solely from a change of order among other paths while q’s precedence status is unchanged. Example: three paths with Sedimentation-Degrees q = 2, r = 3, s = 1 and threshold mechanisms θ\_q = 0.01/Depth(q), θ\_r = 1/Depth(r), θ\_s = 1/Depth(s); with the opening order as Y, the original order is q, r, s, and after leveling to the mean 2 it is q, {r, s}; B\_m is true, q is still sole-first, and B\_m^q is false.

Proposition 1 (B ⇒ D): suppose that in comparison task T, I\_m is defined and is the identity intervention when all members have equal Sedimentation-Degrees (automatic in the path-level model; guaranteed in the aggregation model by the identity requirement on the write-back rule). For any legal m, if B\_m(T) is true, then Differential Accumulation holds on K.  
Proof: suppose Differential Accumulation does not hold on K, i.e., all members have the same Sedimentation-Degree c₀. By idempotence, c\* = c₀; by the identity requirement, the intervention changes nothing, Y is unchanged, and B\_m(T) is false. The proposition follows by contraposition. ∎

Blocked example (D holds while B does not): take the path-level model. Two paths a and b under entry Terminal Ravine e, with no shared segment, confluence, or cross-path write-back; path threshold rule θ = g(Depth), g strictly decreasing, the same mechanism for both paths; entry flow pressure p starts at p₀ and increases by u \> 0 per step, stopping when the first path opens; a is completely blocked, b is unobstructed. Take K = {a, b}, Depth(a) \> Depth(b), Y the first-opened set, and W no shorter than (g(Depth(b)) − p₀)/u steps. Then D holds. For any legal m, c\* lies between Depth(b) and Depth(a); after the intervention a is still blocked, b’s threshold g(c\*) ≤ g(Depth(b)), so b opens within the window, Y = {b} is unchanged, and B\_m is false.  
Contrast: if a is unobstructed and all else is the same, with p₀ \< g(Depth(a)), each step’s increment smaller than the threshold gap g(Depth(b)) − g(Depth(a)), and W long enough, the original reading is Y = {a} (Field Chapter 1, Theorem 17); after the intervention the two paths have the same threshold and open simultaneously, Y = {a, b}, and B\_m is true; a changes from sole-first to tied-first, so B\_m^a is also true.

If: a Sedimentation-Degree difference exists within K while B\_m(T) is false, then: for this comparison task, the sedimentation difference has not altered subsequent progression. Thus: the sedimentation difference has no dynamic effect. When the reading is shares or probabilities, this means “the difference exists but the shares do not change with the difference.”

Note: B detects dependence on sedimentation differences; it does not detect dependence produced by changes in the common level, nor Biased Flow produced by selective bias, tuning, or coloring. β(q,t) ∝ ∇Depth(q,t) of v1.0 and β(q,t) ∝ Depth(q,t) − avg{Depth(q′,t)} of v1.1 are deleted in this version: both wrote a directional relation as a proportionality, and neither declared its category.

1\. Familiar-Route Locking Expression

Define the deepening effect of re-traversal: R(q,t) ≥ 0, denoting the deepening produced at step t by re-traversal along q (corresponding to Dτ in Field Chapter 1, Equation 2). The functional form of R is determined by the specific field; this appendix sets up no proportionality between R and Pr.

Define a Familiar Route: q is a Familiar Route if and only if Depth(q,t) \> 0 and q has a frequent-travel record within the declared window W (the number or frequency of passages meets the declared criterion) (Field Chapter 1, Definition 13).

Define Familiar-Route Locking: F(q;T,W) holds if and only if q is a Familiar Route within W, and W is divided as declared into sub-segments W₁,…,Wₙ such that on each sub-segment the comparison task T\_k (with q ∈ K) satisfies: q is sole-first in the original reading (Lead\_q = sole-first), and B\_m^q(T\_k) is true (after leveling, q is no longer sole-first). Then: Familiar-Route Locking is established.

By definition, F ⇒ B\_m^q ⇒ B\_m; this is the necessity direction of B ≺ F. The bias required by F is bound to q itself: q’s sole precedence in each sub-segment depends on its sedimentation difference.

Witness that B holds while F does not: within W there is only one input, in which the deeper q receives flow first, so B holds (contrast in Section 5); afterward input is interrupted, q has no frequent-travel record within W and is not a Familiar Route, so F does not hold.

Note: v1.1 defined Familiar Routes by R ∝ Pr, by ∂Pr/∂t \> 0 and ∂Cost/∂t \< 0, and by F(q) = lim R; these are deleted in this version. The first two define Familiar Routes by manifestations or result quantities such as coming out first or decreasing cost; the last defines them by a limit of reinforcement. None agrees with “familiar is the name for the deeper; a Familiar Route is a commonly familiar route.”

1\. Closure Return Expression

Define the path mutual-return relation: qᵢ ↔ qⱼ, denoting: stable mutual return between paths.

Define a Closure: C ⊆ 𝒬(t), satisfying: ∀qᵢ,qⱼ ∈ C, there exist qᵢ ↝ qⱼ and qⱼ ↝ qᵢ, and every path in C satisfies Familiar-Route Locking F (Section 6). Then: C is a Closure (the subclass treated in this chapter; see the note to Definition 7).

If no mutual return exists, then: structure can only slide in one direction. Therefore: Closures cannot be established.

1\. Convergence Compression Expression

Define Closure flow intensity: Φ(Cᵢ,t) = ∑Pr(q,t), where q ∈ Cᵢ.

Define the finite competition condition: ∑\_{q ∈ ∪ᵢCᵢ} Pr(q,t) ≤ Λ(t), counted over the union of paths, so that shared paths of overlapping Closures are counted once (summing ∑Φ(Cᵢ,t) Closure by Closure would double-count).

Define resource accounting: Occ(C,t) ≥ 0 is the resource occupancy of Closure C; the total occupancy Occ(𝒞,t) of a family of Closures 𝒞 is computed by a declared overlap-accounting rule, which must satisfy: occupancies of disjoint parts are additive, shared parts are counted once, and the total occupancy is no less than that of any of its parts. Resource capacity constraint: Occ(𝒞,t) ≤ V(t). The correspondence between V(t) and Cap(F) in Field Chapter 1 must be declared separately; this appendix sets up no direct correspondence. The respective uses of the share constraint and the resource constraint are given in the Limit Appendix, Section 4.

Define the Dominant Continuation Structure: C*, satisfying: Φ(C*,t) = max{Φ(Cᵢ,t)}.

If: ∀Cᵢ, Φ(Cᵢ,t) are equal, then: no uniquely preferred continuation exists. Therefore: Dominant Continuation Structures cannot be defined.

Define Convergence Compression: U : {C₁,C₂,…} → C\*.

1\. Dispersal Resistance Expression

Define the natural dispersal function: Ξ₀(q,t) ≥ 0, satisfying: in the absence of re-traversal deepening, Depth(q,t+1) \< Depth(q,t) (when Depth \> 0). This denotes: structure has a natural tendency toward shallowing.

Dispersal Resistance is a qualification; the per-step compensation is the deepening effect of re-traversal R(q,t) (Section 6); the two are distinct. The qualification has two levels: the path-level maintenance qualification k\_W(q), and Closure Dispersal Resistance K\_W(C) in the dependency chain.

Define the path-level maintenance qualification: k\_W(q) = 1 if and only if, within the declared window W = \[t₀, t₁\], Depth(q,t) ≥ δ\_K(q) for all t ∈ W, where δ\_K(q) \> 0 is a declared reusability floor; otherwise k\_W(q) = 0.

Interface (from the total dynamic equation, proved): Depth(q,t) = Depth(q,t₀) + Σ\_{s=t₀}^{t−1}(R(q,s) − Ξ₀(q,s)). Hence k\_W(q) = 1 if and only if, for all t ∈ W, Σ\_{s=t₀}^{t−1}(R(q,s) − Ξ₀(q,s)) ≥ δ\_K(q) − Depth(q,t₀).

Corollary: if Depth(q,t₀) ≥ δ\_K(q) and R(q,s) ≥ Ξ₀(q,s) at every step in W, then k\_W(q) = 1. Equal compensation (R = Ξ₀) suffices for maintenance; strictly greater compensation corresponds to net growth. Compensation may vary step by step, as long as the cumulative deficit does not exceed the initial margin Depth(q,t₀) − δ\_K(q).

k\_W is a general path-level qualification and does not presuppose a Closure: a unidirectional path without Closure Return, with Sedimentation-Degree constantly 2, floor 1, and R = Ξ₀ = 0.1 at every step, also has k\_W = 1. Hence k\_W is not the K in the dependency chain that presupposes C.

Define carrying paths: a path q in Closure C is an irreplaceable carrying path if and only if, after removing q from C, the remaining paths no longer satisfy the Closure condition (Section 7). The other paths are replaceable.

Define Closure Dispersal Resistance: K\_W(C) = 1 if and only if (1) C satisfies the Closure condition (Section 7) at every time in W; and (2) every irreplaceable carrying path q of C satisfies k\_W(q) = 1. Otherwise K\_W(C) = 0.

By definition, K\_W(C) = 1 implies that C is a Closure within W; this is the necessity direction of C ≺ K. The K in the dependency chain refers to K\_W(C).

If: some irreplaceable carrying path q of C has, within W, a cumulative deficit exceeding its initial margin (k\_W(q) = 0), then: K\_W(C) = 0. A replaceable path falling below its floor need not cost C its qualification. Therefore: a Dominant Continuation Structure based on C cannot be sustained within W once its Closure loses the qualification.

Note: v1.1 wrote that the structure can be sustained only if K \> Ξ₀ and eventually disperses if K ≤ Ξ₀, without stating the relation between K and the compensation, and excluding equal compensation; this version separates the qualification from the compensation and places the K of the dependency chain at the Closure level.

1\. Continuation Binding Expression

Define the continuation function: Bind : 𝒬(t) → 𝒬(t+Δ), satisfying: Pr(Bind(q),t+Δ) \> Pr(q′,t+Δ), where q′ denotes other candidate paths. That is: paths preferentially continue themselves.

Define Continuation Binding: S(q) = Bind(q→q).

If: no preferential self-continuation exists, then: the system scatters anew at every moment. Therefore: trans-temporal stable continuation cannot be established.

1\. Total Dynamic Equation

Define the sedimentation-dynamic change (discrete, corresponding to Field Chapter 1, Equation 2):

Depth(q,t+1) = Depth(q,t) + R(q,t) − Ξ₀(q,t)

where:  
R(q,t): deepening effect of re-traversal;  
Ξ₀(q,t): natural dispersal tendency.

This is a declared path-level form of Equation 2; when the aggregation is nonlinear, R and Ξ₀ are effective terms after aggregation.

Dimensional note: R and Ξ₀ have the same dimension as Depth, namely the change in Sedimentation-Degree per step. R is an increase term; Ξ₀ is a decrease term. The precise functional forms of each term depend on the specific field implementation; this equation provides structural constraints without presupposing exact functional forms.

Therefore: a net increase in Sedimentation-Degree arises from re-traversal deepening outweighing dispersal (R \> Ξ₀), with the bottom limit not yet reached.

Bias is not given a separate growth term: sedimentation differences do not directly increase Sedimentation-Degree. Their effect runs as follows: under the comparison conditions the deeper receives flow first (Field Chapter 1, Law 5 and Theorem 17; for paths, premised on the strict threshold order and P₁₇); receiving flow, it is re-traversed; re-traversal produces the deepening effect R.

Further: the effect of changes in Sedimentation-Degree on subsequent progression is taken in direction, not in proportion: among comparable structures of the same kind, the deeper the Sedimentation-Degree, the lower the threshold (Field Chapter 1, Equation 3a). For paths, the two grades of the path threshold rule in Section 1 apply: under the same mechanism with weak monotonicity, the deeper has a threshold no higher than the shallower, and under C₌ the deeper receives flow no later (the argument of Field Chapter 1, Theorem 17(a), holds equally for θ(a) ≤ θ(b)); when the strict threshold order holds (the same strictly decreasing mechanism, or θ(a) \< θ(b) declared and verified) and C₌ and P₁₇ are satisfied, the deeper receives flow first (Theorem 17(b)). With plateaus or different mechanisms, no strict precedence is inferred from the depth difference alone. ∂Pr(q,t)/∂t ∝ ∂Depth(q,t)/∂t of v1.1 is deleted in this version.

1\. Stability Condition Expression

Define the stable structure condition: Depth(q,t+1) = Depth(q,t). That is: structure has reached stable equilibrium.

Thus: R(q,t) = Ξ₀(q,t). That is: re-traversal deepening exactly offsets natural dispersal.

Define the stable Dominant Continuation Structure q*, satisfying: Depth(q*,t+1) = Depth(q*,t) and Depth(q*,t) \> Depth(q,t) for all q ≠ q\* (q among the declared comparison objects).

That is: at stable equilibrium, this path maintains a Sedimentation-Degree advantage over all competing paths.

1\. Total Dynamic Expression

Sedimentation-Dynamics = (I,T,H,D,B,F,C,U,K,S)

Philosophical compression: Structure = History-Bias under Finite-Competition (not a formal proposition; see Section 16)

Formal conclusion: Structure\_sed ⇒ B ⇒ ∂Future/∂History ≠ 0

That is: if a sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structure holds, Continuation Bias B must already hold; if B holds, the future has a non-zero dependence on history. Neither step is reversible; see Section 16.

1\. Master Theorem Expression

If a system satisfies:

1\. actions enter the field;  
1\. history cannot completely reverse;  
1\. history influences the future;  
1\. the field is subject to finite competition;  
1\. all structures are subject to natural dispersal;

and the system is capable of forming and sustaining a sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structure,

then: the system necessarily possesses (I,T,H,D,B,F,C,U,K,S).

If any one is lacking: sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structures cannot be established.

(Category: a conditional structural conclusion relative to this chapter’s definitions; see the note to Section 12.) ∎

Appendix: Limit Formula of Sedimentation Dynamics

1\. The Limit Question

The General Formula of Sedimentation Dynamics has established that: if a system forms and sustains a sedimentary Dominant Continuation Structure, it necessarily possesses Sedimentation-Degree differences, Familiar-Route Locking, Closure Return, Convergence Compression, Dispersal Resistance, and Continuation Binding.

Yet the question continues. Because: the general formula addresses how structure forms. The limit formula further asks: once structure forms, can it grow without limit? If not, why does it stop? If so, why does it not absorb the entire field?

Therefore: the limit formula no longer asks “can structure form?” It asks: “how far can structure grow?”

1\. Limit Constraints

Each item in this appendix states its category: definition, additional assumption, proved proposition, definitional requirement, or summary statement.

First: can Sedimentation-Degree grow without limit?

Field Chapter 1, Definition 9, gives the bottom limit of inward sinking δmax, the maximum depth to which sedimentation can still stably sink under the current field state; δmax may change with field state, bundle boundaries, resolution conditions, and structural change. A current bottom limit is not a uniform upper bound across time, and from it one cannot infer that a path’s Sedimentation-Degree is bounded for all time. Nor can this be inferred from the directional relation “the deeper, the lower the threshold”: a strictly decreasing threshold may tend to a positive lower bound, and the directional relation alone does not suffice to infer single-path domination.

This appendix therefore adopts an additional assumption:

Assumption L1 (uniform upper bound): for each path q under consideration, there exists a time-independent Depth̄(q) \< ∞ such that Depth(q,t) ≤ Depth̄(q) for all t.

Definition 1: Sedimentation-Degree Limit

When L1 holds, Depth̄(q) is called the Sedimentation-Degree Limit of path q. (Category: definition, premised on L1.)

1\. Dispersal Limit

Next: under what conditions does Sedimentation-Degree stabilize?

From the total dynamic equation Depth(q,t+1) = Depth(q,t) + R(q,t) − Ξ₀(q,t), the cumulative form is: Depth(q,t) = Depth(q,t₀) + Σ\_{s=t₀}^{t−1}(R(q,s) − Ξ₀(q,s)).

Note: the presence of dispersal does not by itself prevent Sedimentation-Degree from approaching an upper bound. Example: Ξ₀ ≡ 0.1, R(t) = 0.1 + 2^{−(t+2)}, Depth(0) = 1/2; then Depth(t) = 1 − 2^{−(t+1)}: there is always dispersal, yet Sedimentation-Degree still approaches 1. This appendix makes no assertion that dispersal prevents approach to the upper bound.

Definition 2: Limit Stable State

If, as t → ∞, Depth(q,t) converges to a finite value Depth^∞(q), path q is said to enter the limit stable state. (Category: definition.)

Limit Proposition 1 (proved): if path q enters the limit stable state, then R(q,t) − Ξ₀(q,t) → 0.  
Proof: convergence implies Depth(q,t+1) − Depth(q,t) → 0, and the result follows from the total dynamic equation. ∎

The converse fails: step changes tending to zero do not imply convergence (example: Depth(t) = 1 + ½·sin√t is bounded, its step changes tend to zero, yet it does not converge). Hence R − Ξ₀ → 0 means only that “step changes become small” and must not automatically be called final equilibrium.

Limit Proposition 2 (proved, sufficient condition): if L1 holds and R(q,t) ≥ Ξ₀(q,t) for all t from some step t₂ on, then q enters the limit stable state.  
Proof: for t ≥ t₂, Depth(q,t) is non-decreasing and bounded above by Depth̄(q), hence convergent. ∎

1\. Competition Limit

Next: can the number of Closures grow without limit?

The share constraint ∑Pr ≤ Λ alone does not imply that the number of Closures is finite: a bounded sum of shares still admits infinitely many positive shares (example: Pr(qₙ) = 2^{−n}). When Closures overlap, summing Closure by Closure also double-counts. The question of number is therefore handled by resource accounting (Appendix 1, Section 8: Occ, V(t), the overlap-accounting rule).

Assumption L2 (positive lower bound on cost): for every qualified stable Closure, the resource occupancy of its exclusive part (the part not shared with other Closures) is no less than c\_min \> 0.

Limit Proposition 3 (proved): if the resource capacity constraint and L2 hold, the number N of qualified stable Closures sustained simultaneously satisfies N ≤ V(t)/c\_min.  
Proof: the exclusive parts of the Closures are mutually disjoint. By the overlap-accounting rule, the total occupancy is no less than the occupancy of the union of the exclusive parts, i.e., no less than the sum of their occupancies ≥ N·c\_min; and the total occupancy ≤ V(t). ∎

Three states are distinguished:  
Near capacity: V(t) − Occ(𝒞,t) \< c\_min. Under L2, no further qualified Closure can then be added without compressing existing structures.  
At capacity: Occ(𝒞,t) = V(t).  
Over capacity: Occ(𝒞,t) \> V(t), violating the capacity constraint. Only over capacity can correspond to Use(F) \> 1 in Field Chapter 1 Equation 15, and only if the correspondence of Occ and V with Cap(F) is declared separately.

Definition 3: Competition Saturation

Under L2, when the field is near capacity it is said to enter competition saturation. At this point any new qualified Closure must compress existing structures (from the count in Limit Proposition 3). (Category: definition; the attached conclusion is premised on L2.)

1\. Dominance Limit

Next: can an absolutely dominant continuation structure exist?

This section treats the continuation share outside the dominant Closure. Comparison among the paths inside a Closure, and comparison by Φ among Closures nested in C\*, lie outside this section’s reading; this appendix makes no judgment on whether they constitute competition.

Three quantities are distinguished:  
Unused capacity: Λ(t) − Σ\_{q∈∪ᵢCᵢ} Pr(q,t);  
Capacity reservation margin: Γ(t) = Λ(t) − Φ(C\*,t) (Section 7);  
Non-shared continuation share outside the dominant Closure: Φ\_out(t) = Σ\_{q∈∪ᵢCᵢ∖C*} Pr(q,t), i.e., the share of the paths of Closures not shared with C* (counted over the union of paths). Φ\_out is not a complete reading of competition between Closures: Closures may overlap, and another Closure may be entirely contained in C*. Example: C* = {a, b, c} with shares 0.6, 0.3, 0.1, Φ(C*) = 1; C₂ = {a, b}, Φ(C₂) = 0.9; then Φ\_out = 0, yet C* and C₂ can still be compared by Φ as in Appendix 1, Section 8.

When Φ\_out(t) = 0, there is no non-shared continuation share outside the dominant Closure at that time. This does not assert that there is no competition between Closures (nested Closures may still be compared), is not the same as a single path (C\* may contain several paths), and is not the same as the sedimentation process stopping (C\* can still be re-traversed, deepen, and offset dispersal with R = Ξ₀).

Three cases must further be distinguished:  
(a) Zero at a finite time: at some time Φ\_out(t) = 0;  
(b) Asymptotic vanishing: Φ\_out(t) \> 0 for all t but Φ\_out(t) → 0. Within any finite discrete window, the minimum over the window gives a positive lower bound; over infinite time there is no uniform positive lower bound (example: Φ\_out(t) = 1/(t+1) satisfies Φ\_out(t) ≥ 1/11 for t = 0,…,10, but has no uniform positive lower bound over t ≥ 0);  
(c) Candidate channels still exist: there are Closures or channels with zero share that are still structurally present and unobstructed; the outside share can resume once they regain share.  
Φ(C\*,t) → Λ(t) is a limit relation and does not mean that at a finite time no other path has positive share.

Hereafter “sustained sedimentation dynamics” means: within the declared time range, the non-shared continuation share outside the dominant Closure has a positive lower bound. The time range must be declared as one of the following:  
Finite-window persistence: for the declared finite window W, there exists ε\_W \> 0 (which may depend on W) such that Φ\_out(t) ≥ ε\_W for all t ∈ W; in a discrete finite window, this amounts to Φ\_out(t) \> 0 at every time in the window.  
Infinite-time persistence: there exists a single ε \> 0 such that Φ\_out(t) ≥ ε for all t ≥ t₀.

Definition 4: Dominance Limit

Sustained sedimentation dynamics requires: within the declared time range, Φ\_out(t) has a positive lower bound (ε\_W for a finite window, a uniform ε for infinite time). (Category: definitional requirement of sustained sedimentation dynamics, not a proved conclusion.)

That is: under sustained sedimentation dynamics, there is always a non-shared continuation share of no less than that bound outside the dominant Closure. Case (b) meets finite-window persistence for any finite window but not infinite-time persistence; the mere existence of candidate channels in case (c) meets neither. This requirement concerns only the non-shared share outside the dominant Closure and is not a complete characterization of competition between Closures.

1\. Closure Limit

Next: can Closures expand without limit?

Define Closure complexity: Cx(C,t), satisfying: Cx(C,t) ↑ with \|C\|, where \|C\| denotes the number of paths within the Closure. Cx takes values in a declared domain 𝒳 ⊆ ℝ≥0. (Written Ω(C,t) in v1.1, which shared its name with, but differed in meaning from, Ω (Stable Recurrence Capacity) in Field Chapter 1; renamed Cx in this version.)

Growth of complexity and of dispersal cost does not directly imply a finite upper bound on complexity; bridges are required:

Assumption L3 (dispersal grows unboundedly with complexity): Ξ₀(C,t) ≥ h(Cx(C,t)), with h non-decreasing on 𝒳 and unbounded above.  
Assumption L4 (bounded maintenance resources): the re-traversal deepening available to a Closure satisfies R(C,t) ≤ R̄ \< ∞.

Let S\_R = {x ∈ 𝒳 : h(x) ≤ R̄}.

Limit Proposition 4 (proved): under L3 and L4: (1) if S\_R is empty, no complexity value permits non-receding: every Closure recedes at every step (R − Ξ₀ \< 0); (2) if S\_R is nonempty, let Cxₘₐₓ = sup S\_R (finite); if Closure C does not recede at some step (R(C,t) ≥ Ξ₀(C,t)), then Cx(C,t) ≤ Cxₘₐₓ at that step; a Closure whose complexity exceeds Cxₘₐₓ recedes at every step.  
Proof: at a non-receding step, h(Cx(C,t)) ≤ Ξ₀(C,t) ≤ R(C,t) ≤ R̄, hence Cx(C,t) ∈ S\_R. If S\_R is empty there is no non-receding step, which is (1). If S\_R is nonempty, since h is non-decreasing and unbounded, S\_R is bounded above, Cxₘₐₓ is finite, and Cx(C,t) ≤ Cxₘₐₓ; the second half is the contrapositive. ∎

Definition 5: Closure Limit

Under L3 and L4, when S\_R is nonempty, Cxₘₐₓ is called the Closure Limit; when S\_R is empty, report “no complexity value permits non-receding.” (Category: definition, premised on L3 and L4.)

That is: under the listed assumptions, the complexity of non-receding Closures has an upper bound.

1\. Convergence-Compression Limit

Next: can Convergence Compression strengthen without limit?

If Convergence Compression makes C\* take up all used shares, then Φ\_out(t) = 0: there is no non-shared continuation share outside the dominant Closure at that time (case (a) of Section 5). Closures nested in C\* can still be compared by Φ, C\* may still contain comparable paths, and the sedimentation process itself need not stop.

Definition 6: Capacity Reservation Margin

Define: Γ(t) = Λ(t) − Φ(C*,t), where C* is the current Dominant Continuation Structure.

Then: Γ(t) denotes the part of the share bound not occupied by the dominant Closure, comprising unused capacity and the shares of other Closures.

Γ is a capacity margin, not the outside continuation share. Example 1: there is only one actual path, with share 0.5 and Λ = 1; then Γ = 0.5, but there is no other path with share. Example 2: C\* contains two paths with shares 0.6 and 0.4 and Λ = 1; then Γ = 0, but there are still two comparable paths inside the Closure. If needed, Γ(t) ≥ ε\_Γ \> 0 may be declared as an imposed capacity-reservation condition; it is not equivalent to sustained sedimentation dynamics.

Limit Proposition 5 (proved): Φ\_out(t) ≤ Γ(t).  
Proof: Φ\_out(t) = Σ\_{q∈∪ᵢCᵢ} Pr(q,t) − Φ(C*,t) ≤ Λ(t) − Φ(C*,t) = Γ(t). ∎

Hence sustained sedimentation dynamics implies that, within the same time range, Γ(t) is no less than the same lower bound; the converse fails (Example 1).

1\. Total Limit Formula

The limits in this appendix fall into three categories:  
(1) Proved propositions: limit stable state ⇒ R − Ξ₀ → 0 (Limit Proposition 1); L1 and eventually R ≥ Ξ₀ ⇒ limit stable state (Limit Proposition 2); under L2 the number of qualified Closures is bounded (Limit Proposition 3); under L3 and L4 the complexity of non-receding Closures is bounded, or no complexity value permits non-receding (Limit Proposition 4); Φ\_out ≤ Γ (Limit Proposition 5).  
(2) Additional assumptions: L1 to L4.  
(3) Definitional requirement of sustained sedimentation dynamics: within the declared time range, Φ\_out(t) has a positive lower bound (finite-window persistence or infinite-time persistence); Γ being no less than the same bound follows from it.

Thus: the Sedimentation Dynamics Limit Formula is:

R − Ξ₀ → 0 (a necessary condition of the limit stable state)

under

∑Pr(q,t) ≤ Λ(t), Occ(𝒞,t) ≤ V(t), and Φ\_out(t) ≥ ε within the declared time range (ε = ε\_W for a finite window)

That is: stable structure is not the result of unlimited reinforcement; under the listed assumptions and requirements, the stable state has the offsetting of deepening and dispersal as a necessary condition.

1\. Limit Theorem

If a system satisfies:

1\. Sedimentation-Degree is uniformly bounded (L1);  
1\. the share constraint ∑Pr ≤ Λ and the resource capacity constraint Occ ≤ V hold;  
1\. the exclusive cost of qualified Closures has a positive lower bound (L2);  
1\. Closure dispersal grows unboundedly with complexity, and maintenance resources are bounded (L3, L4);  
1\. sustained sedimentation dynamics holds within the declared time range (finite-window persistence or infinite-time persistence, one of which must be declared);

then:  
(1) the Sedimentation-Degree of each path under consideration has a limit Depth̄(q) (Definition 1);  
(2) if a path enters the limit stable state, then R − Ξ₀ → 0; if eventually R ≥ Ξ₀, it necessarily enters the limit stable state (Limit Propositions 1 and 2);  
(3) the number of qualified stable Closures sustained simultaneously does not exceed V(t)/c\_min (Limit Proposition 3);  
(4) when S\_R is nonempty, the complexity of non-receding Closures does not exceed Cxₘₐₓ; when S\_R is empty, no Closure can avoid receding (Limit Proposition 4);  
(5) within the declared time range, the non-shared continuation share outside the dominant Closure is no less than its lower bound (condition 5), and Γ(t) is no less than the same bound (Limit Proposition 5).

When these conditions do not hold, this theorem makes no assertion. ∎

1\. Final Compression Formula

The following is a summary statement of this appendix, not a separate formal proposition:

The dominant Closure takes up all used shares (Φ\_out = 0) ⇒ no non-shared continuation share outside the dominant Closure (nested Closures may still be compared, paths inside the Closure may still be comparable, and candidate channels with zero share may still exist).  
Prolonged zero non-shared share outside the dominant Closure ⇒ no new structures generated by gaining share outside the dominant Closure (the sedimentation process itself may continue).

Therefore: sustained sedimentation dynamics is not unlimited domination. It is: dynamic equilibrium under finite competition.

Further compressed as (philosophical compression): Structure = Finite-History-Balance

The mathematical expression of stable structure is:

Depth(q,t) → Depth^∞(q) (t → ∞, in the limit stable state), whereupon Depth(q,t+1) − Depth(q,t) → 0

That is: stable structure tends toward a dynamic equilibrium in which Sedimentation-Degree converges, not toward unlimited growth; step changes tending to zero is a necessary, not a sufficient, condition.  
Note: v1.0 wrote ∂Structure/∂∞=0; the notation ∂/∂∞ has no standard mathematical definition. v1.1 substituted a well-formed limit expression; v1.2 rewrites it in discrete form and distinguishes convergence from step changes tending to zero.
