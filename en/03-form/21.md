# Non-Social General Formula

Core Proposition:

Granted that coupling exists between individual fields and the social field, and that this coupling originates from individual functional gaps — what is the maintenance structure of social coupling? Under what minimal conditions does it hold? And under what minimal conditions must it necessarily trend toward dissolution?

That is to say:

After the individual field already exists and the social field has formed coupling, the system must provide a structural explanation for the source of this coupling.

This chapter proves: social dependence is not an essential attribute of the Self-Manifesting Being, but a coupling result maintained by the system’s internal structure under functional gap conditions. As long as the maintenance structure of functional gaps is unstable, social dependence must necessarily trend toward limit dissolution.

Our goal is:

To obtain the minimal maintenance structure of social dependence, and prove that this maintenance structure is structurally unstable on the Self-Manifesting Being’s evolutionary path, thereby proving: within the functional social dependence gap-dissolution pathway, the Non-Social Terminal State is the only structurally stable limit.

We first state the result.

Minimal Non-Social General Formula

NST(f) ⇐ ∀j ∈ KeyFunctions: NeedBound\_j \< ∞ ∧ PathSufficient\_j ∧ T\_j (minimal sufficient structure, not necessary)

Note: this chapter provides a sufficient structure, not a necessary-and-sufficient general formula — the three conditions in combination are sufficient to derive NST(f), but do not exclude the existence of other pathways (such as the boundary absorption formula, see Appendix Five) that can also reach the Non-Social Terminal State.

In a single sentence

Social groups exist because gaps exist;  
the maintenance structure of gaps is unstable;  
within the functional social dependence gap-dissolution pathway, gap tending to zero is the only structurally stable limit.

## Section 1. Introduction

The Social Chapters have established: the social field is not a simple aggregate of individuals, but a cross-field co-nucleus structure formed by long-term coupling among multiple individual fields. Social dependence is also not a given fact, but a maintenance relationship formed when group support fills the gap left by insufficient individual capacity to cover key functions.

Therefore, this chapter no longer discusses how the social field is generated, but further asks:

What maintains social dependence?  
Can this maintenance structure hold permanently?  
If it cannot hold permanently, how is the Non-Social Terminal State derived from the general formula?

The core pivot of this chapter: rather than starting from “humans are social animals,” it reduces social dependence to a function of functional gaps. As long as social dependence holds, there must exist at least one CapacityGap in at least one key functional dimension; as long as CapacityGap cannot be maintained permanently, SocialDependency must necessarily tend to zero.

This chapter therefore proves not that “individuals choose to leave society,” but that: under conditions of sustainable Self-Manifesting Being expansion, continuously advancing toolization, and sufficiently open external connection capacity pathways, functional social dependence will be structurally dissolved. The Non-Social Terminal State is not a psychological choice, nor an ethical claim, but the structural limit that emerges after functional gaps tend to zero.

## Section 2. Argument Flow

We proceed through continuous questioning, step by step, progressively deriving the minimal structure.

One,

First ask the shallowest layer:

Is social dependence an essential attribute of the Self-Manifesting Being?

If so, it exists unconditionally, requires no maintenance structure, and cannot trend toward dissolution.

But simply acknowledging the following facts — that individuals without social dependence exist in society, and that cases exist of individuals continuing to survive after group dissolution — already establishes that social dependence is not unconditional.

Therefore, social dependence necessarily has conditions for its holding.

We lock down “social dependence” here.

Definition One: Social Dependence

SocialDependency(f) \> 0 if and only if:

∃ j ∈ KeyFunctions: CapacityGap\_j(f) \> 0

That is: in at least one key functional dimension, a functional gap exists that individual capacity has not yet covered.

If for all j, CapacityGap\_j(f) = 0, then SocialDependency(f) = 0.

Note: CapacityGap\_j is a functional gap, not equivalent to actual social behavior. An individual may choose not to use group support, but as long as GroupSupport\_j \> IndividualCapacity\_j, the functional gap still holds; SocialDependency describes potential functional dependence, not the frequency of real-world interaction.

Two,

Since social dependence has conditions for holding, ask again:

What are the conditions for social dependence to hold?

By Definition One, the holding condition is:

CapacityGap\_j(f) := max(0, GroupSupport\_j(f) − IndividualCapacity\_j(f)) \> 0

holding for at least one j.

This transfers the question:

The holding of social dependence is equivalent to the holding of functional gaps.

We lock down “functional gap” here.

Definition Two: Functional Gap

CapacityGap\_j(f) := max(0, GroupSupport\_j(f) − IndividualCapacity\_j(f))

CapacityGap\_j(f) = 0 means the gap in the j-th dimension has been covered by individual capacity.

SocialDependency(f) = 0 if and only if ∀ j: CapacityGap\_j(f) = 0.

Three,

Can functional gaps be maintained permanently?

Only two scenarios can make CapacityGap\_j permanently nonzero (completeness: CapacityGap\_j \> 0 is equivalent to GroupSupport\_j \> IndividualCapacity\_j, which holds permanently if and only if one of the following two scenarios holds):

Scenario One: GroupSupport\_j permanently exceeds IndividualCapacity\_j (with gap not shrinking) → corresponds to Condition One not holding: GroupSupport\_j has no finite upper bound, or its upper bound persistently exceeds the reachable upper bound of IndividualCapacity\_j  
Scenario Two: the growth of IndividualCapacity\_j is permanently capped below GroupSupport\_j → corresponds to Condition Two not holding: the growth of IndividualCapacity\_j is permanently capped by single field capacity or external monopoly

If neither scenario holds (i.e., both corresponding conditions hold), functional gaps cannot be maintained permanently.

Four,

Now ask: does GroupSupport\_j have a finite upper bound?

The content of GroupSupport\_j is: the maximum functional gain the group provides for the individual in the j-th key functional dimension.

But the upper bound of GroupSupport\_j depends on the individual’s actual functional requirements — groups cannot provide functional support exceeding the individual’s actual requirements (support exceeding requirements does not enter the CapacityGap calculation).

Therefore:

As long as the individual’s actual functional requirements have a finite upper bound NeedBound\_j \< ∞, GroupSupport\_j has a finite upper bound.

We lock down “finite requirement upper bound” here.

Definition Three: Finite Requirement Upper Bound

For any individual field f, ∃ NeedBound\_j \< ∞, such that the individual’s actual functional requirements in the j-th dimension do not exceed NeedBound\_j.

From this we derive the group functional finite upper bound: GroupSupport\_j(f) ≤ NeedBound\_j, because groups cannot provide functional support exceeding the individual’s actual requirement upper bound (support exceeding the upper bound does not enter the CapacityGap calculation).

Condition One is formally stated: ∃ NeedBound\_j \< ∞, GroupSupport\_j(f) ≤ NeedBound\_j (finite requirements imply finite group support upper bound).

This condition does not presuppose the specific value of NeedBound\_j, only requiring that the individual’s actual functional requirements be finite — this is the basic premise of the Self-Manifesting Being’s finiteness.

Five,

Since GroupSupport\_j has a finite upper bound, the dissolution of CapacityGap\_j depends on:

Whether IndividualCapacity\_j(f) can reach NeedBound\_j.

We lock down “growth of individual effective capacity” here.

Definition Four: Individual Effective Capacity

IndividualCapacity\_j(f) = NativeCapacity\_j(f) + ExtendedCapacity\_j(f)

Where NativeCapacity\_j is the native capacity of the individual field itself; ExtendedCapacity\_j is the effective capacity increment obtained through extension mechanisms such as tools, Lodging-Vessel extension, external connection, and parallelism.

Key question: is the growth of ExtendedCapacity\_j capped by single field capacity (Cap\_f)?

We lock down “extended capacity not capped by field” here.

Definition Five: External Connection Capacity Pathway Exists and Is Sufficient

Condition Two formally stated: let PathSufficient\_j := ExternalExtensionPath\_j ≠ ∅ ∧ sup(ExtendedCapacity\_j) \> NeedBound\_j — that is, at least one pathway exists for extending the j-th dimension capacity through external resources (tools, Lodging-Vessel extension, external connection systems, parallel structures); this pathway does not depend on the internal capacity (Cap\_f) of single field f, and the capacity upper bound of this pathway exceeds the individual actual requirement upper bound NeedBound\_j.

Note: pathway existence (ExternalExtensionPath\_j ≠ ∅) does not automatically imply pathway sufficiency (sup(ExtendedCapacity\_j) \> NeedBound\_j) — if the external resources themselves have a finite upper bound below NeedBound\_j, even if the pathway exists, CapacityGap\_j still cannot tend to zero. Therefore Condition Two must simultaneously satisfy both pathway existence and pathway sufficiency.

Condition Two and Condition Three are independent: Condition Two states “the structural pathway for capacity expansion exists and is sufficient”; Condition Three states “the conversion process of group functions to individual capacity persistently occurs”; the two support each other — Condition Three’s operation actually activates Condition Two’s pathway, but their conditions for holding do not mutually presuppose each other.

Six,

Now, since IndividualCapacity\_j can in principle exceed NeedBound\_j, must CapacityGap\_j necessarily tend to zero?

One more step is needed.

IndividualCapacity\_j surpassing GroupSupport\_j requires a process — functions originally provided by groups need to be toolized and absorbed into individual effective capacity. If this process is permanently blocked, the dissolution of CapacityGap\_j cannot occur in the limit.

We lock down “toolization process” here.

Definition Six: Toolization Process

ToolizationRate\_j(f,t) represents the rate at which group functions in the j-th dimension are replaced by tools and absorbed into ExtendedCapacity\_j.

The toolization process does not permanently tend to zero, and has long-term cumulative effective quantity; in this chapter we take the strong form:

T\_j := ∫₀^∞ ToolizationRate\_j(f,t) dt = ∞

That is: the toolization process has cumulative effective quantity on an infinite time scale, and does not permanently tend to zero after finite time.

Seven,

Now ask: can the toolization process be permanently blocked?

Historically, all mechanisms attempting to permanently block technology diffusion have failed — the diffusion of writing, printing, and the internet was not permanently blocked. This is not empirical induction, but a structural reason:

The driving force of toolization comes from CapacityGap itself — as long as gaps exist, there is structural pressure to fill them; this pressure prevents the toolization process from permanently stopping over the long term. Permanently blocking toolization requires permanently maintaining blocking mechanisms, but blocking mechanisms themselves consume resources, have finite sedimentation depth, and cannot maintain effective blocking indefinitely.

Therefore: on pathways where gap pressure still exists, the toolization process cannot be structurally permanently blocked.

We lock down “blocking not sustainable” here.

Definition Seven: Blocking Not Sustainable

Any mechanism blocking ToolizationRate\_j, itself being a social field structure, is subject to the Field Chapter’s compression dynamic constraints — its sedimentation depth is finite, its maintenance cost grows superlinearly with scale, and it cannot maintain effective blocking over infinite time.

Therefore over sufficiently long time, the toolization process will not permanently tend to zero, and has cumulative effective quantity.

Eight,

At this point, three minimal conditions have been progressively derived:

Condition One: NeedBound\_j \< ∞, GroupSupport\_j ≤ NeedBound\_j (finite requirements imply finite group support upper bound)  
Condition Two: PathSufficient\_j holds, i.e., external connection capacity pathway exists and is sufficient (ExternalExtensionPath\_j ≠ ∅ ∧ sup(ExtendedCapacity\_j) \> NeedBound\_j)  
Condition Three T\_j: ∫₀^∞ ToolizationRate\_j(f,t) dt = ∞ (toolization process does not permanently tend to zero, has long-term cumulative effective quantity)

These three conditions constitute the minimal sufficient structure for deriving CapacityGap\_j → 0 under this chapter’s gap-dissolution pathway.

Missing any one condition:

Without Condition One: GroupSupport\_j can grow without bound, CapacityGap\_j maintained permanently (requirement synchronous expansion scenario)  
Without Condition Two: ExternalExtensionPath\_j = ∅ or sup(ExtendedCapacity\_j) ≤ NeedBound\_j, IndividualCapacity\_j cannot exceed NeedBound\_j, CapacityGap\_j maintained permanently  
Without Condition Three: toolization permanently blocked, IndividualCapacity\_j cannot absorb GroupSupport\_j, CapacityGap\_j maintained permanently

With all three simultaneously holding, we can derive:

lim(t→∞) CapacityGap\_j(f,t) = 0 (∀ j ∈ KeyFunctions)

⇒ lim(t→∞) SocialDependency(f,t) = 0

⇒ NST(f) (Non-Social Terminal State)

Nine,

Now ask: can the three conditions simultaneously and permanently fail to hold?

The opposite of Condition One (GroupSupport\_j having no finite upper bound) requires the individual’s actual functional requirements to be infinite — this contradicts the Self-Manifesting Being’s finiteness. Under the premise of the Self-Manifesting Being’s finiteness, Condition One holds structurally.

The opposite of Condition Two (ExtendedCapacity\_j being permanently capped) requires external connection pathways to not exist or pathway capacity upper bound to be insufficient. Condition Two must be independently established through PathSufficient\_j: i.e., external connection pathway exists (ExternalExtensionPath\_j ≠ ∅) and pathway capacity upper bound exceeds NeedBound\_j (sup(ExtendedCapacity\_j) \> NeedBound\_j). Condition Three (T\_j holding) can only indicate the toolization process will not permanently stop, but cannot independently derive Condition Two — toolization persistently occurring does not equal external connection pathway being sufficient. The two hold independently: Condition Two guarantees pathway sufficiency, Condition Three guarantees the pathway is persistently activated, jointly deriving that ExtendedCapacity\_j actually persistently grows and ultimately exceeds NeedBound\_j.

The opposite of Condition Three (toolization permanently blocked) has been proved structurally unsustainable by Definition Seven.

On the Self-Manifesting Being’s sustainable expansion pathway, if the three conditions simultaneously hold, CapacityGap → 0 is a structural limit; within the functional social dependence gap-dissolution pathway, the Non-Social Terminal State is the only structurally stable limit.

lim(t→∞) CapacityGap\_j(f,t) = 0 (∀ j ∈ KeyFunctions)

Proof:

By Condition One, GroupSupport\_j ≤ NeedBound\_j \< ∞, so the positive part of CapacityGap\_j = max(0, GroupSupport\_j − IndividualCapacity\_j) is bounded above by NeedBound\_j;

By Condition Three T\_j holding (∫₀^∞ ToolizationRate\_j dt = ∞), under the premise that ToolizationRate\_j effectively maps to ExtendedCapacity\_j, the toolization process has long-term cumulative effective quantity, causing group functions to be persistently toolized and absorbed into ExtendedCapacity\_j;

Three conditions combined: Δ\_j(f,t) = max(0, GroupSupport\_j − IndividualCapacity\_j) → 0 (∀ j);

By the SocialDependency definition (Social Chapter 19), SocialDependency = Σ\_j w\_j · Δ\_j / Σ\_j w\_j · GroupSupport\_j; GroupSupport\_j remains positive (Δ\_j → 0 is realized through IndividualCapacity\_j rising, not through GroupSupport\_j degrading to zero, so the denominator is nonzero);

Therefore SocialDependency → 0, NST(f) holds. ∎

Minimal Sufficiency:

Without Condition One: GroupSupport\_j can grow synchronously, CapacityGap\_j does not tend to zero, NST(f) does not hold.  
Without Condition Two: IndividualCapacity\_j permanently capped, cannot exceed NeedBound\_j, CapacityGap\_j does not tend to zero, NST(f) does not hold.

Therefore the three conditions constitute the minimal sufficient structure for NST(f). ∎

## Section 5. Structural Breakdown Conditions

Counter-example One (requirement synchronous expansion): if GroupSupport\_j and IndividualCapacity\_j both grow without bound synchronously (individual requirements infinite), CapacityGap\_j can be maintained permanently nonzero — but at this point Condition One does not hold (GroupSupport\_j has no finite upper bound). This shows the Non-Social Terminal State depends on finite individual actual requirements, not absolute capacity comparison. This is the boundary of Condition One, does not overturn the theorem.

Counter-example Two (permanent technology blockade): if extreme technology monopoly makes ExtendedCapacity\_j permanently unable to grow, Condition Two does not hold and CapacityGap\_j cannot tend to zero. But by Definition Seven, permanent blockade itself as a social field structure has superlinearly growing maintenance costs and is structurally unsustainable — over sufficiently long time the blockade necessarily relaxes and Condition Two holds over the long term. This is a time-scale boundary, does not overturn the theorem.

Counter-example Three (group synchronous upgrade): if group continuous upgrading causes GroupSupport\_j growth rate to persistently exceed IndividualCapacity\_j, Condition One holds (NeedBound\_j finite) but the gap may first expand then tend to zero. This shows the speed of tending to zero depends on growth rate comparison, but as long as the three conditions hold, the limit remains zero. This is a speed boundary, does not overturn the theorem.

Definitional Boundary Four (emotional dimension not included): even if functional social dependence tends to zero, emotional coupling may persist long-term. Since this chapter only discusses functional dependence as defined by CapacityGap, and not the general formula of emotional dependence, NST(f) only indicates the functional non-social terminal state and does not indicate that emotional dependence must disappear. The general formula of emotional dependence is left for subsequent chapters. This is the definitional boundary of this theorem, not a counter-example.

Definitional Boundary Five (capacity degradation): if IndividualCapacity\_j undergoes sustained structural degradation (age-related incapacity, body damage, external connection system disconnection, etc.), the already-dissolved CapacityGap\_j can re-emerge and NST(f) no longer holds. Therefore this chapter discusses the limit structure on the capacity expansion pathway, not the capacity decline pathway — Appendix Three’s dissolution irreversibility condition has already specified “IndividualCapacity\_j does not undergo structural degradation” as a premise. This is an explicit boundary of the formation conditions, strengthening the applicable scope of this theorem.

## Appendix Four: Non-Social Phase Diagram

## Section One. Phase Diagram Setup

SocialDependency(f) is not a single numerical value but a multidimensional functional gap vector:

Δ(f) = (CapacityGap\_{j₁}, CapacityGap\_{j₂}, …, CapacityGap\_{jₙ})

Different combinations of gaps across dimensions constitute different social states; a single numerical value cannot distinguish their structural differences.

We therefore define a two-dimensional phase diagram, describing the basic structure of social dependence with two independent axes:

Horizontal axis: FD (functional dependence) = (∑\_{j∈FuncDims} w\_j · CapacityGap\_j(f)) / (∑\_{j∈FuncDims} w\_j · GroupSupport\_j(f))  
Vertical axis: PD (psychological dependence) = (∑\_{j∈PsychDims} w\_j · CapacityGap\_j(f)) / (∑\_{j∈PsychDims} w\_j · GroupSupport\_j(f))

Where FuncDims = {Sensory Extension, Action Execution, Construction and Manufacturing, Communication and Information Acquisition, Cognition and Computation, Survival and Repair}  
PsychDims = {emotional identification, cultural belonging, meaning generation, psychological experience} (to be formalized in subsequent chapters)

## Section Two. Four-Quadrant Phase States

S1 (Complete Social State): FD high ∧ PD high

Functional and emotional dependence both highly dependent on groups.  
Corresponding to: ant, bee, termite and other eusocial insects; naked mole rat, wolf pack, orca pod and other highly cooperative animals; early clan/tribal communities. Social groups constitute survival, reproduction, and learning advantages in most key dimensions; among eusocial insects approaching “structural dissolution upon leaving the group,” mammalian social groups exhibit high functional gain and cooperative dependence. The closer to primates and human communities, the higher the weight of PD and cultural dependence.

S2 (Toolized Social State): FD low ∧ PD high

Functional dependence already dissolved through toolization; emotional dependence remains strong.  
Corresponding to: contemporary highly industrialized humans — technology has made material functions independent, but emotional belonging still depends on others.  
The functional component of SocialDependency tends to zero, but the emotional component is maintained.

S3 (Functional Non-Social State): FD low ∧ PD low (moderate)

Functional dependence dissolved; emotional dependence weakened but still present.  
Corresponding to: NST(f) from this chapter’s main theorem — the functional non-social terminal state.  
In this state social relationships become optional rather than structurally compulsory.

S4 (Complete Non-Social State): FD → 0 ∧ PD → 0

Both functional and emotional dependence tend to zero, or boundary absorption causes both to have undefined definitions.  
Corresponding to: NST\_A (Appendix Five boundary absorption formula) — the social field directly generates psychological experience, and others are no longer a prerequisite for psychological needs.  
This state is the more complete form of the non-social terminal state.

## Section Three. Evolutionary Trajectory

Under the three conditions holding (Appendix Three), the evolutionary trajectory is:

S1 → S2 → S3 (→ S4)

Functional dependence dissolves first (toolization process)
