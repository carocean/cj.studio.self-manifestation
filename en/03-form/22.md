# General Form of the Observation-Domain

This chapter attempts a general proposition:

To admit the fact that "not everything can manifest" necessarily means this: within the system there exists a set of structures by which a stable and variable division is formed between the manifestable and the unmanifestable. The Observation-Domain is not the physical limit of the receptors, but the structural boundary pressed out jointly by candidacy, admissibility, and convergence reachability; under the outside-existence condition H\_excl it is a genuine limit on the set of structures within the pool, and it varies with capacity, dispersal, Familiar Routes, and orientation.

Note on numbering and position in the derivation chain: this chapter is numbered, according to order of composition, as Form Chapter 22. Its logical position lies after the Observer Forms (Form Chapters 4 and 5) and before the Mis-Externalization Forms (Form Chapters 7 through 9): the Observer gives "who is occupying"; the Observation-Domain gives "what can enter the competition for occupancy"; Mis-Externalization gives "how the occupier is source-attributed."

Division of labor with neighboring chapters:

Form Chapters 4 and 5 treat the generation and mechanism of occupancy: among candidates that have already entered the competition, why exactly one is maintained as current. This chapter treats an earlier division: among all structures within the pool, which structures can enter this competition and can in principle be converged, and which can never do so. The former is the problem of uniqueness inside the competition; this chapter is the problem of eligibility for competition and the boundary of manifestability.

Division of labor with the Recognition-Realm Forms (Form Chapters 13 through 17): the Observation-Domain is the boundary of manifestation - whether a structure can be lit up (its first two gates give eligibility for competition). The Recognition-Realm is the boundary of discrimination - into what distinctions the already manifested can be divided. A thing may be distinguishable yet unable to enter; a thing may be able to enter yet hard to distinguish. The two are orthogonal. The Observation-Domain stretches and contracts in real time with capacity and orientation; the Recognition-Realm is the structural limit reached when indistinguishability relations become stabilized through closure, and can be broken through only when the relations themselves change.

Division of labor with the pool-boundary (Observer Group: the layered definition of pool tracks and the pre-identification protocol, Mathematical Form of Observer Individuation, Section 6): the pool-boundary is the boundary of individuation, delimiting who constitutes one Observer and how many Observers there are in a system. The Observation-Domain operates within an already established Observer, delimiting what that Observer can light up. The pool-boundary is logically prior: only after there is "exactly one Observer" can one speak of its Observation-Domain.

Our aim is:

to obtain the minimal paradigmatic model of the Observation-Domain structure, to give the conditions under which its existence and nontriviality hold, to give the determining factors and dynamics of boundary-change, and to give testable corollaries and breach paths.

First state the result.

Minimal General Form of the Observation-Domain

E(t) = Cand(t) ∩ Adm(t)  (eligibility domain: those already having eligibility for competition, not those already in competition)

W(t) = E(t) ∩ Reach(t)  (manifestable domain, namely the Observation-Domain: those that, under the current structure and parameters, can in principle be lit up)

M(t) = E(t) ∖ W(t)  (boundary-fringe: the retention band of those already having eligibility for competition but unreachable for manifestation under the current parameters)

Under the simultaneous conditions that the pool is established, the premise of Objectification (continuous input and finite resources) holds, and convergence dynamics (the candidate-running definitions of the Observer Mathematical Form; their coefficients are a conditional construction, see Observer Construction Form H6) holds, a manifestable domain W(t) ⊆ E(t) ⊆ Ξ(P) forms over the set of structures within the pool: under the outside-existence condition H\_excl, W(t) is a proper subset; within the unique-occupancy zone (periods in which a maintained occupancy already exists), W(t) is nonempty; within the manifestation-vacancy zone, W(t) = ∅, while E and M still preserve the Observation-Domain structure. W is given by the intersection of three gates: the gate of candidacy, the gate of admissibility, and the gate of convergence reachability. If a structure fails to pass any one of these three gates, then under the current structure and parameters it lies outside the Observation-Domain, no matter how physically near it is to the receptors and no matter how intense it is. If a strong stimulus changes the channel of candidacy or changes the system parameters, that belongs to boundary-change (W itself is rewritten), not to the direct manifestation of content outside the boundary.

In one sentence:

Manifestation has a boundary (a genuine one under H\_excl); the boundary is not range, but structure; the shape of the boundary is not fixed, but varies with parameters.

Abstract

The Observer Forms have already shown: under multiple-candidate coexistence and the determinacy gap, exactly one candidate is maintained as current. The problem then moves one step earlier: where do candidates come from? Why can some structures never become candidates? Why can the same structure be manifestable at one moment and unmanifestable at another?

This chapter proposes: the division between the manifestable and the unmanifestable is neither the physical range of the receptors, nor a synonym for attention, but the conjunction of three independent conditions: a structure must be unfoldable as a candidate (candidacy), must pass candidate admissibility (admission), and under the current parameters there must exist a realizable allocation of resources that makes it convergence-reachable (reachability). The first two gates give the eligibility domain E(t), those already having eligibility for competition; the conjunction of all three gates gives the manifestable domain W(t), namely the Observation-Domain - those that, under the current structure and parameters, can in principle be lit up. Their difference, M(t) = E ∖ W, is the boundary-fringe - the retention band of those having eligibility but being unreachable for manifestation.

The three gates give W ⊆ E ⊆ Ξ(P); under the outside-existence condition H\_excl (there exist structures in the pool that cannot pass the gate of candidacy or of admissibility), both E and W are proper subsets of the whole set of structures in the pool - finite resources and finite resolution by themselves do not suffice to imply this condition, since manifestability is reachability one by one, not simultaneous; nonemptiness holds by zones (within the unique-occupancy zone W is nonempty; within the manifestation-vacancy zone W is empty, while the Observation-Domain structure persists in the form of E and M). The eligibility domain, manifestable domain, and boundary-fringe together induce a four-band division over the structures within the pool - outside the boundary, boundary-fringe (eligible but unreachable), unmanifested within the boundary (manifestable but not currently manifest), and manifestation (maintained occupancy). Manifestation can arrive only along the chain of candidacy and convergence; it cannot jump layers. The distinction between boundary-fringe and the unmanifested within the boundary is itself testable: under unchanged parameters, boundary-fringe content cannot manifest no matter how long it is exposed, whereas content unmanifested within the boundary can manifest if exposure is lengthened or competition is reduced. The precise meaning of boundary-fringe must be noted: its content can be processed by the system, can be acted on by the competition mechanism, can enter pre-manifest dynamics and produce bias, but under the current parameters it cannot reach occupancy - it is "eligible but unreachable," not "already on the field but doomed to lose."

The boundary-shape of W is determined by four classes of factors: structural factors (Receptive-Field configuration and distribution of Familiar Routes), capacity factors (Φ\_cap), dispersal factors (η), and orientation factors (the bias of R through P). From these one obtains the dynamics of boundary-change: capacity decline does not expand the Observation-Domain (under the comparison conditions of Theorem 3(i); and, under H\_ord and the familiarity–threshold bridge H\_fam, it leans in weak order toward more familiar content: the tunnel effect under load); when the convergence-reachability condition is gradually pressed down, the Observation-Domain may first contract and then empty (a conditional interpretation, to be tested: it requires dispersal monotonicity along complete paths; the gradual version of anesthesia; for the criterion of manifestation vacancy see the Observer Mathematical Form, Theorems 7 and 11 — the vacancy criterion is not r ≤ η but the non-existence of any realizable candidate–integration–maintenance path; within the single-candidate, no-overlap, constant-coefficient linear model, r ≤ η excludes only new completion from below threshold and does not by itself decide vacancy); tools and training expand the boundary without increasing the number of Observers; orientation bias reshapes the boundary in real time without canceling the boundary.

The testable corollaries of this chapter include: hemispatial neglect is a criterial case of half-side boundary-fringing (pool-boundary intact, Recognition-Realm intact, eligibility domain retained while reachability of one side goes to zero); neglected content should retain pre-manifest effects, and cueing or motivational intervention should be able to make that side temporarily re-manifest; blindsight is a chronic case of the boundary-fringe (eligibility retained, convergence pathway damaged); under dual-task load (under H\_ord and H\_fam), the loss of peripheral content should follow a weak order: more familiar content is lost no earlier than shallower content (strict unevenness requiring further conditions).

The core conclusion of this chapter is: the Observer solves "which one is here now"; the Observation-Domain solves "what can be lit up"; the boundary-fringe marks out "having eligibility but being unreachable for manifestation." That this boundary is a genuine one rests on H\_excl; finite capacity limits the family of structures that can be jointly carried at the same moment, and whether all of Ξ(P) can be jointly carried must be decided together with the joint allocation constraints and the total resource demand - its impossibility cannot be inferred from finite capacity alone.

Introduction

One fact has long been wrongly explained in two ways.

The fact is: not everything can manifest. Standing at the same place, what one person sees another does not; the same stimulus enters vision at one moment, yet is seen without seeing at another; some content, no matter how one tries, cannot enter the Reality of a certain Observer.

The first wrong explanation attributes this to receptors: one cannot see because the range is insufficient or the resolution is insufficient. But inattentional blindness refutes this - the stimulus is within range and sufficiently intense, yet still may not manifest. Tools also refute it - the telescope does not change the eye, but it changes the manifestable domain.

The second wrong explanation equates it with attention: the manifestable domain is the aperture of attention. But attention is only one of the factors of boundary-change - when there is no active attention, the Observation-Domain still has its default shape; and the Observation-Domain contains the layer of the unmanifested within the boundary, which the aperture of attention does not contain.

This chapter gives a third explanation: the division between the manifestable and the unmanifestable is a structural boundary pressed out jointly by three gates: candidacy, admissibility, and convergence reachability. Inner Chapter 16 has already given the philosophical statement of this boundary - not every input enters Reality; the Observation-Domain determines what can be lit up. Field Chapter 20 gives its dynamical conditions in the Sedimentation-Field (what that citation actually carries is to be re-checked when Field Chapter 20 is upgraded, see matter awaiting formalization (f); this chapter does not use the citation to fill gaps in its own proofs). This chapter gives its minimal paradigm: under what conditions it exists, what determines it, how it changes shape, and what necessarily fails when it is absent.

The Stream of Argument

We proceed by continuous questioning, step by step; hence a stream of argument is formed, and it should be read from the top down.

I. The Existence of the Boundary Is Not Accidental

First ask at the shallowest level: not everything can manifest - is this an empirical accident, or a structural necessity?

Suppose the opposite holds: every structure within the pool can manifest. Finite capacity limits the family of structures that can be jointly carried at the same moment; whether all of Ξ(P) can be jointly carried must be decided together with the joint allocation constraints and the total resource demand, and its impossibility cannot be inferred from finite capacity alone. Moreover, manifestability is reachability one by one, not simultaneous: suppose the pool has only two distinguishable structures, both candidizable and both admitted, and finite resources suffice each time to support either one; then each is reachable, everything can manifest, and no infinite capacity is needed.

The first premise of the General Form of Objectification is finite resources. The Field Chapters give finite resolution (Res) and finite capacity (φ\_f). The Observer Mathematical Form gives finite tuning capacity (Φ\_cap). These premises limit the family of structures that can be jointly carried at the same moment, and whether they exclude all manifesting at once depends on the joint allocation constraints and the total resource demand; finite resolution excludes candidacy only when the declared Ξ(P) contains structural differences below the threshold of discrimination.

Therefore, finite resources and finite resolution alone do not imply that some structures must lie outside the manifestable domain. This chapter declares the existence of an outside as the condition H\_excl: within the declared set of structures in the pool Ξ(P), there exist structures that cannot pass the gate of candidacy or of admissibility. Whether H\_excl necessarily follows from finite resources and finite resolution must be proven separately, together with the range of Ξ(P) and the candidacy mechanism (matter awaiting formalization (d)).

Thus the boundary is a genuine one under H\_excl; this chapter treats its constitution, shape, and dynamics, and lists H\_excl itself as to be proven.

II. Upon What Is the Boundary Drawn?

The boundary presupposes a pool. By the Mathematical Form of Observer Individuation, Section 6, the pool is delimited by the Sep criterion (Sep against Coact + Infilt + Reentry) into candidate tracks and stable tracks — that protocol presently stands at framework grade, its calibration open (Observer Adjudication Form H5, half-closed) — giving the individuation range of one Observer. The whole discussion of this chapter takes place within a single pool. The pool-boundary and the Observation-Domain are different layers: the pool-boundary delimits between Observers; the Observation-Domain delimits, within one Observer, the manifestable and the unmanifestable.

Let the set of structures within the pool be Ξ(P). The Observation-Domain is a division over Ξ(P).

III. The Three Gates

For a structure to manifest, it must in sequence satisfy three independent conditions. All three can be read from the existing structures of the Observer Forms; but in the Observer Forms they are moments in the generation of occupancy, while in this chapter they are restated as boundary conditions.

Definition 1: Gate of Candidacy

A structure x can, at time t, be unfolded by candidate-unfolding C into a candidate. This is written Cand(x, t) = 1. What cannot be unfolded - where unfolding capacity does not cover it, or where no transduction channel exists - can never enter the competition, no matter how physically near it is.

Definition 2: Gate of Admissibility

A candidate x passes through candidate-limitation B and enters the finite competition set. This is written Adm(x, t) = 1. The function of admissibility is to prevent unbounded parallel inflow of candidates (the unified capacity constraint of the Observer Mathematical Form). Its reverse is: what is excluded by admissibility does not enter the competition.

Definition 3: Gate of Convergence Reachability

Under the parameters of time t (r, η, χ, Ovl, Φ\_cap) and the realizable allocation set 𝒳(t), there exist a realizable allocation x⃗ ∈ 𝒳(t) and a complete candidate–integration–maintenance path such that x reaches (or is already at) the level of occupancy (z = 1) in finite time and is maintained (maintenance per the completion-retention condition of the Observer Mathematical Form, Definition 47). This is written Reach(x, t) = 1.

Two points must be made clear. First, Reach is a counterfactual judgment of reachability; it does not require x actually to manifest - "has never manifested" does not imply "unmanifestable," otherwise the Observation-Domain would circularly equal the history of manifestation. Second, "reachable" means reaching in finite time and being maintainable; it does not mean asymptotic tendency. The equilibrium-value criterion is listed separately as the equilibrium-admissibility condition Eq (Definition 3′); Eq is not automatically the same as the existence of an actual convergence, integration and maintenance path. What tends but does not reach under some allocation is placed in the boundary-fringe on that ground only when no other legitimate complete reachable path exists; asymptotic non-arrival under one allocation does not suffice to deny reachability under other allocations.

Definition 3′: Equilibrium-Admissibility Condition

Eq(x, t) = 1 ⟺ ∃ x⃗ ∈ 𝒳(t) such that, under that allocation, the equilibrium value of z\_x is z\*\_x = r·x\_x/d\_x ≥ 1, where d\_x = η + χ·Σ Ovl(x, ·)·ẑ (the opponent term being the effective suppression under that allocation), r \> 0, d\_x \> 0. In the constant-coefficient linear model ż\_x = r·x\_x − d\_x·z\_x, starting from z₀ \< 1, z first reaches 1 in finite time if and only if r·x\_x \> d\_x; in the case of equality z only approaches 1 asymptotically and does not reach it (example: a single candidate without suppression, r = η = 1, allocation share 1, z₀ = 0; then z(t) = 1 − e^{−t}, the equilibrium value is 1, and at no finite time is 1 reached). Hence within this model Reach ⇒ Eq, and not conversely: Eq contains neither the strict inequality nor the integration and maintenance duties.

Definition 4: Realizable Allocation Set

𝒳(t) is the set of resource-allocation schemes that the system can actually realize at time t, determined jointly by orientation-source R, orientation generation and entry-domain P, drive D, and the concrete capacity of the tuning mechanism.

Precision note: the modal quantifier here must be bound to 𝒳(t). It cannot range over all mathematically possible allocations - otherwise Reach would be systematically overestimated and the Observation-Domain would become vacuous. Once bound, Reach depends on the actual realization of R, P, and D. This is not a defect, but precisely the proposition itself: the Observation-Domain is a structural boundary; on the same physical substrate, different orientation and tuning mechanisms give different Observation-Domains.

Definition 5: Eligibility Domain, Observation-Domain, and Boundary-Fringe

Eligibility domain:

E(t) = { x ∈ Ξ(P) : Cand(x, t) = 1 ∧ Adm(x, t) = 1 }

These are those already having eligibility for competition. Note: having eligibility is not the same as already being in competition; whether one enters the domain and joins the competition is decided by allocation dynamics.

Observation-Domain (manifestable domain):

W(t) = E(t) ∩ { x : Reach(x, t) = 1 }

These are those that, under the current structure and parameters, can in principle be lit up. The statement in Inner Chapter 16 that "the Observation-Domain determines what can be lit up" corresponds to this set.

Boundary-fringe:

M(t) = E(t) ∖ W(t)

These are those already having eligibility for competition, but which under the current parameters and realizable allocations have no complete reachable path. The boundary-fringe is decided by E ∖ W: its content may remain below threshold (including those with equilibrium value z\* \< 1 and the asymptotic case z\* = 1), or its scalar progress may already be complete while the integration or maintenance duty is not satisfied; z reaching 1 does not by itself suffice to decide Occ. Boundary-fringe content can be acted on by the competition mechanism, can obtain resources, can form partial progress, and can produce functional effects; but no matter how long it is exposed, it cannot manifest under unchanged parameters.

The three gates stand to W in a conjunctive relation: if any one gate is absent, x ∉ W(t); if the third gate is absent while the first two are present, x ∈ M(t).

IV. Existence and Nontriviality

Theorem 1 (the "unique" in the unique-occupancy zone rests on the pool-level current single-value principle; see the declaration in the appearing-layer clause): Observation-Domain Existence Theorem

Under the simultaneous conditions that the pool has been established, the premise of Objectification holds, and convergence dynamics holds:

(i) proper-subsethood (conditional): the three gates give W(t) ⊆ E(t) ⊆ Ξ(P); under H\_excl, E(t) ⊊ Ξ(P), and therefore W(t) ⊊ Ξ(P);

(ii) nonemptiness (by zone): within the unique-occupancy zone, W(t) ≠ ∅; within the manifestation-vacancy zone, W(t) = ∅, while the Observation-Domain structure persists in the form of E(t) and M(t).

Proof:

Proper-subsethood. W ⊆ E ⊆ Ξ(P) is given directly by Definition 5. The three gates need not each individually be a proper subset - at a given moment Adm may exclude none of the already-candidized structures; Reach is reachability one by one (there need only exist some allocation for each, not simultaneously) and is not automatically limited by finite capacity. Hence finite resources alone do not imply an outside structure: if two distinguishable structures are both candidizable and both admitted, and finite resources suffice each time to support either one, then E = W = Ξ(P). Nor does finite resolution guarantee that Ξ(P) contains elements below the capacity of discrimination; that depends on how Ξ(P) is declared. Under H\_excl there exists x ∈ Ξ(P) with Cand(x, t) = 0 or Adm(x, t) = 0, so x ∉ E(t), E(t) ⊊ Ξ(P), and by conjunction W(t) ⊊ Ξ(P) all the more. Whether H\_excl necessarily follows from finite resources and finite resolution must be proven separately, together with the range of Ξ(P) and the candidacy mechanism (matter awaiting formalization (d)). Q.E.D.

Note: v1.1 argued in three points that proper-subsethood holds unconditionally - finite resolution (Field Chapter 12: finite Res) makes structural differences below the threshold of discrimination non-candidizable; the orientation mechanism cannot direct itself toward targets it cannot represent; the three gates being fully open at once would require infinite capacity. The first two points give reasons to expect H\_excl, not a proof: the former holds only when Ξ(P) contains structural differences below the threshold of discrimination, and the latter limits only 𝒳(t), without excluding reachability one by one; nor does the third point stand as a proof: for a finite Ξ(P), the joint resource demand of all structures may still be finite, and whether simultaneous total manifestation is possible must be decided together with the joint allocation constraints and the total resource demand.

Nonemptiness. The unique-occupancy zone means periods in which a maintained occupancy already exists; the pool-level current single-value principle (a constitutive commitment) gives only at-most-one and does not provide existence, and "unique occupancy" is a work-domain notation under that principle, not a proven theorem. The occupier must already have passed through all three gates, and is already at the level of occupancy and maintained (Definition 3); hence c\*ₜ ∈ W(t), and W(t) ≠ ∅. Within the manifestation-vacancy zone (general criterion: there exists no realizable candidate–integration–maintenance path; within the single-candidate, no-overlap, constant-coefficient linear model, r ≤ η excludes only new completion from below threshold and does not by itself decide vacancy, see Theorem 3(ii)), Reach is zero over the whole domain - by Definition 3, Reach(x, t) = 1 must be witnessed by a complete realizable candidate–integration–maintenance path, which is incompatible with the vacancy criterion; hence W(t) = ∅ - the manifestable domain as a whole disappears. Yet the eligibility domain E(t) may persist, and candidates, partial-progress states, and those whose scalar progress is complete while the integration or maintenance duty is not satisfied are retained in the boundary-fringe M(t). When manifestation is interrupted, the system is not empty of content; rather, all content retreats into the boundary-fringe. This limiting case is treated uniformly by the Boundary-Change Theorem (Theorem 3), in Section 7. Q.E.D.

V. The Four-Band Division and the Impossibility of Layer-Jumping

Definition 6: The Four Bands

The eligibility domain, manifestable domain, and boundary-fringe induce a four-band division over the set of structures within the pool:

Outside the boundary: x ∉ E(t). Under unchanged current structure and parameters, it cannot become a competitor. If a stimulus or intervention changes the candidacy channel, admissibility configuration, or convergence parameters, then this belongs to boundary-change - after the boundary is rewritten, x can enter the domain. It is not the direct manifestation of outside-boundary content under the original boundary-shape (consistent with the downstream consequence of Theorem 2: outside-boundary structures can affect manifestation only by changing the boundary-shape, not by crossing the boundary as content).

Boundary-fringe: x ∈ M(t), decided by E ∖ W, not by z. It can be acted on by the competition mechanism, can obtain partial progress, and can produce functional effects; its scalar progress either remains below threshold or is complete while the integration or maintenance duty is not satisfied; but under the current parameters and realizable allocation no complete reachable path exists for it. Lengthening exposure and increasing stimulus intensity do not change its unmanifestability; what can be changed is only the boundary itself.

Unmanifested within the boundary: x ∈ W(t) ∖ Cur(t). This includes below-threshold advancing states - candidates (z = 0 as the starting point) and pre-manifest states (0 \< z \< 1, structurally real and functionally effective, but not having reached candidate completion and therefore not entering the appearing layer; for the definition of candidate completion see the Observer Mathematical Form, Definition 45) - and may also include completed candidates that have not yet obtained actual occupancy but for which a subsequent complete reachable path exists; it cannot be defined by z \< 1 alone. Its difference from the boundary-fringe: what is unmanifested within the boundary can manifest when competition is reduced or exposure is lengthened.

Appearing layer: x is maintained as occupancy (Occ = 1); pending the closure of the bridge between occupancy and CurrentFact (General Form of the Observer H3, Observer Realization Form H4, Observer Construction Form H10), this read-back is a working convention. This layer has at most one element per pool — not a theorem, but the pool-level current single-value principle: a constitutive commitment openly undertaken and open to empirical breach (Observer Group v1.0, Master Map, Section III; the General Form of Observer Individuation states expressly that one-pool-one-I is not proven). Wherever this chapter uses that at-most-one, it holds within the model in which that commitment holds.

The boundary-fringe and the pre-manifest state within the boundary are isomorphic in functional effect (both can be activated, biased, and sedimented); their criterion of distinction is manifestability, not functionality - and this distinction is itself testable through exposure operations.

Theorem 2: No-Layer-Jumping Theorem

Manifestation can arrive only along the chain "entry into the eligibility domain (if boundary-change occurs) → candidate → pre-manifest → occupancy." The pre-manifest state can advance to occupancy only within the manifestable domain. There is no manifestation that does not pass through candidacy and convergence, and there is no path directly from the boundary-fringe to occupancy (what is boundary-fringe must first move into W through boundary-change).

Proof: the definition of occupancy is a maintained completed state (z = 1 and sustained; candidate completion, Observer Mathematical Form, Definition 45). z is convergence progress, and the object of convergence is, by definition, a candidate (Observer Mathematical Form, Definitions 6 and 9). Therefore the occupier must first have been a candidate and must have been advanced by convergence. "Direct manifestation" without candidacy is not valid at the definitional layer; "instantaneous manifestation" without convergence advance is excluded by the continuity definition of occupancy. Q.E.D.

Direct downstream consequence of this theorem: outside-boundary structures can affect manifestation only indirectly - by changing field parameters and changing the boundary-shape - and cannot appear in manifestation as content.

VI. Determining Factors of Boundary-Shape

W(t) is not a fixed set. Its shape is determined by four classes of factors:

Structural factors: the configuration of Receptive-Fields determines the default coverage of the gate of candidacy; the distribution of Familiar Routes (Field Chapter 11 v1.2: familiar is a name for the deep, and being more familiar is compared by Sedimentation-Degree δ̄) is a slow-changing historical condition of the boundary-shape. Whether it lowers the equilibrium allocation threshold is not derived in this chapter from Field Chapter 11 - that chapter does not set up a condition under which the continuation cost decreases (likewise Form Chapter 10 v2.2, matter awaiting formalization (a)) - and so it is declared as the bridge hypothesis H\_fam (Appendix 1, Section II). Structural factors give the slow-changing default shape of the Observation-Domain.

Capacity factors: Φ\_cap determines the scale of competition that can be maintained at the same time and the richness of the realizable allocation set. If 𝒳(t) shrinks by inclusion as capacity declines, then, other conditions fixed, the pass set of Reach does not expand.

Dispersal factors: η affects the baseline of convergence reachability. Within the single-candidate, no-overlap, constant-coefficient linear model, new completion from below threshold requires r·u \> η (u being the allocation share, 0 ≤ u ≤ 1), so r \> η is a necessary condition of new completion within that model; the maintenance of an existing completed state is a separate matter (see Theorem 3(ii)); the general case is decided by complete paths. Whether Reach contracts as η rises depends on dispersal monotonicity along complete paths (unproven; see the conditional interpretation of Theorem 3(ii)).

Orientation factors: the bias of R entering the domain through P rewrites allocation in real time, thereby reshaping the local shape of Reach in real time; focusing through L rewrites the local passability of Adm. Orientation factors give the fast-changing shape of the Observation-Domain.

VII. Boundary-Change Dynamics

Theorem 3: Boundary-Change Theorem

(i) Capacity monotonicity: fix candidacy, admission and the other dynamical conditions; if, when capacity declines (or is occupied), the realizable allocation set 𝒳(t) shrinks by inclusion, then the pass set of Reach does not expand, and hence W does not expand. If the eligibility domain also changes, E\_low-capacity ⊆ E\_high-capacity is additionally required; a numerical decline in capacity alone cannot exclude new members appearing after admission is rearranged. Ordering: within a common eligibility domain, under fixed comparison conditions and H\_ord, content with a lower equilibrium allocation threshold does not lose reachability earlier than content with a higher one; H\_fam carries this weak order over to the comparison of familiarity (shallower content loses reachability no later than more familiar content), ties allowed. A strict order of precedence further requires a strict threshold difference and the capacity upper bound actually crossing the interval between the two (Appendix 1, Section II).

(ii) Dispersal criticality (restricted model): within the single-candidate, no-overlap, constant-coefficient linear model, with fixed allocation share 0 ≤ u ≤ 1 and η \> 0, starting from z₀ \< 1, if r ≤ η then 1 cannot be first reached in finite time. This conclusion excludes only new completion from below threshold within that model; it does not automatically exclude the maintenance of an existing completed state (example: r = η = 1, u = 1, no suppression; if z₀ = 1, then ż = 1 − z and z(t) ≡ 1), nor does it by itself imply that W or Cur is empty in general. General vacancy is still decided by the non-existence of a complete candidate–integration–maintenance path (Observer Mathematical Form, Theorems 7 and 11).

Conditional interpretation (prediction to be tested): if dispersal weakens reachability monotonically along complete paths, then as η gradually rises relative to r, content moves from W toward M, the manifestable domain thins and the boundary-fringe thickens; before manifestation is interrupted, one should first observe contraction of the manifestable domain and relative persistence of the functional effects of the boundary-fringe (as anesthesia deepens, explicit report disappears before implicit priming disappears). The scalar equation has not proven dispersal monotonicity along complete paths, nor does it guarantee strictly stepwise contraction.

(iii) Orientation reshaping: orientation bias can locally expand or compress W (expansion being constrained by 𝒳(t) and capacity factors); where H\_excl still holds on the rewritten configuration, it cannot cancel the proper-subsethood of W.

Proof sketch: (i) Reach requires ∃ x⃗ ∈ 𝒳(t), so if 𝒳(t) shrinks by inclusion its pass set does not expand; with E fixed or shrinking by inclusion, W does not expand; the ordering follows from Reach ⟺ c \< a(Φ\_cap) under H\_ord, a being monotonically non-decreasing in capacity; the order of familiarity is taken up separately by H\_fam (weak order) and is not derived from Field Chapter 11. (ii) in that model ż = r·u − η·z; first arrival at 1 in finite time from z₀ \< 1 requires r·u \> η (Definition 3′), contradicting r ≤ η and u ≤ 1; with z₀ = 1 and r·u = η, z ≡ 1, so maintenance is not excluded. (iii) orientation factors rewrite allocation and the local passability of Adm; where H\_excl still holds on the rewritten configuration, W ⊊ Ξ(P) follows from Theorem 1. Q.E.D.

Two expansion channels of boundary-change:

Tool expansion: a tool transduces structures that could not originally be candidized into structures that can be candidized (telescope, microscope, instrument), thereby expanding the pass set of Cand. Note that tool expansion does not change the pool-boundary and does not increase the number of Observers - it moves W, not N\_obs.

Training expansion: when training deepens the relevant routes, under H\_fam the equilibrium allocation threshold does not increase; only if the threshold actually decreases and crosses the current allocation upper bound, and the conditions of actual reachability and maintenance hold at the same time, can newly entering content be inferred from it.

VIII. Corollaries

Corollary 1: Criterion of Isolated Observation-Domain Damage (Testable)

The three boundaries can be damaged independently: damage to the pool-boundary changes the number of individuation-unit candidates (Mathematical Form of Observer Individuation: pool count and track count are not observer counters, so only the change in unit candidates is recorded); damage to the Recognition-Realm changes discriminative structure; damage to the Observation-Domain changes the manifestable domain without moving the first two. Hemispatial neglect is a criterial case of half-side boundary-fringing: the patient is a single Observer (pool-boundary intact), distinguishes normally what enters manifestation (Recognition-Realm intact), and the content of one side of space systematically fails to enter manifestation. Its structural location is not a deficit of candidacy or admissibility, but the structural reduction of the orientation factor's allocation for that side to zero, making the Reach of that side tend toward zero: eligibility retained, reachability abolished, content retained in the boundary-fringe. This Form therefore gives two strong predictions: first, neglected content should retain the functional effects of the boundary-fringe - measurable priming, preference, and avoidance bias - despite having no manifestation; second, any intervention that can temporarily rebuild the allocation of that side (exogenous cueing, motivational manipulation, contralateral adaptation) should be able temporarily to re-manifest the content of that side. Together, the two predictions turn neglect from a phenomenon awaiting explanation into criterial evidence of the four-band structure.

Corollary 2: Decoupling of Tools and the Number of Observers (Testable)

Any tool expansion changes no Observer count: expansion acts on Cand, while the pool-boundary criterion (Sep against Coact + Infilt + Reentry) does not involve candidacy channels. Its extrapolation connects with Outer Chapter, Entering-the-World: to construct a surrogate body in another world is, structurally, to re-establish candidacy channels and convergence mechanisms on a heterogeneous substrate - to re-establish the Observation-Domain, not to migrate the pool.

Corollary 3: Load Tunneling (Testable)

When cognitive load occupies capacity factors, W does not expand under the comparison conditions of Theorem 3(i); under H\_ord and H\_fam it also leans in weak order toward more familiar content (Theorem 3(i)). The measurable form is: under dual-task conditions, more familiar stimuli lose detection no earlier than shallower stimuli; a strict difference in detection rate further requires a strict threshold difference, the capacity upper bound actually crossing the interval between them, and a bridge between detection readings and actual manifestation; this order should vary with the gradient of load. This prediction tests H\_ord, H\_fam, the comparison conditions, and the bridge between detection readings and actual manifestation. Only when the other conditions have been independently confirmed can a reversed result be used to refute H\_fam; the absence of strict unevenness does not suffice to refute the weak H\_fam, which allows equal costs.

Corollary 4: Switching of Supply Source

The definitions of the eligibility domain and the manifestable domain contain no requirement that "input must come from receptors." By the General Form of Objectification, candidates have three sources: input compression, historical backflow, and internal unfolding. In dreams, receptor gating closes, and the supply source of candidacy switches to internal unfolding and historical backflow. E and W still hold - dreams have their own Observation-Domain and boundary-fringe (even in dreams, not everything can manifest). This corollary also shows: the Observation-Domain is not the range of receptors; the two are completely decoupled in dreams.

IX. Breach Arguments

We now test the most likely counterexamples and confusions.

Counterexample 1: the Observation-Domain is just the physical limit of the receptors.

This does not hold, for three independent refutations. Inattentional blindness: the stimulus is within range and sufficiently intense, yet because of orientation factors and admissibility it does not enter manifestation. Tool expansion: the receptors do not change, yet W changes. Dreaming: receptor gating closes, yet W remains (Corollary 4). Range is one physical condition of the gate of candidacy; it is not the Observation-Domain itself.

Counterexample 2: the Observation-Domain is just the Recognition-Realm.

This does not hold. Distinguishable yet unable to enter: neglect patients normally distinguish the same content when it is transferred to the intact side, showing that the discriminative structure is intact while the reachability of the neglected side is abolished (half-side boundary-fringing, Corollary 1) - content is unmanifestable on the original side, but once moved to the intact side it can manifest and be discriminated normally. Able to enter yet hard to distinguish: content under low-resolution conditions can manifest while its details remain hard to discriminate (blurred perception; the low-resolution case in the counterexample family of the Observer Group). The two boundaries are orthogonal, and their damages can be separated.

Counterexample 3: the Observation-Domain is just attention.

This does not hold. Attention corresponds to orientation factors and is one of the four factors of boundary-change. When there is no active attention, W still has a default shape given by structural factors. W includes the unmanifested within the boundary, and E further includes the boundary-fringe; the aperture of attention includes neither. Attention reshapes the boundary between W and M (rewriting local Reach through allocation) but does not create the boundary - that the boundary is a genuine one rests on H\_excl (Theorem 1), independent of whether there is attention. The category of the Observation-Domain is strictly broader than the focus of attention.

Counterexample 4: the all-manifest system.

Suppose there is a system with W = Ξ(P). This does not directly contradict finite resources - manifestability is reachability one by one, not simultaneous manifestation; whether simultaneous total manifestation is possible must be decided together with the joint allocation constraints and the total resource demand, and its impossibility cannot be inferred from finite capacity alone. This chapter excludes total manifestation by H\_excl: under H\_excl, E ⊊ Ξ(P), so W = Ξ(P) does not hold. For any claimed all-manifest system, one must check whether its Ξ(P) has been replaced by the set of structures already within the boundary.

Counterexample 5: subliminal perception and blindsight.

These two phenomena are often used to challenge the claim that manifestation has a clear boundary. Within the four-band structure, they are not counterexamples but direct instances of two different bands, and the experimental distinction between them corresponds exactly to the distinction between those bands. Subliminal priming: the content is within the manifestable domain, forms a pre-manifest state, and produces functional effects without reaching occupancy - it is unmanifested within the boundary. Therefore lengthening exposure or reducing competition can make it manifest; this is exactly the standard operation in subliminal paradigms. Blindsight: eligibility is retained (candidacy and admissibility channels are partially intact) while the convergence pathway is damaged, so Reach remains chronically zero - a chronic case of the boundary-fringe. Therefore it cannot manifest no matter how long it is exposed, while forced-choice advantage persists. Both are "having effects without manifestation"; one can turn manifest through exposure, and the other cannot. The four-band structure not only places the two phenomena, but also predicts their criterion of separation.

X. Positioning of the Three Boundaries

At this point the three boundaries can be stated side by side, fixing their respective layers:

Pool-boundary (Mathematical Form of Observer Individuation, Section 6): boundary of individuation. It answers "who is seeing." Criterion: Sep against Coact + Infilt + Reentry. Output: the number of individuation-unit candidates N\_cand; the observer number N\_obs is a conditional quantity under the NoShare and finality clauses.

Observation-Domain (this chapter): boundary of manifestation. It answers "what can be seen." Criterion: the conjunction of the three gates (the first two gates give eligibility domain E; the third gate gives manifestable domain W; their difference is boundary-fringe M). Output: four-band division (outside the boundary, boundary-fringe, unmanifested within the boundary, manifestation).

Recognition-Realm (Form Chapters 13 through 17): boundary of discrimination. It answers "into what can it be distinguished." Criterion: closure of indistinguishability relations. Output: structural limit-division.

The three are mutually presupposing but not interchangeable: the pool-boundary comes first, the Observation-Domain stands in the middle, and the Recognition-Realm acts upon what has already manifested. The three can be independently damaged, independently changed, and independently measured.

XI. Downstream Interfaces

Mis-Externalization interface: the object of Mis-Externalization is the converged-completed imagery (the downstream interface of the Observer Group (the interface sections of the Observer Mathematical Form and the Observer Realization Form)), namely an element of the appearing layer in this chapter. Outside the appearing layer - the unmanifested within the boundary and the boundary-fringe - no source is attributed. Pre-manifest states have no "inner / outer" labels. This supplies a boundary condition for the Mis-Externalization Forms: source attribution is defined only on the appearing layer.

Recognition-Realm interface: the indistinguishability relations of the Recognition-Realm act upon already manifested content. Changes of the Observation-Domain (expansion, contraction) change the domain of operation of the Recognition-Realm, but do not directly change its closure structure. This explains why merely obtaining a new input channel (boundary expansion) does not automatically bring cognitive transition (Recognition-Realm Transition Form, Form Chapter 16): entering the boundary is not the same as being distinguishable.

Entering-the-World interface: see Corollary 2.

Non-Social interface: the continuous increase of capacity, in the form of this chapter, is the continuous expansion of the pass sets of Cand and Reach. Expansion of the Observation-Domain lowers dependency on group supplementation, connecting with Form Chapter 21.

XII. Realization Correspondence (Microkernel Theory)

Gate of candidacy: Receptive-Field and signal-flow unfolding.

Gate of admissibility: Boundary-Gate.

Orientation factors: Intentional Domain through Desire-Gates.

Convergence reachability: the reachability of the Tuning-Field within capacity.

The Boundary-Gate is the institutional landing point of the eligibility domain. Above this, the manifestable domain further requires tuning reachability, whose institutional landing point is the Tuning-Field. This is consistent with the stratified explanation in the realization read-back section of the Observer Construction Form: the Boundary-Gate governs eligibility admission within an Observer (the eligibility domain of this chapter), while the pool-boundary is separately given by crosstalk connectivity (Mathematical Form of Observer Individuation, Section 6).

XIII. Conclusion

The conclusion of this chapter can be compressed into two statements:

The Observer solves "which one is here now";

The Observation-Domain solves "what can be lit up";

The boundary-fringe marks out "having eligibility but being unreachable for manifestation."

Compressed once more into the simplest sentence:

Without convergence, nothing appears here;

without the Observation-Domain, manifestation would have no way to delimit what is reachable - and that the boundary is a genuine one rests on the outside-existence condition H\_excl.

## Appendix 1: Symbolic Expression of the General Form of the Observation-Domain

I. Basic Domain and Notation

Let pool P have been established by the pool-track criteria of the Mathematical Form of Observer Individuation, Section 6. Let the set of structures within the pool be Ξ(P). We use the notation of the Observer Group: z (convergence progress), r (pushing rate), η (dispersal rate), χ (crosstalk coefficient), Ovl (overlap degree), Φ\_cap (capacity), and Occ (occupancy, including continuity).

II. The Three Gates

Cand(x, t) ∈ {0,1}: x can be unfolded by candidate-unfolding C.

Adm(x, t) ∈ {0,1}: x passes through candidate-limitation B.

𝒳(t): the realizable allocation set, determined by R, P, D, and the actual capacities of the tuning mechanism.

Reach(x, t) = 1 ⟺ ∃ x⃗ ∈ 𝒳(t) and a complete realizable candidate–integration–maintenance path such that x reaches (or is already at) the level of occupancy (z = 1) in finite time and is maintained (counterfactual judgment; actual manifestation is not required; maintenance per the Observer Mathematical Form, Definition 47).

Eq(x, t) = 1 ⟺ ∃ x⃗ ∈ 𝒳(t) such that z\*\_x = r·x\_x/d\_x ≥ 1, with d\_x = η + χ·Σ Ovl(x, ·)·ẑ (the opponent term being the effective suppression under that allocation), r \> 0, d\_x \> 0. In the constant-coefficient linear model ż\_x = r·x\_x − d\_x·z\_x, first arrival at 1 in finite time from z₀ \< 1 ⟺ r·x\_x \> d\_x; in the case of equality, only asymptotic approach. Hence within this model Reach ⇒ Eq, and not conversely.

Equilibrium allocation threshold: c(x, t) ≔ d\_x/r, under the comparison condition that the opponent suppression term is held fixed; Eq(x, t) = 1 ⟺ there exists a realizable allocation with x\_x ≥ c(x, t). c is not a general actual-reachability cost.

H\_ord (capacity–reachability ordering bridge, additional): within a common eligibility domain and under fixed comparison conditions, there exists a single-target allocable upper bound a(Φ\_cap), monotonically non-decreasing in Φ\_cap, such that Reach(x, t) = 1 ⟺ c(x, t) \< a(Φ\_cap(t)). This is an additional actual-reachability bridge condition (including the satisfaction of the integration and maintenance duties); it does not follow from the equilibrium formula or from Definition 4.

H\_fam (familiarity–threshold bridge, to be established): other things being equal, content whose carrying route is more familiar (deeper δ̄, by the fixed common aggregation protocol of Field Chapter 11 v1.2) has no higher c; equality is allowed. The comparison of familiarity relies on δ̄; H\_fam does not follow from Field Chapter 11 (which does not set up a condition under which the continuation cost 𝒵 decreases), nor is it a definition of familiarity.

H\_excl (outside-existence condition): within the declared set of structures in the pool Ξ(P), there exists x with Cand(x, t) = 0 or Adm(x, t) = 0.

III. Eligibility Domain, Observation-Domain, Boundary-Fringe, and the Four Bands

E(t) = { x ∈ Ξ(P) : Cand ∧ Adm }

W(t) = E(t) ∩ { x : Reach(x, t) = 1 }

M(t) = E(t) ∖ W(t)

Out(t) = Ξ(P) ∖ E(t)

Pre(t) = { x ∈ E(t) : 0 ≤ z\_x(t) \< 1 }  (the set of below-threshold advancing states; according to whether x ∈ W or x ∈ M, they belong to the unmanifested within the boundary or to the boundary-fringe; Pre is not the definition of a band - the four bands are divided by set qualification, the unmanifested within the boundary being W ∖ Cur and the boundary-fringe being M, and both may contain items with z = 1 but no actual occupancy)

Cur(t) = { x : Occₜ(x) = 1 }, \|Cur(t)\| ≤ 1  (by the pool-level current single-value principle, a constitutive commitment; Observer Group v1.0)

The four bands are:

Out ∪ M ∪ (W ∖ Cur) ∪ Cur

They are pairwise disjoint and together equal Ξ(P).

IV. Theorems (Unified Expression)

Theorem 1:

pool established ∧ finite resources ∧ convergence dynamics ⇒ W(t) ⊆ E(t) ⊆ Ξ(P); H\_excl ⇒ E(t) ⊊ Ξ(P) and W(t) ⊊ Ξ(P); within the unique-occupancy zone W(t) ≠ ∅; within the manifestation-vacancy zone W(t) = ∅ ∧ E(t) persists.

Theorem 2:

Occₜ(x) = 1 ⇒ there exists τ′ \< t such that Cand(x, τ′) = Adm(x, τ′) = 1 and z\_x reaches 1 through continuous pushing; manifestation cannot jump layers.

Theorem 3:

(i) with E fixed (or E\_low ⊆ E\_high) and 𝒳(t) monotone by inclusion in Φ\_cap, ∂W/∂Φ\_cap ≥ 0; within a common eligibility domain, under fixed comparison conditions and H\_ord, content with lower c loses reachability no earlier than content with higher c; under H\_ord ∧ H\_fam, shallower content loses reachability no later than more familiar content (weak order, ties allowed); a strict order further requires a strict threshold difference and the capacity upper bound crossing between them.

Notation note: in this appendix, the ∂ notation applied to sets with respect to parameters (such as ∂W/∂· and ∂Cand/∂·) always means monotonicity in the sense of inclusion (when the parameter increases, the set does not contract; when the parameter decreases, the set does not expand), not numerical differentiation of a set with respect to a scalar. For numerical quantities (such as N\_obs), ∂ is taken in the ordinary sense. Where comparison of set size is involved, the measure in item (a) of the remaining formalization tasks is presupposed.

(ii) single-candidate, no-overlap, constant-coefficient linear model, 0 ≤ u ≤ 1, η \> 0, z₀ \< 1: r ≤ η ⇒ 1 cannot be first reached in finite time; the maintenance of an existing completed state is not excluded (with r·u = η and z₀ = 1, z ≡ 1), and W = ∅ or Cur = ∅ is not implied by this alone. General vacancy (no complete candidate–integration–maintenance path; Observer Mathematical Form, Theorems 7 and 11) ⇒ W = ∅ ∧ Cur = ∅, while E persists and content retreats into M.

(iii) orientation factors reshape the local shape of W; where H\_excl still holds on the rewritten configuration, W ⊊ Ξ(P) is unchanged.
