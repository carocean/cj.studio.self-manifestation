# The Mute-Manifest Mathematical Form

Introduction

This chapter succeeds the Mute-Manifest General Form and treats one task only: to establish, as a mathematical system that can be run, compared, and read back, the question "once the content has really entered, does the same-trace endogenous backward command acquire an independent effect upon trace-formation?"

This chapter does not prove why the mute-manifest phase transition occurs, does not prove that a system must reach the manifest phase, does not prove the source of the manifest phase, does not prove the graded process by which imagery passes from blurred to clear, and does not prove such higher organizations as the realm of observation, unification, or re-continuation.

This chapter directly succeeds On Self-Manifestation · Forms Chapter 23 · Mute-Manifest Group Form 1 v1.2, The Mute-Manifest General Form:

Oᵣ ⇒ Pᵣ ∧ Mᵣ  
\[General Form-H1\]

where Pᵣ first establishes the r trace-formation event domain, and Mᵣ determines same-trace backward command only within that domain. Hence:

Dom(Mᵣ) = {ε ∈ ℰᵣ : P(r, ε) = 1}

P prescribes only the domain of M and is no part of the predicate content of M. This chapter makes P and M precise; it does not upgrade the necessary relation of the general form into a sufficient one.

This chapter likewise succeeds the relevant structural language of On Self-Manifestation v0.7.1, and formalizes it here only:

Eᵣ, Qᵣ, 𝔥ᵣ: succeeding the chapters on experience, the experience target, and sedimentary configuration;

B, Yᵣ: succeeding the chapters on endogeny and the intentional side;

Tᵣ: succeeding the chapters on the Tuning-Field;

Gate/Pass, sedimentary updating, tuning return, and their r-bound relations: succeeding the existing structures of the Theory of the Sedimentation-Field, the Experience-Field, the Intent-Field, the Tuning-Field, the Pivot, and Micro-Core Theory.

This chapter does not re-prove why these structures exist; it prescribes only their types, running, solution, and read-back upon entering the mute-manifest mathematical system.

𝔥ᵣ = (𝒢ᵣ, Λᵣ)

II. Endogenous admission

For the B side to enter the test of backward command it must satisfy:

Rec\_B denotes that the source-motion possesses within the system a dynamic of regeneration, re-initiation, or continued issue; Open\_B denotes that this endogeny has an actual open edge and can become a present causal parent of the subsequent relations Yᵣ, Tᵣ, and the rest.

Let Fᵣᵃᵘᵗᵒ(t) denote the set of non-B-side automatic antecedents held fixed in the counterfactual comparison of M.

and, with Fᵣᵃᵘᵗᵒ fixed, there do not exist two distinct legitimate B conditions available for comparison, then B is merely a renamed variable within the automatic chain and does not bear the B-side duty of this chapter.

What this chapter calls "endogenous freedom" requires only:

∃ b₀, b₁ ∈ 𝓑\_endo^ε, b₀ ≠ b₁

with b₀ and b₁ both permitted conditions of the system under one and the same fixed Fᵣᵃᵘᵗᵒ.

It does not require that B be statistically independent of the whole system history; it requires only that, with the automatic antecedents fixed, the B side still have at least two legitimate conditions available for counterfactual comparison.

VI. Sufficiency of the state

If two histories have the same Sᵣ(t) and Uᵣ(t) at time t yet still admit different successors in the absence of a declared stochastic difference or set-valued dynamic, then the present representation of state is insufficient, and the missing load-bearing historical items must be folded into Sᵣ or 𝔥ᵣ.

If the particular instance adopts a Markov-sufficient representation of state, then it is required that:

Pr(Sᵣ(t + Δt) \| Sᵣ(≤t), Uᵣ(≤t))  
\=  
Pr(Sᵣ(t + Δt) \| Sᵣ(t), Uᵣ(t))

If the particular model is not a Markov representation, then Sᵣ must be extended, or the historical variables that still bear load must be explicitly retained; this chapter does not impose Markov property as a physical property of every implementation.

VIII. Trace-formation equivalence

Definition:

Γ₁ ∼\_ε Γ₂

if and only if the two are equivalent in the trace-formation structural consequence presently declared.

∼\_ε must quotient out microscopic noise, non-load-bearing numerical difference, and difference of representation that do not alter the trace-formation structure; it may not invoke such yet-to-be-proven or subsequent properties as "whether there is consciousness," "whether it is felt," "whether it is reported," "whether it has entered a realm of observation," or "whether it re-continues."

Declaration of properties: ∼\_ε must be an equivalence relation, that is, reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity all hold; antisymmetry does not hold and is not required to hold — different structural trajectories may have the same trace-formation structural consequence, and that is precisely what this relation is to quotient out.

The three properties are not proven in this chapter but required of instantiation. The reason is this: in "equivalent in the trace-formation structural consequence presently declared" above, the content of "equivalent" is declared by the particular model, and this chapter does not prescribe it on the model's behalf; hence the three properties cannot be derived from this chapter's definitions and can only be posed as legitimacy conditions upon instantiation. The particular equivalence rule of ∼\_ε is already listed in Section XXII among "the parameters a particular model must instantiate but which are not this chapter's H"; what this section adds is the legitimacy condition upon that instantiation — the rule given must satisfy the three properties, and a rule that does not is not what this chapter calls trace-formation equivalence, so that every later construction depending on 𝒯raceᵣ/∼\_ε fails to hold. This clause and item six of the breach conditions in Section XXI (∼\_ε defined by a yet-to-be-proven result) are both constraints upon the instantiation of ∼\_ε: the latter governs what it is defined by, this clause governs what properties it satisfies.

If a particular model instantiates "trace-formation structural consequence" as the value of some read-out map — that is, Γ₁ ∼\_ε Γ₂ if and only if that map takes the same value on the two — then the three properties follow directly from the reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity of sameness of value, and need no separate proof. This is the most common legitimate manner of instantiation, but not the only one.

XVIII. The separation of formation from maintenance

The formation map answers whether different effective B conditions can make one and the same r-bound antecedent form different stable trace-formation organizations.

The maintenance map answers whether, once some stable organization has formed, a present change of B will make it hold, re-tune, switch branch, or fall out of phase.

An immediate B response may therefore not be equated with phase-organizational identity. There may exist organizations whose formation depends on B and which, after formation, cease for a time to depend immediately on B by reason of hysteresis or sedimentation.

This chapter classifies 𝒜\_L^r\[Prot\_form\] only by the non-degeneracy of the formation map; dependence in maintenance, hysteresis, and residue are handed to the Mute-Manifest Phase-Transition Form.
