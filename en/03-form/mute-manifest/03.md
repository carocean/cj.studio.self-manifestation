# The Mute-Manifest Phase-Transition Form

Introduction

This chapter succeeds the Mute-Manifest General Form and the Mute-Manifest Mathematical Form, and treats one task only:

to prove how, within the deeply mute working domain, one and the same r-bound generative lineage passes from a B-invariant stable trace-formation organization, by way of a reconstruction of the stable-organization landscape controlled by the sedimentary configuration, into a maintainable B-conditioned stable trace-formation organization.

What this chapter calls a phase transition is not any change of state whatever, not the crossing of some numerical threshold by the strength of B, not a single instance of M\_ε = 1, and not the external inscription of a system directly into the posterior phase.

A phase transition requires all of the following to hold together:

the prior and posterior phases have different organizing criteria;  
the controlling substrate passes through a criticality relevant to the phase transition;  
the actual state undergoes a change of organizational attribution within one and the same generative lineage;  
the posterior phase can maintain itself within the declared domain of perturbation;  
this phase identity is not an accident of the formation protocol.

This chapter does not prove that a system must sooner or later undergo the transition, does not trace the whole historical source of the posterior phase, does not prove that the posterior phase is reachable from an arbitrary initial state, and does not treat the 1→100 grading of manifestation.

X. Continuity of the generative lineage

Define the unified relation of generative lineage:

S\_a ⪯\_gen^r S\_b

if and only if there exists a chain of successors — finite, or continuous/discrete as the declaration permits — generated from S\_a to S\_b by the present legitimate:

satisfying:

no exchange of the present r-bound generative identity;

no replacement of load-bearing structure by privileged internal inscription;

every new structure legitimately generated from the prior state.

"Same-carrier lineage, same-trace-attribution lineage, same-generative lineage" all serve as checking conditions upon:

⪯\_gen^r

Declaration of properties: ⪯\_gen^r is a preorder, not a partial order.

Reflexivity holds: any S has a zero-step chain of successors to itself.

Transitivity holds: if there is a legitimate chain from S\_a to S\_b and a legitimate chain from S\_b to S\_c, then the concatenation of the two is still a legitimate chain from S\_a to S\_c, and the four checking conditions holding segment by segment hold for the whole.

Antisymmetry does not hold and is not required to hold: so long as the system, having evolved from S\_a to S\_b, can still return to S\_a along legitimate successors, one has both S\_a ⪯\_gen^r S\_b and S\_b ⪯\_gen^r S\_a with S\_a ≠ S\_b. The legitimate chains of successors of this chapter do not forbid cycles — the four checking conditions (no exchange of the system's instance identity, no exchange of the r-bound generative identity, no privileged inscription, every new structure legitimately generated from the prior state) contain no requirement of unidirectionality, so that the pairs of states generated by oscillation, cycling, and reversible recursion all fall within this case. This relation therefore gives no partial order on the state space, and its notation ⪯ denotes only a direction of generative reachability, not any unique ordering of states by height or precedence.

Two things follow that must be made clear:

First, this chapter takes no quotient by ⪯\_gen^r. Mutually reachable states are equivalent in generative lineage, but their CurPhase may differ (one in D, the other in L), and taking the quotient would erase the phase transition itself.

Second, the phase transition requires S\_D ⪯\_gen^r S\_L, and this is a requirement of direction, not of ordinality. It requires that there exist a legitimate generative chain from S\_D to S\_L; it does not exclude the simultaneous existence of a chain returning from S\_L to S\_D — should such a chain exist, it shows only that the phase transition is reversible, not that it did not occur.

The phase transition requires:

S\_D ⪯\_gen^r S\_L

and not merely that the prior and posterior structures resemble one another.

XIII. The continuous-destabilization type

The continuous-destabilization type requires that the particular model supply a legitimate continuous structure of organization.

There may be used:

an organizational metric d\_org;

a continuous order parameter ψ;

a stability spectrum;

a Lyapunov indicator;

a rate of recovery;

or any other continuous structure already proven able to read back the D/L organizational change.

If there is no legitimate continuous structure of organization whatever, a D→L phase transition may still be adjudicated, but "the continuous-destabilization type" may not be further claimed.

The universal condition of continuous destabilization is:

there exists a continuous configuration path 𝔥ᵣ(λ)

together with a legitimate criterion of the stability boundary, such that:

for λ \< λ\_c there exists a stable branch with DBranch = 1 whose local stability is positive;

as λ → λ\_c^-, the local stability of D approaches the boundary of destabilization along at least one B-sensitive effective direction;

in the adjacent domain with λ \> λ\_c there exists a stable branch with LFamily = 1;

and the degree of organizational non-degeneracy of the new phase emerges from zero at the criticality without a finite jump.

If the model uses an order parameter ψ\_L, one may write:

lim\_{λ→λ\_c^+} ψ\_L(λ) = 0

while for λ \> λ\_c:

ψ\_L(λ) \> 0

If the model uses a stability margin κ\_D, one may write:

κ\_D(λ) \> 0, λ \< λ\_c

κ\_D(λ\_c) = 0

These scalars are only particular instances, not universally necessary physical quantities.

The continuous-destabilization type does not require:

a finite critical nucleus;

an instantaneous jump of the configuration;

a B-specific familiar route already present before the criticality;

domain fullness or edge-seepage.

XIV. The discontinuous-nucleation type

The discontinuous-nucleation type permits:

PhaseSuppᵣ(𝔥ᵣ) = {D, L}

while:

CurPhaseᵣ(Sᵣ) = D

holds for a long time.

If, within one and the same landscape of coexistence, D and L are both locally stable organizations and the actual state lies in the carrying basin of D, and only when some B-conditioned local organization crosses the D/L boundary of carriage does the actual trajectory enter the carrying domain of L, then it is of the discontinuous-nucleation type.

A particular model may define a model-level nucleus support quantity N and a critical value N\_c:

N \< N\_c ⇒ return to the carrying domain of D

N = N\_c ⇒ at the criticality of carriage

N \> N\_c ⇒ entry into the carrying domain of L

N and N\_c are not required to denote a number of nodes, a physical size, or a uniform strength.

The discontinuous-nucleation type permits D to remain locally stable at the moment of actual transition.

The core of the discontinuous-nucleation type is therefore not "the old phase must destabilize," but:

coexistence;

a finite boundary of carriage;

nucleation or basin-switching;

the maintainability of the posterior L phase.

XV. The r-bound template of nucleating organization

For applicable models of nucleation, a sufficient organizational template may be used:

closure of trace attribution;

penetration of difference;

stable uptake.

Closure of trace attribution: the candidate local organization belongs to the legitimate generative relation of the present r.

Penetration of difference: at least one legitimate B-side difference, after complete automatic recursion, still forms a different stable-organization quotient class.

Stable uptake: that B-conditioned local organization can enter stable L carriage, and not merely a single instantaneous M\_ε = 1.

This "automatic–active co-tuned closed loop" serves only as a sufficient template of nucleation within applicable models; it is not the universal minimal microscopic topology of every discontinuous phase transition.

XIX. The change in the rules of maintenance of the prior and posterior phases

The stable-organization fact of the D phase is: a change in the legitimate B condition still cannot form a different stable-organization quotient class.

The stable-organization facts of the L phase are: with one and the same legitimate B condition b held fixed, ordinary perturbations test only within-class recovery; and upon altering the legitimate B condition, different stable-organization quotient classes may be formed.

What the mute-manifest phase transition changes is therefore not "the system passing from stable to unstable."

What it changes is: the standing of the B-side difference in the generation of a stable trace-formation organization.

In the prior phase: the B-side difference bears no differentiation of stable organization.

In the posterior phase: at least one legitimate B-side direction becomes a condition of the generation of stable organization.

Hysteresis, residue, and irreversibility are none of them universally necessary properties.

XXIII. Exclusion of ordinary transitions

If:

J\_land^r(𝔥ᵣ)

preserves the D/L load-bearing structure unchanged throughout the relevant process, and only the state:

S\_a → S\_b

changes, then at most an ordinary change of state is proven.

If, within a fixed landscape, an external force pushes the state directly from the D carrying basin into the L carrying basin without any landscape reconstruction relevant to phase transition, then at most an externally imposed basin-switch is proven.

A mute-manifest phase transition must possess all of the following together:

a landscape criticality supported by independent organizational facts;

the holding of D/L phase-support identity;

an actual change of the present carrying phase;

one and the same generative lineage;

a maintainable posterior L phase.

Exclusion of the same-phase explanation

What the preceding section excludes is "the landscape unchanged while the state changed." There must further be excluded another route: the landscape changed, the state changed too, and yet the whole process could still be completed continuously within one and the same phase.

Suppose that throughout the process from S\_D to S\_L, CurPhaseᵣ takes one and the same value. Then however J\_land^r may vary and however great the B-side difference may be, no phase transition is constituted — it is an evolution within one and the same phase, its change of degree belonging to the Mute-Manifest Grading Form and its limiting properties to the Limit Form.

Hence when this chapter claims a D→L phase transition it must prove: there exists no legitimate generative chain, preserving the value of CurPhaseᵣ unchanged, that can realize all of the target changes claimed.

This proof may not be obtained from "D and L are mutually exclusive." Section IV has made it plain: DBranch tests only the local B-invariant identity of that D branch itself, within one and the same landscape it is entirely permitted that DBranch = 1 and LFamily = 1 hold together, and the criteria of the two fall respectively upon a property of a single branch and a relational property of a branch family, so that they are not mutually exclusive.

The feasible proof falls upon CurPhaseᵣ, not upon phase support. CurPhaseᵣ adjudicates which side the present carrying organization belongs to: belonging to some stable D branch with DBranch = 1, it takes D; belonging to some B-conditioned stable branch family with LFamily = 1, it takes L. What must be proven is therefore: there exists no legitimate generative chain that keeps the present carrying organization on one and the same side throughout while the target changes claimed are still completed.

The particular manner of proof depends on the content of the target change:

If the content of the target change claimed is just "the present carrying organization passes from the D side to the L side," then the same-phase explanation is excluded automatically — to preserve CurPhaseᵣ unchanged is precisely for that change not to have occurred. In that case this item carries no additional burden and need only be declared.

If the target change claimed has other content (some read-out passing from small to large, some structure from simple to complex), then each such content must be checked item by item for whether it can be completed with the value of CurPhaseᵣ unchanged. Whatever can be so completed does not belong to this chapter's claim of phase transition and must be struck out of it, and handed to the Mute-Manifest Grading Form (if gradable) or to the Limit Form (if the question is one of limiting properties).

The practical effect of this item is therefore a demarcation: it leaves "the change of carrying side" to this chapter and hands out "the change of degree within one and the same side." If, after the striking out, nothing remains, then this chapter's claim of phase transition does not hold.

This item differs from the criterion of duty-family deletion in Section XII: the deletion criterion asks "does the posterior phase depend on the B-side duties," this item asks "can the target change be completed within one and the same phase." The former is a property of the posterior phase, the latter a property of the process.

XXIV. The conditional phase-transition theorem

Declaration of the kind of claim

Under the Phase-Transition Form norms of Canon Chapter 1, claims of phase transition fall into two kinds: one that proves only that the phase structure undergoes a qualitative change at some condition is a phase-structure claim; one that further proves that some system or trajectory has indeed left the prior phase and entered the posterior is a transition-event claim.

This chapter bears both, set out as follows.

Phase-structure claim: the landscape criticality Σ\_land^r established in Section VIII. It is forced out by the change of equivalence class of the independent organizational structural read-out J\_land^r, and asserts only that the D/L phase-support structure changes at that criticality; it asserts of no system that it has now undergone a transition.

Transition-event claim: the actual transition boundary Σ\_trans^{r,det} and the change of the present carrying phase established in Section IX. It asserts that some actual trajectory has indeed left the D carrying domain and entered the L carrying domain.

The relation between the two has been handled by two sections within this chapter. Section IX establishes their distinction: PostCritical ≠ Transition, and PhaseSupp = {D, L} is not CurPhase = L. Section XVIII establishes their moments: t\_land is the moment at which the configuration first enters Σ\_land^r, t\_trans the moment at which the actual trajectory first acquires CurPhaseᵣ = L and enters stable L carriage; the continuous-destabilization type may have t\_land = t\_trans, the discontinuous type may have t\_land \< t\_trans.

The seven conditions of the conditional phase-transition theorem below straddle the two kinds of claim: the second and third belong to the phase-structure claim (landscape criticality and post-critical phase support); the fourth, fifth, and sixth belong to the transition-event claim (generative lineage, the present carrying phase, the maintainability of the posterior phase); the first is an antecedent on the initial state and the seventh an excluding condition, both shared by the two kinds.

Whoever cites this theorem must declare which kind of claim is cited, or that both are taken. Whoever cites only the phase-structure claim may not on that ground assert that any system has undergone a transition.

What this chapter establishes is a conditionalized sufficient determination of phase transition; it does not claim that D→L must occur under arbitrary sedimentation-field conditions.

Within a legitimate 𝔐ᵣ and a fixed 𝒞\_phase^r, if the condition group C\_phase is satisfied in all of the following:

first, there exists a stable initial state S\_D with CurPhase = D;

second, the configuration trajectory 𝔥ᵣ(t) makes J\_land^r cross a landscape criticality Σ\_land^r adjudged to hold by ∼\_land^r;

third, the post-critical landscape satisfies:

L ∈ PhaseSuppᵣ(𝔥ᵣ)

fourth, there exists a legitimate actual trajectory generating from S\_D to some S\_L:

S\_D ⪯\_gen^r S\_L

fifth:

CurPhaseᵣ(S\_L) = L

sixth:

the stable organization to which S\_L belongs, with LFamily = 1, is maintainable under Δ\_ord^r;

seventh, there exists no privileged internal inscription performing the transition in the system's stead;

then:

C\_phase  
⇒  
that trajectory constitutes a local mute-manifest D→L phase transition:

𝔓\_D^r  
⟶  
𝔓\_L^r

If the continuity conditions of Section XIII are further satisfied, it is adjudged of the continuous-destabilization type;

if the coexistence–nucleation–basin-switch conditions of Section XIV are further satisfied, it is adjudged of the discontinuous-nucleation type.

This theorem gives a sufficient determination only.

Which dynamical conditions must make C\_phase hold is not proven by this chapter; it is handed to the Mute-Manifest Necessity Form.

XXV. Breach conditions

Should any of the following occur, this chapter's claim of phase transition fails:

1\. within the legitimate protocol family there appears a conflict of D/L support that cannot be explained by non-load-bearing differences of representation; or, where an LFamily exists in the same landscape, a DBranch that preserves invariance within its own B\_D working domain is wrongly denied merely because the whole system of formation is non-degenerate with respect to B;  
2\. before and after there is only numerical change, without any change in the D/L load-bearing organizational structure of J\_land^r;  
3\. a crossing of Σ\_land^r is claimed, but the landscape of stable organizations has no D/L-relevant reconstruction;  
4\. a D→L is claimed, but the actual trajectory has undergone no change of carriage CurPhaseᵣ : D → L;  
5\. the posterior phase cannot be maintained under the declared Δ\_ord^r;  
6\. the posterior phase comes from another content, another copy, or a rootless external inscription, the lineage being discontinuous;  
7\. there is only a single M\_ε = 1, with no stable posterior L phase;  
8\. within a fixed landscape an external force switches basins directly, yet this is called a self-manifesting phase transition;  
9\. a jump in an order parameter is substituted for a proof of the organizational phase;  
10\. any particular mechanism among domain fullness, edge-seepage, a critical nucleus, pre-sedimentation, hysteresis, or a configuration jump is written as a universally necessary condition;  
11\. PostCritical is written directly as Transition;  
12\. the possibility, reachability, or high probability of phase transition is passed off as its necessity;  
13\. the target change claimed can be fully realized by a legitimate generative chain preserving the value of CurPhaseᵣ unchanged — that is, the same-phase explanation has not been excluded;  
14\. the conditional phase-transition theorem is cited without declaring whether the phase-structure claim or the transition-event claim is cited, or the phase-structure claim alone is cited while asserting that some system has undergone a transition;  
15\. the actual properties of ⪯\_gen^r, of ∼\_land^r, or of a ∼\_B^r introduced by a particular model do not match those declared in this chapter.
