# The Mute-Manifest Source Form

Introduction

This chapter succeeds the Mute-Manifest General Form, the Mute-Manifest Mathematical Form, and the Mute-Manifest Phase-Transition Form, and treats one task only:

for a local mute-manifest D→L phase transition that has actually been established, to trace where the formation-load-bearing structure of the posterior L phase came from, through what legitimate generative process it acquired its present load-bearing standing, and to make plain which sources are already closed, which admit only multiple solutions, and which remain undecidable.

What this chapter calls a "source" is not a trigger, not maintenance, and not some indispensable cause.

A source is first of all an actual generative lineage:

SourceInput  
→  
SourceTransform  
→  
SourceStructure

where:

SourceInput: the external or internal supplying input entering this chain of source;

SourceTransform: sedimentation, tuning, configuration updating, Ξ, edge-seepage, structural generation, and other legitimate generative processes;

SourceStructure: the present configurational components that finally enter the formation-load-bearing set of the posterior L phase.

This chapter does not prove that a D→L transition must occur, does not prove that L is reachable from an arbitrary initial state, does not prove that the source of formation must be unique, and does not raise pre-shaping, first inscription, domain fullness, edge-seepage, or any other particular mechanism into a universally necessary condition.

I. Upstream entry conditions

This chapter traces sources only for an actual phase transition already established by the Mute-Manifest Phase-Transition Form.

The upstream delivers at least:

the legitimate model instance 𝔐ᵣ;

the fixed working context of phase transition 𝒞\_phase^r;

the actual initial state S\_D and the posterior state S\_L;

the configuration trajectory 𝔥ᵣ(t);

witnesses that DBranch and LFamily hold;

PhaseSuppᵣ and CurPhaseᵣ;

the set of landscape criticality Σ\_land^r;

the legitimate generative lineage:

S\_D ⪯\_gen^r S\_L

together with witnesses of the stable maintenance of the posterior phase.

The ⪯\_gen^r here is the relation of generative reachability on the state space of the Mute-Manifest Phase-Transition Form (a preorder, admitting cycles); it is not the same relation as the within-history-graph ancestor relation ⪯\_H^r to be defined in Section III of this chapter (acyclic, antisymmetry holding), and neither may be inferred from the other. This chapter takes up the former only as an upstream witness, and does not use its order properties in derivation.

If a particular case has separately established:

Dep\_{B-family}^form = 1

that may serve as input strengthening the information on formation responsibility, but it is not a universal entry condition of this chapter.

III. The separation of formation duty, candidate ancestry, actual participation, and counterfactual necessity

Formation duty must be defined before Supp.

For any source history graph H = (V\_H, E\_H, τ\_H), define the within-history ancestor relation of source:

z ⪯\_H^r h

if and only if z, h ∈ V\_H and there exists in H a directed generative path from z to h satisfying the temporal partial order τ\_H.

⪯\_H^r denotes only the ancestor relation of source that the candidate history H claims; it does not of itself prove that H is legitimate or that it actually occurred.

Declaration of properties: ⪯\_H^r is an acyclic ancestor order, its reflexivity being determined by the particular model's convention concerning paths.

Transitivity holds: two directed generative paths joined end to end are still a directed generative path, and the temporal partial order τ\_H is transitive, so that the joined path still satisfies τ\_H.

Antisymmetry holds: this relation requires the path to satisfy τ\_H, and τ\_H is a temporal partial order, itself antisymmetric. Were there both z ⪯\_H^r h and h ⪯\_H^r z with z ≠ h, then τ\_H would have z preceding h and h preceding z at once, in conflict with the antisymmetry of a temporal partial order. Hence the generative graph of H is acyclic under the constraint of τ\_H. This item does not depend on the convention concerning path length.

Reflexivity is determined by convention: the definition in this section says only "there exists a directed generative path from z to h," and says nothing of whether the path length may be zero. If a particular model conventions a zero-length path as a path, then z ⪯\_H^r z holds and this relation is a partial order; if it conventions that a path has at least one edge, then z ⪯\_H^r z does not hold and this relation is a strict partial order (irreflexive, transitive, antisymmetric). Both hold under this chapter's usage — this chapter uses ⪯\_H^r only to adjudicate the first condition of FormRole, "there exists z such that z ⪯\_H^r h," and under either convention that condition asks whether h has any ancestor of source, so that whether z = h is counted does not alter the load-bearing conclusion. A particular model must declare the convention it adopts.

Here a distinction from the ⪯\_gen^r of the Mute-Manifest Phase-Transition Form must be drawn: ⪯\_gen^r is a relation of generative reachability on the state space, its conditions of checking containing no requirement of unidirectionality, so that it admits cycles, antisymmetry does not hold, and it is a preorder; ⪯\_H^r is an ancestor relation within a source history graph, its definition explicitly containing the temporal partial order τ\_H, so that it is acyclic and antisymmetry holds. The two are alike in notation and unlike in property, and neither may be inferred from the other in citation.

⪯\_src^r and ⪯\_{H\_actual}^r are instances of ⪯\_H^r on the actual history H\_actual, and their properties follow accordingly, including the same convention on which reflexivity depends.

For any h ∈ Term\_L^r(H), define:

FormRole(h\|H) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}

FormRole = 1 if and only if:  
first, there exists z such that z ⪯\_H^r h;  
second, the structural transduction claimed by H makes h enter into or constitute the formative organizational relation of the LFamily;  
third, h is not merely a non-load-bearing background item;  
fourth, h is not a structure that bears a duty of maintenance only after L has formed and never took part in this formation.

Where it is proven that h bears no duty in this formation, FormRole = 0; where information is insufficient, it is ⊥.

Define:

Supp\_{L,form}^r(H)  
\=  
{h ∈ Term\_L^r(H) : FormRole(h\|H) = 1}

Hence:

H → Term\_L(H) → ⪯\_H^r → FormRole(h\|H) → Supp\_{L,form}(H)

If H = H\_actual is determined, then:

z ⪯\_src^r h  
⇔  
z ⪯\_{H\_actual}^r h

ActualFormRole(h) = 1  
⇔  
FormRole(h\|H\_actual) = 1

If the actual history is determined only to \[H\]\_actual, a single value is given only where the formation duty of physical correspondence is consistent across that class.

Abl\_h^{src} is used only for NecessarySource, and not to define FormRole or Supp.

If FormRole(h\|H) = 1 and, after legitimate ablation, the L formation relation degenerates while the other non-h conditions are preserved, then NecessarySource(h\|H) = 1.

NecessarySource = 0 does not negate FormRole = 1.

Hence:

candidate ancestry ≠ legitimacy of history;  
formation duty ≠ counterfactual necessity;  
candidate history ≠ actual history.

IV. The domain of source histories and a fixed source resolution

One review of source must fix one source resolution:

ρ\_src^r

together with its physical equivalence classes.

All candidate histories must be compared on the same domain of source components:

𝒞\_src^r

different equivalent representations must first pass through physical correspondence before comparison of:

Supp\_{L,form}^r(H)

and of their intersections and unions.

First define the raw space of source histories:

𝓗\_raw^r

whose members are required only to be able, in syntax and temporal structure, to represent a candidate generative history from the boundary of source to the L formation-load-bearing structure.

For each raw history H, first define the terminal candidate configurational components it claims finally enter the L state:

Term\_L^r(H)  
⊆  
𝒞\_src^r

Term\_L(H) is only the syntactic terminal set of H; it does not presuppose that these terminals all genuinely bear a duty in L formation.

The syntactic definition of H does not depend on knowing Supp in advance.

The formation-load-bearing set has already been formally defined in Section III as:

Supp\_{L,form}^r(H)  
\=  
{h ∈ Term\_L^r(H) :  
FormRole(h \| H) = 1}

Then define the predicate of historical legitimacy:

AdmHist\_r(H)  
∈  
{1, 0, ⊥}

AdmHist\_r(H) = 1

if and only if:

first, H begins from a declared BaseSrc^r or a combination thereof;

second, every load-bearing generative edge in H belongs to a SourceTransform permitted by the model;

third, the temporal partial order of H does not conflict with the actual working window of the phase transition, with S\_D ⪯\_gen^r S\_L, or with known records;

fourth, H does not depend on privileged internal inscription, rootless structural leaps, or generation outside the model's domain of definition;

fifth, H is compatible with the present model 𝔐ᵣ, with the working context of phase transition 𝒞\_phase^r, and with organizational read-back.

If some item is proven violated, then:

AdmHist\_r(H) = 0

If the information does not suffice to adjudicate, then:

AdmHist\_r(H) = ⊥

Define the domain of legitimate source histories:

𝓗\_src^r  
\=  
{H ∈ 𝓗\_raw^r : AdmHist\_r(H) = 1}

Thus "whether a candidate history exists" and "whether a candidate history is legitimate" are no longer conflated in advance.

The source structure of a raw candidate history is adjudicated by StructClosed\_r(H); whether its source class is legitimate, by AdmClass\_r(\[H\]); and the state of completion of the review of its quotient class, jointly by Resolved\_r(\[H\]) and HistClosureClass\_r(\[H\]). An illegitimate history does not, by being excluded in advance, deprive the Fail state of definition.

V. The graph structure of a source history

Multi-source formation is not necessarily a single line.

Define a source history:

H  
\=  
(V\_H, E\_H, τ\_H)

where:

V\_H: source inputs, intermediate structures, formation-load-bearing structures, and legitimate boundary nodes;

E\_H: the legitimate generative relations among nodes;

τ\_H: a temporal partial order satisfying the actual order of generation.

Permitted are:

branching;

confluence;

several formation-load-bearing items sharing one ancestor of source;

several different combinations of BaseSrc jointly generating the same load-bearing structure.

A complete source history is therefore in general a directed generative graph, not a single linear chain.

If some particular generative path within the graph is to be discussed, it may be written separately as:

ActualSrcPath

or:

CandidateSrcPath

but these do not stand for the whole source history H.

VI. Actual history, candidate history, and the actual source class

If some microscopic source history H is uniquely supported by actual records, trajectory facts, or model facts, then H is called H\_actual.

In that case Section III has already defined:

z ⪯\_src^r h  
⇔  
z ⪯\_{H\_actual}^r h

If the evidence can determine only a source-structure equivalence class, write \[H\]\_actual.

Conclusions about source are permitted to hold at the level of \[H\]\_actual; there is no requirement to select a microscopic representative to no purpose.

If H is merely compatible with the present model and evidence and has not been determined to be the actual source class, then H is only a candidate history.

Hence:

the existence of a candidate generative graph ≠ that graph being legitimate;  
a candidate graph being legitimate ≠ that graph having actually occurred.

VII. Equivalence of source histories

The Source Form does not exhaust every microscopic historical difference irrelevant to formation.

Define raw equivalence of source histories:

H₁ ∼\_hist^r H₂

with domain:

𝓗\_raw^r × 𝓗\_raw^r

if and only if H₁ and H₂ have the same source structure in the description of the L formation-load-bearing structure of this occasion, including:

the load-bearing categories of SourceInput;

the load-bearing relational types of SourceTransform;

the physical correspondence of terminal candidate configurational components;

the temporal structure of Pre / Onset / Mixed;

the source duties claimed to be borne toward LFamily formation;

and other load-bearing generative facts bearing on the D/L distinction of this occasion.

∼\_hist^r answers only:

"do the two candidate histories belong to the same kind of source structure?"

It does not answer:

"is that source structure legitimate?"

Legitimacy is still adjudicated independently by:

AdmHist\_r

Declaration of properties: ∼\_hist^r is an equivalence relation. Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity all hold — the relation is given by "sameness" in each of the load-bearing source structures listed above, and sameness is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Antisymmetry does not hold and is not required to hold: different candidate histories may belong to the same source structure, and that is precisely what this relation is to quotient out (microscopic historical differences irrelevant to formation belonging to this case).

As with ∼\_ε of the Mute-Manifest Mathematical Form and ∼\_land^r of the Mute-Manifest Phase-Transition Form, the three properties are here a requirement upon instantiation and not proven by this chapter: how "load-bearing" and "physical correspondence" in each listed item are to be adjudicated is declared by the particular model under a fixed ρ\_src^r. When a particular model gives its rule of determination it must at the same time secure the three properties; a rule that does not is not what this chapter calls equivalence of source histories, and the quotient space of source histories accordingly fails to hold.

The strata must be distinguished: the three properties determine whether the quotient space exists, while the two requirements below (preservation of AdmHist\_r and preservation of StructClosed\_r) determine whether the class-level quantities on that quotient space are well defined. Where the former does not hold, the latter cannot be spoken of.

So that legitimacy may be well defined on the quotient space of source histories, it is further required that:

H₁ ∼\_hist^r H₂  
⇒  
AdmHist\_r(H₁)  
\=  
AdmHist\_r(H₂)

If some ignored historical difference would alter the value of AdmHist, then that difference is a load-bearing difference of source legitimacy and may not be ignored by ∼\_hist; H₁ and H₂ must be assigned to different classes of source history.

Hence define class-level legitimacy:

AdmClass\_r(\[H\])  
\=  
AdmHist\_r(H)

whose value is independent of the choice of representative.

On the domain of legitimate source histories define the restricted relation:

∼\_src^r  
\=  
∼\_hist^r \|\_{𝓗\_src^r}

∼\_src^r is likewise an equivalence relation: an equivalence relation restricted to a subset remains an equivalence relation, the three properties being preserved item by item.

Hence:

structural equivalence of histories  
≠  
legitimacy of history.

Equivalence of source histories absorbs only differences of physical source structure; it does not absorb the present state of knowledge.

It is therefore required only that pure structural closure be preserved on classes of historical equivalence:

H₁ ∼\_hist^r H₂  
⇒  
StructClosed\_r(H₁) = StructClosed\_r(H₂)

If an ignored difference would alter whether the source graph is physically structurally complete, that difference may not be ignored by ∼\_hist.

Define:

StructClosureClass\_r(\[H\])  
\=  
StructClosed\_r(H)

whose value is independent of the choice of representative.

Whether the present evidence suffices to complete the review of that class is denoted separately by Resolved\_r(\[H\]), and does not enter the definition of physical equivalence of ∼\_hist.

VIII. The base of source and the duty of truncation

Define the base of source:

BaseSrc^r

The permitted types of termination include:

first, a boundary of theoretical succession: a structure for which the upstream already has an independent proof of source and which is permitted to be used in this chapter as a closed input;

second, a boundary of empirical exogeneity: a normal external input that this task expressly does not explain;

third, a boundary of model initialization: the point from which the model begins, that initial item not to be passed off as a conclusion generated within this chapter;

fourth, background structure expressly inherited from before this chapter's working domain and bearing no source duty for the D/L distinction of this occasion;

fifth, an atomic event of source expressly declared by the present model, whose parents lie outside the boundary of this chapter's task.

Every BaseSrc node must carry:

BoundaryReason(z)

and state expressly to which of the above kinds of boundary it belongs.

If some initial condition or atom of source has already encoded L-specific formative information and its own source has not been closed by the upstream, then this chapter may at most say:

"the tracing of source is closed to the boundary of the model or task."

It may not say:

"the internal generative source of L has been fully explained."

Define the criterion of an L-specific boundary:

LSpec(z) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}

If a BaseSrc node z, without passing through what this chapter calls the internal process of structural formation, already suffices to fix, carry, or directly specify the later B-conditioned stable-organizational distinction of the LFamily, then:

LSpec(z) = 1

If that LFamily distinction is first acquired only after passing through a legitimate SourceTransform within this chapter's working domain, then:

LSpec(z) = 0

If it does not suffice to adjudicate, then:

LSpec(z) = ⊥

If there exists a BaseSrc bearing a key formation duty such that:

LSpec(z) = 1

and its earlier source has not been closed by the upstream, then at most:

ClosureLevel\_src^r = Boundary

may be declared. Internal may not be declared.

Hence:

truncation at a boundary  
≠  
an internal generative explanation.

IX. Structural closure, the state of knowledge, and class-level historical closure

For H ∈ 𝓗\_raw^r define:

StructClosed\_r(H) ∈ {1, 0}

StructClosed = 1 if and only if:  
first, the ancestral relations of all formation-load-bearing nodes are traced along E\_H back to BaseSrc^r;  
second, every load-bearing generative edge belongs structurally to a permitted type of SourceTransform;  
third, there exists no rootless leap, privileged inscription, or undeclared boundary;  
fourth, all formation-load-bearing structural nodes have been given in H;  
fifth, every BaseSrc node carries a BoundaryReason.

Where there is a definite rupture in the source structure itself, StructClosed = 0.

StructClosed adjudicates only whether the source structure itself is closed; it does not denote whether the present evidence suffices to prove that candidate history true, legitimate, or actually occurrent.

Define:

StructClosureClass\_r(\[H\])  
\=  
StructClosed\_r(H)

Define the state of knowledge:

Resolved\_r(\[H\]) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}

Resolved = 1 denotes that the present model, records, and evidence suffice to complete the review of legitimacy and structural closure for that source class;  
Resolved = ⊥ denotes that the evidence is insufficient;  
if it has been proven that the load-bearing facts required cannot be discerned within the declared working domain, Resolved = 0 may be recorded, with the boundary of undecidability made public.

Define:

HistClosureClass\_r(\[H\]) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}

HistClosureClass = 1  
⇔  
AdmClass\_r(\[H\]) = 1  
∧ StructClosureClass\_r(\[H\]) = 1  
∧ Resolved\_r(\[H\]) = 1

If AdmClass = 0 or StructClosureClass = 0, then HistClosureClass = 0.

If AdmClass = ⊥, Resolved ≠ 1, or the evidence is insufficient, then HistClosureClass = ⊥.

Hence:

closure of the physical source structure ≠ our already knowing that it is closed;  
the unknown ≠ failure.

XI. Input source, transform source, and structural source

The load-bearing nodes in a complete source graph fall into three strata:

SourceInput  
→  
SourceTransform  
→  
SourceStructure

SourceInput: the normal external input, or the internally already-closed supplying cause, entering this source history;

SourceTransform: sedimentation, tuning, Φᵣ, F, G, Ξ, edge-seepage, structural generation, and other legitimate transforms;

SourceStructure: the configurational components that finally enter the L formation-load-bearing relation.

For example:

A\_ext  
→  
Φ/Ξ/sedimentation/tuning  
→  
Δ𝔥\_internal

where:

A\_ext may be part of the complete actual source history;

legitimate dynamics within the system bear the formative transform;

Δ𝔥\_internal becomes the present load-bearing structure.

The external may be a supplying source, but a direct privileged external inscription into B, Y, T, E, 𝔥\_L, or a direct specification of the L phase label, can prove only external control and does not prove a self-generated source of formation.

XII. The separation of formation, trigger, and maintenance

For a source history H:

SourceHistory\_L^r(H)

denotes the complete source-generative graph of the L formation-load-bearing structure.

Trigger\_L^r(H)

denotes the triggering event or condition that brings the present actual state from D carriage into L carriage.

Supp\_{L,maint}^r(H)

denotes the structure required for the maintenance of the posterior L phase.

These three may not be conflated.

A source may have formed long before;

a trigger may occur only at the last moment;

and the structure of maintenance may differ in part from the structure of formation.

Hence:

source ≠ trigger ≠ maintenance.

XIII. Multiple sources and redundancy

A source history H may contain the confluence of several sources.

If:

z₁ → h

z₂ → h

and both z₁ and z₂ take part in the formation within the actual history, then both belong to ActualSource.

If only the joint source group can form h, a joint necessary source may be declared further.

If either path alone could form it, but both take part in the actual history, then both still belong to the actual source, and neither need be a NecessarySource.

Closure of source does not require that every source be counterfactually indispensable.

XVI. Closure of generative explanation

Define:

XX. Breach conditions

The claim of this chapter is a claim of source; it is not a claim of necessity, nor a claim of reachability. Breach therefore addresses only what this chapter actually claims.

First, what this chapter claims must be distinguished. For a phase transition already established as actual, this chapter gives its formation-load-bearing set Supp\_{L,form}, the types of source, the level of source closure ClosureLevel\_src^r, and the certificates of exhaustion. Wherever this chapter claims NecessarySource(h\|H) = 1, it claims that item to be counterfactually necessary; wherever it claims only FormRole(h\|H) = 1, it claims only that the item bears a formation duty, not that it is necessary. The breach conditions of the two differ.

Should any of the following occur, the corresponding claim of this chapter fails:

First, the source conditions hold and yet the target structure is not generated. Under the conditions that the declared Supp\_{L,form}^r(H) all hold, that H is a legitimate source history with AdmHist = 1, and that the model instance and the working context of phase transition are fixed, the L formation relation still does not appear. This breaches the claim that "the declared load-bearing set suffices to account for that formation."

Second, the generative chain depends on an undeclared external condition. Exhibit a condition lying neither within 𝒞\_src^r nor within the declared BaseSrc and SourceTransform, whose removal makes the L formation relation degenerate. This breaches the completeness of the source resolution ρ\_src^r — the declared domain of components has omitted a load-bearing item.

Third, intervention proves that the process indicated bears no generative role. For some declared h ∈ Supp\_{L,form}, carry out a legitimate ablation Abl\_h^{src} while the other non-h conditions are preserved; the L formation relation does not degenerate, and that h bears no formation duty in the remaining candidate history classes either. This breaches the single item FormRole(h\|H) = 1; it does not breach the whole load-bearing set.

Fourth (only against a claim of necessary source), the claimed necessary source is absent and yet the target structure still occurs. Exhibit a legitimate generative history H' in which h ∉ Supp\_{L,form}^r(H') while the L formation relation still appears, and H' belongs to the same ∼\_hist^r source-structure class. This breaches NecessarySource(h\|H) = 1. If H' belongs to another source-structure class, no breach is constituted; it shows only multiplicity of source, to be handled under Section XIII, multiple sources and redundancy.

Fifth (only against a claim of unique source), exhibit two legitimate source histories not equivalent under ∼\_hist^r, both able to generate the same L formation-load-bearing structure, and both with AdmHist = 1. This breaches the claim of a unique source; it does not breach this chapter's full candidate load-bearing set or its common load-bearing set — those were established precisely for multiplicity.

The following do not constitute breach:

That the present evidence does not suffice to determine the actual history, making ClosureLevel\_src^r = Partial or the corresponding quantities output ⊥, does not make this chapter's claims fail. This chapter never claims to have identified a unique actual history, unless it has actually written that H\_actual is determined.

Exhibiting another source history that could also generate L shows only multiplicity of source. This chapter's full candidate load-bearing set and common load-bearing set were established for just that, and so no breach is constituted, unless this chapter has at the same time claimed a unique source.

Pointing out that this chapter has not proven the phase transition necessary, or reachable from an arbitrary initial state. That is not this chapter's task, as the Introduction has made plain.

Pointing out that this chapter has not exhausted every microscopic historical difference. ∼\_hist^r quotients out differences irrelevant to formation by design, not by defect; only when a quotiented-out difference actually alters the value of AdmHist or StructClosed is a breach of the instantiation of ∼\_hist^r constituted, and that is already governed by the two requirements of Section VII.
