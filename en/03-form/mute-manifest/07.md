# The Mute-Manifest Grading Form

Introduction

This chapter succeeds the Mute-Manifest General Form, the Mathematical Form, the Phase-Transition Form, and the Reachability Form, and treats one task only:

once a first stable manifest phase has been established, to build the regime of grading by which manifestation develops from an initially low-resolution, low-stability, low-differentiation organization into a clearer, more stable, more finely divided organization.

This chapter does not re-prove the 0→1. Whether a first local mute-manifest phase transition holds is adjudicated by the General Form, the Mathematical Form, the Phase-Transition Form, and their subsequent legitimate target interfaces.

This chapter therefore strictly distinguishes:

0→1: the question of phase transition;

1→100: the question of grading.

"100" is not a presupposed upper bound; it denotes only that the degree of organization continues to develop after the manifest phase is established.

The degree of manifestation is not defined by "strength of consciousness."

This chapter grades only an L-phase organization already established.

Π̂\_L^r is the complete portrait of manifest grading, not a new physical field; Π\_𝔊 is the portrait of comparison within the present grading domain.

III. The grading domain, the portrait space, and the comparison protocol

This chapter strictly distinguishes three strata of object:

first stratum, the physical L organizational object;

second stratum, the raw structural read-out of each dimension;

third stratum, the portrait object that actually bears the comparison of grading.

Define the fixed grading domain:

𝔊  
\=  
(𝒳\_G, J\_G, {M\_j}\_{j∈J\_G})

where:

𝒳\_G: the domain of physical L-phase organizational objects permitted to be graded together under one and the same regime;

J\_G: the set of load-bearing dimensions fixedly used by that grading domain;

M\_j: the legitimate comparison protocol of the j-th dimension.

For any:

j ∈ J\_G

define:

M\_j  
\=  
(m\_j, 𝒟\_j, ≼\_j, 𝒲\_j)

where:

m\_j: the function mapping the raw structural read-out of that dimension to a comparable object;

𝒟\_j: the legitimate value domain in which the comparable objects lie; it may be a numerical domain, a function space, a distribution space, an ordered vector space, or another declared ordered structure;

≼\_j: the non-strict partial order of that dimension;

𝒲\_j: the working domain in which that comparison protocol applies.

From the complete five-dimensional portrait Π̂\_L^r and the fixed dimension set J\_G, the portrait map of the present grading domain is composed:

Π\_𝔊  
:  
𝒳\_G  
→  
𝒫\_G

𝒫\_G  
⊆  
∏\_{j∈J\_G} 𝒟\_j

Π\_𝔊(X)  
\=

\=

\=

Hence:

𝒫\_G

and not on the set of physical system identities 𝒳\_G.

If two different physical organizational objects:

X\_a ≠ X\_b

satisfy:

Π\_𝔊(X\_a) = Π\_𝔊(X\_b)

this shows only that they are grade-equivalent in the present regime of grading:

X\_a ∼\_𝔊 X\_b

Define:

X\_a ∼\_𝔊 X\_b  
⇔  
Π\_𝔊(X\_a) = Π\_𝔊(X\_b)

One may not infer from this that the two are the same physical object.

∼\_𝔊 is an equivalence relation: it is defined by equality of portraits Π\_𝔊(X\_a) = Π\_𝔊(X\_b), and equality is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, so the three properties follow directly and need no separate proof. Antisymmetry does not hold and is not required to hold — different physical objects may have the same portrait, and that is precisely what this relation is to express: "grade-equivalent, not identical."

Declaration of type: this chapter takes, under the Grading Form norms of Canon Chapter 1, the multi-dimensional partial-order type, and not the single-axis stratification type. The reason: Section X of this chapter has already proven that the object of grading is not a single scalar, and that the five dimensions are not guaranteed to aggregate into a total order; forcing them into a single axis by weighting would manufacture spurious comparability. Taking the multi-dimensional partial-order type, one is not required to have levels mutually exclusive and exhaustive, but is required to have the grading domain closed, the properties of the comparison relation proven, the conditions of incomparability explicit, and the rule of projection definite, and, when claiming an overall grade, to prove separately that the aggregation is legitimate. The above items are found respectively in this section and in Sections XI and XII.

Each:

(𝒟\_j, ≼\_j)

must satisfy, on the objects used in the present task:

reflexivity;

antisymmetry;

transitivity.

This chapter formerly used the word "reflexiveness"; it is synonymous with the "reflexivity" used in Canon Chapter 1, and "reflexivity" is now adopted throughout.

Direction of order in each dimension: the direction of each ≼\_j is declared by the legitimate comparison protocol M\_j of that dimension — ascending along ≼\_j is a rise in the read-out of that dimension. The directions of the five dimensions are: the dimension of differentiation ascends with the degree of differentiation of effective B-conditioned trace-formation organizations; the dimension of separability ascends with the degree of distinguishability among different effective trace-formation organizations; the dimension of stability ascends with the degree of resistance to ordinary perturbation and of recovery; the dimension of persistence ascends with the degree of persistence within the declared time domain; the dimension of re-tuning ascends with the capacity of legitimate re-tuning. The direction may not be altered within one and the same grading domain.

Non-degeneracy of each dimension: each dimension must have at least two attainable values under this chapter's entry conditions, or it bears no discrimination.

On this item, this chapter can give only a lower bound and a manner of adjudication, and cannot prove it generally, for the following reason.

The read-out of each dimension is a function on objects (for instance the dimension of differentiation takes Diff\_r = m\_Diff(𝒦\_r^form), and a finite discrete model may use \|Im(𝒦\_r^form)\|), one object giving one value. The first and second items of the upstream LFamily criterion guarantee only that the differentiation read-out of every established L-phase object is not less than two — they speak of two B conditions within one and the same object forming different organizational quotient classes in B\_L, not of read-outs differing between objects. Hence a lower bound may be obtained from that criterion, but not the attainability of two values: if in some particular model all objects within the object domain 𝒳\_G happen to have a differentiation read-out of exactly two, then that dimension has only one attainable value in that model, and remains degenerate.

This chapter therefore handles it in three ways:

First, a lower bound is given. Wherever L is established, the differentiation read-out is not less than two, and the separability read-out is correspondingly not less than two — this follows from the upstream criterion.

Second, the attainability of two values is a requirement upon the declaration of the grading domain, not a theorem of this chapter. When a particular model declares 𝒳\_G and J\_G, it must, for every j ∈ J\_G, give two objects X\_a, X\_b ∈ 𝒳\_G with Q\_j(X\_a) ≠ Q\_j(X\_b); where it cannot, that dimension bears no discrimination in that model and must be removed from J\_G, the grading domain being correspondingly reduced and redeclared.

Third, this item does not negate the five-dimensional portrait itself on account of the reduction of J\_G in some model. The complete portrait Π̂\_L^r remains five-dimensional; the reduction occurs only in the set of load-bearing dimensions J\_G of the particular grading domain 𝔊, and where J\_G ⊂ J\_SM it may not be raised into an overall grade, as already forbidden in Section XII.

Argument for the non-redundancy of each dimension: the five dimensions cannot be inferred from one another, and the manner of arguing this is to give a counterexample pair — two objects taking the same values in the other four dimensions and different values in the dimension at issue. The definitions of the several dimensions below already contain three assertions to the same effect, and are here developed accordingly:

Differentiation and separability: Section VI already asserts "Diff high ≠ Sep high," giving the reason — that there are several organizational classes does not mean they are clear to one another; two organizations may differ formally and yet not be stably distinguishable under the present system's capacity of discrimination. Hence there exist pairs of objects with the same differentiation read-out and different separability read-outs.

Stability and persistence: Section VII already asserts "the clearer the manifest phase, the less stable — not so," and permits "difference preserved between classes ∧ recovery strengthened within class"; Section VIII already makes plain that persistence does not require permanent invariance, permitting short-term stable manifest phases and hysteretic holding. The two measure different things — stability measures the capacity of recovery after perturbation, persistence measures the capacity of continued holding under declared conditions. Hence there exist pairs of objects with the same stability read-out and different persistence read-outs, and conversely.

Re-tuning and the other four dimensions: Section IX already asserts "high sensitivity ≠ high ReTune," "erratic jumping ≠ re-tuning," "changing fast ≠ mature," and divides ReTune into four duties: CorrectSwitch, SelectiveSwitch, Recovery, NoPrivilege. All four are responses to a change of B conditions, and what they measure is not the same as how many organizations there presently are, how separable they are from one another, how stable, or how long-lasting. Hence there exist pairs of objects with the same read-outs in the other four dimensions and different re-tuning read-outs.

Hence each of the five dimensions bears discrimination of its own, and removing any one may turn some pair of objects from comparable to incomparable or the reverse, the order changing accordingly.

This must be distinguished from the item of non-degeneracy: non-redundancy asks whether a dimension can be inferred from the others; non-degeneracy asks whether that dimension can take two values on a given object domain. Where either fails the dimension bears no load, but for different reasons.

If in some particular model two dimensions do in fact imply one another — for instance if its m\_Diff and m\_Sep take the same quantity — then the J\_G of that model must be reduced accordingly.

The strict order is derived from the non-strict:

x ≺\_j y  
⇔  
x ≼\_j y  
∧  
¬(y ≼\_j x)

and hereafter no independent strict relation unrelated to ≼\_j is introduced.

VII. The dimension of stability

For a fixed effective B class \[b\]\_r, a raw read-out of stability is obtained:

Stab\_r(\[b\])

denoting the capacity to return to O\_L(\[b\]) after ordinary perturbation.

The raw object of Stab may therefore be a function defined on effective B classes, and need not be a single scalar.

It is:

m\_Stab

that decides how it forms, in the present grading domain:

Q\_Stab^r ∈ 𝒟\_Stab

adopting, for instance, class-wise pointwise order, the stability of the weakest class, a distributional order, or another declared rule. If two objects have different index domains of effective B classes and there is no legitimate protocol of alignment, that dimension cannot be directly compared.

A model may use:

rate of recovery;

size of the stable basin;

spectral stability quantities;

range of noise resistance;

probability of return;

but the domain of application and the direction of comparison must be stated in M\_Stab.

The clearer the manifest phase, the less stable — not so. A mature L phase should permit:

difference preserved between classes ∧ recovery strengthened within class.

VIII. The dimension of persistence

Define:

Persist\_r

as the capacity of an already formed L organization, under the declared conditions, to go on carrying, to continue holding, or to recur stably again and again.

Persistence does not require permanent invariance.

Permitted are:

short-term stable manifest phases;

medium-range persistence;

hysteretic holding;

historical consolidation.

If persistence depends wholly on an external privilege clamping the internal state, it may not be counted as a capacity of self-manifesting persistence.

IX. The dimension of re-tuning

ReTune\_r is not defined as "the faster the change the higher the grade."

It denotes that when the effective B conditions change, the system can change legitimately under the conditions in which it ought to change, can hold under those in which it ought not, and finally enters the stable organization corresponding to the new effective B conditions.

ReTune therefore contains at least four duties:

CorrectSwitch: entering the correct target organization after the change;

SelectiveSwitch: re-tuning correspondingly only for effective and load-bearing changes of B condition;

Recovery: forming a new stable organization after re-tuning, rather than jumping erratically for long;

NoPrivilege: not depending on external direct inscription into internal load-bearing states.

A particular model may form these duties into a structural vector or a legitimate aggregate quantity, but must declare the comparison protocol.

Hence:

high sensitivity ≠ high ReTune;

erratic jumping ≠ re-tuning;

changing fast ≠ mature.
