# The Mute-Manifest Determination Form

Introduction

This chapter succeeds the preceding seven chapters of the Mute-Manifest Group, and treats one task only:

given a candidate system, a content r, a declared working domain, and a running record, to adjudge to which layer of mute-manifest determination the present evidence suffices to support.

This chapter no longer conflates "L is presently the case," "the present L was formed by mute-manifestation," and "a complete D→L phase-transition history has been observed" into one and the same output.

This chapter establishes five layers of determination:

PhaseDecision: whether the physical L phase is presently the case;

TargetDecision: whether legitimate L target identity is presently acquired;

FormationDecision: whether the present L was formed by the chain of formation responsibility defined by this group;

TransitionHistoryDecision: whether a complete D→L phase-transition history has actually been observed;

GradeDecision: if manifestation is already legitimate, in what relation and state of change the present manifest phase stands within the regime of grading.

The final chief adjudication:

Dec\_SM(Case\_r) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}

answers only:

whether the present Case\_r already suffices to adjudge that "a legitimate L is established, and its chain of formation responsibility may be adjudged as mute-manifest formation."

Whether a complete phase-transition history has been observed is not folded into the minimal condition for Dec\_SM(Case\_r) = 1, but is output independently by TransitionHistoryDecision.

⊥ may not be written by stealth as 0.

Declaration of received relational properties. The trace-formation equivalence relation ∼\_ε used in this chapter is received from the Mute-Manifest Mathematical Form; this chapter neither redefines it nor alters its content. Its properties follow the upstream: ∼\_ε is an equivalence relation, reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity all holding; antisymmetry does not hold and is not required to hold; the three properties are upstream a requirement upon instantiation and not proven upstream, and when a particular model gives the rule of determination of ∼\_ε it must at the same time secure the three properties.

This chapter accordingly uses the quotient-class notations Γ\_ε/∼\_ε and \[Γ\_ε\]\_∼: should the declared ∼\_ε fail the three properties, that quotient class does not hold, and every determination in this chapter grounded on whether \[Γ\_ε^(b₀)\]\_∼ and \[Γ\_ε^(b₁)\]\_∼ differ or agree — the identification of the backward-command map in Section IX, the exclusion of control in Section XI, and the exclusion of random contamination in Section XII — cannot be carried out, the corresponding layers outputting ⊥. This chapter introduces no binary relation of its own.
