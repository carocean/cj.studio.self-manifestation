# General Form of Observer Individuation

Master Proposition

This Form ventures one master proposition:

Within one and the same working time window, how is it that several local manifestation convergences that have already occurred sometimes jointly constitute one minimal observer, and sometimes severally constitute several minimal observers? When the number of contents, of objects, of channels, of body parts, of output ports, and of resource pools can none of them directly determine the number of observers, what structural difference, at minimum, must the system internally possess, for one-I and many-I's to obtain a non-arbitrary, breachable ground of individuation? All conclusions of this Form are argued within, and carry the restriction of, the minimal-position model of the Inner Chapters (the being-here — position — private-exhaustion-gathering-into-one generative model).

In this Form we shall find, under sustained questioning, where the proof must go, and derive the general form of observer individuation.

Goal of proof:

Starting from the minimal "I — being-here" generative chain already established, force out the individuation structure indispensable to one-I and many-I's, and prove its minimality, its unmergeability, and its domain of application.

Completion conditions:

Closure of the constraint recursion under one and the same irreducible task; for every forced-out item, a countermodel that keeps all previously obtained items; item-by-item correspondence between the formal master formula and the master theorem; minimality, breach, the H group, and the upstream and downstream interfaces all checkable.

Breach modes:

While the working domain declared in this Form, the minimal-position model, and the corresponding H conditions hold: exhibit a one-I system lacking the necessary structure listed here; or exhibit several final positions not governed by any common final position which nevertheless strictly constitute one I; or prove that one minimal position can generate at once two genuinely distinguishable I's whose minimal attribution and private-exhaustion bearing nonetheless do not split.

Then let us first give the result.

(What follows is the reading organization set back at the head of the text after the whole was completed; it only previews the result and serves as no premise of the derivation to come.) Preview of conclusions. The Inner Chapters have given the minimal generative chain:

convergence occurs; on the side of what is manifested, "being-here" arises; on the side of intention, a position forms; "being-here" is attributed to its position; the position, in turn, privately exhausts the current and gathers it into one; the "I" thereby comes forth.

On that basis this Form obtains:

1\. A minimal observer is not counted off directly from any arbitrary local position, but from the position that finally bears the attributions of the several locals and privately exhausts and gathers into one the relevant current;

2\. Several local convergences may still be one I, so long as the being-heres of the several locals are still jointly attributed to the same final position, and that position genuinely bears their private-exhaustion gathering-into-one;

3\. The so-called common position is not an externally imposed set name, but a position that within the system actually succeeds to the attributions of the several locals, governs the relevant current, and bears an undeletable constitutive role in the holding of one I;

4\. If no such common position exists, and two or more positions severally succeed each to its own being-here, severally privately exhaust each its own current, and each gathers into one, then the positions are not one;

5\. The positions being not one, the being-heres they generate are not one; each being-here returning each to its own one, many I's arise among them; and of the many I's, each has, for its part, one position. The General Form of Observer Individuation (within the minimal-position model) can be compressed as: several local manifestation convergences → test of joint attribution → test of common position → if there is a genuine common position which privately exhausts and gathers into one, then one I; if there is no genuine common position and the several positions each attribute on their own and each privately exhaust and gather into one, then many I's.

In one sentence:

One I is not fewness of locals, but the several being-heres jointly returning to one position; many I's is not manyness of contents, but the positions being not one, each returning to its own.

Abstract

The General Form of the Observer has shown that if a current holds, it must bear at least the three duties of member constitution, jointly-being-one of composite constituents, and the object-side ground of cross-time identity. The Inner Chapter "The Observation of Observing" has further shown: intention commissions tuning and convergence occurs; this convergence takes position in intention and is one, hence "being-here" is attributed to the sole position of the current; this sole position, in turn, privately exhausts the current and gathers it into one, and the "I" thereby comes forth. Therefore "being-here and the 'I' beget each other; the 'I' and being-here emerge together." If the positions are not one, the being-heres generated are not one; the being-heres, being not one, each return to their own one, and many I's arise among them; and yet, of the many I's, each has, for its part, one position. This Form does not re-prove how "I — being-here" is generated, nor does it interpret position as physical coordinate, module address, or resource pool. This Form treats one independent question only: when several local manifestation convergences, local attributions, or local currents hold at once, why are they sometimes still one I, and sometimes already many I's?

The argument sets out from the generative chain established in the Inner Chapters. It first proves: the number of local closures cannot directly determine the number of observers, because several local closures may still be genuinely governed by one common position. It then asks: what is a common position? If it is only a common name, an upper label, or an external collection, it cannot make one I hold. A common position must, within the system, actually succeed to the attribution of the several locals' "being-here" and privately exhaust and gather into one the relevant current. It asks further: does governance alone suffice? If, upon removing that common position, the several local closures still each hold complete, and the fact of one I is unchanged, then the common position is only an added layer and bears no individuation. Hence the final common position must have an undeletable constitutive role in the holding of one I.

Here the one-I condition closes: when the being-heres of several local convergences are jointly attributed to the same final position, and that position genuinely succeeds, governs, and constitutively privately exhausts and gathers into one, then one I holds.

If no such common position exists, and several positions severally succeed each to its own being-here, severally privately exhaust each its own current and each gather into one, then the positions are not one; their being-heres, being not one, each return to their own one, and many I's arise therefrom. This Form does not use equivalence classes, quotient sets, pool tracks, or Occ as starting points of proof. The precise forms concerned go to the Observer Mathematical Form; the mapping between pools and positions goes to the downstream mathematical and discrimination Forms; cross-time splitting, merging, and the split brain go to the Observer Pool-Split Transition Form.

2\. Working Domain, Inherited Boundaries, and Aim

This Form discusses only minimal observer individuation within one and the same non-zero working time window I\_t.

By local manifestation convergences are meant convergence processes occurring simultaneously or overlappingly within the same system, each generating a local "being-here — position" relation. "Local" signifies only that they have not yet been proven to belong to one final common position, nor proven mutually independent; it does not presuppose that they are several observers.

By final position is meant the position that, at the minimal individuation layer of this Form, bears the final in-system responsibility for the attribution of the several relevant locals' being-heres and the private-exhaustion gathering-into-one of the relevant current. "Final" does not mean a highest-layer personality or a supreme controller of the whole system; it means only that, as this Form continues its questioning, there is no further, more direct position bearing the minimal individuation of the same observer.

This Form inherits from the General Form of the Observer the member constitution of the current, the jointly-being-one of composite constituents, and the object-side ground of cross-time identity. This Form inherits from the Inner Chapter "The Observation of Observing" the minimal generative chain: convergence takes position in intention and is one; "being-here" is attributed to the sole position of the current; the position, in turn, privately exhausts the current and gathers it into one; the I thereby comes forth; being-here and the I beget each other, the I and being-here emerge together; if the positions are not one, the being-heres are not one, each returns to its own one, and many I's arise among them; and of the many I's, each has, for its part, one position.

This Form does not inherit: one pool one current, pool number equals observer number, track number equals observer number, Occ number equals observer number, split brain necessarily many I's, output separation necessarily many I's.

The minimal structure this Form seeks is not a formal quotient set, but the position relation that decides one-I / many-I's:

one I requires a genuine final common position; many I's require several complete position relations not governed by one and the same final position.

3\. Prior Distinctions

The number of observers is not directly determined by the number of contents, objects, channels, outputs, bodies, body parts, or resource pools. One current can manifest at once several objects, backgrounds, bodily feelings, and cross-modal contents; vision, hearing, touch, and inner imagery can also be jointly attributed to the same position. The host and the host vessel provide carrying, but do not automatically yield the boundary of individuation. A pool may serve as a realization read-back only after the mapping between it and the final position has subsequently been proven. Nor is the "common position" of this Form the placing of several local structures into one set, one namespace, or one upper label. If that "common" carries no in-system succession, governance, and constitutive role, it is only an observer's classification, not an observer-individuation structure.

4\. Constraint Recursion

The Inner Chapters have given: in one convergence, the "being-here" generated is attributed to one position; the position, in turn, privately exhausts the current and gathers it into one, and the "I" thereby comes forth.

Now relax the situation within one working time window. Let two local manifestation convergences L1, L2 appear at once in the system. L1 generates its being-here In1 and forms a local attribution P1; L2 generates its being-here In2 and forms a local attribution P2.

On these alone, is the task already closed?

If the number of local attributions directly equaled the number of observers, then with P1, P2 once appeared, two I's should be obtained at once.

Test this conclusion first.

Construct a system: vision and hearing each undergo local convergence; the being-here of vision first lands on a local position Pv, the being-here of hearing on a local position Pa; yet the two do not complete their final private-exhaustion at these local positions, but continue to be jointly attributed to the same final position PG, and PG privately exhausts and gathers into one the relevant current.

In this system the local positions are many, while the minimal observer may still be one.

Therefore:

many local attributions do not suffice to entail many I's.

Then what is the relation that keeps these locals one I? Several explanations offer themselves: common synchrony, common content, common history, common intention, common body, common output, common resources, or joint attribution.

Can these explanations substitute for one another?

Common synchrony cannot.

Two independent systems can be strictly synchronized, yet each has its own "being-here" and attribution; synchrony fixes only temporal relations, not to whom being-here is attributed.

Common content cannot.

Two independent observers can manifest exactly the same content; sameness of content does not make their positions the same.

Common history cannot.

Duplicated systems can carry identical histories, yet attribute each on its own after duplication; sameness of history does not determine that the present being-here is attributed to one position. Common intention cannot.

Two observers can pursue the same goal; sameness of intentional content is not oneness of position on the intention side.

Common body cannot.

One body can internally accommodate several mutually independent final attributions; the bodily boundary gives carrying only, not minimal individuation. Common output cannot.

Two independent closures can contend for one report port, or output in turns through one arbiter; merged output does not make their being-heres jointly attributed. Common resources cannot either.

Shared caches, buses, metabolism, and computing power can only show joint use of something, not to whom manifestation is jointly attributed. These candidates all keep the local structures intact, yet none of them can answer the same unclosed point:

to what, finally, do In1 and In2 return, and why do they become being-heres "for one and the same this-one"?

Within the minimal-position model of the Inner Chapters (the being-here — position — private-exhaustion-gathering-into-one generative model), after the failure of all the foregoing substitute relations, the necessary relation that closes this gap is:

the being-heres of the several locals jointly attributed to the same position. This is not the author's arbitrary choice among options, but because none of the foregoing candidates touches "that to which being-here returns"; only joint attribution directly closes this gap.

This Form calls it joint attribution.

The minimal meaning of joint attribution is:

In1, In2, though generated by different local convergences, do not terminate their final return at P1, P2, but land jointly on PG; PG reversely governs the relevant current, making the being-heres of the several locals jointly the current of this position. With joint attribution in place, is the task closed? Not yet.

For "jointly attributed to PG" may still be only the describer's way of speaking. Several false common positions can be constructed.

First: the set common position.

An observer packs P1, P2 into the set {P1, P2} and calls this set PG. The set contains both, yet succeeds to neither attribution.

Second: the naming common position.

The system stamps two independent paths with one name, one subject number, or one account. The name is the same, yet the fact that In1, In2 are attributed severally to P1, P2 is unchanged.

Third: the encapsulation common position.

P1, P2 sit within one body, one process, one chip, or one pool. The encapsulating boundary is common, yet no position actually receives the being-heres of both sides.

Fourth: the routing common position.

Both paths pass through one bus or relay node. That node only forwards; it bears no final attribution.

Fifth: the record common position.

One database records the states of both sides at once. The recorder possesses the information of both, yet does not thereby become the common I of both. None of these five commons closes the one-I task. They fail together at the same point:

the so-called PG has not, within the system, actually become the position to which the several being-heres finally return.

So, after excluding the false commons, what remains is not "having a common name," but:

the common position must genuinely succeed to the several locals' attributions. Succession here includes at least:

the final landing point of the several locals' being-heres is fixed by PG; PG can reversely govern the relevant current; if some local's being-here is not succeeded to by PG, that local does not belong to the range of this one I.

With genuine succession in place, is the task closed?

Still not.

For actual succession may still be a mere after-the-fact reading. Construct several further degenerate structures.

First: the upper observer.

PG reads two already completed minimal "I — being-here" closures. P1, P2 each attribute and each privately exhaust; PG merely observes them. Second: the broadcast receiver.

After P1, P2 have each completely held, they broadcast their results to PG. PG receives both, yet took no part in the forming of their one I. Third: the unified logger.

PG continually aggregates the histories of both sides, can even compare, predict, and report; but with it removed, P1, P2 are still each a complete minimal observer. Fourth: the action arbiter.

PG uniformly decides which side obtains the right of output. It decides action, not whether the being-heres of the two sides jointly return to one position. Fifth: the post-hoc narrator.

PG weaves the contents of both sides into one narrative and declares "I saw both sides"; but its declaration is built after two closures already made, and cannot reversely generate the two into one I.

These structures are all genuine system structures, and they do succeed to information, results, or control; yet they still cannot make what they succeed to into one I. They fail together at the same point:

with PG removed, the minimal attribution and private-exhaustion gathering-into-one of P1 and P2 remain complete; the original two minimal closures do not lose their holding by PG's disappearance.

So actual succession is not yet individuating succession. To close the one-I task, PG must bear an undeletable constitutive role in the several locals' becoming one I.

This does not say some physical module must be unremovable; it says: if PG's bearing of the several locals' final attribution and private-exhaustion gathering-into-one is removed, the structural fact "the several locals are jointly one I" no longer holds.

Only here is the final common position uniquely forced out: it cannot be substituted by synchrony, content, history, intention, body, output, or resources; it cannot degenerate into set, name, encapsulation, routing, or record; nor can it degenerate into observation, broadcast reception, log aggregation, action arbitration, or post-hoc narration.

Within the minimal-position model, what closes this gap is:

the position that within the system genuinely succeeds to the several locals' final attributions, reversely governs the relevant current, and bears an undeletable constitutive role in the several locals' becoming one I.

This Form notes it as PG.

With PG in place, is the original task closed?

For one I, it is closed.

Local convergences may be many, contents many, channels many, local positions many; so long as the several being-heres finally return jointly to PG, and PG constitutively privately exhausts the current and gathers it into one, the minimal observer is still one. Now re-ask the other end of the same task:

when must there be many I's?

Let L1, L2 form final positions P1, P2 respectively, each succeeding to In1, In2, each privately exhausting and gathering into one H1, H2.

Is not observing any PG sufficient?

It is not.

Non-observation may come from insufficient measurement, model omission, a wrong time window, or proxy failure. Many I's cannot be derived from ignorance. So what must be proven is not "no common position is seen," but: there exists no in-system position able at once to genuinely succeed to the final attributions of In1 and In2, to govern H1 and H2 as one current, and to bear an undeletable constitutive role in the two becoming one I. If one still says one I, what substitute paths remain? One may appeal to common body, common resources, common output, common history, common name, common upper observer. These paths have each already failed above: none can substitute for the final common position.

One may also say that one position P contains two I's at once. Test this claim.

If P holds no internal distinction between the two I's, and the very same attribution, private-exhaustion, and gathering-into-one relations belong completely to both at once, then there is no in-system difference between the two I's; the so-called two are only a duplicate numbering, in fact still one.

If within P there exist two sets of attribution and private-exhaustion relations separately changeable, separately maintainable, separately failing, such that one I changes while the other does not, then P has already divided, in the minimal individuation relation, into two final positions; "one position containing two I's" is only one general name covering two positions.

If the two I's share one attribution but differ in private-exhaustion, the final bearing of attribution has split; if they share one private-exhaustion but differ in attribution, the object of private-exhaustion has split; either way, so long as the two I's are genuinely distinguishable, the minimal position relation is already not one.

So a truly minimal and indivisible position cannot generate at once two mutually independent I's.

This is not fixed by citation alone, but because: two I's without internal difference are one I doubly numbered; two I's with internal difference must have that difference fall on the bearing of attribution or private-exhaustion, whereupon the minimal position has already split.

Thus, of the many I's, each has, for its part, one position.

Now keep in place:

P1, P2 each completely succeeding to its own being-here; each privately exhausting its own current and each gathering into one; no genuine final common position PG existing; and one minimal position unable to bear, without difference, two independent I's at once.

At this point the original task closes.

P1, P2 are, at the minimal individuation layer, positions not one.

The positions being not one, the being-heres generated are not one; the being-heres, being not one, each return to their own one, and many I's arise among them.

Here the recursion stops.

5\. Minimal Structure and Load-Bearing Classification

The minimal individuation structure forced out here is not an arbitrary juxtaposition of items, but one relational chain pressed out continuously by the same task: joint attribution → genuine final common position → constitutive private-exhaustion gathering-into-one → positions one / not one.

1\. Joint attribution

Standing: the sole forced-out item of the first round.

Ground of forcing:

synchrony, content, history, intention, body, output, resources — none can answer "to what the several being-heres finally return"; only joint attribution treats this gap directly.

Failure upon deletion:

the being-heres of the several locals have no common final landing point, and the range of one I cannot hold.

2\. Genuine final common position

Standing: the sole forced-out item of the second round.

Ground of forcing:

set, naming, encapsulation, routing, record can all manufacture surface commonality yet bear no in-system final attribution; hence joint attribution must land on a genuine position.

Failure upon deletion:

any number of independent observers can be written into one I by an external collection or a common name.

3\. Constitutive private-exhaustion gathering-into-one

Standing: the sole forced-out item of the third round.

Ground of forcing:

the observer, the broadcast receiver, the logger, the arbiter, and the narrator can all genuinely read or aggregate, yet do not make what they read one I; hence the final common position must bear an undeletable constitutive role in the holding of one I.

Failure upon deletion:

a structure that from above observes several already-formed observers is misjudged as one observer.

4\. The one / not-one division of positions

Standing: the confluence of the first three rounds, not an additional item. If the several locals jointly return to one genuine final position which constitutively privately exhausts and gathers into one, the position is one.

If no such common position exists, and several final positions each attribute completely and each privately exhaust and gather into one, the positions are not one.

5\. One position per I

Standing: the individual-counting boundary forced out in the fourth round. Ground of forcing:

a minimal position bearing "two I's" without internal difference is only a duplicate numbering; within the minimal-position model — that is, under the model-constitutive clause "observer difference can be realized only through the bearing of attribution and private-exhaustion" — if two I's are genuinely distinguishable, their difference must fall on the bearing of attribution or private-exhaustion, and the minimal position has already split in two. Hence, as a conditional conclusion within the model: one minimal position constitutes at most one minimal observer; and of the many I's, each has, for its part, one position.

Unmergeability. Joint attribution cannot be merged into the genuine common position: the former answers whether the several being-heres head to one place, the latter whether that place is a genuine in-system bearer.

The genuine common position cannot be merged into constitutive private-exhaustion: a genuine node can do reading and aggregating only, without constituting one I. Constitutive private-exhaustion cannot be merged into "one position per I": the former explains how several locals become one, the latter why one minimal position cannot be doubly numbered into many I's.

The four correspond to different countermodels; any merger loses at least one independent breach path.

Minimality holds within the minimal-position model (model completeness rests on H5).

6\. The General Form of Observer Individuation

Define the working time window I\_t.

Let the set of local manifestation convergences be L\_t. For any L\_i ∈ L\_t, L\_i generates its local being-here In\_i and a local attribution relation.

Define the final-common-position predicate:

FinalCommonPos(PG, J, I\_t), J ⊆ L\_t, meaning:

1\. the being-heres of the locals in J finally return jointly to PG;

2\. PG is a genuine in-system bearer, not a set name, a common label, an encapsulation, a routing node, a record, or a shared resource;

3\. PG reversely governs the relevant current and privately exhausts and gathers it into one;

4\. PG has an undeletable constitutive role in the locals of J becoming one I, being no observer, receiver, logger, arbiter, or post-hoc narrator;

5\. PG, at the minimal individuation layer of this Form, is not further divisible into several mutually independent final attributions and private-exhaustion bearings.

The one-I necessary-structure formula (within the minimal-position model):

OneObserver(J, I\_t) ⇒ ∃PG FinalCommonPos(PG, J, I\_t).

Under the condition that the constitutive bridge H1 holds, the constitutive read-back is: ∃PG FinalCommonPos(PG, J, I\_t) ↝ OneObserver(J, I\_t).

The many-I conditional formula:

if there exist non-empty and non-overlapping J1, J2 ⊆ L\_t and final positions P1, P2 such that:

J1 ∩ J2 = ∅; FinalCommonPos(P1, J1, I\_t); FinalCommonPos(P2, J2, I\_t); ¬∃PG FinalCommonPos(PG, J1 ∪ J2, I\_t); and P1, P2 are distinguishable in minimal attribution and private-exhaustion bearing — then P1, P2 are positions not one.

Under the condition that H1 holds:

MultiObserver(J1, J2, I\_t).

The one-position-per-I condition:

if one and the same minimal position P is claimed to constitute at once two mutually independent minimal observers, then:

if the two are indistinguishable in the bearing of attribution and private-exhaustion, they are only a duplicate numbering of one observer; if they are distinguishable, then P is already divisible into two final positions and is no longer one and the same minimal position.

Hence every minimal observer has its own one final position. These expressions only gather the structure this Form has forced out. The complete type, uniqueness, partition, overlap, and cross-time evolution go to the Observer Mathematical Form.

7\. Master Theorems

Theorem of the necessary structure of observer individuation. Let, within one working time window I\_t, the set J of local manifestation convergences jointly constitute one minimal observer.

Then there must exist a final common position PG such that:

1\. the being-heres of the locals in J finally return jointly to PG;

2\. PG is the genuine in-system bearer of attribution — not synchrony, same content, same history, same intention, same body, same output, same resources, nor set, name, encapsulation, routing, or record;

3\. PG reversely governs the relevant current and privately exhausts and gathers it into one;

4\. PG has an undeletable constitutive role in the locals becoming one I — not observation, broadcast reception, log aggregation, action arbitration, or post-hoc narration;

5\. PG, at the minimal individuation layer, is not further divisible into several independent final positions.

Proof.

If there is no joint attribution, the several being-heres have no common final destination; synchrony and the like cannot substitute for "that to which being-here returns."

If there is only surface commonality without a genuine bearer, an external collection can arbitrarily manufacture one I.

If there is genuine reading without constitutive private-exhaustion, the upper observer would be mistaken for the one I of those observed.

If within PG there remain several mutually independent final attributions and private-exhaustion bearings, then PG is not a minimal one position, but the general name of several final positions.

Hence the five items cannot be lacked.

Theorem of the many-I condition. Let non-empty local sets J1, J2 satisfy J1 ∩ J2 = ∅, be completely governed respectively by final positions P1, P2, each holding final attribution and constitutive private-exhaustion gathering-into-one; let there exist no final common position PG governing J1 ∪ J2; and let P1, P2 be genuinely distinguishable in attribution or private-exhaustion bearing. Then P1, P2 are positions not one.

Under the condition that the constitutive bridge H1 holds, J1 and J2 severally constitute two minimal observers.

Proof.

If one still judges one I, one must substitute the final common position with synchrony, content, history, body, resources, output, name, encapsulation, or upper observation; these substitutes have each been excluded by the constraint recursion. If one says one minimal position constitutes two I's at once, then if the two are indistinguishable, it is one I doubly numbered; if distinguishable, the difference must fall on the bearing of attribution or private-exhaustion, and the minimal position has already split. Therefore, when no genuine final common position exists and P1, P2 each hold complete, the positions are necessarily not one.

By the Inner Chapters' proposition, the positions being not one, the being-heres generated are not one; the being-heres, being not one, each return to their own one, and many I's arise among them.

8\. Corollaries

Corollary 1. Many contents do not entail many I's: if several contents are still jointly attributed to the same final position and privately exhausted and gathered into one by it, the one-I condition is unchanged.

Corollary 2. Many channels do not entail many I's: several channels, so long as they enter the same final common position, remain one I. Corollary 3. Many local closures do not entail many I's: local closures are only local structures prior to final individuation. So long as a genuine final common position exists, the number of local closures does not change the number of observers. Corollary 4. Common output does not entail one I: two independent final positions, even sharing one output port, do not thereby generate a common position.

Corollary 5. A common body does not entail one I: one body can carry several minimal observers whose positions are not one; whether there are many I's must be examined at the final common position, not at the bodily boundary. Corollary 6. Upper observation does not merge the observed: a position that observes, reads, or aggregates several already-formed minimal observers does not make the observed automatically one I of that position. Corollary 7. Of the many I's, each is still severally one: if P1, P2 are positions not one, then each final position severally constitutes its minimal observer unit; many I's is not an undifferentiated duplicate numbering inside one position.

9\. The Boundary of Pool-Level Principles

This Form does not prove one pool one current, one pool one I, one track one I, or one Occ one I.

Pools or pool tracks may serve as realization read-backs of the final position only after the downstream has proven that they can bear the full role of FinalCommonPos.

If one pool track carries two final closures with no genuine common position between them, then one pool track can correspond to many I's. If several pool tracks jointly realize one genuine final common position, then several pool tracks can still correspond to one I.

Hence pool number, track number, module number, output number are none of them direct observer counters of this Form.

10\. The Split-Brain Interface

The split brain does not become many I's by the anatomical severing itself. For many I's to be read back from this Form, it must be proven:

the left local set J\_L is completely governed by a final position P\_L; the right local set J\_R is completely governed by a final position P\_R; there exists no genuine final common position PG governing J\_L ∪ J\_R; and P\_L, P\_R each succeed to the attribution of their being-heres and constitutively privately exhaust and gather into one.

If a PG still genuinely bears joint attribution and the constitution of one I, the physical separation after the split brain does not entail many I's.

If PG fails, and left and right each become final positions, then the positions are not one and the many-I condition holds.

The necessary conditions of the transition, its time windows, stability, and hysteresis go to the Observer Pool-Split Transition Form.

11\. Breach Paths

Breach of the first kind — one I without a final common position: if several local manifestation convergences jointly constitute one minimal observer instance, yet no in-system final common position succeeds to their attributions, privately exhausts and gathers into one, and constitutively makes them one, then the one-I necessary structure of this Form is breached.

Breach of the second kind — a common label suffices for one I: if a purely external set name, common encapsulation, or shared resource, without in-system joint attribution and constitutive private-exhaustion, still suffices to make several complete minimal closures one I, then the genuine-common-position requirement is breached.

Breach of the third kind — an upper observer suffices to merge the observed: if an upper observer that only reads several already-formed observers, and whose deletion does not affect their several minimal closures, still suffices to make the observed one I, then the constitutivity requirement is breached. Breach of the fourth kind — strictly one I without a common position: if two final positions each attribute completely and each privately exhaust and gather into one, and no genuine final common position exists, yet the theory can still prove, in a non-naming, non-circular way, that they are strictly one I, then the many-I condition is breached.

The fifth kind — non-breach: many contents, many objects, many channels, many local closures, many body parts, many pools, or many outputs, so long as a genuine final common position remains, none of them breaches the one-I conclusion.

14\. Closing (all conclusions of this Form are argued within the minimal-position model)

"Being-here and the 'I' beget each other; the 'I' and being-here emerge together" does not say that a pre-existing subject later collects objects, nor that a macroscopic current first completes and an I is then carved out of it.

Convergence generates "being-here" on the one hand and forms a position in intention on the other; being-here returns to its position, the position in turn privately exhausts the current and gathers it into one, and the I thereby comes forth. When manifestation is constituted by several local convergences, many locals do not equal many I's.

So long as the being-heres of the several locals still jointly return to one genuine final position, and that position actually succeeds to their attributions and constitutively privately exhausts and gathers the relevant current into one, the several locals remain one I. If there is no such common position, and several positions severally succeed each to its own being-here, severally privately exhaust each its own current and each gather into one, then the positions are not one.

The positions being not one, the being-heres generated are not one; the being-heres, being not one, each return to their own one, and many I's arise among them.

Therefore:

one I lies not in fewness of contents, but in the several being-heres jointly returning to one position; many I's lies not in manyness of parts, but in the positions being not one, each returning to its own.
