# Current Inversion Form

This Form ventures one master proposition: for inferring back from the pre-registered explicit state Γ and licensed observation to the current outcome and the internal bearing structure, there exists a statable identification bound; the determinacy gap has a formal characterization and a field-layer read-back; protocol-relative underdetermination admits positive adjudication, ontic randomness admits no positive adjudication — and the boundary between the two is itself provable.

In this Form we shall find, under sustained questioning, where the proof must go, and derive the general form of current inversion.

Then let us first give the result. (What follows is the reading organization set back at the head of the text after the whole was completed; it only previews the result and serves as no premise of the derivation to come.)

The master inversion formula (preview):

Gap(π, I\_t) ⟺ ∃ω₁, ω₂: Γ\_π(ω₁) = Γ\_π(ω₂) ∧ the current outcomes differ  
The identification bound: under the gap, Γ ↦ outcome reaches at most the possibility set; observation ↦ bearing structure reaches at most the solution set; positive adjudication of ontic randomness is unreachable.

In one sentence: looking in from outside, how far at most can one see — this Form draws that line: outcomes only to the possibility set, structure only to the solution set, "true randomness" only to a perpetual unidentified.

Abstract

The Observer Mathematical Form declared the identifiability bound of the explicit state, the determinacy gap, and the randomness boundary assigned to this Form; the Observer Adjudication Form handed over its adjudication limit 2; the Observer Construction Form gave the field-source candidate of the gap (unmanifest sediment microstructure). This Form delivers: the protocol-type definition of Γ and the formal characterization of the gap; the three inversion-underdetermination theorems (outcome underdetermination, bearing-structure underdetermination, one-way direction); the adjudication-asymmetry theorem for protocol-relative underdetermination and ontic randomness; the field-layer read-back of the gap and the physical mechanism candidate of the identification bound. Honesty clause: ontic randomness is an open slot within this theory — theoretically licensed, operationally not positively confirmable, the adjudication layer yielding ⊥ toward it forever; this conclusion is itself one of this Form's principal outputs.

1\. The Objects of Inversion and the Type of Γ

The registration protocol π: an explicit-state registration scheme fixed prior to the outcome — which variables are registered, at what resolution, at which moments. Γ\_π(ω) is the explicit state of history ω under protocol π. Boundary-closure inheritance (the clauses of the Mathematical Form and the Construction Form): all controllable external inputs are counted into Γ\_π.  
The point: Γ is not "the whole state of the system," but the explicitable part the protocol selects — the inversion problem is thereby ternary: the protocol π, the observation y, the target (the current outcome, or the bearing structure X). Protocols are set relative to models; their normative definition is entered as H1.

2\. The Formal Characterization of the Determinacy Gap

Gap(π, I\_t) ⟺ there exists a boundary-closed history pair ω₁, ω₂ with Γ\_π(ω₁) = Γ\_π(ω₂) while the current outcomes differ.  
The gap-source theorem (inheriting the conditional consequents of the General Form and Correspondence 14 of the Construction Form): where Gap(π) holds, the causal source of the difference in outcomes lies within the boundary and outside Γ\_π — unmanifest historical sediment and microstructure, or an internal random transition. The proof is the formal restatement of that section's argument in the General Form; this Form does not re-prove it.  
The protocol relativity of the gap: Gap is a function of π — a coarse protocol may have a gap where a fine one has none; "whether the system has a determinacy gap" must be said with its protocol, and said bare is meaningless.

3\. The Inversion-Underdetermination Theorem Group

Theorem 1 (outcome underdetermination): where Gap(π) holds, the inversion Γ\_π ↦ current outcome is no function; from Γ\_π one identifies at most the possibility set of outcomes (or their distribution, where a statistical structure can be erected), never the actual outcome. Proof: the definition of the gap is precisely the non-single-valuedness of that mapping. QED.  
Theorem 2 (bearing-structure underdetermination, conditional theorem): if there exist bearing configurations X₁ ≠ X₂ with Obs\_π(X₁) = Obs\_π(X₂) (observationally equivalent distinct solutions under the same Γ and the same y), then inversion reaches at most their observational equivalence class or the solution set, never the solution. The proof status of the antecedent: the Mathematical Form's absence of a single-point requirement on the solution sets proves only that the theory prescribes no uniqueness, not that observationally equivalent distinct solutions exist — the explicit model pair (same Γ, same y, different Bear allocation, both conforming to the upstream, protocol-indistinguishable) has not been supplied; the antecedent is docketed as H5; before the model pair is supplied, no universal bearing-structure underdetermination is claimed. The theoretical ground of Adjudication H4 is conditionalized accordingly.  
Theorem 3 (one-way direction): observation positively accumulates duty-bearing evidence (the Adjudication Form's proxies), but the positive adjudication of CurrentFact is model-relative (the H\_cur obligation) — the halting line of inversion is at the same address as the adjudication limit; this Form gives its inversion-side statement: however fine Γ and however long y, lacking an independent proof of H\_cur, "adjudging manifestation from outside" does not hold. QED upon Theorem 11 of the Mathematical Form and the transmission-misjudgment clause of the Adjudication Form.

4\. Protocol-Relative Underdetermination and Ontic Randomness

Protocol-relative underdetermination (formerly "model-relative randomness," renamed at Batch Nine): Undet\_M ⟺ given the protocol π\_M of model M, Gap(π\_M) holds — the outcome is no function of Γ\_{π\_M}. This admits positive adjudication (proof of the gap is the affirmation); but its meaning is protocol underdetermination, subsuming three cases — unregistered states, model omission, and internal randomness — and only where the model explicitly prescribes a conditional distribution rather than hidden states is it called model-relative randomness. Negative adjudication: find a licensed extension π′ ⊃ π\_M closing Gap(π′) — the randomness dissolved into unregistered microstructure.  
Ontic randomness: RandOnt ⟺ for every licensed extension π′ (the totality of what is explicitable within the boundary), Gap(π′) holds.  
Theorem 4 (adjudication asymmetry, conditional theorem): three antecedents — no operable exhaustiveness proof of the licensed extension class (its well-definedness resting on H1 and H4), evidence limited to finite empirical observation, no appeal to additional ontological axioms. Under the antecedents: RandOnt admits negative adjudication (one gap-closing extension negates it), and admits no positive confirmation by observation — any finite evidence set is compatible with the existence of unincorporated microstructure. Hence on the empirical path the adjudication layer yields ⊥ toward RandOnt; the path of internal theoretical ruling is a separate account (the three-way choice recorded in the corollary, H2). QED (within the antecedents).  
Corollary (the standing of "the rigorously proven internal random transition"): the "rigorously proven internal random transition" that the General Form and the Construction Form reserve among the source options — its "rigorous proof" is precisely the affirmation of RandOnt. By Theorem 4, the observational path cannot reach that proof; the sole remaining path is internal theoretical ruling — the field theory ruling some perturbation term (such as the ξ term of the candidate dynamics) irreducibly random, which is a constitutive choice and no empirical adjudication, its standing folded into the author's ruling of H2. Until that ruling is made, this option is an open slot: not deleted (theoretically licensed), not engaged (both paths unclosed). Whoever explains by "the system's intrinsic randomness" — the adjudication record enters ⊥ only. This corollary is handed over to the Strengthened Form of Current Generation: the source-side re-examination must argue within this bound, and may not employ the randomness option as a proven source.

5\. The Field-Layer Read-Back of the Gap

The field embodiment of unmanifest sediment microstructure (the filling-out of the Construction Form's Correspondence 14): the fine detail of the historical layering of δ(x,t), the micro-configurations of the path coordination of Ω\_c(q,t), the microscopic distribution of Itr Overlap — all real within the field yet ordinarily outside π. The gap thereby has its field-layer mechanism: a coarse protocol's fiber over field states is non-trivial.  
The physical mechanism candidate of the identification bound: extending π means deeper field measurement; and the measurement process itself occupies tuning capacity, perturbing Overlap and load (observational back-action) — extension being limited by measurement back-action, the identification bound is not merely epistemological but physically grounded. The formalization of this mechanism candidate is entered as H3; this Form makes no proven statement of it.

4\. Master discipline: no downstream Form may claim "the current or the bearing structure has been completely inverted from the explicit state and observation" — the bound is a hard bound.

7\. Breach Modes

1\. Construct a protocol π and a system family with Gap(π) holding while Γ\_π ↦ outcome is a function — proof of self-contradiction in the gap characterization (Theorem 1 breached); 2. the model pair of same-image distinct solutions proven unconstructible (a solution-set single-point theorem holding) — Theorem 2 breached; 3. a protocol strictly and positively adjudicating CurrentFact from Γ and y without H\_cur — Theorem 3 breached; 4. a valid procedure positively confirming RandOnt from finite observation — Theorem 4 breached; 5. a field-measurement scheme without back-action (the disproof of the H3 mechanism candidate — this breaches the candidate mechanism, not the theorem group).
