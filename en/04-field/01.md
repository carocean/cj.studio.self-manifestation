# Field Mathematical Formulation

## Section 1 Purpose

To establish the minimal mathematical system by which Trace-Effects within a Sedimentation-Field generate Information-Transformation structures.

This chapter does not begin from Causal-Response Structuring.  
It does not begin from logic.  
It does not begin from observer naming.

Causal-Response Structuring, induction, deduction, Law, and Structural Rationale are not pre-existing logical entities within the Sedimentation-Field.

They are only structural names recognized by observers within Information-Transformation, after Trace-Effects have stabilized into sedimentary structures within the field.

Therefore, what this chapter truly discusses is not: how logic operates.  
But rather: why logic can emerge at all.

Thus this chapter discusses only how Trace-Effects generate within the Sedimentation-Field:  
Groove-Lines, Flow-Hollows, Line-Bundles, Domains, Sedimentation-Degree, Thresholds, Stable Recurrence Capacity (Ω), Familiar Routes, Co-Nuclei, Permeation, Sedimentary Saturation, Domain Fullness, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, Structural Bias, Closures, New Structures.

And further explains how induction, deduction, Causal-Response Structuring, Law, and Structural Rationale are recognized by observers within Information-Transformation through these sedimentary structures.

From this, the first foundational system of Field Mathematics is established:  
foundational objects, foundational constraints, foundational axioms, foundational operations, state equations, foundational laws, foundational theorems, corollaries, and breakdown cases.

So that Field Mathematics may begin from Trace-Effects and trace the generative basis of Information-Transformation structures.

## Section 2 Core Thesis

Trace-Effects are physical phenomena within the Sedimentation-Field. Causal-Response Structuring is not a logic existing prior to Trace-Effects.  
Rather: after observers recognize stable Trace-Effects, logical naming emerges within Information-Transformation.  
Therefore: it is not Causal-Response Structuring that generates Trace-Effects. Rather: Trace-Effects first stabilize into structures within the Sedimentation-Field, and observers, recognizing these structures as repeatedly accompanying, inheriting, and succeeding one another over long durations, come to call them Causal-Response Structuring.

Pulses enter the Sedimentation-Field and form Trace-Effects. Trace-Effects form grooves between Structural Positions, line-like in shape, called Groove-Lines; Groove-Lines have depth. When Trace-Effects repeat over long durations and the continuation structure can stably recur, a Groove-Line becomes a route (stable continuation structure).  
Groove-Lines converge, merge, arrest, or redirect to form Flow-Hollows. Multiple Groove-Lines sustaining long-term co-continuation form Line-Bundles.  
A segment of a Line-Bundle that can stably maintain: Retentive Containment, differentiation, differential accumulation, continuation, and bundle-boundary, is called a Domain.  
Trace-Effects form grooves; Groove-Lines and Flow-Hollows have depth, and that depth is named Sedimentation-Degree. As Trace-Effects repeatedly overlap within a Domain, its depth increases when deepening outweighs shallowing. Sedimentation-Degree, Threshold, and Continuation Structure jointly constitute the convergence condition κ (a derived quantity). The deeper receives flow first (Outer Chapter 3); its formal content is given in Law 5 and Theorem 17.  
Long-term repeated continuation of Pulses with stable continuation structure forms Stable Recurrence Capacity (Ω). Recurrence produces a deepening action; when deepening outweighs shallowing, Sedimentation-Degree along the route increases net. The deeper receives flow first, and signals favor the paths long familiar: these are Familiar Routes (familiar paths, familiar domains; formal content in Definition 13 and Theorem 17).  
Familiar Routes, sustained over long durations, consolidate stable Line-Bundles. Long-term co-occurrence of Trace-Effects, with subsequent continuation persistently maintaining stable co-recurrence, forms Co-Nuclei.  
Continuation directed from boundary-domains toward Co-Nuclei is recognized within Information-Transformation as induction. Continuation expanding from Co-Nuclei toward boundary-domains is recognized within Information-Transformation as deduction.  
When stable Trace-Effects are recognized, they are named Causal-Response Structuring. When stable continuation long-term mutually returns, Closures form. When Closures are recognized within Information-Transformation, they are named Law.  
When stable Deviation Trajectories form new routes, and those new routes hold over long durations and are recognized within Information-Transformation as stable grounds that can traverse, constrain, or explain a set of continuation structures, they are called Structural Rationale.  
When a Domain can no longer deepen, can no longer differentiate, and can no longer admit further resolution, it is called Domain Fullness.  
When Domain Fullness is reached and new Trace-Effects still enter, unable to continue internal sinking, they overflow along the boundary: this is called Boundary Saturation Outgrowth.  
Successive Boundary Saturation Outgrowths generate new Structural Positions. Structural Positions stable over long durations form New Structures.

Therefore: what Field Mathematics truly studies is not logical form itself, but rather: why logical structures can emerge within Information-Transformation.

Note: The term “observer” in this chapter refers to a self-manifesting entity possessing a self-manifesting Sedimentation-Field structure, capable of generating recognition within the Information-Realm. The conditions for observer generation are given in the main text, Formulation Chapter 12 (Self-Manifestation Paradigm). This chapter addresses only how Trace-Effects generate structures that can be recognized. The mechanism of recognition itself is treated separately in the Information-Transformation and Mis-Externalization chapters. This chapter does not presuppose a prior observer. It only establishes: if an observer exists, what it recognizes must be preceded by Trace-Effects.

## Section 3 Foundational Objects

Definition 1: Sedimentation-Field  
Notation: Fₜ=(Ξₜ, Γₜ, Hₜ, ℬₜ, 𝒟ₜ, πₜ, τₜ, δₜ, θₜ, κₜ, Ωₜ, φₜ, λₜ, Resₜ, Volₜ, Capₜ, Useₜ, Boundₜ)  
where:  
Ξₜ: set of Structural Positions   Γₜ: set of Groove-Lines   Hₜ: set of Flow-Hollows   ℬₜ: set of Line-Bundles   𝒟ₜ: set of Domains  
πₜ: Pulse distribution   τₜ: Trace-Effect distribution   δₜ: Sedimentation-Degree distribution   θₜ: Threshold distribution   κₜ: Continuation Potential distribution (derived quantity; see Definition 11)  
Ωₜ: Stable Recurrence Capacity distribution   φₜ: Flow Pressure distribution   λₜ: Field Quality   Resₜ: Resolution  
Volₜ: Field Volume   Capₜ: Field Capacity   Useₜ: Capacity Load  
Boundₜ: Domain Boundary Stability, representing the stability of current domain boundaries, derived from the current state value of Bind(∂𝒟).  
Note: S(x) (the current Continuation Structure State at Structural Position x) is a derived quantity, jointly determined by the route status of Groove-Lines in Γ (Route\_W, Definition 14) and the current states of H and ℬ; it is not listed independently in the tuple. See Definition 14.

Definition 2: Pulse  
Notation: πₜ(x) represents the effective action occurring at Structural Position x at time t.  
A Pulse is not meaning. It is not logic. It is not observer naming.  
A Pulse is only the actual action within the field capable of producing: Trace-Effects, continuation, overlap, deepening, shallowing, Permeation, Sedimentary Saturation, and Boundary Saturation Outgrowth.  
Without Pulses, there can be no Trace-Effects.

Definition 3: Trace-Effect  
Notation: τₜ(x) represents the effective alteration sustained and retained at Structural Position x at time t.  
A Trace-Effect is not an observer-defined causal relation. A Trace-Effect is a real physical alteration occurring within the field.  
If τₜ(x)≠0, then the Structural Position has already been altered.

Definition 4: Structural Position  
Notation: x ∈ Ξₜ  
A Structural Position is not a mere spatial point. A Structural Position is a local field structure capable of: receiving Pulses, retaining Trace-Effects, being deepened through continuation, participating in overlap, and forming Groove-Lines, Flow-Hollows, Line-Bundles, and Domains.

Definition 5: Groove-Line  
Notation: γ = Gr(x, y) denotes the Groove-Line between Structural Positions x and y. This notation denotes only the connection of the groove formed by Trace-Effects and does not presuppose stable continuation; stable continuation is written separately as x ⇝ y (Definition 14).  
Whether the groove connection is maintained is written Brk\_W(γ): Brk\_W(γ)=1 means the groove connection of γ is actually interrupted within W (e.g., the groove is cut, passage is blocked); Brk\_W(γ)=0 means the connection is maintained within W. Brk\_W is a direct fact of structural integrity; it depends neither on route qualification nor on S.  
A Groove-Line is the groove formed by Trace-Effects between Structural Positions, line-like in shape (Outer Chapter 3: Trace-Effects form grooves, line-like in shape, called Groove-Lines). A single Trace-Effect can already form a shallow groove; struck repeatedly, its depth increases (Outer Chapter 3).  
A Groove-Line possesses: Sedimentation-Degree, Threshold, Flow Pressure, outflow conditions, capacity for shallowing, and capacity for deepening.  
A Groove-Line is not an abstract connection. A Groove-Line is a real groove within the Sedimentation-Field, the channel along which continuation proceeds.  
A Groove-Line that undergoes long-term recurrence with cross-time structural stability becomes a stable continuation structure, called a route (Inner Chapter 14: “conduction produces traces; frequent travel forms a route”). Repeated Trace-Effects do not necessarily form a route; only when they simultaneously enter stable recurrence is a route formed.  
Generative order: Trace-Effects form a groove (Groove-Line) → later flow passes along the groove again (recurrence) → recurrence and structure stabilize across time, forming a route (stable continuation structure). A Groove-Line does not presuppose stable continuation, so there is no circular dependence.

Definition 6: Flow-Hollow  
Notation: h ∈ H  
A Flow-Hollow is a Structural Position where multiple Groove-Lines converge, merge, arrest, or redirect.  
A Flow-Hollow can accommodate multiple incoming flows; whether it continues to emit flow is jointly constrained by Sedimentation-Degree, Threshold, and Flow Pressure.  
A Flow-Hollow can produce: confluence, transfer-continuation, arrested holding, and flow-diversion.  
A Flow-Hollow is not an abstract node. A Flow-Hollow is a real confluence structure within the field.  
At least two Groove-Lines must participate in convergence or merging to form a Flow-Hollow. A single Groove-Line does not constitute a Flow-Hollow; it forms only the outflow node of the Groove-Line itself.

Definition 7: Line-Bundle  
Notation: β=Bundle(γ₁,γ₂,…,γₙ)  
A Line-Bundle is a bundle structure formed through long-term co-continuation of multiple Groove-Lines. A Line-Bundle need not be uniform.  
Some Groove-Lines terminate within the Line-Bundle; others pass through it without terminating inside.

Definition 8: Sedimentary Domain  
Notation: 𝒟 ⊂ Ξ  
A Domain is a segment of a Line-Bundle that can stably maintain: Retentive Containment, differentiation, differential accumulation, continuation, and bundle-boundary.  
A Domain is not an arbitrary set; a Domain is not any arbitrarily chosen segment of a Line-Bundle.  
Only a segment of a Line-Bundle that can stably sustain: Retentive Containment, differentiation, differential accumulation, continuation, and bundle-boundary may be called a Domain.  
Note: The original formulation in Outer Chapter 3 (Number Theory) states “any segment of a Line-Bundle is a Domain.” This chapter introduces a strictness revision, adding five stability conditions as the threshold for Domain formation, to prevent over-generalization. The original formulation is an intuitive description; this definition is its mathematically precise version.

Definition 9: Sedimentation-Degree  
Notation: δ(x) represents the depth of sedimentation at a Structural Position, Groove-Line, Flow-Hollow, Line-Bundle, or Domain, named Sedimentation-Degree. Sedimentation-Degree is borne by the hosting mechanism and need not be geometric depth (Outer Chapter 3: “one may also fill the grooves with material so that they are more travelled and more easily emit—by transmitters, by ion pumps, by internal and external pressure”).  
The passage-behavior and effects of Sedimentation-Degree are not written into this definition; see Equation 3a (the deeper, the lower the Threshold) and Law 5 (the deeper receives flow first).  
A deep structure may currently not emit flow, for example: later flow has not yet reached its outflow condition; blocked passage (broken continuation structure, corresponding to Inner Chapter 15 “when it meets obstruction, it detours”); Tuning suppression (Definition 26). These are examples, not an exhaustive list.

Also: δmax(x) represents the maximum Sedimentation-Degree at which stable internal sinking can continue at a Structural Position, Groove-Line, Flow-Hollow, Line-Bundle, or Domain under the current field state.  
When δ(x)=δmax(x), the current structure has reached the internal sinking limit.  
However, δmax may change due to field state changes, bundle-boundary conditions, resolution conditions, and structural changes.

Definition 10: Threshold  
Notation: θ(x) represents the condition that must be exceeded for a Structural Position, Groove-Line, Flow-Hollow, Line-Bundle, or Domain to undergo outflow, inflow, Permeation, Sedimentary Saturation, or Boundary Saturation Outgrowth.  
Every Groove-Line has a Threshold; Line-Bundles also have Thresholds; Domains also have Thresholds. Threshold values differ; within the same Groove-Line, values may also differ.

Definition 11: Continuation Potential  
Notation: κ(x) represents the convergence capacity of Structural Position x under the current field state.  
κ is a derived quantity: the combined convergence condition given jointly by Sedimentation-Degree, Threshold, and current Continuation Structure State (Equation 3a); it introduces no new independent variable.  
κ is not Sedimentation-Degree itself, does not equal Threshold, and does not equal Stable Recurrence Capacity. High κ does not necessarily form Ω.  
κ does not decide priority independently of δ: under equal comparison conditions, κ does not reverse the δ-order, i.e., δ(x₁)\>δ(x₂) implies κ(x₁)≥κ(x₂) (note to Equation 3a); strict inequality is not guaranteed.  
Note: In the main text Field Chapter v1.0, κ is defined as “positional potential” and Ω as “path tendency,” forming a positional-potential / path-tendency dual. This chapter defines Ω as the historical qualification of stable recurrence actually exhibited within W (Definition 12), with no forward-looking guarantee. The positional-potential nature of κ is preserved within the monotonicity constraints of Equation 3a.

Definition 12: Stable Recurrence Capacity  
Notation: Ω\_W(x→y) represents the historical qualification, within a declared window W, of Pulses stably recurring from x to y along a Groove-Line. This chapter takes Ω as a historical qualification predicate: Ω\_W(x→y)∈{0,1}. Wherever the text writes Ω(x→y), it means Ω\_W(x→y) under the declared window W; different windows may give different values. “Stable Recurrence Capacity” in this chapter means stable recurrence actually exhibited within W; it does not assert that recurrence must persist after W. A field implementation that introduces a degree quantity or a forward-looking capacity quantity must separately declare its input conditions, maintenance conditions, evaluation period, and criterion.  
Ω is not a Familiar Route. A Familiar Route is a familiar path or familiar domain (Definition 13). Ω≠Familiar Route.  
Ω\_W checks three verifiable facts, all restricted to W: first, long-term repeated continuation (the recurrence record within W reaches the declared criterion); second, the connection of the Groove-Line followed is maintained within W (Brk\_W(γ)=0; where a Line-Bundle is involved, the same holds for each relevant Groove-Line in it); third, recurrence along the same class of path persists within W (W is divided into declared sub-intervals, each containing recurrence events along the same class of path). If all three hold, Ω\_W(x→y)=1; if any fails, Ω\_W(x→y)=0.  
Relation of Ω to δ: recurrence produces a deepening action (Dτ\>0, within the deepenable range); net deepening requires Dτ\>Eλ (Equation 2). Ω\_W is not given by current δ alone; witness: routes a and b have δ(a)=δ(b) at time t; a recurs over W with stable structure and recurrence events in every sub-interval, so all three facts hold and Ω\_W=1; b also recurs over W and reaches the same δ, but its groove connection is interrupted repeatedly (Brk\_W=1), so the second fact fails and Ω\_W=0. Ω\_W is derived from the recurrence record within W (Pass\_W and recurrence events in each sub-interval) and the integrity record of the groove connection (Brk\_W), without reference to Route\_W or S; it is a derived historical qualification predicate, not a new fundamental quantity independent of field history. Inner Chapter 9 “Law is recurrence, is depth”: Ω corresponds to recurrence, δ to depth.  
A single strong burst does not satisfy Ω\_W: a single action has no recurrence record, so the first fact fails—this is a choice of definition. Whether a single action can establish conditions for later sustained recurrence is a separate capacity proposition on which this chapter makes no claim.  
Ω does not equal κ. κ provides the convergence condition; Ω provides the stable recurrence condition.  
“Long-term” does not require infinite repetition; it means the recurrence record within W reaches the declared criterion.  
Repetition does not automatically form Ω. High convergence does not automatically entail stable recurrence.

Definition 13: Familiar Route  
Familiar Route: the familiar path; the original text also speaks of the familiar domain (Outer Chapter 3: “because what is deep first receives the flow, signals favor the paths long familiar. These are the familiar paths, the familiar domains”). A Familiar Route is an existing deep-sedimented path or domain along which signals readily travel.  
“Familiar” is the name for what is deep, with a comparative sense (Outer Chapter 3: “because new learning accumulates upon old recognition, it is called more familiar than old recognition. Therefore the deep may be called the familiar”; Inner Chapter 9: “deep sediment rises to the top; this is called the well-worn. Thus the well-worn appears first”). Receiving flow first is its manifestation (Law 5), not its definition.  
This chapter distinguishes two judgments:  
First, comparison “more familiar”: within a declared comparison domain K (a set of comparable routes of the same kind), a is more familiar than b if and only if δ(a)\>δ(b). This relation is irreflexive and transitive; when δ(a)=δ(b), the two are equally familiar, with no strict order.  
Second, existence “Familiar Route”: a Familiar Route is defined by its role—a route with existing deep sedimentation along which signals habitually travel (Outer Chapter 3: “signals favor the paths long familiar. These are the familiar paths”). Judgment condition: a is a Familiar Route if and only if δ(a)\>0 and a has a habitual-travel record within the declared window W (actual passages, in number or frequency, reaching the declared criterion; this criterion is an operationalization choice of this chapter, the original text giving no specific threshold). This judgment does not depend on whether a deeper route exists in the comparison domain: adding a deeper route does not change a’s existing status as a Familiar Route; it only changes the “more familiar” comparison. A route in comparison domain K with no deeper route is separately called “the most familiar in K” (Operation 11) and is not the judgment of Familiar Route. A field implementation that sets an additional threshold such as δᵣ must declare its operational purpose; it does not replace the general meaning of “familiar.”  
A Familiar Route does not presuppose Ω. Groove-Line≠Familiar Route; Ω≠Familiar Route. Recurrence produces a deepening action; if deepening outweighs shallowing and the bottom limit is not reached (Dτ\>Eλ, δ\<δmax), Sedimentation-Degree along the route increases net, making the Familiar Route more familiar.

Definition 14: Continuation  
Notation: x ⇝ y represents stable convergence of Pulses along a route-qualified Groove-Line or a Line-Bundle. A Groove-Line γ=Gr(x,y) becomes a route, written Route\_W(γ)=1, if and only if Ω\_W(x→y)=1 (Definition 12); being a route is a qualification state of a Groove-Line in Γ. Passing along a Groove-Line again (recurrence) is written Pass\_W(γ), the number of actual passages of later flow along γ within W, without presupposing stability.  
Continuation is not a single passage. Continuation means a structure that can receive, recur, and persistently enter subsequent formation.  
Continuation Structure: the overall combination of route-qualified Groove-Lines, Flow-Hollows, and Line-Bundles on which Pulses stably converge within the field.  
Continuation is the action; Continuation Structure is the field-structural basis bearing that action.  
When the Continuation Structure is stable, continuation can recur. When the Continuation Structure breaks, continuation cannot persist, and κ declines with S (Equation 3a).

Notation: S(x) represents the current Continuation Structure State at Structural Position x.  
S(x) is a derived quantity, jointly determined by the route status of Groove-Lines in Γ (Route\_W) and the current states of H and ℬ; it introduces no new independent variable.  
S(x) stable: the route-qualified Groove-Lines, Flow-Hollows, and Line-Bundles involving x are all in a sustainably continuing state. A shallow groove that is not yet a route does not make S stable.  
S(x) broken: the continuation structure involving x has undergone interruption or destabilization.

Definition 15: Co-Nucleus  
Notation: Core(x₁,x₂,…) represents the stable common structure formed when multiple structures sustain long-term co-Trace-Effects and subsequent continuation persistently maintains stable co-recurrence.  
A Co-Nucleus is not a set intersection. Co-occurrence≠Co-Nucleus; co-continuation≠Co-Nucleus. Stable co-recurrence is required for Co-Nucleus formation.

Definition 16: Retentive Containment  
Notation: Cnv(𝒟)\>0 means that subsequent Pulses entering a Domain do not immediately exit, but instead lag, remain, or accumulate.  
Retentive Containment allows differentiation to accumulate over long durations. Without Retentive Containment, differentiation cannot deepen. Without differential accumulation, Structural Bias cannot form.

Definition 17: Flow Pressure  
Notation: φ represents the outflow drive intensity within continuation structures under the current field state. φ is not a uniform field-wide quantity; Flow Pressure is attached to specific Groove-Lines, Flow-Hollows, and continuation structures.  
φ(γ) denotes Groove-Line flow pressure; φ(h) denotes Flow-Hollow flow pressure; φ(x⇝y) denotes flow pressure along a specific continuation structure.  
κ determines whether entry is possible; φ determines how continuation proceeds after entry. φ does not determine whether continuation can occur; φ only affects outflow state after continuation conditions have already been met.  
A decline in φ does not necessarily cause κ to decline. Weakened Flow Pressure does not equal disappearance of Familiar Routes. High φ does not automatically entail stable continuation.

Definition 18: Permeation  
Notation: Y(x)=1 means new Trace-Effects can still further deepen the original structure—wherever Sedimentation-Degree can still deepen, Permeation occurs.  
Permeation means the original structure retains the capacity for further internal sinking. Permeation does not equal mere increase in input.  
Increased input that cannot continue internal sinking does not constitute Permeation.

Definition 19: Sedimentary Saturation  
Notation: Q(x)=1 means a Structural Position, Groove-Line, Line-Bundle, or Domain has reached bottom—new Trace-Effects can no longer increase internal Sedimentation-Degree.  
Sedimentary Saturation means the local structure has reached the internal-sinking limit (bottom reached), but Sedimentary Saturation does not necessarily constitute Domain Fullness.  
Domain Fullness means the Domain as a whole has reached the state of no further internal sinking and no further differentiability. Sedimentary Saturation≠Domain Fullness.  
Sedimentary Saturation is a judgment for the current moment, not a fixed state. If δmax rises due to field state changes, the Sedimentary Saturation condition can be revoked, and the structure re-enters the Permeation state. Sedimentary Saturation depends on the current δmax, not on historical judgment.

Definition 20: Domain Fullness  
Notation: Full(𝒟)=1 means a Domain can no longer deepen, can no longer differentiate, and can no longer admit resolution.  
Domain Fullness is not Shallowing; Domain Fullness is not Resolution Loss. Domain Fullness means the capacity and internal differentiability have reached their limits.  
Resolution Loss means the stable differential capacity has declined. Domain Fullness may lead to Resolution Loss, but Domain Fullness≠Resolution Loss.  
δ reaching the current bottom limit only means the corresponding structure cannot continue internal sinking. Full requires three conditions to hold simultaneously: no further deepening, no further differentiation, no further admittable resolution.  
Therefore: Q does not automatically entail Full. Sedimentary Saturation does not equal Domain Fullness.

Definition 21: Boundary Saturation Outgrowth  
Notation: B(𝒟)=1 means that after Domain Fullness, new Trace-Effects, unable to continue internal sinking, overflow along the Domain boundary, generating new Structural Positions.  
The structural content carried by Boundary Saturation Outgrowth closely correlates with the structural content of the Domain core. Boundary Saturation Outgrowth is not random diffusion.  
Boundary Saturation Outgrowth requires: Domain Fullness already established, new Trace-Effects continuing to enter, and inability to continue internal sinking.

Definition 22: Structural Bias  
Notation: Bias(𝒟,γ) or Bias(𝒟,β) means that after long-term differential accumulation within a Domain, continuation gradually forms stable biased continuation along certain Groove-Lines or Line-Bundles.  
Structural Bias is the biased-continuation result formed after long-term differential accumulation in sedimentary structure; it is not the original structure. Its formation conditions are given in Equation 11 (four independent verifiable conditions).  
Long-term input favoring a particular structure does not necessarily form Structural Bias. Sustained external pressure does not equal Structural Bias. Input-bias≠structural-bias; input-pressure-bias≠structural-pressure-bias.  
Only when already-formed structures persistently bias future continuation does Structural Bias hold.  
Structural Bias may occur within Familiar Routes; Structural Bias does not require Familiar Routes. Structural Bias may exist within Familiar Routes, Line-Bundles, Domains, or other stable structures. Structural Bias≠Familiar Route; Familiar Route≠Structural Bias.  
Structural Bias and Biased Flow: in the Inner and Outer Chapters, Biased Flow has several contextual uses (prior traces guiding later flow, differential output of the Experience-Field, intention selecting or redirecting flow, coloration altering experiential biased flow; see Inner Chapters 15, 7, 4 and Outer Chapter 9), to be identified by context. Structural Bias denotes only the stably established biased continuation produced by sedimentation differences; it does not replace the name Biased Flow.  
Note: In the main text Field Chapter v1.0, Bias contains a direction parameter v, written Bias(𝒟,v)\>0, indicating continuation preference toward direction v. This chapter redefines Bias as structural pressure-bias, removing the explicit direction parameter; the direction of biased continuation is instead implicitly determined by the skew of the δ distribution. Relative to the definition containing direction parameter v, this revision makes the formation conditions of Structural Bias more independently verifiable, avoiding presupposition of direction. The direction information is not deleted; it is internalized as the differential direction of δ(γ₁) and δ(γ₂) in condition (4a) of Equation 11.

Definition 23: Shallowing  
Notation: δ(x)↓ or κ(x)↓ represents weakening of original Sedimentation-Degree or current convergence capacity.  
Shallowing is not total disappearance, not deletion; it is only shallowing of prior sedimentation or weakening of the current convergence condition.  
δ declining does not automatically entail κ declining. κ is still jointly influenced by Threshold, current Continuation Structure, and field state. δ↓≠κ necessarily declines. Shallowing does not automatically damage convergence capacity.

Definition 24: Continuation Closure  
Notation: ℒ=Cl(x₁⇝x₂⇝…⇝xₙ) represents a repeatedly returnable closed structure formed after multiple continuation segments stably mutually return over long durations.  
Closure is a field structure. Law is only the observer-recognition name of Closure within Information-Transformation.

Definition 25: Deviation Trajectory  
Notation: Dev(ℒ)→ℒ′ represents subsequent Pulses, over long durations, deviating from the old Closure and forming a new stable path.  
Deviation Trajectory is not Structural Rationale; Structural Rationale is not the Deviation Trajectory itself. When a stable Deviation Trajectory forms a new route, and that new route holds over long durations and is recognized within Information-Transformation as a stable ground that can traverse, constrain, or explain a set of continuation structures, it is called Structural Rationale.

Definition 26: Tuning  
Notation: Tune(x) represents adjustment of current flow-priority order, the display level of Sedimentation-Degree, and Domain Boundary Thresholds.  
Tuning does not delete historical Trace-Effects; Tuning only changes current preferential continuation order.  
Tune does not delete τ, does not delete δ, does not delete Ω. Tune changes the current preferential continuation structure.  
Tuning is not rebuilding history, not deleting history; Tuning means the current continuation ordering has changed.

Definition 27: Boundary-Binding  
Notation: Bind(∂𝒟) represents constraining the Domain boundary.  
Boundary-Binding prevents retrieval from dispersing infinitely outward and maintains stable Domain boundaries.  
Weakened Boundary-Binding allows retrieval to disperse more easily and Domain boundaries to destabilize more easily. Boundary-Binding is not the Domain itself; Boundary-Binding represents the stability capacity of the Domain boundary.

Definition 28: Field Quality  
Notation: λ(F) represents the Sedimentation-Field’s capacity for: retention, shallowing, diffusion, disturbance resistance, and differentiation maintenance.  
Field Quality determines shallowing speed; Field Quality influences stable continuation maintenance; Field Quality influences differentiation structure maintenance.  
λ is not a mere forgetting term; λ does not equal Shallowing; λ represents the overall structural stability capacity of the field.  
Note: In Outer Chapter 3 (Number Theory), Field Quality is defined as “the measure of shallowing speed,” with shallowing rate as the core. This chapter extends Field Quality to a multi-dimensional capacity description, where the shallowing component corresponds to the Number Theory definition. The basis for extension is: shallowing rate alone cannot distinguish Field Quality’s differential effects on different structures, hence the multi-dimensional characterization.

Definition 29: Resolution  
Notation: Res(F) represents the number of differentiable Domains per unit volume.  
The more Domains the field can accommodate, differentiate, and stably maintain, the higher Res. Res represents differential capacity.  
A decline in Res does not automatically entail Resolution Loss. Changes in the number of differentiable Domains do not automatically equal destabilization of stable differential capacity.  
Note: Inner Chapter 6 (Sedimentation-Field Theory) of the main text specifies that Resolution is jointly determined by four field elements: temporal sequence, frequency, intensity, and density. Res is the result of these four elements jointly acting on the differentiable Domain density of the field; it is not an independent fundamental quantity. This chapter treats Res as a parameter; its internal structure is left for a dedicated chapter.

Definition 30: Field Capacity  
Notation: Cap(F)=Vol(F)×Res(F) (under uniform approximation)  
Field Capacity represents the number of stably differentiable Domains the field can bear.  
If the field’s Resolution distribution is approximately uniform, then Vol(F)×Res(F) can approximately describe Field Capacity.  
If local Resolution within the field is significantly non-uniform, then Cap(F)=∫Res(x)dVol, where Res(x) denotes local Resolution and dVol denotes the local field volume element.  
The uniform approximation uses the Vol×Res form; the integral form describes non-uniform fields.  
Note: This definition has been in use since Sedimentation-Field Theory v1.2, replacing the original definition in which Field Capacity was the ratio of information-count to field volume. The original definition conflated the dimensions of the physical layer (volume, domain count) with the Information-Transformation layer (information count), making Field Capacity unquantifiable. This definition is based on field volume and Resolution, both in the physical layer, making Field Capacity quantifiable. See Outer Chapter 3 (Number Theory).

Definition 31: Capacity Rate  
Notation: CapRate(F)=Cap(F)/Vol(F), i.e., capacity per unit volume.  
Capacity Rate and Resolution are the same quantity under two names: when speaking of differential capacity, it is called Resolution; when speaking of the number of Domains per unit volume, it is called Capacity Rate.  
CapRate≡Res. CapRate introduces no new state variable and is not an independent state quantity.

Definition 32: Used Domains  
Notation: UsedDomains(F) represents the number of Domains in the field that are stably occupied, can participate in stable continuation, and remain differentiable.  
Used Domains does not equal the total count of Structural Positions; Used Domains represents the number of Domains currently stably participating in structure.  
UsedDomains(F) changes dynamically with new Domain formation, Compression, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, and Resolution Loss; it is not a fixed quantity.

Definition 33: Capacity Load  
Notation: Use(F)=UsedDomains(F)/Cap(F)  
Capacity Load represents the ratio of Used Domains to Field Capacity; Use represents the degree of stable-capacity loading of the field.  
Higher Use indicates higher stable differential pressure. Use\>1 indicates entry into over-capacity conditions.  
However, Use is not a simple binary variable. Use=1.01 and Use=100 are not at the same level.  
Actual release pressure is jointly influenced by: degree of overrun, field state, structure distribution, stable bundle-boundary, and local capacity structure.  
Use\>1 indicates entry into load-release conditions; it does not mean release intensity has been determined.  
Use is not static; it changes jointly with UsedDomains and Cap. Changes in Cap may arise from changes in Res or changes in Vol.

Definition 34: Resolution Loss  
Notation: LossRes(𝒟) means the stable differential structure can no longer be maintained.  
Resolution Loss is not forgetting, not Shallowing, not Compression; Resolution Loss means stable differential capacity has declined.  
Resolution Loss has two types:  
Local Resolution Loss: the internal differential structure of a Domain undergoes merging.  
Field-wide Resolution Loss: multiple Domain boundaries simultaneously destabilize over long durations, causing overall stable differential capacity to decline.  
Load release by the field does not necessarily restore stable differentiation. Stable differentiation is restored only when stable differential conditions, stable Retentive Containment, stable continuation, stable bundle-boundary, and stable capacity conditions are re-formed.  
A decline in Res does not automatically entail Resolution Loss. Resolution Loss requires the stable differential structure to already be unable to continue.  
Local Resolution Loss does not automatically entail field-wide Resolution Loss. Field-wide Resolution Loss requires multiple stable Domains to jointly destabilize over long durations.

Definition 35: Compression  
Notation: Compress(x,y)→p means multiple Structural Positions, unable to stably differentiate, merge into a common Domain.  
Compression is not deletion, not Resolution Loss; Compression means structural reorganization. Compress≠LossRes.  
Compression may cause reduction in differentiation, but reduction in differentiation does not automatically entail that stable differential capacity has already become unstable.  
Compress may occur when stable differentiation still exists. Resolution Loss requires that stable differential capacity has already become unable to continue.

Definition 36: Field Graph  
Notation: G(F)=(Ξ,H,Γ,ℬ,𝒟,δ,θ,κ,Ω,φ)  
A Field Graph is a depth-weighted graph of the Sedimentation-Field. Flow-Hollows are nodes; Groove-Lines are connecting structures; Line-Bundles form structural bundles; Domains form locally stable structures.  
Sedimentation-Degree represents depth-weight; Threshold represents in-out conditions (among comparable structures, the deeper, the lower the Threshold; Equation 3a); Flow Pressure represents current drive state.  
Structural Positions provide the structural basis for Groove-Lines, Flow-Hollows, Line-Bundles, and Domains. A Sedimentation-Field is not a flat set; it is a depth-weighted graph.  
Note: G(F) extracts the topological and depth structure of the field; it does not include management parameters such as Field Quality (λ), Field Capacity (Cap), Capacity Load (Use), and Domain Boundary Stability (Bound). These management parameters are listed separately in Fₜ and do not participate in the structural description of the Field Graph.

Definition 37: Information-Transformation  
Information-Transformation is cited from the main text. This chapter does not establish an independent formal definition (Ψₜ), but provides the following working definition for use within this chapter.  
Outer Chapter 7 original text: “The Sedimentation-Field pivots to generate the Information-Realm; in substance, it transforms matter into information; since it can transform its host into information, it is called Information-Transformation. Information-Transformation is the method of studying how the Information-Realm is generated: the mechanism by which all things are pivotally generated.” (End of citation)  
In the Formulation chapters, the Information-Transformation mechanism is denoted Ψₜ; its complete formal definition is given in the Formulation chapters (Mis-Externalization Limit Formulation). This chapter does not independently introduce the symbol Ψ, to avoid symbol collision.  
Within this chapter’s context, “recognized within Information-Transformation” means: after stable structures in the field enter the Information-Transformation action chain, they acquire structural names that can be recognized by observers.  
Information-Transformation is not an internal operation of the field; Information-Transformation is the cross-layer transition through which field structures enter the recognition layer.  
Information-Transformation does not change field state; Information-Transformation only changes the observer’s recognition layer of field state.  
Without Information-Transformation, field structures have no names. With Information-Transformation, structural names come after structural existence.  
Note: Information-Transformation operates on two levels—the generative level (field transforms matter into information, pivotally generating the Information-Realm) and the recognition level (observer recognizes and names stable structures in the field). This chapter primarily addresses the recognition level; the generative level is treated in Outer Chapter 7.

## Section 4 Foundational Constraints

Constraint 1: No Pulse, no Trace-Effect. If ∀x∈Ξ π(x)=0, then τ(x)=0. No Trace-Effects, no sedimentation, no Groove-Lines.

Constraint 2: No Trace-Effect, no accumulation change. If ∀x∈Ξ τ(x)=0, then Δδ(x)=0. No change in Sedimentation-Degree means no Groove-Line deepening and no Familiar Route formation. Note: κ is also influenced by θ and S(x); the absence of Trace-Effects only guarantees Δδ(x)=0 and does not directly guarantee Δκ(x)=0.

Constraint 3: No stable recurrence, no route. If Trace-Effects repeat over long durations but the continuation structure cannot stably recur, the Groove-Line does not become a route (stable continuation structure). Repetition≠route; stable recurrence is a necessary condition for forming a route.

Constraint 4: No Groove-Lines, no stable continuation. If Γ=∅, then x⇝y cannot stably hold.

Constraint 5: No Flow-Hollows, no confluence or redirection. If H=∅, multiple Groove-Lines cannot stably converge, merge, arrest, or redirect. At least two Groove-Lines are required to form a Flow-Hollow; a single-Groove-Line field cannot form a Flow-Hollow.

Constraint 6: No Line-Bundles, no Domains. If ℬ=∅, no stable Retentive Containment, stable differentiation, differential accumulation, stable continuation, stable bundle-boundary, or Domain structure can form.

Constraint 7: No Domains, no differentiation structures. If 𝒟=∅, the field has no stable Retentive Containment, stable differentiation, Compression, Permeation, Sedimentary Saturation, Domain Fullness, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, or local structure.

Constraint 8: No Thresholds, no in-out conditions. If θ does not exist, Groove-Lines, Flow-Hollows, Line-Bundles, and Domains all lack in-out conditions, Permeation conditions, Sedimentary Saturation conditions, and Boundary Saturation Outgrowth conditions.

Constraint 9: No sedimentation or no habitual travel, no Familiar Routes. A Familiar Route requires existing deep sedimentation (δ\>0) and habitual travel by signals (Definition 13). Equal depth only means no strict order; it does not affect Familiar Route status.

Constraint 10: No stable co-recurrence, no Co-Nucleus. Co-occurrence does not constitute a Co-Nucleus; co-continuation does not constitute a Co-Nucleus. If there is no stable co-recurrence, Co-Nucleus does not form.

Constraint 11: No Retentive Containment, no differential accumulation. If Cnv(𝒟)=0, differentiation cannot accumulate over long durations, and Structural Bias cannot hold.

Constraint 12: No Permeation, no deepening. If Y(x)=0, new Trace-Effects cannot continue to deepen the original structure.

Constraint 13: No Sedimentary Saturation, no internal-sinking limit. If Q(x)=0, there is no way to explain why Trace-Effects do not deepen without limit.

Constraint 14: No Domain Fullness, no Boundary Saturation Outgrowth conditions. If Full(𝒟)=0, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth lacks its structural necessary condition.

Constraint 15: No Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, no New Structures. If Full(𝒟)=1 and B(𝒟)=0, New Structures cannot be generated through Domain overflow.

Constraint 16: No Closure, no structural basis for Law. If Cl(ℒ) does not hold, stable Closures cannot form.

Constraint 17: No long-term stable new route, no structural basis for Structural Rationale. If Dev(ℒ) cannot form a new route, or the new route cannot hold over long durations, Structural Rationale has no structural basis.

Constraint 18: No Field Quality, the field eventually loses differentiation. If δ only increases and never shallows, and old Trace-Effects never shallow, the field will eventually reach Domain Fullness and lose differentiability.

Constraint 19: No Boundary-Binding, retrieval destabilizes. If Bind(∂𝒟)=0, retrieval disperses infinitely outward and Domains cannot stably return.

Constraint 20: Over-capacity, new differentials cannot be stably placed. If UsedDomains(F)\>Cap(F), new differential Domains cannot be stably placed; the field may release load through Compression, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, or Resolution Loss, but the actual release path is jointly constrained by field state.

## Section 5 Foundational Axioms

Axiom 1: Field Entry Axiom. Once effective input enters the Sedimentation-Field, at least some Pulses must form.

Axiom 2: Trace-Effect Axiom. When effective Pulses act upon Structural Positions, Trace-Effects necessarily emerge.

Axiom 3: Groove-Line Formation Axiom. Trace-Effects form Groove-Lines between Structural Positions (Outer Chapter 3); when Trace-Effects repeat over long durations and the continuation structure can stably recur, the Groove-Line becomes a route.

Axiom 4: Flow-Hollow Axiom. Multiple Groove-Lines converging, merging, arresting, or redirecting form a Flow-Hollow.

Axiom 5: Line-Bundle Axiom. Multiple Groove-Lines sustaining long-term co-continuation form a Line-Bundle.

Axiom 6: Domain Formation Axiom. A segment of a Line-Bundle that can stably maintain: Retentive Containment, differentiation, differential accumulation, continuation, and bundle-boundary, may form a Domain.

Axiom 7: Deepening Axiom. Long-term repeated continuation produces a deepening action (Dτ\>0); within the deepenable range and when deepening outweighs shallowing (Dτ\>Eλ), Sedimentation-Degree increases and the convergence basis changes (consistent with Equation 2).

Axiom 8: Shallowing Axiom. Positions not receiving long-term Trace-Effects undergo Shallowing of Sedimentation-Degree and Continuation Potential due to Field Quality.

Axiom 9: Stable Recurrence Axiom. Within W, long-term repeated continuation, maintained connection of the Groove-Line followed (Brk\_W=0), and persistent recurrence along the same class of path give Ω\_W=1, and the Groove-Line becomes a route.

Axiom 10: Familiar Route Axiom. The deeper receives flow first; signals favor the paths long familiar, forming familiar paths and familiar domains (Outer Chapter 3). The formal content of “receives flow first” is given in Law 5 and Theorem 17.

Axiom 11: Overlap-Nucleation Axiom. Long-term co-Trace-Effects with subsequent stable co-recurrence form Co-Nuclei.

Axiom 12: Retentive Containment Axiom. Subsequent Pulses entering a Domain may lag, remain, or accumulate.

Axiom 13: Permeation Axiom. If a Domain or Groove-Line can still continue deepening, new Trace-Effects entering it produce Permeation.

Axiom 14: Sedimentary Saturation Axiom. If a Domain or Groove-Line has reached its bottom, new Trace-Effects cannot continue internal sinking, producing Sedimentary Saturation.

Axiom 15: Domain Fullness Axiom. If a Domain can no longer deepen, can no longer differentiate, and can no longer admit resolution, it is called Domain Fullness.

Axiom 16: Boundary Saturation Outgrowth Axiom. After Domain Fullness, when new Trace-Effects continue to enter and cannot continue internal sinking, new Structural Positions are generated along the boundary.

Axiom 17: Closure Axiom. Continuation structures mutually returning over long durations form stable Closures.

Axiom 18: Deviation Trajectory Axiom. Stable continuation that deviates from prior Closures over long durations forms a Deviation Trajectory. If a stable Deviation Trajectory forms a new route that holds over long durations, the structural basis for Structural Rationale is established.

Axiom 19: Tuning Axiom. Tuning does not delete historical Trace-Effects; it only changes current preferential continuation order.

Axiom 20: Capacity Axiom. Cap(F)=Vol(F)×Res(F) (under uniform approximation)

Axiom 21: Capacity Load Axiom. Use(F)=UsedDomains(F)/Cap(F)

## Section 6 Foundational Operations

Operation 1: Field Entry. Pulse(s,F)→π　Input enters the field to form Pulses.

Operation 2: Trace-Effect Formation. Tτ(π,F,x)　Pulses form Trace-Effects at a Structural Position.

Operation 3: Groove-Line Formation. OpenLine(τ,x,y)→γ=Gr(x,y)　Trace-Effects form a Groove-Line between x and y; with long-term repetition and stable recurrence, the Groove-Line becomes a route.

Operation 4: Flow-Hollow Formation. MakeHollow(γ₁,γ₂,…)→h　Multiple Groove-Lines converging form a Flow-Hollow.

Operation 5: Bundle Formation. Bundle(γ₁,γ₂,…)→β　Multiple Groove-Lines co-continuing form a Line-Bundle.

Operation 6: Domain Formation. Domain(βᵢ…βⱼ)→𝒟　A segment of a Line-Bundle that stably maintains Retentive Containment, differentiation, differential accumulation, continuation, and bundle-boundary forms a Domain.

Operation 7: Deepening. Deepen(x)　Trace-Effects deepen Sedimentation-Degree.

Operation 8: Shallowing. Eλ(x)　A Structural Position shallows due to Field Quality.

Operation 9: Continuation. x⇝y　Pulses stably converge along a route-qualified Groove-Line (Route\_W=1) or a Line-Bundle.

Operation 10: Stable Recurrence. RepeatStable\_W(γ)→Ω\_W(x→y)　From the Pass\_W record, recurrence events in each sub-interval, and the Brk\_W record within W, the three facts of Definition 12 are judged to hold, forming the stable-recurrence qualification; hence Route\_W(γ)=1.

Operation 11: Familiar Route Judgment. Fam\_K(a≻b) ⇔ δ(a)\>δ(b) (a, b∈K)　compares “more familiar”; FamRoute\_W(a) ⇔ δ(a)\>0 ∧ Pass\_W(a) reaches the declared habitual-travel criterion　judges a to be a Familiar Route; MaxFam\_K(a) ⇔ δ(a)\>0 ∧ ¬∃b∈K: δ(b)\>δ(a)　denotes that a is the most familiar in K, for task comparison only, not the judgment of Familiar Route.

Operation 12: Overlap-Nucleation. Core(x₁,x₂,…)→z　Long-term co-Trace-Effects with stable co-recurrence form common stable Structural Position z; z is the Co-Nucleus.

Operation 13: Compression. Compress(x,y)→p　Multiple Structural Positions unable to stably differentiate merge into a common Domain.

Operation 14: Permeation. Y(x)=1　New Trace-Effects continue to deepen the original structure.

Operation 15: Sedimentary Saturation. Q(x)=1　The structure reaches the internal-sinking limit.

Operation 16: Domain Fullness. Full(𝒟)=1　A Domain can no longer deepen, differentiate, or admit resolution.

Operation 17: Boundary Saturation Outgrowth. B(𝒟)=1　After Domain Fullness, new Structural Positions are generated along the boundary.

Operation 18: Closure. Cl(x₁⇝x₂⇝…)　Formation of stable closed structures.

Operation 19: Deviation Trajectory. Dev(ℒ)→ℒ′　Deviation from old Closure forms a new route.

Operation 20: Tuning. Tune(x)　Changes current preferential continuation order.

Operation 21: Boundary-Binding. Bind(∂𝒟)　Constrains Domain boundaries.

Operation 22: Resolution Loss. LossRes(𝒟)　Stable differential structure can no longer be maintained.

Operation 23: Capacity Calculation. Cap(F)=Vol(F)×Res(F)

Operation 24: Capacity Load Calculation. Use(F)=UsedDomains(F)/Cap(F)

Operation 25: Extended Continuation. Extend(x⇝y)  
Pulses extend from current Structural Position x along domain-overlap adjacency relations to converge into adjacent high-κ Structural Position y.  
Occurrence conditions: x and y have a domain-overlap adjacency relation, and κ(y) exceeds the current extension threshold.  
Extended Continuation is not primary-route continuation; it is continuation extending along neighboring-domain relations after primary-route continuation is complete.

## Section 7 State Equations

Equation 1: Trace-Effect Formation Equation. τₜ(x)=Tτ(πₜ,Fₜ,x)

Equation 2: Sedimentation-Degree Evolution Equation. δₜ₊₁(x)=δₜ(x)+Dτₜ(x)−Eλₜ(x)  
where Dτₜ(x) represents depth increase caused by Trace-Effects, and Eλₜ(x) represents shallowing caused by Field Quality.

Equation 3: Convergence Basis Relation Equation. κ is derived from δ, θ, and S (Equation 3a); δ≠κ.  
f is monotone non-decreasing in δ: with θ and S unchanged, when δ increases, κ does not decrease; but κ is not guaranteed to increase strictly (f may have plateaus). “κ does not decrease,” “κ strictly increases,” and “outflow actually begins” must be judged separately.

Equation 3a: Continuation Potential Constraint Equation. Equation 3a refines Equation 3 by giving the qualitative functional form of κ constrained by δ, θ, and S.  
κ(x)=f(δ(x), θ(x), S(x))  
δ(x): greater Sedimentation-Degree raises the convergence basis of κ.  
θ(x): higher Threshold suppresses actual convergence of κ.  
S(x): if the continuation structure breaks or destabilizes (blocked passage), κ declines accordingly.  
f is monotone non-decreasing in δ, monotone non-increasing in θ, and when S is stable maintains its value; when S breaks, it declines.  
Threshold and depth: among comparable structures of the same kind, the deeper the Sedimentation-Degree, the lower its Threshold (Outer Chapter 3: “Every Gully-Line possesses its threshold. Their values are unequal. Even within the same Gully-Line, threshold-values may likewise differ. The deeper the Sedimentation-Degree, the smaller its threshold-value; contrariwise, the greater the threshold-value becomes”). The original text gives a direction, not a precise function, and does not state whether Threshold has other determinants; this chapter introduces none, and any field implementation that does must declare them as premises.  
Note: it follows that under equal comparison conditions (S stable on both, comparable structures of the same kind), δ(x₁)\>δ(x₂) implies θ(x₁)\<θ(x₂), and hence κ(x₁)≥κ(x₂): κ does not reverse the δ-order; strict inequality is not guaranteed.  
Note: The precise functional form of κ depends on specific field implementations; this equation gives constraint directions only, not a precise function.

Equation 4: Preferential Continuation Equation.  
Comparison condition C₌: (i) the comparison objects are the two routes x₁ and x₂ under the same entry e; (ii) the two routes share the entry flow pressure p, which is non-decreasing step by step before either opens; (iii) both routes keep their groove connection and are unblocked (Brk=0; being a route is not required), and a route opens if and only if p reaches its Threshold; (iv) there is no difference of selective bias or Tuning between them; (v) they are comparable structures of the same kind. Other channels that change p within the comparison window are outside the scope of this equation and must be declared separately.  
Under C₌: if δ(x₁)\>δ(x₂), then θ(x₁)\<θ(x₂) (Equation 3a), and x₁ satisfies the outflow condition no later than x₂ (Theorem 17(a)); for x₁ to satisfy it strictly before x₂, the condition of Theorem 17(b) (denoted P₁₇) must also hold.  
Criterion for “receives flow first”: this chapter takes “first satisfies the outflow condition,” i.e., the entry flow pressure first reaches the Threshold of x₁. This is a formalization choice, based on Outer Chapter 3 (“Every Gully-Line possesses its threshold … The deeper the Sedimentation-Degree, the smaller its threshold-value … it must first fill fa, and only thereafter seep into the two sides”) and Inner Chapter 6 (flows arrive, later flows arrive … converging and waiting, combining and emitting); it is not the unique meaning proven by the original text. Wherever this chapter asserts strict first-receipt of flow under this criterion, it relies on C₌ and P₁₇.  
Note: when C₌ is not satisfied (blocked passage, Tuning, selective bias, different entries, Thresholds differing for other reasons), the above comparison does not apply and must be stated and judged separately.

Equation 5: Stable Recurrence Formation Equation.  
Within the declared window W, long-term repeated continuation (recurrence record reaching the criterion), maintained connection of the Groove-Line followed (Brk\_W=0), and recurrence along the same class of path persisting in every sub-interval give Ω\_W(x→y)=1, and the Groove-Line becomes a route.  
If structure is broken over long durations or recurrence cannot stabilize, Ω does not form. Long-term repetition does not automatically form Ω.

Equation 6: Familiar Route Equation. A Familiar Route is a route with existing deep sedimentation along which signals habitually travel (Definition 13). Under C₌, a deeper route satisfies the outflow condition no later than a shallower one; under C₌ and P₁₇ it receives flow first (Law 5, Theorem 17)—this is the formal content of “because what is deep first receives the flow, signals favor the paths long familiar.” Recurrence produces a deepening action; when Dτ\>Eλ and the bottom limit is not reached, the Familiar Route’s Sedimentation-Degree increases net, making it more familiar.  
Ω does not equal Familiar Route; Familiar Route does not presuppose Ω.

Equation 7: Co-Nucleus Formation Equation.  
Long-term co-Trace-Effects with subsequent stable co-recurrence: Co-Nucleus forms.  
Co-occurrence does not constitute a Co-Nucleus; co-continuation does not constitute a Co-Nucleus; stable co-recurrence and long-term co-Trace-Effects must both hold to form a Co-Nucleus.  
After Co-Nucleus formation, if its Sedimentation-Degree is deeper than that of the comparison object, under C₌ it satisfies the outflow condition no later than the latter, and under C₌ and P₁₇ it receives flow first (Law 5, Theorem 17). Where comparison conditions differ (e.g., blockage, Tuning, selective bias), a Co-Nucleus need not emerge first.

Equation 8: Permeation and Sedimentary Saturation Judgment Equation. Under the precondition that πₜ(x)\>0 (new Trace-Effects entering): if δ(x)\<δmax(x), then Y(x)=1; if δ(x)≥δmax(x), then Q(x)=1.  
Those that can continue deepening undergo Permeation; those at the limit undergo Sedimentary Saturation. Sedimentary Saturation does not equal Domain Fullness.  
When no new Trace-Effects are entering, the Permeation and Sedimentary Saturation judgment does not apply.  
Note: δmax is a dynamic quantity; this judgment is a current judgment at time t and does not guarantee holding at subsequent moments. If δmax rises due to field state changes, Q(x)=1 can be revoked and the structure re-enters Y(x)=1 state.

Equation 9: Domain Fullness Judgment Equation. Full(𝒟)=1 if and only if 𝒟 can no longer deepen, can no longer differentiate, and can no longer admit resolution.  
Domain Fullness means the conditions for stably forming new differentials within that Domain have reached their limit. Domain Fullness does not require exhaustion of field volume, nor complete occupation of total field capacity.

Equation 10: Boundary Saturation Outgrowth Generation Equation. Full(𝒟)=1, and π continues to enter 𝒟, and new Trace-Effects cannot continue internal sinking, then B(𝒟)=1; new Trace-Effects overflow along the Domain boundary, and new Structural Positions are generated. If new Structural Positions are stable over long durations, New Structures form.

Equation 11: Structural Bias Formation Equation. Independent verifiable conditions for Structural Bias to hold:  
(1) Retentive Containment exists: Cnv(𝒟)\>0.  
(2) Differential accumulation exists: δ of some Groove-Lines or Line-Bundles has undergone stable differentiation.  
(3) Long-term unequal continuation exists: the historical continuation frequency of Groove-Lines or Line-Bundles shows significant differences.  
(4a) The Sedimentation-Degree distribution has undergone structural skew: δ(γ₁)−δ(γ₂) exceeds a declared difference cutoff ε\_δ (ε\_δ has the same dimension as δ; it is not the Threshold θ).  
(4b) Actual stable biased continuation: over a declared long time window W, the skew persistently biases later flow, so that later flow stably favors the skew-deep side. When selective bias or Tuning participate, (4b) can still hold as long as the sedimentary structure still bears this bias; C₌ is not a necessary condition of Structural Bias.  
(4a) and (4b) must both hold for Structural Bias to hold. (4a) describes the already-formed structural state; (4b) describes its action on later flow; their conjunction avoids circular definition.  
Test method for (4b): under C₌, apply inputs repeatedly within W and record whether the skew-deep side satisfies the outflow condition no later than the skew-shallow side (Theorem 17(a)) and whether it does so strictly first (Theorem 17(b)); judge by a declared criterion (e.g., the proportion of strict-first instances).  
Model sufficient condition for (4b): if every input within W satisfies C₌ and P₁₇, and the skew is maintained within W (Dτ−Eλ on the two sides does not reduce the difference below ε\_δ), then at every input the skew-deep side satisfies the outflow condition strictly first, and (4b) holds over W.  
(4a) holding while (4b) fails is not limited to failures of C₌: input interruption, changes in initial pressure, later simultaneous opening, shallowing of the depth difference, etc., can all make the long-window condition fail.  
Input-bias does not constitute Structural Bias; sustained external pressure does not constitute Structural Bias; long-term unequal continuation does not automatically constitute Structural Bias. Only when (4a) and (4b) both hold does Structural Bias hold.

Equation 12: Resolution Loss Formation Equation.  
If stable differential conditions, stable Retentive Containment, stable continuation, stable bundle-boundary, or stable capacity conditions destabilize over long durations, Resolution Loss conditions hold.  
Resolution Loss is not Compression, not Shallowing, not forgetting. A decline in Res does not automatically entail Resolution Loss.  
Resolution Loss requires that the stable differential structure has already become unable to continue. Local Resolution Loss does not automatically entail field-wide Resolution Loss. Field-wide Resolution Loss requires multiple stable Domains to jointly destabilize over long durations.

Equation 13: Capacity Equation. Under uniform approximation: Cap(F)=Vol(F)×Res(F); if Resolution distribution within the field is non-uniform: Cap(F)=∫Res(x)dVol.

Equation 14: Capacity Load Equation. Use(F)=UsedDomains(F)/Cap(F)  
Use represents the degree of stable-capacity loading. Use\>1 indicates entry into over-capacity conditions. Use is not a binary variable; Use\>1 does not mean release intensity has been determined.

Equation 15: Over-Capacity Release Equation. If Use(F)\>1, new differentials cannot be stably placed, and the field enters load-release conditions.  
Actual release mode is jointly constrained by: degree of overrun, field state, structure distribution, stable bundle-boundary, and local capacity structure. May manifest as: Compression, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, Resolution Loss, or their combination.

Equation 16: Closure Formation Equation. Long-term mutual-return continuation holds, and stable continuation structure can repeatedly return: Cl(ℒ) holds.  
Structural Bias may participate in Closure; Structural Bias does not require Closure; Structural Bias does not automatically lead to Closure. Familiar Routes may participate in Closure; Familiar Routes do not require Closure; Closure does not require Familiar Routes either.

Equation 17: Deviation Trajectory Equation. A stable Deviation Trajectory forms a new route; the new route holds over long durations and can stably constrain subsequent continuation: Structural Rationale has its structural basis. If the new route cannot hold over long durations, or cannot stably constrain subsequent continuation, it does not constitute the structural basis for Structural Rationale.

Equation 18: Boundary Stability Equation. Strengthened Bind(∂𝒟) weakens retrieval dispersion and strengthens Domain boundary stability. Weakened Bind(∂𝒟) allows retrieval to disperse more easily and Domain boundaries to destabilize more easily.

Equation 19: Tuning Rearrangement Equation. Tune(x) changes current preferential continuation order but does not delete τ(x), δ(x), or Ω(x). Tuning changes current flow-priority order; it does not change the fact that history has already occurred.

## Section 8 Foundational Laws

Meta-note: The Distinction between Laws and Theorems (and their relation to Axioms)  
Axiom: a foundational assumption about field behavior accepted without proof.  
Law: a first-order regularity about field structural behavior, directly stated from axioms, requiring no proof steps. Axioms describe “when it occurs”; Laws describe the regularity of “why it is so.” Laws are not repetitions of axioms; they restate axioms at the level of regularity.  
Theorem: a conclusion derived jointly from definitions, constraints, axioms, and laws; requires proof.

Law 1: Trace-Effect Priority Law. Trace-Effects precede Causal-Response Structuring. Causal-Response Structuring is not pre-existing logic prior to Trace-Effects; Causal-Response Structuring is the recognition-name of stable Trace-Effects within Information-Transformation.

Law 2: Groove-Line Formation Law. Trace-Effects form Groove-Lines; when Trace-Effects repeat over long durations and the continuation structure stably recurs, the Groove-Line becomes a route. Repetition itself does not equal a route.

Law 3: Flow-Hollow Confluence Law. Multiple Groove-Lines converging, merging, arresting, or redirecting form a Flow-Hollow. Flow-Hollows are confluence structures, not abstract nodes.

Law 4: Line-Bundle Domain Formation Law. A segment of a Line-Bundle that can stably maintain: Retentive Containment, differentiation, differential accumulation, continuation, and bundle-boundary, may form a Domain. Any arbitrary segment of a Line-Bundle does not necessarily form a Domain.

Law 5: Deep-First Law. Outer Chapter 3: “Such is the nature of flowing water. Therefore what is deep first receives the flow, and commonality first emerges.” This chapter formalizes this law with the criterion “first satisfies the outflow condition”: under C₌, the deeper satisfies the outflow condition no later than the shallower (Theorem 17(a)); under C₌ and P₁₇, the deeper satisfies it strictly first (Theorem 17(b)). Sedimentation-Degree, Threshold, and Continuation Structure jointly constitute the convergence condition κ; κ is a derived quantity, and under C₌ κ does not reverse the δ-order, making no separate judgment of priority.  
What changes flow direction other than depth has other sources, for example: selective bias (Inner Chapter 15 “to select is to bias its flow”), Tuning (Definition 26), and blocked passage (Inner Chapter 15 “when it meets obstruction, it detours”). Where comparison conditions differ (e.g., different entries, Thresholds differing for other reasons), they must be stated and judged separately.  
Note: v2.6 conditionalized this law as “depth alone does not determine priority; κ mediation is required.” v2.7 restores the original direction and gives the conditions for strict priority in Theorem 17; κ becomes a derived quantity.

Law 6: Stable Recurrence Law. Long-term repeated continuation, with stable Groove-Line or Line-Bundle structure, and continuation structure capable of persistently recurring along the same class of path, forms Stable Recurrence Capacity. Repetition does not automatically form Ω.

Law 7: Familiar Route Law. The deep is the familiar, and the familiar emerges first (Outer Chapter 3: “Therefore the deep may be called the familiar. Hence the common saying ‘the familiar emerges first’ means precisely that the deep emerges first”). A Familiar Route does not presuppose Ω; Ω≠Familiar Route. Familiar Routes can shallow; Familiar Route shallowing does not mean the Familiar Route never existed.

Law 8: Stable Co-Recurrence Core Formation Law. Long-term co-Trace-Effects with subsequent stable co-recurrence form a Co-Nucleus. Co-occurrence≠Co-Nucleus; co-continuation≠Co-Nucleus; stable co-recurrence must also hold jointly with long-term co-Trace-Effects to form a Co-Nucleus.

Law 9: Co-Nucleus Preferential Continuation Law. A Co-Nucleus is where commonality accumulates. Let t₀ be the starting time and t the comparison time. If: (i) initially the Co-Nucleus is no shallower than each edge, δ\_core(t₀)≥δ\_edge(t₀); (ii) superposition premise P\_sup: for every compared edge-domain e, at every update step s from t₀ up to before t (s=t₀,…,t−1), the net deepening of the Co-Nucleus is no less than that of e, i.e., Dτ\_core(s)−Eλ\_core(s) ≥ Dτ\_e(s)−Eλ\_e(s); and for every e there is at least one step with strict inequality (P\_sup is stated in terms of deepening action and shallowing, not by adding input amounts in place of adding sediment); (iii) no saturation: within \[t₀,t\] the Co-Nucleus does not reach its bottom limit (δ\_core\<δmax), so every deepening action enters depth—then by Equation 2, δ(t)=δ(t₀)+Σ\_{s=t₀}^{t−1}(Dτ(s)−Eλ(s)); comparing term by term gives δ\_core(t)\>δ\_e(t) for every e, and the Co-Nucleus is deeper than each edge. Outer Chapter 3 (depth arises from the overlapping of domains; “the Sedimentation-Degree of fa is deeper than that of s1 and s2”) gives the textual direction for (ii). Under these conditions together with C₌ and P₁₇, the Co-Nucleus satisfies the outflow condition first (Outer Chapter 3: “Therefore what is deep first receives the flow, and commonality first emerges”). Where these conditions fail or comparison conditions differ, a Co-Nucleus need not emerge first.

Law 10: Induction Recognition Law. Continuation directed toward Co-Nuclei is recognized within Information-Transformation as induction. Induction is not pre-existing logic within the field; induction is the recognition-name of the core-converging structure within Information-Transformation.

Supplementary Note: Two Structural Sources of Induction and Deduction  
The core-boundary structure of induction and deduction may arise from two field mechanisms:  
First: after long-term co-Trace-Effects form a Co-Nucleus, the Co-Nucleus and its boundary-domains form a core-boundary relation, producing induction (boundary-toward-core) and deduction (core-toward-boundary).  
Second: after Domain Fullness, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth occurs, generating new Structural Positions; these new Structural Positions and the original Domain core form a core-boundary relation, also producing induction and deduction.  
The two sources differ at the structural level but are both recognized as induction and deduction at the Information-Transformation recognition level.  
Outer Chapter 3 (Number Theory) takes the second source as its primary expository path; this chapter takes the first source (Co-Nucleus) as its primary definitional path, and is compatible with the second.

Law 11: Deduction Recognition Law. Continuation directed from Co-Nuclei toward boundary-domains is recognized within Information-Transformation as deduction. Deduction is not pre-existing logic within the field; deduction is the recognition-name of the core-expanding structure within Information-Transformation.

Law 12: Causal-Response Structuring Recognition Law. After stable Trace-Effects are recognized, Causal-Response Structuring forms. Causal-Response Structuring follows Trace-Effects; Causal-Response Structuring is not the cause of Trace-Effects.

Law 13: Law Recognition Law. After stable Closures are recognized, Law forms. Law is not a pre-existing rule; Law is the recognition-name of stable Closures within Information-Transformation.

Law 14: Structural Rationale Recognition Law. When a stable Deviation Trajectory forms a new route, the new route holds over long durations, and it can stably constrain subsequent continuation, it is recognized within Information-Transformation as Structural Rationale. Structural Rationale is not the Deviation Trajectory itself; it is the Information-Transformation recognition after the new route has held over long durations and can constrain subsequent continuation.

Law 15: Structural Bias Future-Continuation Pressure Law. Once Structural Bias holds, the already-formed biased continuation persistently biases future continuation. Structural Bias may shallow; it does not require permanent stability or Familiar Routes. Familiar Routes may participate in Structural Bias; Familiar Routes do not require Structural Bias.

Law 16: Permeation and Sedimentary Saturation Distinction Law. Those that can continue deepening undergo Permeation; those at the limit undergo Sedimentary Saturation. Permeation means still capable of internal sinking; Sedimentary Saturation means current internal-sinking limit has been reached. Sedimentary Saturation does not equal Domain Fullness.

Law 17: Domain Fullness Boundary Saturation Outgrowth Law. After Domain Fullness, when new Trace-Effects continue to enter and cannot continue internal sinking, new Structural Positions are generated along the boundary. Boundary Saturation Outgrowth is not random diffusion; Boundary Saturation Outgrowth requires Domain Fullness to hold, new Trace-Effects continuing to enter, and inability to continue internal sinking.

Law 18: Layered History Law. New Trace-Effects do not overwrite old Trace-Effects. New structures only accumulate, bias, and reorganize; they do not restore already-occurred history to having not occurred. History may shallow; history may weaken; history cannot un-occur.

Law 19: Tuning Does Not Alter History Law. Tuning does not delete historical Trace-Effects. Tune changes current preferential continuation order; it does not change the fact that history has already occurred, and does not delete τ, δ, or Ω.

Law 20: Finite Capacity Law. Field Capacity is finite; stable differential Domains cannot be placed without limit. Finite Cap means the ability to form stable structures has a stable capacity boundary.

Law 21: Over-Capacity Release Law. If capacity is exceeded over long durations, the field enters load-release conditions, which may manifest as Compression, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, Resolution Loss, or their combination. Use\>1 does not determine release intensity. Use=1.01 and Use=100 are not at the same level of overrun. Release path is jointly constrained by field state.

## Section 9 Foundational Theorems

All theorems below are derived jointly from definitions, constraints, axioms, and laws; each is accompanied by a proof.

Theorem 1: Route Formation Theorem. Trace-Effects form Groove-Lines; a Groove-Line with long-term repetition and stable recurrence of the continuation structure becomes a route (stable continuation structure).  
Proof: By Axiom 3, Trace-Effects form Groove-Lines between Structural Positions. Mere repeated Trace-Effects, if recurrence cannot stabilize (Constraint 3), leave the Groove-Line in place but do not form a route. Only when repeated Trace-Effects persistently recur along similar Groove-Lines with cross-time structural stability does the Groove-Line become a stable continuation structure. A route is therefore not repetition itself; it is the stable continuation structure formed by a Groove-Line within stable recurrence. ∎

Theorem 2: Familiar Route Theorem. Suppose route a undergoes long-term repeated continuation within W, its actual passages reach the habitual-travel criterion, and deepening outweighs shallowing without reaching the bottom limit (Dτ\>Eλ, δ\<δmax); then the Sedimentation-Degree of a increases net and is positive, and a is a Familiar Route. If a is more familiar than a comparable route b (δ(a)\>δ(b)), then under C₌ a satisfies the outflow condition no later than b, and under C₌ and P₁₇ strictly first.  
Proof: By Equation 2, Dτ\>Eλ implies a net increase of δ, so δ(a)\>0; δ\<δmax ensures deepening can continue (Equation 8, Y=1). The habitual-travel record of a reaches the criterion, so by Definition 13 a is a Familiar Route. If δ(a)\>δ(b), the last two clauses follow from Theorem 17(a) and (b). The holding of a Familiar Route rests on existing sedimentation and habitual travel, not on Ω; the structural stability and persistent recurrence in every sub-interval required by Ω are not conditions of the Familiar Route. ∎

Theorem 3: Commonality Enters Information-Transformation First Theorem. Under the conditions of Law 9, C₌, P₁₇, and the entry premise P\_entry, the Co-Nucleus enters the Information-Transformation action chain before local detail; if the order-preservation premise P\_order is also assumed, the Co-Nucleus is recognized no later than local detail; if instead the strict order-preservation premise P\_order⁺ is assumed, the Co-Nucleus is recognized strictly before local detail.  
Entry premise P\_entry: a structure can be recognized only after entering the Information-Transformation action chain (Definition 37); a structure that first satisfies the outflow condition enters that chain first.  
Order-preservation premise P\_order: the Information-Transformation action chain preserves entry order, i.e., a structure entering earlier completes recognition no later than one entering later (simultaneous completion allowed).  
Strict order-preservation premise P\_order⁺: a structure entering earlier completes recognition strictly before one entering later. Equal and finite recognition delays for all structures is one sufficient condition for P\_order⁺: strictly earlier entry plus the same finite delay gives strictly earlier completion.  
Outer Chapter 3 (“This is why the observer first apprehends the whole, and only thereafter enters detail”) is the textual basis for P\_entry together with the order-preservation premises; this chapter declares them as premises and does not prove them.  
Proof: By Law 9, the Co-Nucleus is deeper than each edge. By Theorem 17(b), under C₌ and P₁₇ the Co-Nucleus first satisfies the outflow condition. By P\_entry, the Co-Nucleus first enters the Information-Transformation action chain, proving the first clause. By P\_order, a structure entering earlier completes recognition no later than one entering later, proving the second clause. Under P\_order⁺, a structure entering earlier completes recognition strictly first, proving the third clause. Commonality-priority is the result of deep-first flow at the Co-Nucleus recognized through Information-Transformation; it holds subject to the above conditions and premises. ∎

Theorem 4: Structural Basis of Induction Theorem. Induction is not pre-existing logic within the field; the structural basis of induction is continuation from boundary-domains toward Co-Nuclei.  
Proof: Multiple local structures gradually form Co-Nuclei. Subsequent Pulses continue from boundary-domains toward Co-Nuclei; observers recognize this core-converging structure within Information-Transformation as induction. Induction is therefore not pre-existing logic; it has a sedimentary structural basis.

Theorem 5: Structural Basis of Deduction Theorem. Deduction is not pre-existing logic within the field; the structural basis of deduction is continuation expanding from Co-Nuclei toward boundary-domains.  
Proof: After Co-Nucleus formation, subsequent Pulses can expand from core toward boundary; observers recognize this core-expanding structure within Information-Transformation as deduction. Deduction is therefore not pre-existing logic; it has a sedimentary structural basis.

Theorem 6: Structural Basis of Causal-Response Structuring Theorem. Causal-Response Structuring is not pre-existing logic within the field; the structural basis of Causal-Response Structuring is stable Trace-Effects recognized by observers.  
Proof: Stable Trace-Effects first exist within the field; observers recognize them as repeatedly accompanying, inheriting, and succeeding one another—thus Causal-Response Structuring forms. Causal-Response Structuring therefore follows Trace-Effects.

Theorem 7: Structural Basis of Law Theorem. Law is not a pre-existing rule; the structural basis of Law is stable Continuation Closure.  
Proof: Multiple continuation segments mutually return, inherit, and interlock over long durations; after Closure stabilizes, its structure becomes repeatedly returnable; observers recognize the stable Closure within Information-Transformation as Law. Law is therefore not a pre-existing rule; it has a sedimentary structural basis.

Theorem 8: Structural Basis of Structural Rationale Theorem. Structural Rationale is not a pre-existing idea; the structural basis of Structural Rationale is a stable Deviation Trajectory forming a new route that holds over long durations.  
Proof: Long-term biased continuation persists; old Closure undergoes deviation. If the Deviation Trajectory cannot form a new route, it does not constitute Structural Rationale. If the new route cannot hold over long durations, it does not constitute Structural Rationale. Only when it holds over long durations and can stably constrain subsequent continuation is the structural basis for Structural Rationale established. Structural Rationale is therefore not the Deviation Trajectory itself; it is the Information-Transformation recognition after the new route has held over long durations.

Theorem 9: Permeation-Sedimentary Saturation Distinction Theorem. Whether new Trace-Effects entering the field can continue internal sinking depends on whether the corresponding structure has reached bottom.  
Proof: When the structure has not reached bottom, new Trace-Effects can continue deepening—this is Permeation; when the structure has reached bottom, new Trace-Effects cannot continue internal sinking—this is Sedimentary Saturation. The distinction between Permeation and Sedimentary Saturation therefore lies in whether the sedimentary structure has reached the current internal-sinking limit.

Theorem 10: Domain Fullness Non-Internal-Sinking Theorem. When Full(𝒟)=1, new Trace-Effects in that Domain cannot continue internal sinking; the reason is not Shallowing but that the Domain as a whole has reached the simultaneous satisfaction of three limits: no further deepening, no further differentiation, and no further admittable resolution.  
Proof: Shallowing means Sedimentation-Degree declining or convergence capacity weakening—a dynamic process; Sedimentary Saturation (Q=1) means a local structure has reached the internal-sinking limit—a local judgment; Domain Fullness (Full=1) requires all three limits to hold simultaneously, covering the Domain as a whole. The three are different in nature and cause. The inability to sink internally caused by Full=1 cannot be attributed to Shallowing, nor equated with Sedimentary Saturation. Domain Fullness therefore has its own independent reason for non-internal-sinking.

Theorem 11: Boundary Saturation Outgrowth Generation Theorem. After Domain Fullness, new Trace-Effects generate new Structural Positions.  
Proof: Domain Fullness means stable formation of new differentials internally is no longer possible; if new Trace-Effects continue to enter and cannot continue internal sinking, they can only overflow along the boundary; long-term boundary-overflow forms new Structural Positions; long-term stability forms New Structures. Boundary Saturation Outgrowth can therefore generate New Structures.

Theorem 12: Finite Capacity Theorem. A Sedimentation-Field cannot infinitely and stably accommodate differential Domains.  
Proof: Field volume is finite; Resolution per unit volume is finite; therefore Cap(F) is finite. If the count of new Domains continues to increase beyond stable accommodation conditions, new differentials cannot be stably placed. Stable capacity is therefore finite.

Theorem 13: Over-Capacity Release Theorem. After capacity is exceeded over long durations, the field enters load-release conditions.  
Proof: Use(F)=UsedDomains(F)/Cap(F); if Use\>1, stable differential pressure exceeds stable capacity, and the field enters load-release conditions. Release path may manifest as Compression, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, Resolution Loss, or their combination. Use\>1 indicates entry into release conditions; it does not mean release intensity has been determined. Use=1.01 and Use=100 are not at the same level. Over-capacity therefore leads to entry into release conditions.

Theorem 14: Resolution Loss Distinction Theorem. Resolution Loss is not forgetting, not Shallowing, not Compression.  
Proof: Forgetting means old Trace-Effects no longer receive priority flow; Shallowing means Sedimentation-Degree or convergence capacity weakening; Compression means structural merging; Resolution Loss means stable differential capacity can no longer be maintained. Forgetting, Shallowing, and Compression do not automatically entail Resolution Loss. Resolution Loss must therefore be independently defined.

Theorem 15: Historical Irreversibility Theorem. After effective Trace-Effects form, under the condition of not deleting historical Trace-Effects, the Sedimentation-Field cannot fully return to its state prior to those Trace-Effects.  
Proof: Effective Trace-Effects alter τ, δ, θ, κ, Ω, and structural boundaries. Even subsequent Shallowing only enters a new shallowed state; it does not restore the pre-occurrence state. History therefore cannot be fully reversed.

Theorem 16: Tuning-Retrieval Theorem. Retrieval is not static reading; the structural basis of retrieval is the target Domain regaining priority flow after Tuning.  
Proof: Tune does not delete historical Trace-Effects; it only changes current preferential continuation order. Once the target Domain receives higher flow priority, old Trace-Effects re-enter continuation. Retrieval is therefore not reading but renewed flow acquisition.

Theorem 17: Deep-First Condition Theorem (discrete version). Suppose x₁ and x₂ satisfy C₌ (Equation 4: only these two routes are compared; they share the entry flow pressure p, which is non-decreasing step by step before either opens; when unblocked, a route opens if and only if p reaches its Threshold), and θ(x₁)\<θ(x₂) (which holds when δ(x₁)\>δ(x₂), by Equation 3a). Let τ(xᵢ) be the first step at which p reaches θ(xᵢ), recorded as +∞ if never reached. This theorem concerns only the first set to open, not what follows.  
(a) Weak order: τ(x₁)≤τ(x₂).  
(b) Strict priority: suppose p reaches θ(x₁) within the declared time window, and let t₁=τ(x₁). The first set to open is {x₁} if and only if p(t₁)\<θ(x₂). This condition is denoted P₁₇.  
Proof: (a) If τ(x₂)=+∞, the claim is immediate. Otherwise p(τ(x₂))≥θ(x₂)\>θ(x₁), so p has reached θ(x₁) at or before τ(x₂), i.e., τ(x₁)≤τ(x₂). (b) Before t₁, p\<θ(x₁)\<θ(x₂), so neither opens; at t₁, x₁ opens; x₂ opens simultaneously if and only if p(t₁)≥θ(x₂). ∎  
Sufficient condition for P₁₇: initial pressure p(0)\<θ(x₁), and each step increment smaller than the threshold gap θ(x₂)−θ(x₁); then p(t₁)\<θ(x₁)+(θ(x₂)−θ(x₁))=θ(x₂). This condition is sufficient, not necessary.  
Continuous version: under C₌, if p is continuous, p(0)\<θ(x₁), strictly increasing before opening, and reaches θ(x₁) within the time window, the first set to open is {x₁}; neither the low initial pressure nor the reaching condition can be omitted.  
Boundaries: (1) Initial pressure already at θ(x₂), or one step crossing both Thresholds: P₁₇ fails and strict priority does not hold, but (a) and the necessary-and-sufficient characterization in (b) still apply. (2) Blocked passage, other channels changing p, or Threshold having other determinants: C₌ or θ(x₁)\<θ(x₂) fails, and this theorem does not apply. (3) This theorem does not imply sustained flow, cumulative flow dominance, or long-term deepening.

## Section 11 Breakdown Cases

Counterexample 1: Pulse-free field. Without Pulses: no Trace-Effects, no Groove-Lines, no sedimentation.

Counterexample 2: Trace-Effect-free field. Without Trace-Effects: no Sedimentation-Degree, no Familiar Routes, no sedimentary structures.

Counterexample 3: Stable-recurrence-free field. If Trace-Effects repeat but cannot stably recur, the Groove-Line does not become a route.

Counterexample 4: Groove-Line-free field. Without Groove-Lines: no stable continuation paths.

Counterexample 5: Flow-Hollow-free field. Without Flow-Hollows: multiple Groove-Lines cannot converge, merge, arrest, or redirect.

Counterexample 6: Line-Bundle-free field. Without Line-Bundles: no Domains.

Counterexample 7: Domain-free field. Without Domains: no stable Retentive Containment, stable differentiation, Compression, Permeation, Sedimentary Saturation, Domain Fullness, or Boundary Saturation Outgrowth.

Counterexample 8: Threshold-free field. Without Thresholds: in-out conditions, Permeation conditions, Sedimentary Saturation conditions, and Boundary Saturation Outgrowth conditions for Groove-Lines, Flow-Hollows, Line-Bundles, and Domains cannot be distinguished.

Counterexample 9: Stable-co-recurrence-free field. Without stable co-recurrence, co-occurrence cannot form a Co-Nucleus; the core-boundary relation (first source) loses its basis. If edge-overflow new-core formation (second source) is also absent, induction completely loses its structural basis.

Counterexample 10: Core-boundary-free field. Without core-boundary structures, deduction loses its structural basis.

Counterexample 11: Retentive-Containment-free field. Without Retentive Containment: differentiation cannot accumulate; Structural Bias cannot hold.

Counterexample 12: Input-bias field. If input merely favors a particular structure over long durations without forming structural biased continuation, Structural Bias does not hold.

Counterexample 13: Permeation-free field. Without Permeation: structures cannot continue deepening.

Counterexample 14: Sedimentary Saturation-free field. Without Sedimentary Saturation: structures cannot reach the internal-sinking limit; Boundary Saturation Outgrowth conditions have no basis for arising.

Counterexample 15: Domain-Fullness-free field. Without Domain Fullness: Boundary Saturation Outgrowth lacks its necessary condition.

Counterexample 16: Boundary Saturation Outgrowth-free field. If Domain Fullness occurs but Boundary Saturation Outgrowth cannot, New Structures cannot be generated.

Counterexample 17: Closure-free field. Without stable Closures: Law loses its structural basis.

Counterexample 18: Long-term-stable-new-route-free field. If Deviation Trajectories cannot form long-term stable new routes: Structural Rationale loses its structural basis.

Counterexample 19: Field-Quality-free field. If old Trace-Effects never shallow: Domain Fullness eventually occurs; stable differentiability disappears.

Counterexample 20: Boundary-Binding-free field. Without Boundary-Binding: retrieval disperses infinitely outward; Domains cannot stably return.

Counterexample 21: Capacity-free field. Without acknowledging capacity limits: the occurrence of Compression, Domain Fullness, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, and Resolution Loss cannot be explained.

Counterexample 22: Resolution-Loss-undifferentiated field. If Resolution Loss is not distinguished from forgetting, Shallowing, and Compression: decline in stable differential capacity cannot be explained.

Counterexample 23: Causal-Response-first field. If Causal-Response Structuring is treated as pre-existing logic: the generation of Causal-Response Structuring itself cannot be explained.

Counterexample 24: Logic-first field. If induction, deduction, Law, and Structural Rationale are treated as pre-existing logic within the field: their dependence on sedimentary structure formation cannot be explained.

Counterexample 25: Structural-Bias-free field. If Retentive Containment does not hold, or differentiation cannot accumulate, or unequal continuation has not formed structural skew (condition 4a not satisfied), or the skew has not persistently biased later flow over a long time window (condition 4b not satisfied): Structural Bias does not hold; already-formed structures cannot persistently bias future continuation.

Counterexample 26: Tuning-free field. Without Tuning: flow-priority cannot be switched, old Trace-Effects cannot regain priority flow, retrieval loses its structural basis, and the switching of memory layers also loses its mechanism.

Counterexample 27: Shallowing-free field. If Field Quality fails and δ only increases without shallowing: old Trace-Effects accumulate without limit, differential structures are persistently displaced by new Trace-Effects, the field eventually loses differentiability, and New Structures cannot be stably placed.

## Section 14 Final Compression

After Pulses enter the Sedimentation-Field, Trace-Effects form. Trace-Effects form grooves between Structural Positions, called Groove-Lines; after long-term repeated Trace-Effects with stable recurrence of continuation structure, a Groove-Line becomes a route. Groove-Lines converge to form Flow-Hollows. Multiple Groove-Lines sustaining long-term co-continuation form Line-Bundles. A segment of a Line-Bundle that can stably maintain Retentive Containment, differentiation, differential accumulation, continuation, and bundle-boundary becomes a Domain. Groove-Lines and Flow-Hollows have depth, named Sedimentation-Degree; as Trace-Effects repeatedly overlap within a Domain, its depth increases when deepening outweighs shallowing. Sedimentation-Degree, Threshold, and Continuation Structure jointly constitute the convergence condition κ. The deeper receives flow first (Outer Chapter 3): under equal comparison conditions the deeper satisfies the outflow condition no later than the shallower, and strictly first when the conditions of Theorem 17 hold. Long-term repeated continuation of Pulses with stable continuation structure forms Stable Recurrence Capacity (Ω). The deeper receives flow first, and signals favor the paths long familiar: these are Familiar Routes.

Tuning does not alter history. Tune changes current preferential continuation order, and does not delete τ, δ, or Ω. Trace-Effects that have already occurred are not eliminated by Tuning; they are only weakened by Field Quality shallowing.

Long-term co-Trace-Effects with persistently stable co-recurrence form Co-Nuclei. Stable co-recurrence alone does not constitute a Co-Nucleus; long-term co-Trace-Effects alone do not constitute a Co-Nucleus; both must hold simultaneously for Co-Nucleus formation. Structural Bias may form before a Co-Nucleus; Structural Bias does not require a Co-Nucleus; Structural Bias arises from already-formed structures persistently biasing future continuation after long-term differential accumulation.

After Co-Nuclei form, core-boundary structures emerge. Continuation directed toward Co-Nuclei is recognized within Information-Transformation as induction. Continuation directed from Co-Nuclei toward boundary-domains is recognized within Information-Transformation as deduction. After stable Trace-Effects are recognized, Causal-Response Structuring forms. After stable Closures are recognized, Law forms. When stable Deviation Trajectories form new routes, those new routes hold over long durations and can stably constrain subsequent continuation; after recognition within Information-Transformation, Structural Rationale forms.

Those that can continue deepening undergo Permeation. Those at the limit undergo Sedimentary Saturation. Sedimentary Saturation does not equal Domain Fullness. When a Domain can no longer deepen, differentiate, or admit resolution, Domain Fullness holds. After Domain Fullness, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth occurs, generating new Structural Positions; new Structural Positions stable over long durations form New Structures.

Field Capacity equals the product of Field Volume and Resolution—this is the uniform approximation condition. If Resolution distribution within the field is non-uniform, Cap(F)=∫Res(x)dVol. When Capacity Load exceeds Field Capacity, new differentials cannot be stably placed; the field enters load-release conditions and may release load through Compression, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, or Resolution Loss. However, release intensity and release path are still jointly influenced by degree of overrun, field state, structure distribution, stable bundle-boundary, and local capacity structure.

Therefore: induction, deduction, Causal-Response Structuring, Law, and Structural Rationale are not pre-existing logic within the field. They are only the structural names assigned by observers within Information-Transformation to already-formed sedimentary structures. What Field Mathematics truly studies is not the form of completed logic, but rather: why logic can be generated at all.
