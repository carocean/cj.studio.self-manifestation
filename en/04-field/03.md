# Co-Nucleus Mathematics

## Section 1 Purpose

Domain Mathematics (Field Chapter 2 v1.5) has established how localized stable continuation structures form Domains within the Sedimentation-Field, and how Domains sustain stability, locking, and synchronization through dynamics such as Lock, Sync, and MaintainFlow.

Yet Domains alone remain insufficient to explain why different Domains gradually produce:

• commonality  
• classification  
• abstraction  
• associative centers  
• conceptual structures  
• semantic convergence  
• generalization

Therefore:

this chapter no longer examines isolated Domain stability.

Instead, it turns toward:

shared convergence structures produced through long-term Domain overlap.

That is:

Co-Nuclei.

A Co-Nucleus is not a linguistic definition. Not a subjective categorization imposed by an observer. Not an abstract label within formal logic.

A Co-Nucleus is: the stable shared continuation core formed within the Sedimentation-Field through prolonged co-continuation among multiple Domains.

Only afterward does the observer recognize such structures within Information-Transformation as: concepts, commonality, categories, and centers of meaning.

Therefore:

the true question of this chapter is not: how concepts are named. But rather: why concepts can emerge at all.

This chapter further examines:

• how Co-Nuclei form  
• how Co-Nuclei become locked  
• how generalization emerges  
• how semantic synchronization forms  
• why concepts drift  
• why abstraction emerges  
• how Recognition-Realms produce conceptual layers

Thereby establishing: concept dynamics within the Sedimentation-Field.

Note: The correspondence this chapter draws between Co-Nucleus dynamics and concepts, commonality, categories, abstraction, meaning, induction, and deduction is an interpretive correspondence (a claim of real-world applicability). It requires empirical verification and is not derived from the equations of this chapter.

Note: The term “Co-Nucleus” (Core(C)) used in this chapter is a dynamic elaboration of Core(x₁,x₂,…) as defined in Field Chapter 1 Definition 15. Field Chapter 1 established the generative conditions for Co-Nuclei (multiple Structural Positions sustaining long-term co-Trace-Effects and stable co-recurrence); this chapter proceeds on that basis, using C to denote an already-established Co-Nucleus object, and further examines the internal dynamics, stability, generalization, and drift of Co-Nuclei. Proposition 1 (Co-Nucleus Sedimentation-Degree and Overlap-Zone Layering): Let δ\_core,0 ≥ 0, HistLayer\_0(C) ≥ 0, and for all t, τ^C\_t ≥ 0, 0 ≤ DecayRate\_t(C) ≤ 1, 0 ≤ ShallowRate\_t ≤ 1. (a) If for all t, c\_D × OverlapDensity\_t × InitialSync\_t ≤ 1 and ShallowRate\_t ≥ DecayRate\_t(C), and δ\_core,0 ≤ HistLayer\_0(C), then for all t, 0 ≤ δ\_core,t ≤ HistLayer\_t(C). (b) If for all t, OverlapDensity\_t × InitialSync\_t = k (a constant) and ShallowRate\_t = DecayRate\_t(C), and δ\_core,0 = c\_D × k × HistLayer\_0(C), then for all t, δ\_core,t = c\_D × k × HistLayer\_t(C).

Proof: In both cases the truncation of HistLayer is inactive (DecayRate ≤ 1, τ^C ≥ 0), so HistLayer\_{t+1}(C) = (1 − DecayRate\_t(C)) × HistLayer\_t(C) + τ^C\_t; and δ\_core,t+1 = (1 − ShallowRate\_t) × δ\_core,t + c\_D × OverlapDensity\_t × InitialSync\_t × τ^C\_t ≥ 0. (a) By induction on t: suppose 0 ≤ δ\_core,t ≤ HistLayer\_t(C). Since ShallowRate\_t ≥ DecayRate\_t(C) and δ\_core,t ≥ 0, (1 − ShallowRate\_t) × δ\_core,t ≤ (1 − DecayRate\_t(C)) × δ\_core,t ≤ (1 − DecayRate\_t(C)) × HistLayer\_t(C); since c\_D × OverlapDensity\_t × InitialSync\_t ≤ 1, the deepening term is ≤ τ^C\_t. Adding gives δ\_core,t+1 ≤ HistLayer\_{t+1}(C). (b) By induction on t: if δ\_core,t = c\_D × k × HistLayer\_t(C), then δ\_core,t+1 = (1 − DecayRate\_t(C)) × c\_D × k × HistLayer\_t(C) + c\_D × k × τ^C\_t = c\_D × k × HistLayer\_{t+1}(C). ∎
