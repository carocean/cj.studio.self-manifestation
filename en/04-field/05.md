# On Sedimentation Irreversibility

1\. Purpose

The preceding four chapters have established:

\- Field Chapter 1: how Pulses enter the field, how Trace-Effects form, how sedimentation structures (Groove-Lines, Domains, Familiar Routes, Co-Nuclei, Closures) arise;  
\- Field Chapter 2: how the Domain network synchronizes and how the Recognition-Realm forms;  
\- Field Chapter 3: how Co-Nuclei stabilize and form consensus structure;  
\- Field Chapter 4: how the coordination level of structure, once formed, continues to reinforce, decay, migrate, and exchange places.

But one question has not yet been established:

Why history does not exit the field.

If historical influence had fully dissipated before each subsequent action, with no continued replenishment (the extreme case of Section 3), then:

\- all Continuation Bias would reset to zero;  
\- all structures would become equivalent again;  
\- all reality would scatter again;  
\- all present moments would drift again.

Thus:

\- Familiar Routes cannot form;  
\- Dominant Continuation cannot stabilize;  
\- the world cannot persist over time.

Therefore:

What this chapter asks is not that “long-term structure necessarily makes the field lose the ability to dissipate bias,” but three things: under what conditions historical bias persists; how the cost of dissipating bias changes; and when, within a declared action set and window, bias cannot be dissipated.

Maintaining bias and the ability to dissipate it are two different matters: a system can maintain bias over the long term by compensating shallowing through continued re-traversal while still retaining the ability to dissipate it; and the effects of individual historical actions fading over time does not exclude later inputs continually replenishing and maintaining the overall structure.

Note: v1.1 wrote “Any field capable of forming long-term structure must gradually lose the ability to fully dissipate Continuation Bias”; this version withdraws that: Theorem 1 proves persistence only within a declared window, and Theorem 5 treats internal unreachability only under additional conditions; neither implies this general conclusion.

1\. Sedimentation

Sedimentation is not the preservation of the past.

Sedimentation is: after history enters the field, the field itself has already been rewritten by history.

Therefore: subsequent continuation no longer faces the field as it was before. It unfolds within a field that has been persistently altered by history.

Therefore: history is not an appended record. History itself has entered field structure.

The field is not a container. The field itself is a process of persistent historicization.

Therefore: the rules, regularities, Familiar Routes, and causal stability found within the field may themselves be the result of long-term sedimentation.

Regularity is not absolutely static. Regularity itself may be a stable structure formed after history has compressed the future.

1\. Continuation Bias

Suppose a field in the following extreme case: before each subsequent action begins, all historical influence relevant to that continuation has already fully disappeared, and there is no continued replenishment or other historical constraint.

Then: no history H influences the direction of future continuation—i.e., historical change produces no persistent effect on the future.

Then: every continuation event is equivalent to entering the field for the first time.

Then:

\- the field cannot form long-term Dominant Continuation;  
\- the field cannot form reality lock-in;  
\- the field cannot form a stable world.

Therefore: in this extreme case, the world cannot form.

Note: “all Continuation Bias ultimately disappears” alone does not imply “every continuation event is equivalent to entering the field for the first time”: ultimate disappearance does not mean that the previous influence has already disappeared when the next continuation occurs, and later inputs may continually replenish and maintain the overall bias (as stated in the opening). This section therefore treats only the extreme case above; the general case rests on the window persistence of Theorem 1 and the relative unreachability of Theorem 5. v1.1 inferred “every time is the first time” from “ultimately disappears”; this version narrows that.

Note: this section argues by contrapositive for the necessity of Continuation Bias persistence. “History does not affect the future” is an extreme assumption. In Field Chapter 1 v2.7, Sedimentation-Degree δ, under the comparison conditions, makes the deeper receive flow no later than the shallower (Law 5, Theorem 17), and history thereby influences the future; κ is a derived entry condition (Definition 11) and makes no separate judgment of precedence. This section restates at the whole-field level: in the extreme case above, long-term Dominant Continuation could not form.

If structure has already formed within the field, then: the direction of expansion, mode of convergence, and continuation tendency of subsequent continuation have necessarily already been influenced by prior traces.

Thus: Continuation Bias begins to be persistently retained.

Therefore: once structure forms, the field can no longer fully return to its state before the Trace-Effects (Field Chapter 1, Theorem 15).

Therefore:

Long-term stable structure → Continuation Bias Persistence

The more stable the structure, the harder it becomes for Continuation Bias to dissipate. (Interpretation; formally, the difficulty of dissipation is read by the Field-Wide Bias Dissipation Cost Cd, whose change over time is treated in Theorem 2.)

1\. Depth

Depth is neither the number of path traversals nor the capacity for Continuation Bias to persist.

Depth is Sedimentation-Degree: the degree of sedimentation of a Structural Position, Groove-Line, Terminal Ravine, Line-Bundle, or Domain, borne by the hosting mechanism (Field Chapter 1, Definition 9). Inner Chapter 6: “Sedimentability is: acting upon it, it can respond with a trace; repeated, the trace deepens; seldom used, the trace fades.” Repeated continuation is the source of deepening (Dτ in Field Chapter 1, Equation 2); but depth is not the count: for the same count, the net of deepening and shallowing (Dτ − Eλ) can differ.

Whether Continuation Bias can persist is one of depth’s effects: under the comparison conditions the deeper receives the flow first (Field Chapter 1, Law 5), and the sedimentation difference thereby takes part in subsequent continuation (Formula Chapter 20, B). If a path is traversed repeatedly but its net deepening does not exceed shallowing (Dτ ≤ Eλ), its Sedimentation-Degree does not increase net; if the sedimentation difference cannot take part in subsequent continuation (for example, the channel is blocked; Inner Chapter 15, “meeting obstruction, it goes around”), Continuation Bias does not hold. These belong respectively to depth and to bias; neither defines the other.

Depth is first of all a state of Structural Positions, Groove-Lines, and Terminal Ravines. At the whole-field level there is a separate effect quantity: the Field-Wide Bias Dissipation Cost, below.

Note: v1.1 wrote “Depth is: the capacity for Continuation Bias to persist after history has entered the field,” “depth belongs first to the whole field. A local Deep Trace is only the local expression of the field’s historical inertia along a particular continuation direction,” and “If a path has been traversed repeatedly but Continuation Bias still dissipates easily, no genuine sedimentation forms”; this version withdraws these: the first writes depth’s effect as depth itself, the second inverts the order of priority, and the third conflicts with Inner Chapter 6, “repeated, the trace deepens.”

Definition of the Field-Wide Bias Dissipation Cost (Cd(F)):

Cd(F) is an effect-level cost quantity: the minimum cost required to bring the whole-field Continuation Bias below the threshold for sustaining stable continuation. v1.1 named it “Field Depth” Depth(Field); this version renames it (author’s decision).

The following five items must be declared:

(1) Target reading and post-action test protocol: declare a family of comparison objects K, weights w, the differential threshold ε\_δ and the Structural Bias criterion (Field Chapter 1, Equation 11), and a post-action test protocol P\_test: test input, comparison protocol (e.g., C₌), and test-window length L\_test. Bias\_glob(F′) is defined as: starting from field state F′, applying the test input according to P\_test, the w-weighted proportion of members of K on which Structural Bias holds within a test window of length L\_test. The field state F′ must contain the historical record needed for this determination; alternatively, the reading is given entirely by the post-action test under P\_test and does not borrow the window before the action. K, w, ε\_δ, and P\_test are fixed in comparison: the target determination may not be changed by stopping or altering the test input, shortening the test window, or changing the comparison objects. If an admissible action changes the field state F′ through shallowing or similar mechanisms (for example, stopping input to some route so that its sedimentation difference shallows through Field Quality), its effect is still tested under the same P\_test, and the reading may fall accordingly; this counts as genuine dissipation. Threshold ε \> 0; target set T\_ε = {F′ : Bias\_glob(F′) \< ε}.

(2) The admissible action set Π(F): action sequences that can be applied starting from field state F. Their source (internal continuation, or external perturbation) and constraints must be declared: the shallowing rate determined by Field Quality λ, the bottom limit of inward sinking δmax, Field Capacity, and no deletion of historical Trace-Effects (Field Chapter 1, Theorem 15). It must further be declared that every admissible action sequence has finite cost.

(3) A cost function c(π) ≥ 0, additive over action sequences; c(π) \< ∞ for every π ∈ Π(F).

(4) Action outcome: π(F) denotes the field state reached by applying π from F.

(5) Action window: actions must be completed within a window \[t, t+L\] of length L. When Cd(F\_t) is compared over time, a subsequent window of the same length L (sliding window) is always used, not mixed with “the remaining window up to a common deadline.”

Then:

Cd(F) = inf{ c(π) : π ∈ Π(F), π(F) ∈ T\_ε }

If no action in Π(F) can bring the field into T\_ε within the window, Cd(F) = +∞ (the infimum of the empty set). Since every admissible action has finite cost, Cd(F) = +∞ if and only if the target is unreachable within the window. Cd is an infimum; it does not guarantee that some action attains this minimum cost.

When Π is restricted to internal continuation, it is written Cd\_int(F).

The greater Cd(F), the harder Continuation Bias is to dissipate. Cd depends on the five declared items; if the declaration changes, Cd changes with it. Cd(F) = +∞ means only that, within the declared admissible action set and window, the whole-field bias cannot be brought below the threshold (relative irreversibility); it does not mean absolute irreversibility.

Note: Cd is structurally analogous to the energy barrier in thermodynamics and the attractor escape cost in dynamical systems, but applies to the continuation field. This is an analogy, not a derivation.

Whether a whole-field Sedimentation-Degree is established: this chapter does not establish one. If a specific field requires a whole-field Sedimentation-Degree reading (δ\_F), its aggregation must be declared separately; it is a state quantity, distinct from the effect quantity Cd, and their relation must be proved separately.

Note: v1.1 wrote “Depth(Field) ∝ inf{ Φ : Bias\_global(F after Φ) \< ε },” without stating the admissible actions, target set, cost, or reachability, and wrote a definition with a proportionality sign; this version replaces it with the declared definition above.

1\. Deepening

The self-reinforcement of Continuation Bias runs through the deeper receiving the flow first, not through bias directly amplifying itself:

The Dominant Continuation route’s Sedimentation-Degree advantage  
→ under the comparison conditions, the deeper receives the flow first (Field Chapter 1, Law 5, Theorem 17)  
→ flow passes and the route is re-traversed  
→ deepening (Dτ in Field Chapter 1, Equation 2; net increase when Dτ \> Eλ)  
→ the Sedimentation-Degree advantage is kept or enlarged (conditions below)

Proposition (Sedimentation-Degree Advantage Enlargement Proposition, conditional): let a be the Dominant Continuation route and b a competing route, with δ(a) ≥ δ(b) at start time t₀. If at every step s in \[t₀, t): (i) a and b satisfy C₌ and P₁₇, and a alone satisfies the outflow condition first; (ii) the net change of a is no less than that of b, i.e., Dτ\_a(s) − Eλ\_a(s) ≥ Dτ\_b(s) − Eλ\_b(s), with strict inequality at at least one step; (iii) neither a nor b has reached its bottom limit; then δ(a,t) − δ(b,t) \> δ(a,t₀) − δ(b,t₀) ≥ 0: the Sedimentation-Degree advantage enlarges.

Proof: by Field Chapter 1, Equation 2, δ(t) = δ(t₀) + Σ\_{s=t₀}^{t−1}(Dτ(s) − Eλ(s)); subtracting the two equations and comparing term by term gives the result. ∎

Condition (ii) does not follow from (i): receiving flow first guarantees only that a satisfies the outflow condition first; it guarantees neither that a’s net deepening exceeds b’s nor that b only recedes. When the conditions fail, the Sedimentation-Degree advantage need not enlarge.

Enlargement of the advantage is not the same as net deepening of the Dominant Continuation itself: for example, a recedes from 4 to 3 and b from 3 to 1; both shallow, yet the advantage enlarges from 1 to 2. For the Dominant Continuation itself to deepen net, a further condition is required: (iv) Dτ\_a(s) \> Eλ\_a(s) at every step, whereupon δ(a,t) \> δ(a,t₀).

The remaining edges of the sedimentation feedback loop lie outside the Proposition above and are marked one by one:

Proved edges: under conditions (i) to (iii), the Sedimentation-Degree advantage enlarges (the Proposition above); adding (iv), the Dominant Continuation itself deepens net.  
Edge premised on a bridge assumption: Sedimentation-Degree advantage does not decrease → the accessible future set does not increase (A\_F, Section 7).  
Feedback conjectures to be proved: the accessible future set does not increase → Accessible-Future Stability rises → Continuation Locking rises → Continuation Bias Persistence rises → the Sedimentation-Degree advantage continues to rise. A smaller candidate set is not the same as stronger resistance to perturbation, nor a higher exit cost; these edges are not proved in this chapter and are listed as conjectures.

where:

Accessible-Future Stability denotes the capacity of the current Dominant Continuation, under historical perturbation, to stably control “which futures can enter the current continuation.”

Continuation Locking denotes the difficulty of the current continuation leaving the Dominant Continuation structure. The stronger the locking, the harder it is for the system to break free of the current reality continuation. As locking strengthens, Reality Continuity is supported (interpretation).

Under conditions (i) to (iv): Deep Traces deepen further. Conditions (i) to (iv) do not imply that shallow traces shallow: b may still deepen net—for example, in the same step a deepens from 3 to 3.3 and b from 2 to 2.2; both deepen, yet the advantage enlarges. For shallow traces to shallow, a further condition (v) is required: Dτ\_b(s) \< Eλ\_b(s) at every step. The “seldom used” of Inner Chapter 6, “seldom used, the trace fades,” is not given by this Proposition and cannot replace condition (v).

Saturation Constraint Note: the bottom limit of inward sinking δmax (Definition 9) and Domain Fullness (Full(𝒟)) of Field Chapter 1 v2.7 constrain sedimentation deepening: once the bottom limit is reached no further inward sinking is possible, and after Domain Fullness new Trace-Effects overflow along the boundary (Equation 10). But δmax may change with field state; the current bottom limit implies neither a uniform upper bound across time nor eventual saturation of the loop. To assert eventual saturation, a uniform-boundedness condition must be set up separately (for example, Assumption L1 of the Limit Appendix of Formula Chapter 20).

Relation to Field Chapter 4: the edges above are at the level of Sedimentation-Degree and receiving flow first. The coordination reinforcement of Familiar Routes in Field Chapter 4 v1.2 (Theorem 5, region R⁺) is a positive feedback at the coordination level; the two are not the same process and may run in parallel; this chapter sets up no correspondence between them. v1.1 said the two “describe the same physical process at different levels”; this version withdraws that.

AF(t) = { continuation f ∈ U : f can enter and sustain the current continuation at time t }

That is: AF(t) is the set of all continuations in the candidate universe U that can extend Dominant Continuation without causing the continuation to break.

The following must be declared and held fixed in comparison: the candidate universe U, the identity of the Dominant Continuation, the criterion for “enter and sustain,” and the other comparison conditions (including the coordination state and other admission conditions). Dominant Continuation Sedimentation-Degree advantage does not decrease  ⟹  AF(t) does not enlarge (set inclusion), and \|AF(t)\|\_m does not increase (under A\_F)

1\. Theorems

Theorem 1: Continuation Bias Persistence Theorem (conditional)

If, within a declared window W, long-term stable structure has formed and its sedimentation difference stays above the declared threshold ε\_δ, then wherever a comparison satisfies C₌, the deeper satisfies the outflow condition no later than the shallower (Field Chapter 1, Law 5, Theorem 17(a)); if Structural Bias (Field Chapter 1, Equation 11) holds within W, Continuation Bias has not fully dissipated within W. Moreover: without deleting historical Trace-Effects, the field cannot fully return to its state before the Trace-Effects (Field Chapter 1, Theorem 15).

Proof sketch: when the sedimentation difference is above ε\_δ, by Field Chapter 1, Equation 3a, among comparable structures of the same kind the deeper has the lower threshold; under C₌, by Theorem 17(a), the deeper satisfies the outflow condition no later than the shallower. Structural Bias holding within W means (4a) and (4b) both hold, so Continuation Bias persists within W. The last sentence is Field Chapter 1, Theorem 15. ∎

Note: v1.1’s proof sketch cited “the existence of δ and κ in Field Chapter 1” and “Field Chapter 4, Familiar-Route Reinforcement Theorem”; this version withdraws these: κ is a derived quantity that makes no separate judgment of precedence, and Theorem 5 of Field Chapter 4 v1.2 is a coordination-level positive feedback that does not imply persistence of Continuation Bias. Moreover, a sedimentation difference can fall below the threshold through shallowing (Inner Chapter 6, “seldom used, the trace fades”), so this theorem takes the persistence of the sedimentation difference as a condition.

Theorem 2: Field-Wide Bias Dissipation Cost Trending-Increase Theorem (conditional)

Let the five declared items of Section 5 be fixed in comparison (including the comparison family K, weights w, the post-action test protocol P\_test, and the sliding action window of length L), and declare a preorder ⪯ on sedimentation states (reflexive and transitive; for example: the Sedimentation-Degree differences of the comparison objects under consideration all do not decrease).

Assumption M\_Cd: Cd is non-decreasing with respect to ⪯, i.e., F ⪯ F′ implies Cd(F) ≤ Cd(F′).

If, within the comparison period, the field state satisfies F\_t ⪯ F\_{t+1} along historical continuation (no wholesale structural breakdown, Dominant-Continuation reversal, or strong external inverse perturbation having occurred), then Cd(F\_t), computed with the sliding window of length L, is monotonically non-decreasing within that period.

Proof sketch: follows step by step from F\_t ⪯ F\_{t+1} and M\_Cd. ∎

Note: ⪯ is taken as a preorder and need not be antisymmetric: the example “the Sedimentation-Degree differences all do not decrease” is not antisymmetric; if a partial order is wanted, it must be built on the equivalence classes. M\_Cd is a model assumption, not derived from the definition. The preorder must match the declaration of Bias\_glob: if Bias\_glob reads sedimentation differences, then all Structural Positions deepening together need not increase Cd. This theorem does not assert strict monotonicity. Traumatic forgetting, neural pruning, ecological collapse, and catastrophic restructuring correspond to Sedimentation Reversal (Section 11) and can make F\_t ⪯ F\_{t+1} fail, so that Cd decreases locally or even globally. v1.1’s proof sketch said that “the infimum of Φ increases overall with this accumulation” without justification; this version takes M\_Cd as a premise.

Theorem 3: Future Compression Theorem (conditional)

Under the conditions that the items listed in Section 7 (candidate universe U, Dominant Continuation identity, admission criterion, other comparison conditions, size reading \|·\|\_m) are fixed within the window, Dominant Continuation is stable, the direction of future compression has not reversed, and bridge assumption A\_F holds: if the Dominant Continuation Sedimentation-Degree advantage does not decrease within the window, then the future set AF(t) that the current Dominant Continuation permits to enter the current continuation does not enlarge within the window, and its size \|AF(t)\|\_m does not increase:

Dominant Continuation Sedimentation-Degree advantage does not decrease  ⟹  AF(t) does not enlarge, \|AF(t)\|\_m does not increase

Note: this theorem is limited to “the future range that the current Dominant Continuation permits to enter the current continuation,” not the total reachable space of all continuations in the field. Learning generalization, capability growth, and neural expansion can enlarge the field’s overall reachable space; this corresponds to the generation of new structure (Field Chapter 1, Equation 10) or Dominant-Continuation reversal (Section 11), not to the AF(t) contraction described in this theorem. If Dominant Continuation reverses, AF(t) may expand, but at that point the original current continuation has already broken and is outside this theorem’s domain. v1.1 said this “corresponds to Field Chapter 4’s Restructuring Capacity (R) or Field Phase Transition”; Field Chapter 4 has no Restructuring Capacity R, and this version corrects the reference.

Proof sketch: let t₁ ≤ t₂ in the window with advantage d(t₂) ≥ d(t₁). By A\_F, AF(d(t₂)) ⊆ AF(d(t₁)); since \|·\|\_m is monotone with respect to inclusion, \|AF(t₂)\|\_m ≤ \|AF(t₁)\|\_m. ∎

Note: v1.1’s proof sketch cited “the higher Ω\_c, the stronger the tendency for subsequent continuation to recur along Familiar Routes (Field Chapter 4 Theorem 1)”; this version withdraws that: Theorem 1 of Field Chapter 4 v1.2 concerns only coordination cost under the same reference conditions and does not imply a recurrence tendency or receiving flow first.

Theorem 4: Reality Dominance Theorem (defining requirement)

In the sedimentation field, the stable sustenance of the same current continuation requires a dominant Dominant Continuation to exist—a historical structure with persistent dominant capacity over the compression direction of AF(t). This dominance need not be absolutely exclusive, but requires that no equal competitor durably undermines its dominant position during the current period.

Note: this theorem is this chapter’s defining requirement for Reality Continuity: Section 9 defines Reality Continuity as “the capacity of the same Dominant Continuation to persistently maintain present unity through time,” and if no dominant Dominant Continuation exists, this definition cannot be satisfied. It is not a conclusion derived from Field Chapter 1. This theorem weakens “unique controller” to “dominant controller,” accommodating multi-attractor, parallel-module, and brain-competition phenomena. If two Deep Traces are long-term evenly matched with no dominant one emerging, this corresponds to Sedimentation Reversal/present fragmentation (Section 11), not stable coexistence of two Dominant Continuations.

Note: v1.1’s proof sketch said that “the Priority Continuation Equation (Field Chapter 1) requires the current state to uniquely determine the highest-tendency subsequent continuation”; this citation is inaccurate, and this version withdraws it: Field Chapter 1, Equation 4, gives only that, under the comparison conditions C₌, the deeper satisfies the outflow condition no later than the shallower, with strict precedence additionally requiring P₁₇; it does not require the current state to uniquely determine subsequent continuation.

Theorem 5: Sedimentation Irreversibility Theorem (conditional)

Two action sets are distinguished: the reference action set Π\_int(F), i.e., all internal continuation sequences that can be applied (each of finite cost, but with no upper limit); and the budget-feasible action set Π\_int^B(F) = { π ∈ Π\_int(F) : c(π) ≤ B\_int }, where B\_int \< ∞ is a declared internal budget. Cd\_int(F) is computed over the reference action set, with the sliding window of length L.

(a) If Cd\_int(F) = +∞, then within the window no internal continuation can bring the whole-field Continuation Bias below threshold ε.

(b) If Cd\_int(F) \> B\_int, then within the window no budget-feasible internal action can bring the whole-field Continuation Bias below the threshold.

(c) If it is further declared that all internal actions actually implementable within the window lie in Π\_int^B (Assumption L\_int), then the conclusion of (b) becomes: within the window, internal continuation cannot bring the whole-field Continuation Bias below the threshold. If, along history, Cd\_int(F\_t) \> B\_int holds from some time t\* on (for example, Cd\_int does not decrease under the conditions of Theorem 2 and has exceeded B\_int at some time), this conclusion holds in every window from t\* on.

This is irreversibility relative to the declared action sets, budget, and window.

Proof sketch: (a) every admissible action has finite cost, so by the definition of Cd\_int, Cd\_int = +∞ means the target is unreachable under internal actions. (b) If some π ∈ Π\_int^B brought the field into T\_ε, then Cd\_int(F) ≤ c(π) ≤ B\_int, contradicting Cd\_int(F) \> B\_int. (c) Follows from (b) and L\_int at each time. ∎

Strength note: (b) is a direct consequence of the definition of Cd\_int; it says that “the cost required to reach the target exceeds the budget.” To infer from it that “internal continuation cannot reach the target,” L\_int is additionally required, i.e., what is actually implementable does not exceed the budget. If the reference action set were itself taken to be the budget-feasible set, then reaching the target would imply Cd\_int ≤ B\_int, so that “Cd\_int \> B\_int” would be equivalent to “unreachable” and would not form a new finite-cost branch; this theorem therefore keeps the reference action set and the budget-feasible set separate.

Note: this theorem concerns relative irreversibility; it differs from Field Chapter 1, Theorem 15 (without deleting historical Trace-Effects, full return is impossible): the latter concerns full return, while this theorem concerns whether the whole-field bias can be brought below the threshold. v1.1’s proof sketch said that “Φ\_critical increases overall with the growth of Depth(Field) (by Theorem 2),” whereas Φ\_critical is Field Depth itself, making the argument circular; it also cited “Field Chapter 1 Constraint 18” as the basis for Field Quality determining the shallowing amount, whereas Constraint 18 concerns the field eventually losing resolution without Field Quality. This version makes the action sets, budget, and L\_int explicit declarations and proves the result directly from the definition of Cd\_int.

Summary: World Sedimentation Stability (interpretation)

The stable existence of the world is the result of history persistently compressing the future—not a prior condition of existence.

Note: this is an interpretive summary of this chapter, not a proved theorem. Theorems 1 to 5 each have their conditions; together they suggest that, within the range where those conditions hold, Continuation Bias has not fully dissipated, the Field-Wide Bias Dissipation Cost does not decrease, the accessible future range does not increase, a dominant Dominant Continuation exists, and internal continuation cannot dissipate the whole-field bias. That the stable historicizing structural process thereby formed can be called “the world” is this chapter’s interpretation. v1.1 listed it as “Theorem 6” and said that “these five together guarantee the long-term dominant stability of Dominant Continuation”; this version makes it an interpretive summary.
