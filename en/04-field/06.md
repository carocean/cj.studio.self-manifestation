# Differentiation Equations

1\. Purpose

The preceding five chapters have established:

\- how Trace-Effects form;  
\- how Familiar Routes stabilize;  
\- how field dynamics evolve;  
\- how history compresses the future;  
\- how the present and reality remain continuous.

But: all of these belong to the aftermath of what has already formed. They assume: differences already exist; structure has already formed; continuation can already be sustained over time.

Yet: the truly more fundamental question is:

Why does an undifferentiated field not remain undifferentiated forever?

Why does the field begin to exhibit: differences; converging flows; boundaries; nuclei; domains; referentiable structure; classifiable structure; reality.

If the Undifferentiated State were always stable, then:

\- no structure would exist;  
\- no objects would exist;  
\- no reality would exist;  
\- no history would exist.

Therefore: this chapter does not discuss how structure evolves after it forms. It discusses: why the Undifferentiated-State Recovery mechanism begins to be unable to sustain itself.

This is: differentiation dynamics.

1\. The Undifferentiated State

The Undifferentiated State is not absolute emptiness. It can still contain Pulses, perturbations, transient continuations, and transient biased flows. But: after these continuations, everything can still be pressed back into the Undifferentiated State. Therefore: differences cannot persist long-term. Thus: boundaries, nuclei, domains, and referentiable structures cannot form.

Therefore: the Undifferentiated State truly means: continuation cannot long-term depart from the undifferentiated condition.

Therefore: the Undifferentiated State is not stillness. It is: a stable mechanism that persistently restores itself.

1\. Undifferentiated-State Recovery

If after any continuation, all differences can be pressed back into the Undifferentiated State, the field will remain undifferentiated forever.

Therefore: the Undifferentiated State can be sustained not because differences never arise in the field, but because: Undifferentiated-State Recovery always succeeds.

Variable Definitions:

Formal definition of continuation fragment s:

s denotes a continuation event or sequence of continuation events capable of leaving residual differences in the field. s is neither a routed Groove-Line in the sense of Field Chapter 1 (Route\_W = 1, per Ω\_W of Definition 12) nor a Familiar Route (Definition 13); it is a shorter-lived continuation fragment that may disappear once Recovery succeeds.

Correspondence to Field Chapter 1’s symbol system:  
s corresponds to the τ (Trace-Effect) fragment produced after one or more π (Pulse) actions in Field Chapter 1—particularly those brief Trace-Effect sequences not yet stably sedimented (Dτ not yet persistently exceeding Eλ, δ not yet persistently increasing on net).

Aggregation declaration: the terms of Field Chapter 1 Equation 2 are defined at structure sites x. This chapter adopts a fixed linear aggregation: δ\_s(n) = Σ\_{x∈X\_s} w\_x·δ\_n(x), w\_x ≥ 0, Σ\_x w\_x = 1, where the set X\_s of structure sites involved in fragment s and the weights are fixed during comparison; Dτ\_s(n) and Eλ\_s(n) use the same weights. Hence δ\_s(n+1) − δ\_s(n) = Dτ\_s(n) − Eλ\_s(n). This gives only the change of the aggregated Sedimentation-Degree and cannot recover the distribution of differences across sites. The discrete update step is a declared fixed duration Δt; this chapter writes Dτ(s,t), Eλ(s,t) for Dτ\_s(n), Eλ\_s(n) at t = n·Δt.

Undifferentiated-State Recovery capacity directly corresponds to the Field Quality shallowing quantity of Field Chapter 1 Operation 8:

Eλ(s,t): the Field Quality shallowing quantity of continuation fragment s at time t—the capacity of Field Quality λ to press residual differences back into the Undifferentiated State (Field Chapter 1, Operation 8).

Note (Eλ and Field Quality): per Outer Chapter 3, the speed of shallowing is received from the nature of Field Quality—"Field-Quality determines the speed of forgetting"; Eλ in Field Chapter 1 Equation 2 is the shallowing caused by Field Quality. This chapter reads Eλ(s,t) as the shallowing actually achieved on fragment s; Field Quality λ itself does not change with differentiation. The main equation of Section 9, in which residual structures weaken Eλ(s,t), is this chapter's modelling hypothesis H\_E (residual structures lower the effective shallowing on a fragment); it does not follow from Field Chapter 1, and it does not alter the original meaning that shallowing is decided by Field Quality.

If Eλ(s,t) persistently exceeds Dτ(s,t) (the Trace-Effect depth quantity), the aggregated Sedimentation-Degree does not increase step by step; whether residuals fully shallow and Recovery succeeds depends on whether cumulative shallowing suffices to remove existing residuals (Theorem 7). If Dτ(s,t) persistently exceeds Eλ(s,t), the aggregated Sedimentation-Degree increases; this alone does not constitute Recovery Failure or differentiation (Proposition 1).

Therefore: the Undifferentiated State is not a blank. It is itself a stable structure that persistently restores itself. This chapter measures its shallowing side by Eλ(s,t).

1\. Recovery Complexity

Undifferentiated-State Recovery is not unlimited and costless. Because: each act of Recovery requires re-unifying residual differences, biased flows, return structures, and convergence tendencies.

Therefore: Recovery itself has persistent Recovery Complexity.

Let Recovery Complexity(s,t) denote the complexity of the associated continuation structures that must be simultaneously unified for Undifferentiated-State Recovery.

If residuals in the field are isolated from each other, Recovery can still be completed with relative ease. But as: residuals multiply; Mutual Continuation Support strengthens; boundaries form; returns interlock; Recovery is no longer merely a matter of pressing back a single difference—it must simultaneously press back an entire network of associated continuation structures. Thus (proposed): Recovery Complexity may rise continuously.

Whether Recovery has a limit, and when the limit is reached, must be decided separately together with the evolution of RC and the effective-shallowing model.

Relation to (Dτ−Eλ)(s,t) and K(s,t):

This chapter takes Recovery Complexity(s,t) as a given state input describing the recovery complexity of associated structures; its value is not computed from the current Dτ−Eλ and K. Residuals and Mutual Continuation Support may affect the historical evolution of RC; this is only a proposed driving relation, and this chapter does not prove its monotonicity. The effect of existing RC on the effective shallowing quantity is expressed in the model by the term −b·RC of the main equation; the self-dynamics of RC are not yet closed.

Therefore: this chapter takes Recovery Complexity(s,t) as a given state input; (Dτ−Eλ) and K only indicate the direction of its drivers, and the instantaneous function RC = f((Dτ−Eλ), K) is not adopted (if it were, RC could not at the same time be treated as an independent memory state). The self-dynamics of RC are not closed in this chapter and are left to subsequent chapters.

1\. Recovery Failure

If after certain continuations, Undifferentiated-State Recovery begins to fail, then some residuals cannot be pressed back into the Undifferentiated State. Thus: the field begins to lose the ability to fully recover the Undifferentiated State.

This is: Recovery Failure.

Therefore: differentiation is not the generation of objects. It is: the Undifferentiated-State Recovery mechanism beginning to be unable to continue unifying all continuation.

1\. Non-Recoverable Undifferentiated Residue

If after certain continuations, residuals still exist that cannot be pressed back into the Undifferentiated State, the Undifferentiated State can no longer be fully recovered.

This chapter uses the net Trace-Effect depth quantity of Field Chapter 1 Equation 2 to record the single-step change of Sedimentation-Degree; it is not itself the non-recoverable undifferentiated degree:

(Dτ−Eλ)(s,t): the net Trace-Effect depth quantity of continuation fragment s at time t (Field Chapter 1 Equation 2: δₜ₊₁(x) = δₜ(x) + Dτₜ(x) − Eλₜ(x)).

(Dτ−Eλ)(s,t) ≤ 0 says only that Sedimentation-Degree does not increase in this step; it does not suffice to prove that existing residuals or differences have vanished: even if Eλ exceeds Dτ at every step, the total shallowing may be insufficient to remove existing residuals (example: initial value 1, net change −2^{−(n+2)} at each step; the cumulative decrease is only 1/2).  
If (Dτ−Eλ)(s,t) \> εₙ \> 0 at every step, the aggregated Sedimentation-Degree increases step by step (Proposition 1); this alone does not imply that stable differences arise between continuations or that Recovery fails.

where εₙ denotes the net-increase threshold declared in this chapter.

Therefore: Dτ persistently exceeding Eλ increases the aggregated Sedimentation-Degree and is one source of residual accumulation; it does not by itself constitute differentiation (Theorem 7).

Simultaneously: as Eλ(s,t) ↓ with Dτ unchanged, (Dτ−Eλ)(s,t) ↑; whether residuals that could previously be pressed back by shallowing thereby survive long-term is judged by cumulative updates.

1\. Mutual Continuation Support

Not all residuals can survive long-term. Large numbers of residuals will mutually annihilate, mutually scatter, or be pressed back into the Undifferentiated State by each other.

Therefore: what truly survives long-term is not isolated residuals, but residual structures that begin to provide continuation conditions for each other.

Let K(s,t) denote the degree of Mutual Continuation Support among continuation residuals.

The higher the Mutual Continuation Support, the more easily different residuals sustain each other. Isolated residuals are readily absorbed by Field Quality shallowing; mutually supporting residuals begin to form local recovery-failure chains. Thus: Eλ finds it increasingly difficult to re-unify these residuals.

Therefore (proposed): besides residual retention and maintained differences (not requiring net deepening at every step, see Theorem 7), stable differentiation depends on residuals providing continuation conditions for each other.

Simultaneously (under hypothesis H\_K, see Proposition 2): as (Dτ−Eλ)(s,t) ↑, residuals begin to form persistent interlocking, and thus K(s,t) ↑.

Precursor-layer relation to Field Chapter 1’s Ω(x→y):

K(s,t) and Field Chapter 1’s Ω\_W belong to different layers. Ω\_W(x→y) (Definition 12) is a historical-qualification predicate of stable recurrence actually exhibited within the declared window W, valued in {0,1}; it checks three verifiable facts (the recurrence record within W reaches the declared criterion; the connection of the Groove-Lines followed is kept, Brk\_W = 0; every sub-segment contains recurrence events of the same kind of path), is derived from Pass\_W and Brk\_W, is not given by the current δ alone, and is not a measure applicable once paths have stably sedimented. K(s,t) is a degree quantity newly introduced in this chapter, describing the degree to which not-yet-stably-sedimented residuals provide each other with continuation conditions. High K does not imply Ω\_W = 1; the determination of Ω\_W does not refer to K. Whether residual structures with high K more easily form, in later windows, recurrence records satisfying Definition 12 is a separate capacity proposition (Field Chapter 1, Definition 12: whether a single action can establish conditions for later persistent recurrence is not asserted there), and this chapter does not assert it. K cannot be substituted for Ω\_W, and this chapter does not claim a definite developmental relation between them.

1\. Undifferentiated Stability

If Eλ(s,t) persistently exceeds Dτ(s,t), the aggregated Sedimentation-Degree does not increase step by step; whether all residuals are pressed back and the Undifferentiated State is stable is judged by cumulative updates (Theorem 7).

With Dτ fixed, a decline of Eλ raises Dτ−Eλ; with Eλ fixed, a rise of Dτ also raises that difference. When both change at once, the direction of the difference depends on the joint change; only when the difference is positive does the aggregated Sedimentation-Degree increase in that step. Whether residuals survive long-term and whether stable differences form must be judged separately by cumulative updates and the criterion of difference.

Therefore: what this chapter attends to is whether the Undifferentiated-State Recovery mechanism can still sustain itself; the comparison of Eλ and Dτ gives only the single-step increase or decrease of Sedimentation-Degree and does not by itself decide differentiation.

1\. Differentiation Dynamics

Let:

D(s,t) denote the Stable Differentiation Degree between continuations. (Note: the original v1.0 used Δ(s,t), but Δ is already defined as the difference operator Δ(x,y) in Engineering Chapter 1; this chapter therefore uses D(s,t).)

(Dτ−Eλ)(s,t) denote the net Trace-Effect depth quantity—the single-step change of the aggregated Sedimentation-Degree (directly referencing the terms of Field Chapter 1 Equation 2); it is not a measure of non-recoverable undifferentiated residue.

K(s,t) denote the degree of Mutual Continuation Support among residuals (see Section 7).

If Eλ persistently exceeds Dτ, the aggregated Sedimentation-Degree does not increase step by step; whether D tends to 0 and the field returns to the Undifferentiated State further requires cumulative shallowing sufficient to remove existing differences (Theorem 7).

Therefore: differentiation dynamics is not, at its core, the growth of differences. It is: the survival of differences after Eλ fails.

The differences that can persist long-term are not those that grow fastest, but those most resistant to being pressed back by Eλ.

Therefore: this chapter uses this model to discuss one possible mechanism of decline of the effective shallowing quantity; the decline must satisfy the conditions of Theorem 3. With Eλ fixed, a rise of Dτ raises Dτ−Eλ, but only when Dτ actually exceeds Eλ is there a positive single-step net increase (example: Eλ = 2, Dτ = 1 − e^{−t}; Dτ rises persistently while the net change is always negative). Net increase by itself still does not imply differentiation.

Main Equation:

∂Eλ(s,t)/∂t = −a·(Dτ−Eλ)(s,t) − c·K(s,t) − b·Recovery Complexity(s,t) + d·Eλ(s,t)

where:

a·(Dτ−Eλ)(s,t): the weakening of Eλ by net Trace-Effect depth residuals (the greater the net depth, the harder it becomes to sustain shallowing capacity);

c·K(s,t): the persistent damage to Eλ by Mutual Continuation Support structures (interlocked residuals are increasingly difficult to unify and shallow);

b·Recovery Complexity(s,t): the dissipation of Eλ as Recovery Complexity continuously rises;

d·Eλ(s,t): Field Quality shallowing’s own stable self-maintenance capacity (positive term).

Dimensional Note:

To ensure dimensional consistency on both sides of the equation, we take Eλ, (Dτ−Eλ), K, and Recovery Complexity to all be dimensionless relative quantities (ratios relative to their respective reference baseline values). Then:

Left side: \[t⁻¹\]  
Right side, negative terms: a × \[(Dτ−Eλ)\] = a × \[dimensionless\], a has dimension \[t⁻¹\]; c × \[K\] = c × \[dimensionless\], c has dimension \[t⁻¹\]; b × \[RC\] = b × \[dimensionless\], b has dimension \[t⁻¹\].  
Right side, positive term: d × \[Eλ\] = d × \[dimensionless\], d has dimension \[t⁻¹\].

All four terms have dimension \[t⁻¹\], consistent with the left side.

Coefficient Note:

a \> 0 (net Trace-Effect depth weakening coefficient): larger a means net depth residuals weaken shallowing capacity more strongly.  
b \> 0 (Recovery Complexity dissipation coefficient): larger b means already-accumulated high-complexity structures dissipate shallowing capacity more significantly.  
c \> 0 (Mutual Continuation Support destruction coefficient): larger c means residual interlocking damages shallowing capacity more severely.  
d \> 0 (Field Quality stability coefficient): larger d means Field Quality shallowing has greater self-maintenance capacity.

Note: In the first term on the right side, −a·(Dτ−Eλ), the quantity (Dτ−Eλ)(s,t) should be understood as the net Trace-Effect depth immediately observed at time t—i.e., the instantaneous observed value of the difference between current Dτ and Eλ, not a recursive definition. Expanding this term, the equation is equivalent to:

∂Eλ(s,t)/∂t = (d+a)·Eλ(s,t) − a·Dτ(s,t) − c·K(s,t) − b·Recovery Complexity(s,t)

With Dτ, K and Recovery Complexity taken as given inputs (this chapter takes RC as given, Section 4), this is a linear differential equation in Eλ.

Continuous-modelling note: this equation is a continuous modelling of the evolution of the effective shallowing quantity under the fixed update duration Δt; it is not derived directly from the discrete update formula of Field Chapter 1. The original formulation treats (Dτ−Eλ) as a single term in order to maintain direct correspondence with the terms of Field Chapter 1 Equation 2, making physical origins easy to trace.

d·Eλ(s,t) term note:

d·Eλ(s,t) is a positive term representing the tendency of Field Quality λ, under conditions of no strong external perturbation, to maintain shallowing capacity. But this term does not represent unlimited recovery—d is a positive constant, while Eλ(s,t) itself declines as differentiation advances; therefore the absolute magnitude of d·Eλ(s,t) decreases as Eλ falls, forming an intrinsic positive feedback (the lower Eλ, the smaller the stabilizing term, and the faster Eλ declines further).

Boundary rule (a modelling rule newly added in this chapter): after expansion, the equation contains the positive term (d+a)·Eλ; with Dτ = K = RC = 0, ∂Eλ/∂t = (a+d)·Eλ \> 0, which would still grow at the upper bound. This chapter declares an upper bound Eλ\_max for Eλ (a hypothesis: Field Chapter 1 Operation 8 only defines shallowing and gives no upper bound; constraining the upper bound of Eλ by Field Quality λ and Field Capacity (Cap(F)) is this chapter's declaration and does not follow from Field Chapter 1). Writing E = Eλ(s,t) and V for the right side of the main equation, take: dE/dt = max(V, 0) at E = 0; dE/dt = V for 0 \< E \< Eλ\_max; dE/dt = min(V, 0) at E = Eλ\_max. The main equation is the interior equation, and the domain is the closed interval \[0, Eλ\_max\]; the upper bound is enforced by constraining the whole right side, and the d term is no longer said to saturate automatically.

The recovery threshold is defined as the level of Recovery Complexity at which the sum of the three negative terms exceeds d·Eλ(s,t) (varying with the other terms). When Recovery Complexity(s,t) persistently exceeds that threshold, the sum of the three negative terms persistently exceeds d·Eλ(s,t), and Eλ persistently declines on the interior interval (Theorem 3); this does not prove that structural history is irreversible, nor that Eλ cannot recover once the condition is withdrawn.

This is the core mechanism of differentiation dynamics:

Differentiation dynamics is, at its core, the competitive imbalance between Eλ (shallowing) and Dτ (depth accumulation).

1\. Boundary

If certain differences persist long-term, different continuation structures gradually become unable to continue translating into each other. Thus continuation begins to exhibit: different convergence flows; different returns; different coordination.

Therefore: boundaries begin to form.

A boundary is not a geometric line. A boundary truly means: between different stable continuations, it is no longer possible to compress them into the same coordinated structure.

Therefore: a boundary is essentially the impossibility of continued coordinative unification. (The above is a delimitation and a proposed mechanism; for the criterion of a boundary see Criterion 4 in Section 19.)

1\. Nucleus

If a certain continuation structure persistently attracts continuation, differences begin to concentrate. Thus: more and more continuation flows more easily toward that structure.

Therefore: differences no longer exist only in dispersed form; they begin to form a stable convergence center.

This is: a Nucleus.

A Nucleus is not an object. A Nucleus is: the stable convergence center formed by the persistent contraction of long-retained differences. (For the criterion see Criterion 5 in Section 19; the formation mechanism is to be proven.)

1\. Domain

When multiple stable boundaries and Nuclei begin to interlock, stable continuation ranges begin to form in the field. Thus internal continuation flows back more easily to each other; external continuation finds it harder to enter internal coordination.

Therefore: Domains begin to form.

A Domain is not a spatial region. A Domain truly means: stable coordination relations begin to form a self-sustaining range. (For the criterion see Criterion 6 in Section 19.)

1\. Differentiation Feedback Loop

Proposed mechanism: after Eλ falls, residuals begin to survive. After residuals survive, convergence flows begin to shift. After convergence flows shift, boundaries begin to stabilize. After boundaries stabilize, different continuations find it further difficult to be re-unified. Thus more residuals continue to survive.

Therefore, the proposed Differentiation Feedback Loop is as follows. The chain below is a proposed formation mechanism; each arrow depends respectively on H\_E, H\_K, biased-flow positive feedback and the formation conditions of boundary, Nucleus and Domain, and is not a necessary chain proven by the main equation:

Eλ ↓  
⇒ (Dτ−Eλ) ↑  
⇒ K ↑  
⇒ convergence-flow shift ↑  
⇒ boundary stabilization ↑  
⇒ return interlocking ↑  
⇒ Recovery Complexity ↑  
⇒ Eλ ↓

If the conditions of each link hold, stable structure can begin to form.

Simultaneously (interpretive): history becomes possible. Because: the field can no longer fully recover the Undifferentiated State. Therefore: the past can begin to influence the future over the long term. Thus: sedimentation can influence subsequent continuation over the long term.

Saturation Constraint Note: the Differentiation Feedback Loop does not strengthen without limit. The Field Capacity (Cap(F)) and Domain Saturation (Full(𝒟)) mechanisms of Field Chapter 1 v2.7 impose a structural upper-bound constraint—once Field Capacity reaches its limit or a Domain reaches saturation, new Trace-Effects cannot continue sedimenting inward, and the further deepening of the Differentiation Feedback Loop is subject to a saturation limit. Therefore, further growth of the Differentiation Feedback Loop is constrained; boundedness does not guarantee convergence, and whether it converges to a stable-differentiation state is to be proven.

If differences cannot stabilize, they cannot be repeatedly recognized. Therefore: classes, references, numbers, and reality cannot form.

Therefore: all referentiable structure is built upon stable differentiation.

Therefore: objects do not exist a priori. Objects are only continuation compressions that are long-term recognized after stable differentiation.

1\. Theorems

Proposition 1: Net Increase of Sedimentation-Degree

Under the fixed linear aggregation of Section 3 and within the declared update interval, if Dτ\_s − Eλ\_s \> εₙ \> 0 at every step, the aggregated Sedimentation-Degree δ\_s increases step by step. This conclusion does not by itself imply that stable differences arise between continuations, that Recovery fails, or irreversibility.

Note: v1.1 called this the "Recovery Failure Theorem," taking "(Dτ−Eλ) \> εₙ holding long-term" as the trigger condition for the onset of differentiation; but when all sites deepen equally on net, δ grows while D = 0 (Theorem 7), so net increase does not constitute differentiation. This version changes it into a proposition on net increase of Sedimentation-Degree; εₙ is the net-increase threshold declared in this chapter.

Proof sketch: by the aggregation formula of Section 3, δ\_s(n+1) − δ\_s(n) = Dτ\_s(n) − Eλ\_s(n) \> εₙ \> 0. ∎

Proposition 2: Mutual Continuation Support Reinforcement (hypothesis H\_K)

If (Dτ−Eλ)(s,t) rises persistently, K(s,t) tends to rise.

Motivating remark (not a proof): as net Trace-Effect depth residuals increase, residuals may form shared continuation conditions (providing each other with the biased flows and return structures needed for maintenance); the probability of isolated residuals being absorbed by Eλ shallowing may decrease as (Dτ−Eλ) rises; Mutual Continuation Support forms local recovery-failure chains. This chapter takes this as hypothesis H\_K, whose truth is to be proven; v1.1 called it a "theorem," and this version changes it to a hypothesis.

Theorem 3: Conditional Theorem of Non-Recovery of the Effective Shallowing Quantity

Write V for the right side of the main equation. If a·(Dτ−Eλ) + c·K + b·RC \> d·Eλ (i.e., V \< 0) holds persistently on an interior interval on which a solution exists, Eλ declines on that interval and does not recover; if V ≤ −ε \< 0 persists during the positive period until 0 is first reached, the time to first reach the boundary does not exceed Eλ(t₀)/ε, and after reaching the boundary the evolution follows the boundary rule. This theorem does not prove that structural history is irreversible, nor that Eλ cannot recover once the condition is withdrawn.

Note: RC here abbreviates Recovery Complexity(s,t), taken as a given input per Section 4.

Proof sketch: on the interior interval dEλ/dt = V \< 0, so Eλ strictly declines; if V ≤ −ε, then Eλ(t) ≤ Eλ(t₀) − ε·(t − t₀), so 0 is reached at the latest by t₀ + Eλ(t₀)/ε; after reaching the boundary, by the boundary rule, dEλ/dt = max(V, 0) = 0 when V \< 0. v1.1's "differentiation enters the irreversible phase" and "shallowing capacity approaches extinction" are withdrawn in this version. ∎

Criterion 4: Boundary Criterion

The criterion of a boundary is "stable continuations can no longer be unified into the same coordinated structure." D(s,t) \> 0 holding long-term is its background condition, but does not by itself imply a decline in the capacity for coordinative unification.

Note: v1.1 called this the "Boundary Formation Theorem," inferring from D(s,t) \> 0 a persistent decline of coordinative unification and the formation of boundaries; stable difference does not automatically lead to a decline of coordinative capacity, so this version withdraws that inference and sets up a criterion instead.

Criterion 5: Nucleus Criterion

The criterion of a Nucleus is "a stable convergence center has actually formed." A local rise of K and biased-flow positive feedback (a hypothesis, cf. H\_fb of Field Chapter 11 v1.2) are only formation mechanisms to be proven; a rise of K is not the same as net attraction of surrounding continuation.

Note: v1.1 called this the "Nucleus Formation Theorem," inferring the formation of a Nucleus from a local rise of K; this version sets up a criterion instead, the formation mechanism being to be proven.

Criterion 6: Domain Criterion

The criterion of a Domain is "stable coordination relations have formed a self-sustainable range"; actual return and maintenance must be verified separately and cannot be evidenced merely by the "interlocking" of boundaries and Nuclei.

Note: v1.1 called this the "Domain Formation Theorem," inferring a self-sustaining range from the interlocking of boundaries and Nuclei; this version sets up a criterion instead.

Theorem 7: Distinction between Net Increase and Differentiation

Field Chapter 1 Equation 2 gives the correspondence between the single-step change of Sedimentation-Degree and Dτ − Eλ. Persistent net increase does not imply stable differentiation; the retention of existing differences does not require net increase at every step. Whether residuals survive and whether differences vanish must be judged together with the initial structure, cumulative updates and the criterion of difference.

Proof sketch: (i) persistent net increase does not imply differentiation: at every structure site Dτ − Eλ is equal and positive, δ increases on net by equal amounts everywhere, and there is no stable difference between continuations, D = 0. (ii) retention of difference does not require net increase: two sites already have δ₁ = 2 and δ₂ = 1, and thereafter Dτ = Eλ at each, so the difference in depth is retained while net deepening is always 0. (iii) net decrease at every step does not imply that residuals vanish: initial value 1, net change −2^{−(n+2)} at each step, and the cumulative decrease is only 1/2. v1.1 equated D(s,t) \> 0 with (Dτ−Eλ)(s,t) \> 0 and said "stable differentiation is the logical prerequisite of sedimentation"; this version corrects it. ∎
