# Separability Threshold Equations

1\. Purpose

The preceding seven chapters have established:

\- how Trace-Effects form;  
\- how sedimentation deepens;  
\- how Familiar Routes stabilize;  
\- how history compresses the future;  
\- how the field forms structure;  
\- how differentiation continues;  
\- how depth dominates continuation.

But all of these chapters have in fact assumed one thing:

that differences in the field can already be separated.

That is, they assume:

\- Domains have already formed;  
\- boundaries already exist;  
\- different Trace-Effects can already be distinguished.

Yet the more fundamental question is:

Why do different Pulses in the field not remain mixed together forever?

Why can some differences come to be established, while others are compressed into the same class?

Why does the world neither infinitely subdivide nor collapse into total chaos?

These questions the preceding seven chapters have not truly addressed.

This chapter therefore discusses: under what conditions a difference can come to be established. And: under what conditions the field can recognize two Pulses as two different Domains.

This is: the Separability Threshold.

1\. Core Proposition

Differences do not exist by nature. Whether a difference can be established depends on whether the field can separate them.

If the field cannot resolve them, then:

\- the different is compressed into the same;  
\- diversity is collapsed into one class;  
\- detail disappears;  
\- boundaries collapse.

Thus:

\- nothing can be counted;  
\- nothing can be referenced;  
\- nothing can be classified;  
\- nothing can be compared.

Therefore: whether a difference is established depends not only on the objects themselves. It depends more on: whether the field permits them to be split into two.

1\. Separability Threshold

Let:

s₁, s₂ denote two Pulses.

d(s₁,s₂) denote the difference quantity between them.

Symbol note: this chapter uses d(s₁,s₂) to denote the difference quantity between Pulses s₁ and s₂. (Note: the original v1.0 used Δ(s₁,s₂), but Δ is already defined in Engineering Chapter 1 as the difference operator Δ(x,y); this chapter therefore uses d(s₁,s₂).) d(s₁,s₂) is a non-negative real number satisfying: d(s₁,s₂) = 0 if and only if s₁ and s₂ leave the same Trace-Effect distribution in the field; whether the two can be distinguished in the field is decided by the Separability Threshold below (d(s₁,s₂) ≤ θ\_sep means inseparable); d(s₁,s₂) \> 0 means the two have a measurable differential in their Pulse structure.

Relation to Field Chapter 7: Field Chapter 7’s δ(x,t) is the sedimentation degree of local Domain x at time t, describing the accumulation depth of a single Domain; this chapter’s d(s₁,s₂) is the difference quantity between two Pulses, describing the magnitude of the Trace-Effect difference left in the field by two Pulses. d(s₁,s₂) can be understood as: after s₁ and s₂ each act on the field, the distance measure between their respective Trace-Effect distributions. Its precise functional form (such as the L² distance of Trace-Effect differences, weighted integral of sedimentation differences, etc.) is left to subsequent chapters to establish.

θ\_sep denotes the current Separability Threshold of field f. (Note: the original v1.0 used θ\_f, but θ is already defined in Field Chapter 1 as the threshold θ(x); this chapter therefore uses θ\_sep, with the subscript sep from “separability”.)

Foundational assumption: θ\_sep \> 0. Any finite field has a minimum resolution granularity, so the Separability Threshold cannot be zero. This is the foundational assumption of this chapter; Theorem 2 and Theorem 7 both directly depend on this assumption.

Then:

If d(s₁,s₂) \> θ\_sep, the two are separable.

If d(s₁,s₂) ≤ θ\_sep, the two are classified as the same.

Note: the inseparability relation fixed by d ≤ θ\_sep is a pairwise relation and is in general not transitive (s₁ with s₂ and s₂ with s₃ may each be inseparable while s₁ and s₃ remain separable). "The same class" here means only pairwise inseparable; the formation of classes further requires the closure of the indistinguishability relation (the Recognition-Realm Forms).

Therefore: difference is not absolute. Difference is a result within the field. There is no absolute “same” in the world, and no absolute “different.” Same and different both depend on the field’s current resolution capacity.

1\. Why Separability Can Obtain

For the field to truly resolve differences, it must simultaneously possess:

\- boundary-formation capacity;  
\- Trace-Effect retention capacity;  
\- neighborhood isolation capacity;  
\- tuning-convergence capacity.

Without boundaries, there are no Domains. Without Domains, all Pulses disperse into each other.

Without tuning, noise cannot be excluded.

Without Trace-Effects, differences cannot persist.

Therefore: “separability” is not an attribute of Pulses themselves, but the result of field structure reaching a certain condition.

1\. Resolution Floor

If d(s₁,s₂) → 0, then even if the field wishes to separate them, it cannot.

Thus:

\- detail begins to collapse;  
\- boundaries begin to disappear;  
\- multiple Pulses begin to merge into one flow.

This is called: the Resolution Floor.

All differences below this floor—even if they possess a differential in the Pulse relation—cannot be established as distinguishable differences in that field.

Thus: sub-threshold differences cannot be distinguished by this criterion; how categories form still requires a closure or classification rule to be given separately.

Therefore: finite resolution is one of the conditions of category formation; “categories” are not a logical invention, but their formation further requires a closure or classification rule (note in Section 3).

1\. The Conditional Relation between Infinite Subdivision and Repeated Reinforcement

Infinite subdivision by itself does not decide whether structure sites are repeatedly updated, nor does it by itself exclude stable shared Domains. Whether actual updates repeat depends on the input history and the structure-site mapping; this chapter separately restricts the update history under discussion by H\_res.

Infinite subdivision by itself does not exclude the recurrence of exactly identical Pulses, nor does it exclude different Pulses sharing some structure sites and repeatedly reinforcing them. This chapter therefore sets up hypothesis H\_res: in the input history and structure-site mapping under consideration, each structure site receives at most one entered update, and there is no further update caused by other inputs, internal unfolding or return. Under H\_res there are no repeated Trace-Effects at the same structure site, and no stable repetition.

Without stable repetition, sedimentation cannot deepen through repetition (Inner Chapter 6: "repeated, the trace deepens; little used, the trace fades").

Not being able to deepen through repetition is not the same as sedimentation being unable to form: first sedimentation and the retention of existing sedimentation may still exist. (Interpretive) If sedimentation cannot deepen through repetition, history's bias on the future can rely only on first sedimentation and the retention of existing sedimentation (the future compression of Field Chapter 5 v1.2 depends further on A\_F).

(Interpretive) Reality persists precisely because history can persistently bias the future. Without sedimentation, every moment of the future is unrelated to the preceding, no stable present exists, and reality degrades into a disconnected scattered flow.

Therefore: in the model delimited by H\_res, there is no repeated reinforcement at the same structure site; this does not prove that infinite resolution is impossible in general (Theorem 3). The relation of this to the persistence of reality is interpretive.

Finite resolution by itself does not guarantee that merging or omission of positive differences occurs in the current set of Pulses. Under H\_dense there exist Trace-Effect differences that are strictly positive but not greater than θ\_sep, and these differences are therefore indistinguishable by this criterion. Whether class merging or other compression structures further form requires a closure or classification rule to be given separately.

(Interpretive, only for the H\_dense case above and not a necessary conclusion for every finite-resolution field) Therefore: the unknown is not incidental. The unknown is the price that must be paid for reality to obtain.

1\. Separability and Reality

The Numericology says: “Thus number, reality, and its Observation realm are one mother with three sons.” The original continues: “The mother comes forth only after divisibility becomes possible; therefore the three sons are born under the same constraints.”

We can now go further: the obtaining of reality itself depends on separability.

If nothing is separable, then:

\- boundaries cannot form;  
\- Domains cannot form;  
\- the present cannot form;  
\- reality cannot form.

Therefore: the root of reality does not lie in objects themselves, but in: whether the field can fracture the world into different parts.

Therefore: reality is not the complete unfolding of all potential differentiation in the field. Reality is only: the stable fracturing that the field can maintain under its finite separability capacity.

1\. The Separability Threshold Is Not Fixed

θ\_sep is not constant. It varies with:

\- Field Quality;  
\- history;  
\- depth;  
\- tuning;  
\- capacity;  
\- noise;  
\- boundary density.

Boundaries do not exist without cost. Any boundary must sustain:

\- Domain isolation;  
\- noise suppression;  
\- tuning stability;  
\- Trace-Effect continuity.

Therefore: the more boundaries, the more:

\- inter-Domain spacing decreases;  
\- mutual interference strengthens;  
\- tuning cost rises;  
\- noise isolation becomes more difficult.

Thus (this chapter's hypothesis H\_B): the cost of maintaining boundaries rises with boundary density, and the field raises its Separability Threshold to maintain overall stability.

Therefore:

θ\_sep ↑ as BoundaryDensity ↑

That is: the higher the boundary density, the higher the Separability Threshold, and the harder it becomes for the field to sustain new subdivision boundaries.

Therefore: differences cannot grow without limit, because the field does not have the capacity to sustain boundaries without limit.

1\. Deep Nuclei and Familiar Recognition Suppression

After a Deep Nucleus forms, the deep obtain its flow first: per Field Chapter 1 Law 5, under C₌ a deeper route meets the out-flow condition no later than a shallower one, and under C₌ and P₁₇ it obtains the flow strictly first; how much effective input is received depends separately on Field Chapter 7 v1.2 (the remaining-capacity gate σ(G) and overlap; as a deep region approaches capacity, its effective input is in fact gated down). Whether new Pulses are actually absorbed into the old Nucleus, and whether the old Nucleus suppresses new differences, depends separately on hypothesis H\_occ (Theorem 5) and does not follow from the precedence of out-flow. (What follows is a conditional interpretation under H\_occ.)

Thus: old structures continuously expand their range of interpretation; commonality preferentially covers detail; familiar meaning preferentially conceals anomalies.

Therefore: the deeper the sedimentation of old structures, the more, under these conditions, it tends to interpret new reality using old structures.

Thus:

\- familiar things are perceived first;  
\- subtle differences are noticed later;  
\- the unknown is compressed into the familiar;  
\- anomalies are compressed into old classes.

It is not that differences do not exist—it is that the Deep Nucleus has already preempted the flow.

1\. The Limit of Separability

The field’s resolution capacity must ultimately have a limit. Because every boundary formed consumes field capacity. The finer the resolution, the more:

\- boundaries multiply;  
\- Domains become denser;  
\- noise strengthens;  
\- stability becomes harder.

Eventually a situation arises: newly added boundaries are no longer sufficient to sustain themselves stably.

Thus: the maintenance of newly added boundaries is limited (Theorem 6). If, further, existing subdivisions fail and fine classes merge back into broader classes, this is called Resolution Collapse; it is a phenomenon to be explained, requiring separate witness of the failure of existing subdivisions and of a coarsening mechanism, and does not follow from the limited maintenance of newly added boundaries. Since pairwise inseparability is not transitive, the sub-threshold relation cannot be taken directly as a rule of class merging.

(Interpretive) Therefore: reality does not unfold without limit. Reality is always in dynamic equilibrium between “resolution” and “compression.”

1\. Summary Expression

General condition for separability to obtain:

d(s₁,s₂) \> θ\_sep ⟺ s₁ and s₂ are separable in field f

where:

θ\_sep = Θ(Q, H, Dp, T, Cap, N, B\_d)

Meaning and directional influence of each parameter:

Q: Field Quality. The more Q supports stable difference (i.e., Field Quality increases in the direction of sustaining boundary stability), the finer the boundaries the field can maintain, θ\_sep ↓. Note: if Q manifests as excessive shallowing (shallowing rate far exceeding what is needed to maintain differences), it may instead erase boundaries; such effects are handled under the noise-dispersal term (N) and are not within the positive contribution range of Q. Per Outer Chapter 3, Field-Quality determines the speed of forgetting: "Without it, the field would easily saturate. Saturation would swiftly lose distinguishability." Moderate shallowing keeps the field from saturating and so supports separability; the direction of Q is read in this sense.

H: History. The deeper the historical depth, the stronger the occupation of new Pulses by old structures, the harder new differences are to establish, θ\_sep ↑.

Dp: the full-field Sedimentation-Degree reading δ\_F. Per Field Chapter 5 v1.2, a full-field Sedimentation-Degree reading requires its aggregation to be declared separately; this chapter takes δ\_F = Σ\_x w\_x·δ(x), w\_x ≥ 0, Σ\_x w\_x = 1, with the weights fixed during comparison. δ\_F is a state quantity; it is not the Field-Wide Bias Dissipation Cost Cd(F) of Field Chapter 5 v1.2 (named "Field Depth" in v1.1), nor a reading of Cd. The larger δ\_F, the more prominent the Deep Nucleus flow-occupation effect (this chapter's hypothesis), the harder fine differences are to sustain, θ\_sep ↑. (Note: the original v1.0 used D, but D is already defined in Field Chapter 6 as Stable Differentiation Degree D(s,t); this chapter therefore uses Dp for the depth parameter.)

T: Tuning. The stronger the tuning capacity, the better the noise isolation, the finer the boundaries the field can maintain, θ\_sep ↓.

Cap: Capacity (Cap(F) of Field Chapter 1 v2.7, Definition 30; written C in v1.1). The larger the field capacity, the more boundaries it can support in total, θ\_sep ↓.

N: Noise. The stronger the noise, the less stable the fine boundaries, θ\_sep ↑.

B\_d: Boundary Density. As stated in Section 8, the higher the boundary density, θ\_sep ↑ (the more existing boundaries, the harder it is to sustain new ones).

Standing note: the directions of influence of the seven items above are all modelling hypotheses of this chapter and do not follow from Field Chapters 1 to 7.

Minimal computable directional approximation:

Synthesizing the above directional influences, θ\_sep can be written in the following ratio approximation (minimal computable form; precise exponents to be determined in subsequent chapters):

θ\_sep ∝ (H^α · Dp^β · N^γ · B\_d^η) / (Q^μ · T^ν · Cap^ξ)

where α, β, γ, η, μ, ν, ξ \> 0 are all positive exponents. The numerator terms (H, Dp, N, B\_d) drive θ\_sep upward; the denominator terms (Q, T, Cap) drive θ\_sep downward—consistent with the directional influences of each parameter. Category: demonstration approximation, only one possible form satisfying the directional hypotheses above; the directional hypotheses do not imply the power-law form. This form expresses only directional structure, without presupposing specific exponent values; the precise functional form (including nonlinear coupling terms, saturation constraints, etc.) is left to subsequent chapters to establish by specific field type.

Domain of application: this demonstration approximation is used only on the working domain in which all seven parameters are strictly positive and finite, with proportionality coefficient k \> 0 fixed during directional comparison. Zero values and degenerate boundaries are not defined by this formula and must be declared separately; θ\_sep = 0 may not be inferred from it.

Therefore: whether a difference is established depends not only on the objects themselves, but more on: whether the field still has the capacity to sustain it as “two.”

1\. Theorems

Theorem 1: Difference Relativity Theorem

Whether a difference is established is not an intrinsic attribute of objects, but the result of the field’s current resolution capacity. That is: d(s₁,s₂) \> θ\_sep is not an absolute proposition about s₁ and s₂ themselves, but a conditional proposition about the state of field f.

Proof sketch: d(s₁,s₂) is the Pulse difference quantity; θ\_sep is the current Separability Threshold of field f; the same pair of Pulses may be separable in some field states and inseparable in others (θ\_sep varies with field state, see Section 8); therefore whether a difference obtains depends on field state, not on the Pulses themselves. ∎

Theorem 2: Resolution Floor Theorem

If d(s₁,s₂) \< θ\_sep, then s₁ and s₂ are inseparable. In particular, under finite-field conditions (foundational assumption θ\_sep \> 0), when d(s₁,s₂) → 0, necessarily d(s₁,s₂) \< θ\_sep, and s₁ and s₂ are classified as the same—inseparable.

Proof sketch: by the separability criterion (Section 3), d(s₁,s₂) \< θ\_sep directly corresponds to inseparable; by the foundational assumption θ\_sep \> 0 (Section 3), when d(s₁,s₂) → 0, necessarily d(s₁,s₂) \< θ\_sep; therefore under finite-field conditions, difference tending to zero is necessarily inseparable, and the Resolution Floor cannot be breached. ∎

Theorem 3: Conditional Theorem of No Repeated Reinforcement

In the model delimited by H\_res, no deepening update occurs at a structure site after its first update, and hence there is no repeated reinforcement at that site. This conclusion does not prove that infinite resolution is impossible in general, nor does it deny first sedimentation, the retention of existing sedimentation, or other historical effects.

Proof sketch: by H\_res, each structure site receives at most one entered update; by Field Chapter 1 Equation 2, Dτ = 0 at each step after the first update, so there is no deepening update at that site and no repeated reinforcement (the repetition spoken of in Inner Chapter 6, "repeated, the trace deepens," does not occur). v1.1 said "with infinite resolution sedimentation cannot form and reality cannot persist"; infinite subdivision does not exclude the recurrence of identical Pulses or different Pulses sharing structure sites, "mutually distinct Pulses" cannot imply Dτ = 0 after the first step, "cannot deepen through repetition" is not the same as "sedimentation cannot form," and the link to reality depends on A\_F of Field Chapter 5 v1.2 and is interpretive; this version restates it as above. ∎

Theorem 4: Boundary Density Increase Theorem (under hypothesis H\_B)

Under H\_B, when boundary density B\_d increases, the Separability Threshold θ\_sep rises accordingly, and the field’s capacity to sustain new subdivision boundaries declines.

Proof sketch: by Section 8, increasing boundaries leads to decreased inter-Domain spacing, strengthened mutual interference, higher tuning cost, and more difficult noise isolation; these effects together lead the field to raise θ\_sep to maintain overall stability; this is this chapter's modelling hypothesis H\_B and does not follow from the Field Chapters; therefore, under H\_B, B\_d ↑ → θ\_sep ↑. ∎

Theorem 5: Three Layers of Deep Nucleus Flow-Occupation

The precedence of out-flow under comparison conditions follows Field Chapter 1 Law 5 (no later under C₌; strictly first under C₌ and P₁₇). That old nuclei actually occupy resolution resources and suppress new differences is a separate hypothesis H\_occ and does not follow from the precedence of out-flow. Suppression may be realized through a change of the Trace-Effect distance d, a change of θ\_sep, or other processing conditions; this chapter has not yet proven the specific mechanism. To say that θ\_sep rises, one can only cite the directional hypotheses for Dp and H in Section 11 with their fixed comparison conditions; it is not a consequence of Law 5.

Proof sketch: the first layer is given directly by Field Chapter 1 Law 5; Law 5 gives only the precedence of meeting the out-flow condition under comparison conditions and does not directly guarantee the actual input share, absorption into the old nucleus, or interpretive suppression. The second layer is hypothesis H\_occ. Third layer: a decline of separability is not the same as a rise of the threshold—for instance, with θ\_sep constantly 1, a Trace-Effect distance falling from 2 to 0.5 likewise turns separable into inseparable; so v1.1's "the probability of becoming established decreases, i.e., θ\_sep tends to rise" is withdrawn. The v1.1 proof said that "high δ causes Effective Input I\_eff to preferentially flow toward old nuclei in the high-match direction (Finite-Field Input Attenuation Equation)"; per Field Chapter 7 v1.2, I\_eff = A·σ(G) is decided by the remaining-capacity gate and overlap, overlap concerns only the amount of effective input after entry and does not decide precedence, and as a deep region approaches capacity its effective input in fact becomes small; that citation does not hold and is withdrawn in this version. ∎

Theorem 6: Proposition of Limited Maintenance of Newly Added Boundaries

Under the condition that the necessary resource demand of boundary maintenance has been declared and that demand and available budget are measured in the same dimension (condition H\_col), a newly added boundary whose demand exceeds the budget cannot be stably sustained under that scheme. This conclusion does not by itself imply that existing fine classes merge back; Resolution Collapse (Section 10) is a phenomenon to be explained, requiring separate witness of the failure of existing subdivisions and of a coarsening mechanism.

Proof sketch: given directly by H\_col. Existing fine classes remaining unchanged while newly added boundaries merely fail to be established also satisfies H\_col, with no recompression occurring; so merging back cannot be inferred from this proposition. The v1.1 proof cited Field Chapter 7’s Λ(x), "Field Chapter 7 Theorem 6" and the degradation terms −q₀δ − β·φδ of the main equation; per Field Chapter 7 v1.2, Λ(x) is the local resolution capacity, the former Theorem 6 is now Criterion 6 (a finite-window stability condition on the Sedimentation-Degree of an existing nucleus, which cannot by itself prove the existence or maintenance of nuclei or boundaries), and the main equation is a demonstration model; none suffices to infer collapse, so this version makes it a proposition of limited maintenance of newly added boundaries. ∎

Theorem 7: Necessity of the Unknown Theorem

In any finite-resolution field, if Pulse space contains pairs of Pulses whose difference quantity falls in (0, θ\_sep\] (hypothesis H\_dense), there necessarily exist indistinguishable differences—unknowable parts exist for that field. (Interpretive: the unknown is not incidental; it is the structural price of reality obtaining.)

Proof sketch: by the foundational assumption θ\_sep \> 0 and the separability criterion, pairs with d(s₁,s₂) ∈ (0, θ\_sep\] are inseparable; H\_dense guarantees that such pairs exist (for instance, if there is a sequence of Pulse pairs whose difference quantities are strictly positive and tend to 0, then for fixed θ\_sep \> 0 there eventually exists a pair whose difference quantity falls in (0, θ\_sep\]; "Pulse space continuous and d continuous" does not guarantee this, since d identically 0 also satisfies continuity). "Unknowable" here means only indistinguishable for that field, not absolutely unknowable. The v1.1 proof took Theorem 3 as a premise and called "Pulse space dense" without declaring it as a hypothesis, with an interval including 0; this theorem needs only θ\_sep \> 0 and H\_dense, and this version corrects it. ∎
