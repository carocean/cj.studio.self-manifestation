# Closure Dynamics Equations

1\. Purpose

The preceding chapters have established:

\- why separability obtains;  
\- why separability is finite;  
\- why the threshold drifts;  
\- how Deep Nuclei occupy flow;  
\- why reality continuously compresses and reconstructs.

Yet the preceding chapters have not truly explained: why structures do not disperse without limit.

Why:

\- Familiar Routes can persist over the long term;  
\- Co-Nuclei can remain continuously stable;  
\- history can persistently compress the future;  
\- the present cannot dissipate instantaneously.

This chapter studies how stable mutual return supports the continuation of structures; it does not thereby assert that, without closed loops, no Deep Nucleus, Co-Nucleus or reality structure can be maintained.

Therefore this chapter discusses: why flows in the field continuously form re-entry. And: why multiple Domains gradually form stable closed-loop systems.

This is: Closure Dynamics.

1\. Core Proposition

This chapter proposes: if stable mutual channeling forms between Domains, subsequent flows may return to previously active Domains. The formation and long-term maintenance of this mechanism must be verified separately.

If this re-entry is stable over the long term, flows can form locally cycling continuation, and structures can gradually form closed-loop structures capable of persistently returning to themselves (proposed).

Therefore (interpretive): Closure is not an additional structure. Closure is: an important dynamic for reality to remain stable over the long term. The holding of Closure follows Field Chapter 1, Definition 24 and Equation 16 (multiple continuation segments stably return to one another over the long term and can repeatedly return); this chapter does not prove that Closure is a necessary condition of reality's stability.

1\. Single-Domain Re-entry

Let x\_i denote a local Domain position in the field (directly referencing Field Chapter 7’s x and Field Chapter 9’s x\_i: a local Domain capable of forming Familiar Routes, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, and nuclear stabilization).

Formal definition of Re-entry Flow Intensity:

Reentry(x\_i, t) denotes the Re-entry Flow Intensity of local Domain x\_i at time t—the ratio of the total subsequent flow that re-converges into x\_i after being channeled through other Domains (x\_j ≠ x\_i) to the direct new input. The higher Reentry, the more x\_i depends on the cycling continuation of existing field structure rather than external new input to maintain its sedimentation. Write further R\_abs(x\_i, t) for the total subsequent flow re-converging into x\_i (an absolute quantity, with the same dimension as input).

Reading rule: the re-entry quantity and the direct new input must be read over the same declared time window, in the same flow unit and under a fixed counting rule. Only when the direct new input in that window is greater than zero is Reentry defined as the ratio of the two; when the direct new input is zero, this chapter does not define the re-entry ratio and makes no numerical ordering by it, but R\_abs can still be read on its own. The direct new input is not the same as ExternalDisruption. (The re-entry ratio adopted by Field Chapter 11 v1.2 is likewise "defined only when the direct new input is greater than 0," so the two agree.)

Use of Reentry: Field Chapter 11 v1.2 adopts this chapter's re-entry ratio as an input to the Continuation Competition Index family CI; Reentry is not a component of familiarity, and the comparison of familiarity relies only on Sedimentation-Degree δ (Field Chapter 11 v1.2; Field Chapter 1, Definition 13).

Relation to Field Chapter 7: J in Field Chapter 7 v1.2 is the local Resolution Flow (migration of sedimentation, J = −r·∇ρ + γχ·∇M), and ∇·J(x\_i) \< 0 means neighboring sedimentation flows into x\_i; Reentry is the re-entry ratio of subsequent flow. The two are different objects: inflow by migration of sedimentation is not the same as re-entry of subsequent flow; this chapter does not measure Reentry by ∇·J, and their relation is yet to be set up.

Demonstration hypothesis H\_F of Field Chapter 9 v1.2 (direct reference):

Flow(x\_i) = k\_F·δ(x\_i, t)·σ(G(x\_i, t))

where σ(G) = max(G,0)/(max(G,0)+η) (Field Chapter 7’s gating function). Per Field Chapter 9 v1.2: this formula is a demonstration hypothesis and does not appear in Field Chapter 7; with k\_F and G the same, Flow is non-decreasing in δ, strictly increasing for a common G \> 0, and cutoff requires δ to be bounded; it may not be cited as the deep necessarily occupying more flow. Precedence follows separately Field Chapter 1 Law 5 (no later under C₌; strictly first under C₌ and P₁₇).

Under the comparison conditions of H\_F, a deep Domain receives no less flow than a shallow one; whether repeated traversal of old paths deepens them follows Field Chapter 1 Equation 2 (Dτ \> Eλ). Meanwhile, the higher the total LoopCoupling from surrounding Domains directed toward x\_i, the more easily other Domains, once activated, re-channel flows toward x\_i, further strengthening re-entry. This chapter takes the following positive feedback as hypothesis H\_loop, declared link by link (cf. H\_fb of Field Chapter 11 v1.2); Flow↑ does not imply a rise of the re-entry ratio Reentry, and each link must be verified separately; the gating conclusion of H\_F directly constrains only Flow and does not constitute a proof of an upper bound for the whole feedback chain:

δ(x\_i) ↑ → Flow(x\_i) ↑ → Reentry(x\_i) ↑ → δ(x\_i) ↑

This is called: Single-Domain Re-entry.

Therefore: a Deep Nucleus is not static sedimentation. A Deep Nucleus is: a depth structure that, by deepening sedimentation, more easily guides subsequent continuation flows.

1\. Multi-Domain Closure Loop

Let LoopCoupling(x\_i, x\_j) denote the Loop Coupling Degree between local Domains x\_i and x\_j—the bidirectional stable channeling intensity whereby activating x\_i leads to activating x\_j, and activating x\_j in turn leads to re-activating x\_i. LoopCoupling(x\_i, x\_j) ≥ 0, and LoopCoupling(x\_i, x\_j) = LoopCoupling(x\_j, x\_i) (symmetric).

Note: the symmetry assumption is the minimal computable approximation. Flows in the field are inherently directional; the channeling intensity from x\_i → x\_j need not equal that from x\_j → x\_i. In the complete model, Closure should be represented by directed LoopCoupling(x\_i → x\_j); this chapter adopts the symmetric form as the minimal closure approximation. In asymmetric-flow scenarios (e.g., structures dominated by unidirectional Familiar Routes), directed LoopCoupling(x\_i → x\_j) ≠ LoopCoupling(x\_j → x\_i); this is left to subsequent chapters.

If multiple Domains persistently channel flows to each other, then once a Domain is activated, it more easily re-activates preceding Domains. Thus: flows begin to form locally cycling continuation.

For example: x\_a → x\_b → x\_c → x\_a

If this cycle is stable over the long term, subsequent flows preferentially re-enter the cycle, and the cycle gradually forms a closed-loop structure.

Relation of LoopCoupling(x\_i, x\_j) to Field Chapter 7’s J: LoopCoupling describes the degree to which subsequent flows form stable closed circuits among multiple Domains; J of Field Chapter 7 is a flow of sedimentation migration, the two are different objects, and LoopCoupling is not defined by J.

Therefore: Closure is not the deepening of a single Domain; Closure is the formation of a stable re-entry ring among multiple Domains (Field Chapter 1 Equation 16). A cycle being stable over the long term and subsequent flows preferentially re-entering it is a proposed mechanism of closed-loop formation; the holding of Closure must be verified separately by Equation 16.

1\. Familiar Route Flow Locking

If a path persistently carries re-entry flow and deepening exceeds shallowing (Field Chapter 1 Equation 2), the sedimentation of this path deepens; the precedence of a deeper route follows Field Chapter 1 Law 5. Familiar Route Flow Locking is judged by Familiar-Route Locking in Definition 6 of Form Chapter 20 v1.2: a Familiar Route (Field Chapter 1, Definition 13) to which subsequent flow goes uniquely first in each declared sub-segment of the window, this unique precedence depending on its sedimentation difference (Continuation Bias B). The unique precedence and its dependence on the sedimentation difference must be verified in each sub-segment and do not follow from deepening.

Within a declared window in which the Familiar-Route Locking criterion above is met, subsequent flow goes uniquely first to that Familiar Route in each sub-segment. This judgment does not automatically extend to new entries, Pulses or future windows, nor does it by itself imply that new routes are suppressed, that the Familiar Route keeps deepening, or that Closure Loops are reinforced; these results must each be verified.

Thus (interpretive): the majority of stable structures in reality are, at their core, the result of Familiar Route Flow Locking.

1\. Co-Nucleus Closure Loop

If multiple Domains persistently share re-entry flow, their overlapping portions may deepen persistently; the formation of a Co-Nucleus follows Field Chapter 1 Equation 7 (Co-Nucleus Formation Equation).

After a Co-Nucleus forms, under the conditions of Field Chapter 1 Law 9 (the initial Co-Nucleus no shallower than each edge, the superposition premise P\_叠, non-saturation) the Co-Nucleus is deeper than each edge, and under C₌ and P₁₇ it meets the out-flow condition first (Law 9, Theorem 17; Field Chapter 3 v1.5); more converging flow or a larger share does not follow from this. Thus the Co-Nucleus can serve as the re-entry center of local flows (proposed).

A Co-Nucleus can take part in multi-Domain re-entry structures; this is this chapter's proposed mechanism. The formation of a Co-Nucleus and its out-flow precedence do not by themselves imply a stable Closure Loop. Whether a Closure forms must be verified separately by Field Chapter 1 Equation 16.

1\. Why Closure Is Stable

This chapter proposes hypothesis H\_IR: within the declared closed-loop structure, measurement conventions and conditions of application, the comparison of internal re-entry flow and external disruption is taken as the flow criterion of maintenance and fracture.

Let:

InternalReentry(t) denote the total re-entry flow of the declared closed-loop structure at time t, i.e., the sum of R\_abs(x\_i, t) over the Domains of the structure with declared weights (with the same dimension as ExternalDisruption; not a sum of re-entry ratios, nor the sedimentation migration ∇·J of Field Chapter 7). The set of Domains aggregated, the weights and the counting rule of re-entry events must be declared in advance; they are kept unchanged during comparison, and the weights are not adjusted according to the result to be judged. ExternalDisruption uses the same time window and a compatible unit of measurement.

ExternalDisruption(t) denote the total intensity of external disruption acting on the field at time t (corresponding to the disruption component of I(x,t) in Field Chapter 7—i.e., the total new Pulse input not propagating along existing Familiar Routes).

Under hypothesis H\_IR, if:

InternalReentry(t) \> ExternalDisruption(t)

then: Closure is maintained (flow criterion).

Under hypothesis H\_IR, if:

InternalReentry(t) ≤ ExternalDisruption(t)

then: Closure begins to fracture (flow criterion).

Standing note: the comparison above is this chapter's flow criterion, declared as hypothesis H\_IR. The holding of Closure follows Field Chapter 1 Equation 16; the structural judgment of Closure maintenance and breakdown follows Field Chapter 4 v1.2 (return-bearing group, finite return cut set, bridge hypotheses H\_c and H\_b). The relation of H\_IR to the judgment of Field Chapter 4 is to be proven.

Therefore (interpretive): Closure stability is, at its core, dynamic equilibrium between re-entry continuation and external disruption.

1\. Closure and Reality Continuation

(This whole section is interpretive.)

Closed-loop structures can provide one explanation of reality's continuation; this chapter does not prove that they are a necessary condition or the only mechanism. Reality persists not because it is fixed and unchanging; closed-loop structures can continuously re-enter, lock flow, compress and continue.

Thus (interpretive): reality acquires long-term continuation capacity.

Therefore: reality’s stability is not static stability. Reality’s stability is, at its core, the dynamic stability produced by large numbers of Closures persistently continuing.

1\. Closure Over-Dominance

This chapter proposes: where mechanisms of old-loop occupation and suppression of new structures are separately supported, closed loops may hinder the entry of new differences and the stabilization of new fracturing. Loss of resolution must still be verified separately under the conditions listed in Proposition 5.

Therefore (conditional interpretation, see Proposition 5): Closure, while maintaining reality’s continuation, can also suppress the stabilization of new fracturing.

Thus: a natural tension exists between reality’s stability and reality’s renewal.

1\. Closure Fracture

If: external Pulses continuously strengthen; tuning begins to destabilize; sedimentation degree begins to fall; new differences continuously enter; then: previously stable Closures begin to exhibit flow leakage, boundary fracture, weakening re-entry, and Familiar Route breakage. Thus: Closure gradually loses its capacity to lock subsequent flows.

This is called: Closure Fracture. The causes above are proposed inducing factors; the structural judgment of Closure breakdown follows Field Chapter 4 v1.2, Theorem 9 (finite return cut set, R⁻, H\_b, common interruption condition J, cut efficacy).

After Closure Fracture, new fracturing may grow again (proposed).

1\. Summary Expression

General Closure Dynamics Expression:

Flow criterion of Closure maintenance under hypothesis H\_IR (not to be read independently as a stability condition already proven by Field Chapter 1 or Field Chapter 4):

InternalReentry(t) \> ExternalDisruption(t)

And:

Reentry(x\_i, t) is jointly affected by δ(x\_i, t)·σ(G(x\_i, t)) and the total LoopCoupling directed toward x\_i from surrounding Domains (under H\_F and H\_loop; this chapter does not prove the positive correlation)

And:

LoopCoupling ↑ → Reentry ↑ → Closure stability tends to strengthen (a proposed mechanism)

Closure stability tends to strengthen → LoopCoupling ↑ (positive feedback, a proposed mechanism)

Gating constraint: in H\_F, if k\_F is fixed and δ is bounded, then Flow → 0 as σ(G) → 0. This conclusion constrains only Flow in this model; this chapter has not proven that R\_abs, the re-entry ratio Reentry or LoopCoupling are thereby bounded, nor that the strengthening of Closure necessarily stops or tends to a steady state (example: with R\_abs constantly 1 and direct new input 1/n, the re-entry ratio is n). The relevant conclusions must be proven after setting up the corresponding relations and conditions.

Therefore (under H\_IR and the proposed positive feedback): the more stable the inter-Domain re-entry, the more stable the Closure.

Thus (interpretive): reality is not an accidental structure after dispersing without limit. Reality is: the dynamic stable structure long-term maintained by the field—under finite separability conditions—through re-entry, flow locking, and closed-loop continuation.

1\. Theorems

Proposition 1: Single-Domain Re-entry (under H\_F and H\_loop)

Under the comparison conditions of demonstration hypothesis H\_F of Field Chapter 9 v1.2 (k\_F and G the same), Flow(x\_i) is non-decreasing in δ, and strictly increasing for a common G \> 0; Reentry(x\_i, t) is further affected by the total LoopCoupling directed toward x\_i from surrounding Domains. δ ↑ → Flow ↑ → Reentry ↑ → δ ↑ is the positive-feedback hypothesis H\_loop declared link by link; each link of the positive feedback must be declared and tested separately, and the gating conclusion of H\_F directly constrains only Flow and does not constitute a proof of an upper bound for the whole feedback chain.

Note: the first link follows H\_F (not to be cited as the deep necessarily occupying more flow); in the second link Flow↑ does not imply a rise of the re-entry ratio Reentry, which depends further on the relation between LoopCoupling and R\_abs; in the third link, whether convergence deepens δ follows Field Chapter 1 Equation 2 (Dτ \> Eλ). v1.1 called this a theorem, its proof citing "Field Chapter 9's Flow ∝ δσ(G)" as an established relation and saying that the I\_eff term of Field Chapter 7's main equation makes ∂δ/∂t \> 0 (the main equation of Field Chapter 7 is a demonstration model); this version makes it a proposition under H\_F and H\_loop.

Proposition 2: Multi-Domain Closure Loop Formation (proposed)

If LoopCoupling among multiple Domains persistently rises, stable cycling continuation may form among those Domains and closed-loop structure may gradually stabilize.

Note: this is a proposed formation mechanism. The holding of Closure must be verified separately by Field Chapter 1 Equation 16 (long-term mutual-return continuation holds, and stable continuation structures can repeatedly return); the v1.1 proof inferred closed-loop formation from Theorem 1 and "the positive feedback of Field Chapter 7's main equation," neither of which is proven, so this version makes it a proposed proposition.

Criterion 3: Familiar Route Flow Locking Criterion

Familiar Route Flow Locking is judged by Familiar-Route Locking in Definition 6 of Form Chapter 20 v1.2: a Familiar Route (Field Chapter 1, Definition 13) to which subsequent flow goes uniquely first in each declared sub-segment of the window, this unique precedence depending on its sedimentation difference. That persistent re-entry flow deepens a path's δ requires Dτ \> Eλ (Field Chapter 1 Equation 2); the precedence of a deeper route follows Field Chapter 1 Law 5 (no later under C₌; strictly first under C₌ and P₁₇); the unique precedence and its dependence on the sedimentation difference must be verified in each sub-segment and do not follow from deepening. Familiar Route Flow Locking and the Bias of Field Chapter 1 (Equation 11) are different judgments.

Note: v1.1 called this the "Familiar Route Flow Locking Theorem," its proof inferring locking from "deep δ gives a high matching rate M, high M gives a larger I\_eff, and subsequent flows preferentially propagate in the direction of high I\_eff"; per Field Chapter 7 v1.2, overlap M concerns only the amount of effective input after entry and does not decide precedence, and I\_eff is further gated by remaining capacity (becoming small as a deep region approaches capacity); that inference is withdrawn and a criterion is set up instead.

Criterion 4: Closure Flow Criterion (hypothesis H\_IR)

On a declared closed-loop structure, this chapter takes InternalReentry(t) \> ExternalDisruption(t) as the flow criterion of Closure maintenance, and the reverse as the flow criterion of the onset of Closure fracture (hypothesis H\_IR).

Note: InternalReentry is taken as a weighted sum of R\_abs, with the same dimension as ExternalDisruption. The holding of Closure follows Field Chapter 1 Equation 16; the structural judgment of Closure maintenance follows Field Chapter 4 v1.2, Theorem 8 (return-bearing group, H\_c), and that of breakdown follows Theorem 9 (finite return cut set, H\_b, common interruption condition J, cut efficacy). The relation of H\_IR to these is to be proven. v1.1 called this the "Closure Stability Theorem," its proof inferring Closure maintenance from ∇·J \< 0 of Field Chapter 7 (migration of sedimentation, not re-entry of subsequent flow) and "the main equation's stability condition" (now Criterion 6, a finite-window stability condition on the Sedimentation-Degree of an existing nucleus); that does not hold, and this version makes it a declared criterion.

Proposition 5: Closure Over-Dominance (conditional interpretation)

If InternalReentry greatly exceeds ExternalDisruption, subsequent flows may find it hard to leave old loops, new differences hard to enter, and new fracturing hard to stabilize.

Note: this is a conditional interpretation, depending on H\_IR and hypothesis H\_occ of Theorem 5 of Field Chapter 8 v1.2 (old nuclei occupying resolution resources, the mechanism of suppression unproven; a decline of separability is not the same as a rise of θ\_sep); whether the field tends toward low resolution must be verified separately by Resolution Collapse as delimited by the long-term separable set in Field Chapter 9 v1.2. The v1.1 proof said "new Pulses’ I\_eff largely flows into old loops due to high matching with old paths"; per Field Chapter 7 v1.2 this does not hold and is withdrawn.

Proposition 6: Closure Fracture (proposed)

External Pulses strengthening, tuning destabilizing, δ falling, or new differences persistently entering are proposed inducing factors for Closure losing its capacity to lock subsequent flows. The structural judgment of Closure breakdown follows Field Chapter 4 v1.2, Theorem 9: in the demonstration model and under H\_b, if a finite return cut set X has its paths in R⁻ from a common starting point, satisfies the common interruption condition J, and remains a cut set throughout the corresponding interval, Closure loses stable re-entry. That the inducing factors lead to the interruption of a cut set must be proven separately.

Note: the v1.1 proof chain "ExternalDisruption rises → the condition reverses → Field Chapter 7's degradation terms dominate and δ falls → Flow↓ (Field Chapter 9) → Reentry↓ → δ falls further" depends on the unproven H\_IR, the degradation terms of a demonstration model and H\_F, and the reversed positive feedback is unproven; this version makes it a proposed proposition. Whether new fracturing grows again after Familiar Routes break is also proposed.
