# Compression Dynamics Equations

1\. Purpose

The preceding chapters have established:

\- why the field has resolution;  
\- why differentials cannot be sustained without limit;  
\- why a Domain reaches Margin Exhaustion (Field Chapter 12 v1.2; Margin Exhaustion is not Domain Fullness in Field Chapter 1, Definition 20);  
\- under what conditions a Boundary Saturation Outgrowth candidate obtains;  
\- why new structures fracture outward from boundaries.

Yet the preceding chapters have not truly explained: why compression occurs when the field is overloaded.

Why:

\- differentials tend to merge into existing Domains;  
\- Familiar Routes absorb fine subdivisions;  
\- Co-Nuclei continuously form;  
\- the world tends toward finite stable structures;  
\- unlimited unfolding cannot be sustained long-term.

The starting viewpoint of this chapter (interpretation): if the field could independently sustain all differentials without limit, it would not need to form shared structures through compression; whatever in Familiar Routes, Co-Nuclei, Closure, and stable reality depends on compression would then remain unexplained.

Therefore this chapter discusses: compression as a dynamic of one of the paths of overload release.

Per Field Chapter 1, Law 21 and Equation 15: if capacity is exceeded over long durations, the field enters load-release conditions, which may manifest as Compression, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, Resolution Loss, or their combination, the actual release path being jointly constrained by field state. This chapter discusses the compression path among them and does not assert that compression is the only or a necessary mode of release.

1\. Core Proposition

Compression is not human classification, nor simplification of observation.

Compression truly means: multiple differentials that originally required independent maintenance begin sharing stable maintenance relations.

Per Field Chapter 1, Definition 35, Compression (Compress) means multiple Structural Positions, unable to stably differentiate, merge into a common Domain; Compression is not deletion and not Resolution Loss (Compress ≠ LossRes). At the Domain level this chapter expresses that merging as “multiple differentials that originally required independent maintenance begin sharing stable maintenance relations.” Shared maintenance in this chapter is a candidate manifestation of compression; compression cannot be certified from a sharing relation alone. Only when the merging condition of Field Chapter 1, Definition 35, is separately met is that shared maintenance counted in the Compression Intensity K\_f.

Therefore (proposed in this chapter): if differentials continuously increase while the field’s stable maintenance capacity is finite, the field enters load-release conditions; compression is one path among them, and Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, Resolution Loss, or their combination may also occur (Field Chapter 1, Law 21).

Therefore (interpretation): compression is not merely a consequence; in a finite field it is one dynamic of load release.

1\. Compression Formation

Let Domain f contain fa₁, fa₂, fa₃…

This chapter proposes: as differentials continuously increase, overlap, interference, sharing, proximity, and convergence among differentials may continuously increase.

Thus (proposed): the field’s independent maintenance pressure for all differentials rises. Its growth comes not only from increasing differential count but also from rising overlap complexity among differentials; the more complex the overlap, the higher the cost the field must bear to keep all differentials independently stable—this is a hypothesis of this chapter, expressed by the illustrative formula below.

Let:

P\_s(f,t) denote the Differential Stability Maintenance Pressure of Domain f at time t—the amount of stable maintenance the field requires to independently sustain all differentials within Domain f without compression. To be comparable with SDC\_f of Field Chapter 12 v1.2, P\_s uses the same declared unit as SDC\_f. P\_s(f,t) ≥ 0. P\_s is the demand for independent maintenance and by definition does not decrease through compression; the actual burden after compression is written separately as the Effective Maintenance Pressure P\_eff (Section 4).

Illustrative formula (a model choice of this chapter, not derived from upstream):

P\_s(f,t) = u·N\_f(t)·(1 + κ·O\_f(t))

where u \> 0 is the occupancy of each stable differential in Field Chapter 12 v1.2, κ ≥ 0 is a declared overlap-cost coefficient, N\_f(t) is the differential count in Domain f, and O\_f(t) ∈ \[0,1\] is the average overlap among differentials in Domain f:

O\_f(t) = (1/(N\_f(N\_f−1))) · Σ\_{i≠j} Overlap(fa\_i, fa\_j) (N\_f ≥ 2; O\_f = 0 when N\_f \< 2)

where Overlap(fa\_i, fa\_j) ∈ \[0,1\] is a declared pairwise overlap of differentials. It differs from Field Chapter 7’s M(x,t): M is the overlap-match rate between input structure and existing sedimentation structure, not the pairwise overlap among existing differentials; their relation is not established in this chapter. When κ \> 0, the more differentials and the stronger the overlap, the higher P\_s. v1.1 wrote P\_s ∝ N\_f·O\_f: with zero overlap P\_s would be zero and would not increase with differential count, which contradicts “increasing as differential count increases and overlap complexity rises”, and a proportionality cannot be compared with SDC\_f; this version uses the formula above, which for κ = 0 gives P\_s = u·N\_f, the sum of the separate independent occupancies of all differentials. The precise functional form is left to subsequent chapters.

Distinction from Field Chapter 12’s P\_Δ(f,t): P\_Δ (Boundary Differential Pressure) in Field Chapter 12 v1.2 is the weighted boundary integral of the unabsorbed input R, used in the Boundary Saturation Outgrowth candidacy criterion (Criterion 2, requiring both R\_f ≤ 0 and P\_Δ \> B\_f); this chapter’s P\_s is the demand for independent maintenance of all differentials within the Domain, used in the compression candidacy criterion. The two act at different levels: P\_s within the Domain, P\_Δ at the boundary; their quantitative relation is not established in this chapter.

Relation to Field Chapter 12’s S\_f(t): S\_f(t) (Compression Saturation) is the amount of differential maintenance occupied by Familiar Route compression, Co-Nucleus consolidation, and Closure locking, in the same declared unit as SDC\_f. P\_s is the independent-maintenance demand, S\_f the occupied amount. This chapter’s directional reading (a hypothesis): when P\_s persistently exceeds SDC\_f and compression occurs, S\_f tends to rise. The two differ in meaning: P\_s is demand, S\_f is occupancy.

SDC\_f(t) denotes the Stable Differentiation Capacity of Domain f at time t (directly referencing Field Chapter 12’s SDC\_f(t)), in the same declared unit as P\_s.

Then (Criterion 1, compression candidacy):

P\_s(f,t) \> SDC\_f(t) ⇒ Domain f becomes a compression candidate (load-release condition)

Note: P\_s \> SDC\_f means that, if all differentials were each independently maintained, the requirement would exceed the Domain’s stable maintenance capacity; this chapter takes it as the Domain-level counterpart reading of Field Chapter 1, Equation 15 (Use \> 1) (a proposed correspondence; the quantities used differ). Per Law 21, the release path may then be Compression, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, Resolution Loss, or their combination; this criterion yields only a compression candidate, and whether and to what extent compression actually occurs is judged separately by structural criteria (Field Chapter 1, Definition 35).

Therefore: compression is not the disappearance of differentials (Field Chapter 1, Definition 35: Compression is not deletion, not Resolution Loss). Compression is: multiple differentials beginning to share stable maintenance relations.

1\. Compression Dynamic

Let:

K\_f(t) denote the Compression Intensity of Domain f at time t—the degree to which differentials in the Domain have entered shared maintenance certified by Field Chapter 1, Definition 35, 0 ≤ K\_f(t) ≤ 1; its reading must be declared (for example, as the proportion of differentials that have entered certified shared maintenance; when read as a proportion of a finite number of differentials, the continuous dynamic equation of Section 4 is its continuous approximation). Counting rule (declared): the identities of merged objects are kept according to a declared register of differentials; merging does not reduce N\_f, nor is a merged object counted as another item of the original register; additions, departures from the Domain, and other reorganizations are booked separately. K\_f = 0 in general means only that no object in the register is counted as certified compression, and means that all are independently maintained only under the dichotomy condition (Section 8); K\_f = 1 means only that all counted objects have entered shared maintenance and does not entail a sparse-core structure (sparse cores are proposed in Section 9). Under the dichotomy condition and comparison with the same N\_f, the higher K\_f, the fewer differentials are independently maintained in the Domain (Section 8); whether the shared structure is stable is checked separately.

Relation to SDC\_f(t) (model of this chapter): the actual maintenance burden after compression is written as the Effective Maintenance Pressure P\_eff(f,t) = Φ(P\_s(f,t), K\_f(t)). This chapter’s hypothesis H\_eff: Φ is continuous, Φ(P\_s, 0) = P\_s, and Φ is nonincreasing in K\_f (shared maintenance does not increase the maintenance burden; H\_eff admits Φ ≡ P\_s, in which case there is no pressure-reducing feedback). v1.1 stated that “Compression Intensity K\_f rises until enough differentials are compressed into shared structures to bring the effective maintenance pressure back within SDC\_f”: whether K\_f can bring P\_eff back within SDC\_f depends on Φ; if Φ(P\_s, 1) \> SDC\_f, K\_f tends to 1 while P\_eff still exceeds it (Theorem 2). When K\_f rises, Ind\_f(t) falls under the dichotomy condition with N\_f unchanged (see Section 8).

Then:

P\_eff(f,t) \> SDC\_f(t) and K\_f(t) \< 1 ⇒ dK\_f/dt \> 0 (in the model below)

where the illustrative Compression Dynamic Equation (a model choice of this chapter) is:

dK\_f/dt = a·(1 − K\_f(t))·max(0, P\_eff(f,t) − SDC\_f(t))

where a \> 0 is a declared constant. When K\_f = 0, P\_eff = P\_s, so the onset of compression is judged by P\_s \> SDC\_f (Criterion 1). Taking Φ ≡ P\_s (without the feedback of H\_eff) returns the v1.1 equation dK\_f/dt ∝ (1 − K\_f(t))·max(0, P\_s(f,t) − SDC\_f(t)).

Saturation term note: when the effective excess max(0, P\_eff − SDC\_f) is bounded, the (1 − K\_f) factor makes dK\_f/dt → 0 as K\_f → 1; this restriction is met under constant parameters and the conditions of Theorem 2, whereas for a time-varying and unbounded excess the vanishing of the rate cannot be inferred from this factor alone. If K\_f(0) ∈ \[0,1\], then K\_f(t) ∈ \[0,1\] (Theorem 2), closing with the definition of K\_f. In this equation dK\_f/dt ≥ 0: K\_f only increases; compression fracture and recovery (K\_f ↓) are not in this illustrative model and require a separately introduced decrease term (Sections 10 and 11 are proposed).

Therefore (in this model, under comparison at the same K\_f): the more the Effective Maintenance Pressure exceeds SDC\_f, the faster compression strengthens. This chapter proposes: compression serves to reduce independent maintenance cost. v1.1’s “if all differentials demand independent stability, maintenance cost, overlap complexity, and maintenance pressure would continuously explode, making the field unable to remain stable long-term” is unproven and withdrawn.

1\. Compression and Familiar Routes

If a Familiar Route persistently occupies large quantities of subsequent flow (this is a premise; Theorem 1 of Field Chapter 11 v1.2 gives only that Familiar Routes come out first, not long-term flow shares), this chapter proposes: large numbers of stable maintenance relations already exist within it, and new differentials may need not re-establish independent stable structures but be absorbed into existing stable structures (hypothesis H\_reuse, see Proposition 4).

Therefore (proposed): Familiar Routes are not merely stable paths; they may also be the reuse cores of stable maintenance relations.

1\. Compression and Co-Nuclei

If multiple differentials long-term share the same stable maintenance relation, a stable shared core may form among them. Per Field Chapter 1, Definition 15 and Equation 7, a Co-Nucleus (Core) obtains only with long-term co-Trace-Effects and subsequent stable co-recurrence; a shared maintenance relation by itself does not constitute a Co-Nucleus (“Co-occurrence≠Co-Nucleus; co-continuation≠Co-Nucleus”). This chapter calls a set of differentials long-term sharing the same stable maintenance relation a shared maintenance structure; whether it becomes a Co-Nucleus is judged by Definition 15 and Equation 7; Field Chapter 3 v1.5 is the dynamical development of the Co-Nucleus.

Connection to Outer Chapter 3, the Numericology: the Numericology says: “If f1 and f2 intersect at domain fa, then fa necessarily indicates the commonality of s1 and s2. This is induction fallen into the field.” fa is the domain of commonality formed by the overlap of two signals’ sedimentation domains; this chapter’s shared maintenance structure is the structure formed when multiple differentials long-term share stable maintenance relations. This chapter proposes: a Co-Nucleus can be viewed as the structural expression of fa at the compression-dynamics level; both are mechanical realizations of “induction fallen into the field”—commonality is not an a priori abstraction but a structural result in the sedimentation field. This correspondence is an interpretation.

Therefore (interpretation): Co-Nuclei are not abstract generalizations. Co-Nuclei are: the structural result of long-term co-Trace-Effects and stable co-recurrence, of which compression may be one source.

1\. Compression and Reality Conservatism

This chapter proposes: reality tends toward stability not merely because Familiar Routes exist, but also because reality continuously compresses new differentials.

Therefore (proposed): large numbers of new differences may get compressed into old classes; into old nuclei; into old Familiar Routes; into old reality structures.

Thus (interpretation): reality gradually forms stable conservatism. The Numericology says: “Beyond its capacity, lateral branches of things can no longer be incorporated, nor can novelty be explored. One thereby becomes fixed upon what one already holds.”

1\. Compression Collapse

This chapter proposes: if compression continuously strengthens, differentials that were originally independently maintained increasingly share the same stable maintenance relation.

Let:

Ind\_f(t) denote the Independent Differential Count of Domain f at time t (note: the original v1.0 used I\_f(t), but I is already defined in Field Chapter 7 as Pulse Input I(x,t); this chapter therefore uses Ind\_f(t)). Ind\_f(t) ≥ 0.

Illustrative relation between Ind\_f and K\_f: only when the objects in the declared register fall exactly into the two classes “independently maintained” and “compressed sharing certified by Field Chapter 1, Definition 35,” the two classes being mutually exclusive and exhaustive (called the dichotomy condition in this chapter), is the following used:

Ind\_f(t) = N\_f(t)·(1 − K\_f(t))

i.e. (under the dichotomy condition), when K\_f = 0, Ind\_f = N\_f (all differentials independently maintained); when K\_f = 1, Ind\_f = 0 (all differentials enter certified shared maintenance). With N\_f unchanged, dInd\_f/dt = −N\_f·dK\_f/dt; when N\_f changes, there is an additional term (1 − K\_f)·dN\_f/dt. If the register contains uncertified shared maintenance, this chapter does not read the number of independently maintained differentials from this equation: for example, with a register of 10 items—4 in certified sharing, 3 in uncertified sharing, 3 independently maintained—K\_f = 0.4, while Ind\_f = 3, not 10 × 0.6 = 6. v1.1’s “Equivalent differential form: dInd\_f/dt ∝ −dK\_f/dt” holds only with N\_f unchanged; this version adds that condition. The precise functional form is left to subsequent chapters.

Then (under the dichotomy condition with N\_f unchanged):

dK\_f/dt \> 0 ⇒ dInd\_f/dt \< 0

Therefore (interpretation): the stronger the compression, the fewer differentials are independently maintained in the Domain, and the more the field tends toward low-resolution structures. “Low resolution” here is an interpretation: it is not Resolution Collapse in Field Chapter 9 v1.2 (delimited by the long-term separable set), not Resolution-Margin Collapse in Field Chapter 12 v1.2 (a persistent fall of R\_f), and not Resolution Loss in Field Chapter 1, Definition 34 (Definition 35: Compress ≠ LossRes).

1\. Compression Limit

This chapter proposes: if compression continuously strengthens, the field may tend toward high-degree co-nucleation, with large numbers of differentials sharing few stable maintenance structures.

Thus (proposed): the field gradually forms: few nuclei; few Familiar Routes; high reuse; high stability; high conservatism.

The state K\_f → 1 is called: Compression Limit (this chapter’s operational delimitation). In this chapter’s illustrative model, whether K\_f tends to 1 depends on whether, under H\_eff, P\_eff can fall to no more than SDC\_f (Theorem 2).

1\. Boundary Saturation Outgrowth and Compression Fracture

This chapter proposes: if new differentials continuously enter while old compressed structures can no longer stably bear the load, compression may destabilize, and differentials that previously shared the same stable maintenance relation fracture apart again.

Therefore (proposed): destabilization of compressed structures may accompany Boundary Saturation Outgrowth. A Boundary Saturation Outgrowth candidate follows Criterion 2 of Field Chapter 12 v1.2 (R\_f ≤ 0 and P\_Δ \> B\_f); certification follows Field Chapter 1, Definition 21 and Equation 10 (Domain Fullness already established, new Trace-Effects unable to continue internal sinking, overflow along the Domain boundary, generation of new Structural Positions). v1.1’s “Overflow Sedimentation is also: the fracture of old compressed structures” equates Boundary Saturation Outgrowth with compression fracture and is withdrawn: they are different judgments; compression fracture does not by itself constitute Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, nor does Boundary Saturation Outgrowth by itself prove compression fracture.

1\. Compression Recovery

This chapter proposes: if old compressed structures begin to loosen, the field re-acquires the capacity to independently sustain new differentials, and previously compressed differences may re-form fine subdivisions (subject to the necessary condition of Theorem 1 of Field Chapter 12 v1.2 and the separability condition).

Therefore (interpretation): falling compression does not mean structures disappear—it means the field has re-acquired independent differential maintenance capacity. Falling compression is not in the illustrative model of Section 4 (in that model dK\_f/dt ≥ 0).

Co-Nucleus ↑ ⇒ Familiar Route stability ↑ (proposed)

Feedback chain (a conditional proposal): if a rise in P\_s makes P\_eff actually exceed SDC\_f and K\_f \< 1, K\_f grows (at K\_f = 1 the rate is still zero); as K\_f grows, P\_eff does not increase under comparison with P\_s unchanged, and falls when Φ is strictly decreasing on the relevant interval. The effect of a change in P\_s on Φ is not declared in this chapter, so this chain is not a proved result of the model. The equilibrium condition of Theorem 2 concerns the autonomous model with P\_s and SDC\_f unchanged; when they vary over time, if the global solution still stays in \[0,1\] and is nondecreasing, K\_f still has a limit, but whether the effective pressure converges, and what equilibrium condition the limit satisfies, must be proved separately.

1\. Theorems

Criterion 1: Compression Candidacy Criterion

This chapter takes “P\_s(f,t) \> SDC\_f(t)” as the condition for Domain f to become a compression candidate.

Note: this is a declared condition. P\_s \> SDC\_f means only that, if all differentials were each independently maintained, the requirement would exceed the Domain’s stable maintenance capacity; per Field Chapter 1, Law 21 and Equation 15, load release may manifest as Compression, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, Resolution Loss, or their combination, the actual path being jointly constrained by field state. In the illustrative model of Section 4, dK\_f/dt \> 0 when K\_f \< 1 and P\_eff \> SDC\_f; only at K\_f = 0, since Φ(P\_s, 0) = P\_s, can P\_s \> SDC\_f be used to judge the onset of compression. P\_s exceeding the capacity does not by itself guarantee that existing compression keeps strengthening: for example, with Φ(P\_s, K) = P\_s·(1 − K), which satisfies H\_eff, and P\_s = 2, SDC\_f = 1, K\_f = 0.75, we get P\_eff = 0.5 and a zero rate. Compression candidacy (P\_s \> SDC\_f) and the actual rate are different judgments. Growth within the model is compression within the model, not a proof that compression necessarily occurs in every finite field. v1.1 Theorem 1 (Compression Inevitability Theorem) is withdrawn: its proof—“if compression does not occur, independent maintenance costs continuously accumulate and Field Chapter 7’s degradation terms (−q₀δ − β·φδ) dominate in each Domain, causing differential structures to collapse unstably”—does not hold: the main equation of Field Chapter 7 v1.2 is an illustrative model whose degradation terms have no established relation to this chapter’s maintenance demand; and per Law 21, compression is not the only release path.

Theorem 2: Boundedness and Convergence of Compression Dynamics (within the illustrative model, under H\_eff)

Let the illustrative model of Section 4 hold: dK\_f/dt = a·(1 − K\_f)·max(0, Φ(P\_s, K\_f) − SDC\_f), a \> 0; P\_s and SDC\_f constant; Φ continuous and nonincreasing in K\_f (H\_eff), making the right-hand side locally Lipschitz in K\_f; K\_f(0) ∈ \[0,1\]. Then: (a) K\_f(t) ∈ \[0,1\] and is monotonically nondecreasing in t; (b) K\_f(t) converges to some K\* ∈ \[0,1\], with K\* = 1 or Φ(P\_s, K*) ≤ SDC\_f; (c) if Φ(P\_s, 1) \> SDC\_f, then K* = 1; (d) at the same K\_f \< 1, the rate a·(1 − K\_f)·max(0, Φ − SDC\_f) is nondecreasing in the excess Φ − SDC\_f, and strictly increasing when the excess is positive.

Proof sketch: (a) the right-hand side is ≥ 0, so K\_f is nondecreasing; K\_f ≡ 1 is a solution, so by uniqueness a solution with K\_f(0) ≤ 1 does not cross 1. (b) Monotone and bounded, hence convergent; call the limit K*. If K* \< 1 and Φ(P\_s, K*) \> SDC\_f, by continuity there exist ε \> 0 and T such that the right-hand side is ≥ ε for t \> T, so K\_f(t) ≥ K\_f(T) + ε(t − T) is unbounded, a contradiction. (c) Since Φ is nonincreasing in K\_f, Φ(P\_s, K*) ≥ Φ(P\_s, 1) \> SDC\_f, and (b) gives K\* = 1. (d) Read directly from the equation. ∎

Note: (i) The equilibrium conditions listed in Theorem 2 concern the autonomous model with P\_s and SDC\_f unchanged. When they vary over time, if the global solution still stays in \[0,1\] and is nondecreasing, K\_f still has a limit; but whether the effective pressure converges, and what equilibrium condition the limit satisfies, must be proved separately. (ii) In this model dK\_f/dt ≥ 0 and K\_f cannot fall; compression fracture and recovery require a separately introduced decrease term. (iii) If Φ ≡ P\_s (the v1.1 equation, without feedback) and P\_s \> SDC\_f, then K\* = 1. The proof of v1.1 Theorem 2 stated that “compression growth stabilizes at the level where effective maintenance pressure returns to SDC\_f”; this does not hold in the v1.1 equation (P\_s does not vary with K\_f) and is withdrawn; equilibrium is now read under H\_eff.

Proposition 3: Expansion of the Shared Maintenance Structure (proposed)

When K\_f persistently rises, Ind\_f falls under the dichotomy condition with N\_f unchanged, and more differentials share the same stable maintenance structure; this shared maintenance structure may develop into a Co-Nucleus.

Note: the fall of Ind\_f is read from the illustrative relation of Section 8 (only under the dichotomy condition); the rest is proposed. A Co-Nucleus obtains per Field Chapter 1, Definition 15 and Equation 7 (long-term co-Trace-Effects and subsequent stable co-recurrence); Field Chapter 3 v1.5 is its dynamical development, and the “Field Chapter 3’s Shared Nucleus stabilization mechanism” cited in v1.1 cannot by itself derive a Co-Nucleus from shared maintenance. v1.1’s “the lower the resolution (consistent with Field Chapter 12’s Resolution Collapse Theorem)” is withdrawn: Resolution-Margin Collapse in Field Chapter 12 v1.2 is a persistent fall of R\_f in a window (Theorem 4 gives its condition), and the relation between K\_f and R\_f is not established in this chapter.

Proposition 4: Familiar Route Reuse (under H\_reuse)

Let hypothesis H\_reuse hold: a new differential entering a Familiar Route reuses its existing stable maintenance relations and does not set up an independent stable structure. If this process is separately certified as compression by Field Chapter 1, Definition 35, and the fixed counting rule of Section 4 is used, then when K\_f \< 1 its shared proportion rises and the number of independently maintained differentials does not increase.

Proof sketch: by the declared register, let m be the number of differentials in certified sharing, K\_f = m/N\_f; independently maintained members are counted directly, without using the complement equation. Let the other members and their states remain unchanged, with no departures or other reorganizations; absorbing one certified new differential increases N\_f and m by 1 each, leaving the number of independently maintained members unchanged. K\_f \< 1 means m \< N\_f, so (m + 1)/(N\_f + 1) \> m/N\_f. ∎

Note: the substantive content of this proposition lies entirely in H\_reuse. The proof chain of v1.1 is withdrawn: (i) Familiarity is re-expressed per Field Chapter 11 v1.2: comparison of familiarity follows the path Sedimentation-Degree statistic δ̄, and overall advantage in continuation competition is measured separately by CI, which is not a degree of familiarity; (ii) “new differentials preferentially enter Familiar Routes through the high-match effect (paths with high M in Field Chapter 7’s I\_eff preferentially receive flow)” does not hold per Field Chapter 7 v1.2: overlap M concerns only the amount of effective input after entry and does not fix precedence, and I\_eff is gated by residual capacity (Field Chapter 8 v1.2, Theorem 5, has already withdrawn the same kind of inference); precedence follows only Field Chapter 1, Law 5, and Field Chapter 11, Theorem 1 (under H\_δ); (iii) “closed-loop structures (Field Chapter 10)”: Closure is checked separately by Field Chapter 1, Equation 16; Familiar Routes do not carry closed loops by themselves.

Proposition 5: Compression Fracture (proposed)

When old compressed structures can no longer stably bear new differentials, old shared structures may fracture, K\_f falls, and new differentials may re-form independent fine subdivisions; this process may accompany Boundary Saturation Outgrowth.

Note: this is proposed; a fall of K\_f is not in the illustrative model of Section 4.
