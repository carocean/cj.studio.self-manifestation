# Interpenetration Dynamics Equations

1\. Purpose

The preceding chapters have established:

\- why the field has resolution;  
\- why resolution reaches a limit;  
\- why compression inevitably occurs;  
\- why Shared Nuclei form;  
\- why Overflow Sedimentation occurs after old compressed structures destabilize.

Yet the preceding chapters have not truly explained: why Vitalities in the sedimentation field will not remain permanently separated from each other.

Why:

\- multiple Vitalities begin to interpenetrate each other;  
\- interpenetration forms Shared Nuclei;  
\- interpenetration increases Continuation Pressure;  
\- excessive interpenetration causes compression to strengthen;  
\- persistent compression strengthening leads to Overflow Sedimentation.

Because: if all Vitalities were always completely isolated, the field would never form: Shared Nuclei; Familiar Route reuse; stable sedimentation; categories; long-term stable reality.

Therefore this chapter discusses: why Vitalities undergo Interpenetration. And thereby establishes: the dynamic relations among Interpenetration, compression, resolution, and Overflow Sedimentation.

1\. Core Proposition

Interpenetration is not noise, nor erroneous mixing.

Interpenetration truly means: multiple Vitalities in the sedimentation field have already begun to be unable to fully separate. Thus their continuation expansions begin to mutually penetrate each other.

Therefore: Interpenetration is neither complete identity nor complete independence. Interpenetration means: partial co-continuation has begun to appear between Vitalities.

Therefore: as long as multiple Vitalities long-term co-exist in the same finite field, they cannot remain completely isolated forever. They will inevitably, through Familiar Routes; depth differences; boundaries; tuning; compression; and re-entry, gradually form varying degrees of Interpenetration.

Differentials do not exist independently prior to Interpenetration. Many stable differentials are themselves the result of gradually stabilizing after long-term mutual interpenetration, compression, and fracture.

1\. Interpenetration Formation

Let V\_i and V\_j denote two Vitalities in Domain f (V stands for Vitality; distinct from Field Chapter 9’s L(t) = Tuning Load).

If the two are repeatedly co-activated in subsequent flows, and their continuation expansion regions begin to persistently interpenetrate, or parts of their continuation maintenance begin to be shared, then Interpenetration begins to form between V\_i and V\_j.

Denoted: Itr(V\_i, V\_j, t) \> 0

where Itr denotes the degree of penetration between the expansion regions of two Vitalities in the sedimentation field—not a connection between two points.

Range and definition of Itr(V\_i, V\_j, t): Itr ≥ 0; when two Vitalities are completely independent, Itr = 0; Itr has no strict upper bound but is constrained by field capacity Λ (interpenetration degree does not exceed total field capacity). Relation to Field Chapter 7’s M(x,t) (Structural Overlap Matching Degree): Itr(V\_i, V\_j, t) corresponds to the domain-level integral of M(x,t) over the x-space sedimentation structures of V\_i and V\_j—regions with higher M are more prone to mutual penetration between V\_i and V\_j’s continuation expansions, corresponding to higher Itr.

Therefore: Interpenetration is not the complete collapse of Vitalities into one another, but the onset of stable mutual penetration among multiple Vitalities.

1\. Total Interpenetration

Let Domain f contain n Vitalities: V₁, V₂, …, Vₙ

Then:

Itr\_f(t) = Σ\_{i\<j} Itr(V\_i, V\_j, t)

Itr\_f(t) is called the Total Interpenetration of Domain f at time t—the sum of pairwise penetration degrees among all Vitalities in the Domain.

Therefore: the higher the Total Interpenetration, the deeper the mutual penetration among Vitalities in the Domain.

1\. Interpenetration Complexity

Interpenetration has not only quantity differences. Different interpenetration structures have different Continuation Pressures.

Let Ω\_f(t) denote the Interpenetration Complexity of Domain f.

If: depth-differential interpenetration increases; boundary-Domain interpenetration increases; nucleus-boundary interpenetration increases; Familiar Route interpenetration increases; Shared Nucleus interpenetration increases; tuning interpenetration increases; compression interpenetration increases; Seepage interpenetration increases; then Continuation Pressure rises markedly.

Therefore: what truly overwhelms resolution is often not Total Interpenetration alone, but the Continuation Pressure jointly formed by Total Interpenetration and Interpenetration Complexity.

This can be written as:

P\_s(f,t) = Ψ(Itr\_f(t), Ω\_f(t))

with: ∂P\_s/∂Itr\_f \> 0, ∂P\_s/∂Ω\_f \> 0

i.e.: Itr\_f ↑ ∧ Ω\_f ↑ ⇒ P\_s ↑

Consistency with Field Chapter 13’s P\_s ∝ N\_f·O\_f: Field Chapter 13’s P\_s ∝ N\_f·O\_f is the minimal computable expansion of this chapter’s P\_s = Ψ(Itr\_f, Ω\_f)—N\_f is the differential count (corresponding to Vitality count), O\_f = (1/(N\_f(N\_f−1)))·Σ\_{i≠j} Overlap(fa\_i, fa\_j) is the average overlap complexity (corresponding to the mean of Itr(V\_i,V\_j,t)—the minimal approximation of Ω\_f). Both describe the same phenomenon: the more differentials/Vitalities and the stronger the overlap, the higher P\_s. This chapter’s Ψ is the structural-level generalization of Field Chapter 13’s N\_f·O\_f.

1\. Interpenetration Growth

Interpenetration growth comes from:

\- multiple Vitalities being repeatedly co-activated;  
\- continuation expansion regions repeatedly interpenetrating;  
\- Familiar Routes continuously absorbing new Vitalities;  
\- tuning continuously pressing toward field tendency;  
\- Closure long-term returning to the same region.

Therefore: Interpenetration is not accidental mixing. Interpenetration is: the natural mutual-penetration phenomenon that forms after multiple Vitalities continuously proceed in the same finite field over the long term.

Dynamic equation (minimal computable approximation):

dItr\_f/dt ∝ Coact(f,t) + Infilt(f,t) + Reentry(f,t) − Sep(f,t)

where:

Coact(f,t) (Co-activation Rate): the frequency with which multiple Vitalities in Domain f are co-activated within the same time window; corresponds to the intensity of Field Chapter 7’s Resolution Flow J simultaneously converging toward multiple V\_i in Domain f. Coact ≥ 0.

Infilt(f,t) (Field Infiltration Rate): the rate of mutual infiltration between continuation expansion regions in Domain f; corresponds to the diffusion intensity of Field Chapter 7’s sedimentation field δ(x,t) across adjacent Vitality regions (analogous to the f·∇²δ diffusion term). Infilt ≥ 0.

Reentry(f,t) (Re-entry Coupling Rate): the frequency with which Closure re-entry causes multiple Vitalities to repeatedly traverse the same Deep Nucleus region in Domain f; directly referencing Field Chapter 10’s Reentry(x\_i,t) weighted-summed at the Domain f level. Reentry ≥ 0.

Sep(f,t) (Separation Rate): the degree to which Vitalities re-separate, corresponding to the rate at which originally interpenetrating Vitalities re-form independent continuation through Overflow Sedimentation, Familiar Route fracture, or tuning refinement. Sep ≥ 0.

After long-term Interpenetration stabilizes, Shared Nuclei begin to deepen. As long as multiple Vitalities long-term co-exist in the same finite field, their interpenetration tendency must continuously grow, because continuation reuse in a finite field continuously promotes: shared continuation; shared tuning; shared compression; shared maintenance formation. Thus Vitalities cannot remain permanently fully separated.

1\. Interpenetration and Continuation Pressure

Increasing Interpenetration produces two opposing effects.

First, moderate Interpenetration: can reduce independent continuation cost, because multiple Vitalities can share part of their continuation maintenance; thus Shared Nuclei begin to form, Familiar Routes begin to be reused, and stable sedimentation forms.

Second, excessive Interpenetration: increases Continuation Pressure, because Vitalities find it increasingly difficult to maintain independent continuation, and the field must persistently sustain the state of being close yet not collapsing—so Continuation Pressure persistently rises.

Therefore: Interpenetration has a dual character—moderate Interpenetration forms Shared Nuclei; excessive Interpenetration overwhelms resolution.

1\. Interpenetration Threshold

Let T\_o(f,t) denote the Interpenetration Threshold that Domain f can stably bear at time t.

Relation to Field Chapter 13’s SDC\_f(t): T\_o(f,t) and SDC\_f(t) describe two levels of carrying capacity for the same Domain—SDC\_f is the stable maintenance capacity at the differential level (how many independent differential relations can be maintained); T\_o is the interpenetration carrying capacity at the Vitality level (how large a Total Interpenetration can be stably borne). The two are directionally consistent (when SDC\_f is high, T\_o also tends to be high), but at different levels: T\_o is at the Vitality level, SDC\_f is at the differential structure level. When Itr\_f exceeds T\_o, P\_s tends to exceed SDC\_f, thereby triggering Field Chapter 13’s compression dynamic.

If Itr\_f(t) ≤ T\_o(f,t), Interpenetration can still be stably maintained; at this point Interpenetration promotes Shared Nucleus formation, Familiar Route reuse, and stable sedimentation.

If Itr\_f(t) \> T\_o(f,t), Interpenetration exceeds the field’s continuation carrying capacity; at this point Vitalities begin to find it hard to maintain independent continuation, so compression strengthens; Domain merging increases; resolution falls; boundary convergence pressure increases.

1\. Interpenetration and Shared Nuclei

If multiple Vitalities long-term share the same portion of continuation maintenance, that portion continuously deepens and gradually forms a Shared Nucleus.

Therefore: Shared Nuclei are not a priori abstractions, nor externally imposed classifications. Shared Nuclei are: the maintenance centers that the field has compressed and deepened after long-term mutual interpenetration.

1\. Interpenetration and Compression

When Interpenetration continuously grows and the field can no longer maintain independent continuation for all Vitalities, compression begins to strengthen.

Therefore: Interpenetration is the antecedent dynamic of compression. Compression is: the stabilization mechanism that forms after Interpenetration exceeds the field’s continuation carrying capacity.

This relation can be written as:

Itr\_f(t) \> T\_o(f,t) ⇒ K\_f(t) ↑

where K\_f(t) is the Compression Intensity (directly referencing Field Chapter 13’s K\_f(t)).

1\. Interpenetration and Resolution

Interpenetration does not necessarily lower resolution. Moderate Interpenetration can form stable Shared Nuclei and Familiar Routes, thereby reducing independent continuation cost.

But: when Interpenetration exceeds the threshold, Vitalities begin to find it hard to stably maintain independent continuation, so the Resolution Margin falls.

Let R\_f(t) denote the Resolution Margin (directly referencing Field Chapter 12’s R\_f(t)).

Then: Itr\_f(t) \> T\_o(f,t) ⇒ R\_f(t) ↓

Therefore: the direct cause of falling resolution is not an increase in Vitality count per se, but Interpenetration Complexity exceeding the field’s continuation carrying capacity.

1\. Interpenetration and Overflow Sedimentation

If Itr\_f(t) \> T\_o(f,t) and new Vitalities continue to enter, Interpenetration accumulation pressure continuously deepens. When old compressed structures can no longer stably absorb this interpenetration accumulation pressure, boundaries begin to persistently form convergence pressure. If the boundary carrying limit is exceeded, Overflow Sedimentation occurs.

Therefore: Overflow Sedimentation is not simple spillover, but the outward fracture resulting from the joint action of interpenetration accumulation pressure, compression saturation, and boundary convergence pressure.

1\. Interpenetration Fracture

If: Familiar Routes fracture; Shared Nuclei loosen; boundaries reactivate; tuning refines again; new Vitalities re-acquire independent continuation capacity; then previously excessive Interpenetration begins to decompose, and Vitalities re-form maintainable independent continuation. This is called: Interpenetration Fracture.

After Interpenetration Fracture: Resolution Margin recovers; Compression Intensity falls; new fine subdivisions form; new structures begin to sediment stably.

1\. Dynamic Equation

Interpenetration dynamic equation:

Itr\_f(t) = Σ\_{i\<j} Itr(V\_i, V\_j, t)

dItr\_f/dt ∝ Coact(f,t) + Infilt(f,t) + Reentry(f,t) − Sep(f,t)

And:

Itr\_f(t) ≤ T\_o(f,t) ⇒ Shared Nucleus ↑

Itr\_f(t) \> T\_o(f,t) ⇒ K\_f ↑

Itr\_f(t) \> T\_o(f,t) ⇒ R\_f ↓

Itr\_f(t) \> T\_o(f,t) ∧ P\_Δ \> B\_f(t) ⇒ Overflow Sedimentation occurs

Interpenetration Fracture ↑ ⇒ Itr\_f(t) ↓

1\. Summary Expression

General Interpenetration Dynamics Expression:

Vitality mutual interpenetration strengthens ⇒ Itr\_f ↑

Itr\_f ≤ T\_o ⇒ Shared Nucleus ↑ → P\_s ↓ (moderate Interpenetration positive path: Shared Nucleus forms, independent continuation cost falls)

Itr\_f ↑ ∧ Ω\_f ↑ ⇒ P\_s ↑

P\_s \> SDC\_f ⇒ K\_f ↑ (compression strengthens; directly referencing Field Chapter 13)

K\_f ↑ ⇒ R\_f ↓ (resolution falls; directly referencing Field Chapter 12)

R\_f ↓ ∧ P\_Δ \> B\_f ⇒ Overflow Sedimentation occurs (directly referencing Field Chapter 12)

Therefore: Interpenetration is not noise. Interpenetration is: the antecedent dynamic of Shared Nuclei, Familiar Routes, compression, categories, and new structure generation.

Stable structures in reality are not the result of isolated differentials forming. They are: the result of multiple Vitalities gradually deepening and stabilizing in the sedimentation field after long-term mutual interpenetration, deep accumulation, compression, and fracture.

1\. Theorems

Theorem 1: Interpenetration Inevitability Theorem

Under normal evolutionary conditions without persistent strong separating forces, as long as multiple Vitalities long-term co-exist in the same finite field, their interpenetration must gradually grow; complete isolation cannot be long-term maintained in a finite field.

Proof sketch: in a finite field, Coact \> 0 (co-activation of multiple Vitalities is unavoidable, since Field Chapter 7’s Resolution Flow J cannot remain fully separated when converging in a finite region) and Infilt \> 0 (finite field capacity Λ(x) is finite; the sedimentation diffusion term f·∇²δ inevitably causes mutual seepage in neighboring regions); under normal evolution (Sep is not persistently driven above Coact + Infilt + Reentry by strong forces such as Overflow Sedimentation or drastic fracture), dItr\_f/dt \> 0 holds long-term; Itr\_f(t) monotonically non-decreases toward a stable point; complete isolation (Itr = 0) cannot be maintained in a finite field. Note: if intense Overflow Sedimentation or Familiar Route fracture occurs (Sep spikes briefly), Interpenetration may locally decrease; but once the field restabilizes, the interpenetration tendency grows again. ∎

Theorem 2: Moderate Interpenetration Promotes Shared Nucleus Theorem

When Itr\_f(t) ≤ T\_o(f,t), Interpenetration promotes Shared Nucleus formation and Familiar Route reuse, lowering independent continuation cost.

Proof sketch: when Itr\_f ≤ T\_o, shared maintenance among Vitalities does not exceed the field’s carrying capacity; the shared maintenance portion forms a Shared Nucleus (Field Chapter 13’s Shared Nucleus Formation Theorem); the Shared Nucleus drives P\_s to tend downward (Field Chapter 13’s negative feedback closed loop); Familiar Route reuse efficiency increases (Field Chapter 13 Theorem 4: Familiar Route Reuse Theorem); independent continuation cost falls and Resolution Margin R\_f does not decrease. ∎

Theorem 3: Excessive Interpenetration Suppresses Resolution Theorem

When Itr\_f(t) \> T\_o(f,t), P\_s exceeds SDC\_f, compression strengthens, and Resolution Margin R\_f falls.

Proof sketch: Itr\_f \> T\_o and Ω\_f not decreasing or continuing to rise: P\_s = Ψ(Itr\_f, Ω\_f) tends to exceed SDC\_f (by Section 5’s ∂P\_s/∂Itr\_f \> 0 and ∂P\_s/∂Ω\_f \> 0, both positively contributing; if Ω\_f simultaneously drops sharply, partial cancellation is possible, but under normal overload conditions Ω\_f rises with overlap complexity) → K\_f ↑ (Field Chapter 13’s Compression Inevitability Theorem) → Ind\_f ↓ (Field Chapter 13) → S\_f ↑ (Field Chapter 12) → R\_f = SDC\_f − S\_f ↓ (Field Chapter 12’s Resolution Margin Theorem); hence excessive Interpenetration suppresses resolution. ∎

Theorem 4: Interpenetration Drives Compression Theorem

When Itr\_f exceeds threshold T\_o and Ω\_f does not decrease or continues to rise, Compression Intensity K\_f rises, forming the causal chain: Itr\_f \> T\_o → P\_s \> SDC\_f → K\_f ↑; Interpenetration is the structural antecedent of compression.

Proof sketch: by Theorem 3’s qualification (Itr\_f \> T\_o and Ω\_f not decreasing or continuing to rise), P\_s = Ψ(Itr\_f, Ω\_f) tends to exceed SDC\_f; by Field Chapter 13’s Compression Inevitability Theorem, P\_s \> SDC\_f → K\_f ↑; hence Itr\_f \> T\_o under this condition is the antecedent dynamic of K\_f rising, and Interpenetration is the structural antecedent of compression. ∎

Theorem 5: Interpenetration Fracture Restores Resolution Theorem

When Interpenetration Fracture occurs, Itr\_f falls, P\_s tends to fall, K\_f falls, R\_f recovers, and new differentials re-form independent fine subdivisions.

Proof sketch: Interpenetration Fracture → Sep(f,t) ↑ → dItr\_f/dt \< 0 → Itr\_f ↓ → Ψ(Itr\_f, Ω\_f) ↓ → P\_s ↓ → K\_f growth rate tends to zero (Field Chapter 13’s Compression Intensity Theorem) → S\_f stabilizes or falls → R\_f recovers (Field Chapter 12’s Resolution Recovery Theorem) → new differentials can stably obtain. ∎

Theorem 6: Interpenetration–Overflow Sedimentation Trigger Theorem

When Itr\_f \> T\_o and P\_Δ \> B\_f, Overflow Sedimentation occurs; interpenetration accumulation pressure, compression saturation, and boundary convergence pressure jointly act, and new Vitalities fracture outward through the boundary.

Proof sketch: Itr\_f \> T\_o → K\_f ↑ → S\_f ↑ → R\_f ↓ (Theorem 3) → old compressed structure approaches carrying limit → P\_Δ ↑ (Field Chapter 12’s Boundary Differential Pressure rises as R\_f falls); when P\_Δ \> B\_f and R\_f ≤ 0, Overflow Sedimentation triggers (Field Chapter 12’s Overflow Sedimentation Trigger Theorem); overflow Vitalities independently obtain in neighboring Domains, new Domains form (Field Chapter 12 Theorem 6).
