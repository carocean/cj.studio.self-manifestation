On Self-Manifestation · Field Chapter 15 v1.2

# Field Capacity Equations

1\. Purpose

The preceding chapters have established:

\- why resolution falls;  
\- why Interpenetration grows;  
\- under what conditions a compression candidate obtains (Field Chapter 13 v1.2);  
\- under what conditions a Boundary Saturation Outgrowth candidate obtains (Field Chapter 12 v1.2).

Yet the preceding chapters have not truly explained: why some fields compress more easily, destabilize more easily, and undergo Boundary Saturation Outgrowth more easily, while other fields can long-term maintain high resolution, multiple Co-Nuclei, and complex continuation.

This chapter takes the finite capacity of Field Chapter 1, Law 20, as a premise (Field Capacity is finite, and stable differential Domains cannot be placed without limit), and studies the openness of Effective Continuation Domain and its changes; it does not infer from this premise that the various mechanisms would necessarily vanish in an infinitely open case. v1.1’s “if the field were infinitely open, resolution would not fall, compression would not strengthen, Overflow Sedimentation would not occur, and stable reality would not form” is an unproven counterfactual judgment and is withdrawn.

Therefore: there exists within the field an effective domain limit that can be continued, differentiated, tuned, compressed, and maintained. This is called: Field Capacity. The Numericology says: “The striking does not cease, yet the field possesses physical limits. Domains must possess interstitial gaps for misalignment. When no further gaps for misalignment remain, the upper limit of differentiation is reached. Therefore this upper limit is called the capacity of the field.” Field Chapter 1, Definition 30, writes the field-wide capacity as Cap(F); this chapter discusses its Domain-level reading (Section 3).

And: what truly determines the field is not total capacity, but the Residual Capacity Ratio—how much effective continuation can still be maintained.

Therefore this chapter discusses: why the Residual Capacity Ratio falls. And (proposed): how a falling Capacity Ratio relates to compression, rigidity, Familiar Route lock-in, falling resolution, and Boundary Saturation Outgrowth.

1\. Core Proposition

The field is not an infinitely continuable empty domain.

This chapter proposes: Familiar Routes, Co-Nuclei, compression, Interpenetration, and deep sedimentation in the field all persistently occupy Effective Continuation Volume.

Therefore (proposed): although the total domain still exists in the field, the domain truly available for new continuation to stably form continuously falls.

Therefore: the field’s true problem is not “how much exists,” but how much new domain remains for continued continuation.

This chapter proposes: the openness of Effective Continuation Domain affects the stability and instability of the field; this chapter does not give a stability judgment from the number of objects or from the Capacity Ratio alone.

1\. Field Capacity

Let:

Cap\_f denote the Sustainable Field Capacity of Domain f. It represents the total effective domain capacity within which multiple independent continuation structures can simultaneously be stably maintained. Note: the original v1.0 used V\_f(t), but V is already defined in Field Chapter 14 as Vitality V\_i; this chapter therefore uses Cap\_f. Relation to Field Chapter 1, Definition 30: this chapter takes Cap\_f as the local reading of Cap(F) on Domain f, Cap\_f = ∫\_f Res(x)dVol (the non-uniform form of Definition 30), counted in Domains. Per Field Chapter 1, Definition 33, Cap may change with Res and Vol; this chapter declares H\_Cap: within the comparison window considered, Cap\_f is treated as constant (v1.1 simply wrote “a constant”; this is now a declaration), with 0 \< Cap\_f \< ∞; when Cap\_f = 0, ratios such as φ\_f and Use\_f are not used.

A\_f(t) denote the Effective Continuation Volume of Domain f at time t still available for new continuation—defined as: the open field domain that can still support new continuation stably forming and will not be immediately absorbed by existing Familiar Routes, Co-Nuclei, or compressed structures, in the same unit as Cap\_f (counted in Domains).

Then: A\_f(t) ≤ Cap\_f (Effective Continuation Volume does not exceed total Field Capacity, A\_f(t) ≥ 0).

Hierarchical relation to Field Chapter 7’s G(x,t): Field Chapter 7’s Residual Resolution Capacity G(x,t) = Λ(x) − ρ(x,t) is a spatial-point-level residual capacity, and G ≤ 0 is called capacity fullness; this chapter’s A\_f(t) is the Domain-level Effective Continuation Volume. The two have the same directional reading (ample G and larger A\_f both indicate remaining margin) but are at different levels. v1.1 called A\_f “the aggregated measure of G(x,t) within Domain f”; their quantitative relation is not established in this chapter, so the sentence is changed into a directional reading.

Hierarchical relation to Field Chapter 12’s R\_f(t): the Resolution Margin R\_f(t) = SDC\_f − S\_f of Field Chapter 12 v1.2 is a differential-level measure; this chapter’s A\_f(t) is a capacity measure at the continuation-domain level. This chapter’s directional hypothesis H\_φR: other conditions being equal, higher A\_f corresponds to higher R\_f. v1.1’s “A\_f is the spatial-level prerequisite, R\_f is the differential-level result” is unproven and withdrawn (Field Chapter 12 v1.2 has already withdrawn the similar “G is the spatial-level prerequisite for R\_f”).

1\. Occupied Interpenetration

Let:

OccItr\_f(t) denote the Occupied Interpenetration of Domain f at time t—the structural residue produced by the interpenetration process that stably occupies Effective Continuation Volume in the field. Note: the original v1.0 used I\_f(t), but I is already defined in Field Chapter 7 as Pulse Input I(x,t); this chapter therefore uses OccItr\_f(t). OccItr\_f(t) ≥ 0, corresponding to the stable domain-occupying component formed by Field Chapter 14’s Itr\_f(t) at the Domain level; its counting must be declared. Interpenetration differs from Sedimentary Saturation in Field Chapter 1, Definition 19 (a Structural Position having sunk to the bottom).

1\. Residual Capacity Ratio

φ\_f(t) = A\_f(t) / Cap\_f

This is called the Residual Capacity Ratio. Note: the original v1.0 used ρ\_f(t), but ρ is already defined in Field Chapter 7 as Resolution Occupancy Pressure ρ(x,t) = ∫K(x−y)δ(y,t)dy; this chapter therefore uses φ\_f(t). φ\_f differs from Field Chapter 7’s local resolution competition function φ(·) and is distinguished by the subscript f and the time variable. φ\_f(t) ∈ \[0,1\].

Relation to the “Capacity Utilization” of Outer Chapter 3, the Numericology: the Numericology says: “The ratio between the number of domains already in use within the field and its capacity is called Capacity Utilization.” Field Chapter 1, Definition 33, writes it as Use(F) = UsedDomains(F)/Cap(F), and Use may exceed 1 (over-capacity). If this chapter declares H\_A: A\_f = max(0, Cap\_f − UsedDomains\_f) (UsedDomains\_f being the used Domain count in Domain f), then φ\_f(t) = max(0, 1 − Use\_f(t)): when Use\_f ≤ 1 the two sum to 1, and when Use\_f \> 1, φ\_f = 0. Without H\_A, this chapter establishes no necessary link between Capacity Utilization and the Residual Capacity Ratio: the definitions of UsedDomains\_f and A\_f do not guarantee that the two change in opposite directions, and any direction must be declared separately with comparison conditions and hypotheses. v1.1 simply wrote “φ\_f(t) = 1 − Capacity Usage(f,t)”, which did not cover Use \> 1, and the definition of A\_f (open domain not immediately absorbed by existing structures) is not automatically the unused domain; this version replaces it with the conditional formula above. The Numericology also says: “Beyond its capacity, lateral branches of things can no longer be incorporated, nor can novelty be explored. One thereby becomes fixed upon what one already holds.”—this chapter takes it as corresponding to the Rigidity Mechanism of Section 9 and the decline of creativity in Section 10 when φ\_f → 0 (interpretation).

The Residual Capacity Ratio represents: the proportion of the field in which new continuation can still be generated.

When φ\_f(t) is high (proposed): large quantities of open continuation domain not yet locked by deep sedimentation still exist; new continuation can more easily differentiate, stabilize, extend, tune, and form Co-Nuclei.

When φ\_f(t) is low (proposed): the field increasingly tends to reuse old continuation rather than form new continuation.

1\. Falling Residual Capacity Ratio

The Residual Capacity Ratio falls due to (proposed): deepening Familiar Routes; growing Interpenetration; strengthening compression; accumulating deep sedimentation; rigidifying tuning.

Its essence (proposed): existing continuation structures persistently and preferentially occupy Effective Continuation Domain.

Dynamic relation (illustrative model, a model choice of this chapter):

dφ\_f(t)/dt = F\_f(t), F\_f(t) = −b\_S·S\_f(t) − b\_O·OccItr\_f(t) − b\_K·K\_f(t) + α\_f(t) (for 0 \< φ\_f \< 1)

Boundary rule: at φ\_f = 0, dφ\_f/dt = max(F\_f, 0); at φ\_f = 1, dφ\_f/dt = min(F\_f, 0), keeping φ\_f in \[0,1\].

Meaning of solution (declared): the driving terms are measurable and locally integrable; a solution is an absolutely continuous function satisfying the above equation and boundary rule almost everywhere; the sign conditions in the theorems below are read almost everywhere.

Compatibility condition for joint use with H\_A: when H\_A and this dynamic equation are used together, their compatibility along the same history must be checked separately. While UsedDomains\_f persistently exceeds Cap\_f, H\_A requires φ\_f to stay at 0, and open capacity cannot be judged to have appeared merely from the positive recovery term: for example, with Cap\_f = 10 and UsedDomains\_f = 12 held constant, H\_A requires φ\_f to remain 0, whereas if F\_f = 1 the boundary rule makes φ\_f rise immediately. On intervals where 0 \< φ\_f \< 1 and H\_Cap holds, joint use with H\_A requires dUsedDomains\_f/dt = −Cap\_f·F\_f. This chapter does not independently establish the dynamics of the used Domain count and does not regard the two sets of relations as automatically compatible.

Symbol note: b\_S, b\_O, b\_K ≥ 0 are declared conversion coefficients that convert S\_f (an amount of differential maintenance, Field Chapter 12 v1.2), OccItr\_f (per its counting), and K\_f (∈ \[0,1\], Field Chapter 13 v1.2) into rates of change of the Capacity Ratio; v1.1 wrote dφ\_f/dt ∝ −S\_f − OccItr\_f − K\_f + α\_f, adding three terms of different units directly, and this version adds the declaration. Directions (hypotheses of this chapter): with the other drivers and conversion coefficients fixed, an increase of S\_f, OccItr\_f, or K\_f makes F\_f not increase (corresponding to more occupancy by old structures, interpenetration residue, and compression); this does not guarantee that the net change is already negative, nor continued decline at the boundary; when a coefficient is zero, that term has no effect. α\_f(t) ≥ 0 is the Positive Recovery Term, corresponding to processes that re-increase Effective Continuation Domain such as tuning refinement, Familiar Route fracture, Interpenetration Fracture, or new domain opening after Boundary Saturation Outgrowth (proposed correspondences; Theorem 5 of Field Chapter 12 v1.2 is Margin Recovery, and Interpenetration Fracture follows the current version of Field Chapter 14). This equation lets the levels of the occupying quantities drive the rate of change of φ\_f; to deduct occupancy directly from A\_f, a separate static formula would be needed. When F\_f \< 0, φ\_f strictly decreases on intervals where it has not yet reached 0, and once it reaches 0 it stays at 0 while that sign condition persists; the case F\_f \> 0 is symmetric: φ\_f strictly increases on intervals where it has not yet reached 1, and once it reaches 1 it stays at 1. A pointwise strict sign does not guarantee reaching the boundary in finite time (Theorems 1 and 5). v1.1’s “under normal deep-sedimentation accumulation, α\_f is typically insufficient to offset the three negative-direction drivers, so φ\_f tends to fall long-term” is an unproven empirical judgment and is changed into a proposal: the sign of F\_f depends on the actual values of its terms.

1\. Resolution Relation

R\_f(t) represents the margin by which the Domain can continue to sustain new stable differentials under the budget conditions declared in Field Chapter 12 v1.2; it is not the fineness of differentiation, and it replaces neither Res(F) (Field Chapter 1, Definition 29) nor the Separability Threshold θ\_sep.

φ\_f(t) and R\_f(t) are related but not equivalent (φ\_f is at the continuation-domain level, R\_f is at the differential level).

φ\_f(t) ↓ ⇒ R\_f(t) ↓ (under hypothesis H\_φR, in comparison under H\_Cap with other conditions fixed; Proposition 2). A fall of the margin must not be read directly as a fall of actual resolution.

1\. Residual Capacity Ratio and Compression

φ\_f(t) ↓ ∧ S\_f(t) \> 0 ⇒ K\_f(t) ↑ (proposed; subject to the conditions of Field Chapter 13 v1.2: P\_eff \> SDC\_f and K\_f \< 1; Proposition 3)

i.e. (proposed): when Effective Continuation Domain decreases and old-structure Compression Saturation is non-zero, Compression Intensity may strengthen; actual growth is judged by the illustrative model of Field Chapter 13 v1.2, Section 4, and compression must be separately certified by Field Chapter 1, Definition 35.

1\. Rigidity Mechanism

This chapter proposes: after φ\_f(t) falls long-term, the field tends to interpret new input through old continuation: old Familiar Routes absorb new input; old Co-Nuclei rewrite new structures; old compression overlays new continuation; old reality reinterprets new entries. Familiar-Route Locking is judged by Definition 6 of Form Chapter 20 v1.2 (a Familiar Route, unique precedence of subsequent flow in each sub-segment, dependence on the sedimentation difference).

1\. Creativity Conditions

This chapter proposes: high-creativity states correspond to the field still allowing large quantities of new continuation to long-term exist independently (φ\_f(t) relatively high). New tuning, new differentiation, new Co-Nucleus, and new compression paths form more easily.

1\. Boundary Saturation Outgrowth Mechanism

This chapter proposes: when φ\_f(t) persistently falls, new continuation finds it hard to stably enter the field, and Interpenetration continuously deepens and reinforces boundary convergence; when old compressed structures can no longer absorb new continuation, boundary pressure may accumulate. One portion of subsequent flow may be absorbed by existing Familiar Routes; a Boundary Saturation Outgrowth candidate follows Criterion 2 of Field Chapter 12 v1.2 (both R\_f ≤ 0 and P\_Δ \> B\_f), and certification follows Field Chapter 1, Definition 21 and Equation 10. Per Field Chapter 1, Law 21, overload release may manifest as Compression, Boundary Saturation Outgrowth, Resolution Loss, or their combination; this section discusses only the Boundary Saturation Outgrowth path.

1\. Dynamic Equation

Field Capacity Dynamic Equation (illustrative model):

dφ\_f(t)/dt = −b\_S·S\_f(t) − b\_O·OccItr\_f(t) − b\_K·K\_f(t) + α\_f(t) (for 0 \< φ\_f \< 1; boundary rule in Section 6)

And (proposed):

φ\_f(t) ↓ ⇒ new continuation generation failure rate ↑

φ\_f(t) ↓ ⇒ R\_f(t) ↓ (under H\_φR)

φ\_f(t) ↓ ∧ S\_f(t) \> 0 ⇒ K\_f(t) ↑ (requires P\_eff \> SDC\_f and K\_f \< 1)

φ\_f(t) ↓ ⇒ Familiar Route lock-in strengthens (Familiar-Route Locking judged by Definition 6 of Form Chapter 20 v1.2)

1\. Summary Expression

General Field Capacity Dynamics Expression (proposed chain):

Deep sedimentation growth ⇒ A\_f(t) ↓

A\_f(t) ↓ ⇒ φ\_f(t) ↓

φ\_f(t) ↓ ⇒ new continuation generation becomes difficult

New continuation generation difficult ⇒ subsequent flow absorbed by existing Familiar Routes

Subsequent flow absorbed ⇒ reality lock-in strengthens

Reality lock-in strengthens ⇒ Familiar Routes deepen and Interpenetration strengthens

Interpenetration and compression strengthen ⇒ φ\_f(t) continues to fall (proposed positive feedback: old structures strengthening further lowers the Residual Capacity Ratio; this chapter does not prove that the chain accelerates)

φ\_f(t) persistently falls ∧ R\_f(t) ≤ 0 ∧ P\_Δ \> B\_f ⇒ Boundary Saturation Outgrowth candidate (Criterion 2 of Field Chapter 12 v1.2; certification per Field Chapter 1, Definition 21 and Equation 10)

The field’s true constraint is not the number of objects, but the effective domain capacity available for sustainably opening new continuation (interpretation, following Field Chapter 1, Law 20).

What is called rigidity, ossification, reality lock-in, and declining creativity is interpreted in this chapter as the structural result of the field’s Residual Capacity Ratio falling long-term (following the Numericology: “Beyond its capacity … One thereby becomes fixed upon what one already holds”); this chapter does not prove it to be the only cause.

1\. Theorems

Theorem 1: Capacity Ratio Decline Theorem (within the illustrative model)

Let the illustrative model of Section 6 hold, φ\_f(t₀) ∈ \[0,1\], and the solution exist on \[t₀, t₁\]. (a) If F\_f ≤ 0 on \[t₀, t₁\], then φ\_f is monotonically nonincreasing on \[t₀, t₁\]; if F\_f ≤ 0 for all t ≥ t₀ and the solution exists globally, φ\_f converges. (b) If further b\_S·S\_f + b\_O·OccItr\_f + b\_K·K\_f − α\_f ≥ c \> 0 on \[t₀, t₁\], then φ\_f(t) ≤ max(0, φ\_f(t₀) − c(t − t₀)); when t₁ ≥ t₀ + φ\_f(t₀)/c, φ\_f falls to 0 no later than t₀ + φ\_f(t₀)/c.

Proof sketch: (a) by the meaning of solution, φ\_f(t) − φ\_f(s) = ∫\_s^t φ\_f′ dτ; in the interior φ\_f′ = F\_f ≤ 0, at φ\_f = 0, φ\_f′ = max(F\_f, 0) = 0, and at φ\_f = 1, φ\_f′ = min(F\_f, 0) ≤ 0, so φ\_f′ ≤ 0 almost everywhere and φ\_f does not increase; monotone and bounded, hence convergent. (b) On intervals where φ\_f has not yet reached 0, φ\_f′ = F\_f ≤ −c almost everywhere, and integral comparison gives φ\_f(t) ≤ φ\_f(t₀) − c(t − t₀); once φ\_f reaches 0, it stays at 0 by the boundary rule (max(F\_f, 0) = 0). ∎

Note: under the pointwise condition F\_f \< 0 alone, φ\_f strictly decreases on intervals where it has not yet reached 0, but is not guaranteed to fall to 0 in finite time (for example φ\_f(t) = (1 + e^{−t})/4, t ≥ 0); when φ\_f is already 0, the boundary rule keeps it there and it does not strictly decrease; when F\_f ≤ 0, φ\_f converges, but its limit need not be 0. v1.1 Theorem 1 (Residual Capacity Ratio Decline Inevitability Theorem) is withdrawn: (i) “α\_f is typically insufficient to offset the three negative-direction drivers” is an unproven judgment, and the sign of F\_f depends on actual values; (ii) the cited “Field Chapter 12’s Familiar Route Suppresses Resolution Theorem” is now Proposition 3 of Field Chapter 12 v1.2 (under H\_S), “Field Chapter 13’s Compression Inevitability Theorem” is now Criterion 1 of Field Chapter 13 v1.2 (compression candidacy, not inevitability), and “Field Chapter 14’s Interpenetration Inevitability Theorem” follows the current version of Field Chapter 14, from which this chapter does not infer that OccItr\_f necessarily rises; (iii) “φ\_f monotonically falls toward a stable point (when the three negative-direction drivers and α\_f tend toward equilibrium)” is replaced by (a): when F\_f ≤ 0, φ\_f is monotone and bounded and so converges, and whether the limit is an equilibrium with F\_f = 0 must be proved separately.

Proposition 2: Capacity Ratio and Resolution Coupling (under H\_φR)

Under hypothesis H\_φR, in comparison under H\_Cap with other conditions fixed, a fall in φ\_f(t) corresponds to R\_f(t) tending to fall. A fall of the margin must not be read directly as a fall of actual resolution.

Note: the substantive content of this proposition lies entirely in H\_φR. The proof chain of v1.1 is withdrawn: “A\_f ↓ (Effective Continuation Domain decreases) → G(x,t) overall tends to fall within Domain f (Field Chapter 7 spatial-level prerequisite)”—the quantitative relation between A\_f and G is not established, and the “spatial-level prerequisite” has been withdrawn by Field Chapter 12 v1.2; “SDC\_f’s margin relative to S\_f contracts” requires a separately established relation between A\_f and SDC\_f, S\_f; the cited “Field Chapter 12’s Resolution Margin Theorem” is, in Field Chapter 12 v1.2, Theorem 1 (necessary condition of the margin, under the fixed-budget comparison), which gives no coupling with φ\_f.

Proposition 3: Capacity Ratio and Compression Coupling (proposed)

When φ\_f(t) falls and S\_f \> 0, K\_f may rise.

Note: this is proposed. Per Field Chapter 13 v1.2, growth of K\_f in its illustrative model requires P\_eff \> SDC\_f and K\_f \< 1; P\_s \> SDC\_f yields only a compression candidate (Criterion 1), and compression must be separately certified by Field Chapter 1, Definition 35. This chapter would further need the hypothesis that a decrease of A\_f raises the overlap among differentials and thus raises P\_s (H\_AP), which is unproven here. The “Field Chapter 13’s Compression Inevitability Theorem” cited in v1.1 is now Criterion 1, not inevitability; “S\_f \> 0 ensures … further accelerating compression” is unproven and withdrawn.

Proposition 4: Familiar Route Lock-in (proposed)

When φ\_f(t) falls long-term, new input may be absorbed more by existing Familiar Routes, the field tends toward Familiar Route lock-in, and new continuation finds it increasingly hard to stably form.

Note: this is proposed; Familiar-Route Locking is judged by Definition 6 of Form Chapter 20 v1.2 (a Familiar Route, unique precedence of subsequent flow in each sub-segment, dependence on the sedimentation difference), and this chapter does not derive it from a fall of φ\_f alone. The proof chain of v1.1 is withdrawn: (i) “high-Familiarity Familiar Routes” is re-expressed per Field Chapter 11 v1.2: comparison of familiarity follows the path Sedimentation-Degree statistic δ̄, and overall advantage in continuation competition is measured separately by CI, which is not a degree of familiarity; (ii) the cited “Field Chapter 11’s Familiar Route Priority Theorem” is, in Field Chapter 11 v1.2, Theorem 1 (Familiar Routes Come Out First, under Bridge Assumption H\_δ), which gives only the order of outflow under comparison conditions, not absorption shares; (iii) “Field Chapter 11’s Reality Conservatism Theorem” is, in Field Chapter 11 v1.2, an interpretation (Familiar Routes and reality conservatism), not a theorem.

Theorem 5: Capacity Ratio Recovery Theorem (within the illustrative model)

Let the illustrative model of Section 6 hold, φ\_f(t₀) ∈ \[0,1\], and the solution exist on \[t₀, t₁\]. (a) If F\_f ≥ 0 on \[t₀, t₁\], then φ\_f is monotonically nondecreasing. (b) If further α\_f − (b\_S·S\_f + b\_O·OccItr\_f + b\_K·K\_f) ≥ c \> 0 on \[t₀, t₁\], then φ\_f(t) ≥ min(1, φ\_f(t₀) + c(t − t₀)).

Proof sketch: as for Theorem 1, with the sign reversed; for (b), on intervals where φ\_f has not yet reached 1, φ\_f′ = F\_f ≥ c almost everywhere, and integral comparison gives φ\_f(t) ≥ φ\_f(t₀) + c(t − t₀); once φ\_f reaches 1, it stays at 1 by the boundary rule (min(F\_f, 0) = 0). ∎

Note: v1.1’s condition “S\_f + OccItr\_f + K\_f \< α\_f” added three terms of different units and is replaced by a weighted condition. “new continuation can again stably form” and “R\_f tends to rise (Field Chapter 12’s Resolution Recovery Theorem)” are proposed: the latter is now Theorem 5 of Field Chapter 12 v1.2 (Margin Recovery), its coupling with φ\_f following H\_φR; a recovery of R\_f is also not the restoration of stable differentiation (Field Chapter 1, Definition 34). Under the pointwise condition F\_f \> 0 alone, φ\_f strictly increases on intervals where it has not yet reached 1, but is not guaranteed to reach 1 in finite time; when φ\_f is already 1, the boundary rule keeps it there and it does not strictly increase.

Proposition 6: Field Capacity and Boundary Saturation Outgrowth (proposed)

If a persistent fall of φ\_f(t) is accompanied by R\_f(t) ≤ 0 and P\_Δ \> B\_f, a Boundary Saturation Outgrowth candidate obtains per Criterion 2 of Field Chapter 12 v1.2; this chapter proposes that a falling Field Capacity Ratio is one antecedent of Boundary Saturation Outgrowth.

Note: a Boundary Saturation Outgrowth candidate requires both R\_f ≤ 0 and P\_Δ \> B\_f, and certification follows Field Chapter 1, Definition 21 and Equation 10 (Domain Fullness already established, new Trace-Effects unable to continue internal sinking, overflow along the Domain boundary, generation of new Structural Positions); per Field Chapter 1, Law 21, overload release may also be Compression, Resolution Loss, or their combination. The proof chain of v1.1 is revised: “P\_Δ ↑ (Field Chapter 12’s Boundary Differential Pressure rises as R\_f falls)” is, in Field Chapter 12 v1.2, a separate hypothesis, and a fall of R\_f does not by itself entail an increase of P\_Δ; the cited “Field Chapter 12’s Overflow Sedimentation Trigger Theorem” is now Criterion 2; “Field Chapter 14’s Interpenetration Drives Compression Theorem” follows the current version of Field Chapter 14; “K\_f ↑ → S\_f ↑” follows the directional reading (a hypothesis) of Field Chapter 13 v1.2, Section 3; that after Boundary Saturation Outgrowth the neighboring Domain opens new domain and φ\_{f’}(t) rises is proposed, new Domains being certified by Field Chapter 1, Definition 8.
