# General Formula of Continuation-Dynamics

1\. Core Proposition

This chapter advances a core proposition:

To accept the fact that “flow is compelled to continue along a biased path” necessarily means: the system contains not only continuation-flow, but also a set of structures—such that when multiple flows cannot simultaneously occupy the same Continuation-Position, the system can proceed from Original Continuation to loss of Original Continuation, through Tuning, and into Re-Continuation, forming Continuation-Dynamics.

That is: if flow within a system can continuously propagate, why does it not forever flow in all directions simultaneously, scatter randomly, completely rigidify, or completely dissolve—but instead form river channels, Familiar Routes, electric currents, directional desires, current continuity, and historical inertia?

The goal: to arrive at the minimum paradigm model of Continuation-Dynamics structure, and to provide both positive proof and falsification paths.

The result first:

Minimum Continuation-Dynamics General Formula:

Continuation-Dynamics = (F, O, L, P, T, Rc)

Under conditions where continuation-flow exists, Continuation-Positions are mutually exclusive, existing held-flows are present, and new continuation-flows press into the same Continuation-Position, any system in which a Potential State forms and is closed must simultaneously possess:

Continuation-Flow Structure F; Continuation-Position Exclusion Structure O; Original-Continuation Structure L; Pressure-Displacement Structure P; Tuning Structure T; Re-Continuation Structure Rc.

Of these, the formation of Potential requires F, O, L, P (with Pressure-Displacement succeeding); the closure of Potential further requires T and Rc. That the system merely continues to proceed does not entail that a Potential State forms: it is also possible that new continuation presses in while the old hold holds throughout and the system proceeds as usual (the Full-Hold case).

Therefore: Potential is not a probabilistic tendency, not a preference for outcomes, not an optimal path—but the unclosed continuation-tension in which the system exists after old-hold can no longer maintain Original Continuation and before new continuation has re-formed sustainable Original Continuation.

In one sentence: Law keeps flow maintaining Original Continuation; Principle makes hold unable to continue Original Continuation; Potential arises between the failure of Original Continuation and the unclosed state of Re-Continuation.

2\. Abstract

The preceding chapters have established: sedimentation forms continuation-traces; continuation-traces form Familiar Routes; Familiar Routes form stable structures.

But the problem does not end there—it presses further: since continuation-flow can form stable structures, why does the system not permanently rigidify along old structures? And since old-hold is compelled to lose Original Continuation, why does the system not completely dissolve—but instead form new biased flows, new Familiar Routes, a new present, and new stable structures?

This chapter proposes: Potential is not the “more likely” at the result level, nor the “optimal choice” in nature—but the unclosed continuation-tension formed within a continuation-flow system by the impossibility of multiple dominant co-occupancies of the same Continuation-Position and the loss of Original Continuation before Re-Continuation is achieved.

To this end, we first elevate “biased flow” from an empirical phenomenon to a structural problem (limited to biased continuation under co-position competition), then prove: under continuation-flow, Continuation-Position exclusion, existing held-flows and new continuation pressing in, if a Potential State forms in the system, the system must possess F, O, L, P; if that Potential State is moreover closed, the system further possesses T and Rc. That the system merely continues to proceed does not entail that a Potential State forms.

Core conclusion: Law is not an abstract regularity but the maintenance by continuation-flow of its own Original Continuation; Principle is not an externally imposed law but the Pressure-Displacement that makes hold unable to continue Original Continuation; Potential is not a result-tendency but the continuation-tension between the failure of Original Continuation and the unclosed state of Re-Continuation.

3\. Introduction

People commonly understand Potential as a kind of “tendency”: water tends downward; electricity tends toward the negative; heat tends toward equilibrium; vines tend toward light; nerves travel Familiar Routes; consciousness generates desire-directions.

So people easily mistake Potential for: a result-preference, a probability of occurrence, or an optimal path.

This is wrong. Because “more likely,” “easier,” “more energy-efficient,” and “more optimal” already belong to the result level. They presuppose that biased flow has already formed, but do not explain: why biased flow can form.

The real question is not: why does the result favor one side? But rather: why does the structure that produces “go here and not there” arise within the system? That is: why, once flow enters the system, does it not forever continue in all directions simultaneously—but is instead pressed by old-hold, Pressure-Displacement, and Re-Continuation into biased flow?

Tradition says “what flows well grows familiar”—but this belongs to Law, explaining how old flow maintains Original Continuation. Tradition also says “go here and not there”—but this belongs to Principle, explaining why existing held-flows cannot forever continue Original Continuation.

Therefore, what this chapter truly asks is: why can Law be maintained? Why can Principle break Hold? Why does Potential arise between the failure of Original Continuation and the unclosed state of Re-Continuation?

4\. Proof-Flow

The argument unfolds by persistent questioning, step by step.

### 4.1 Locating Potential

First question, at the shallowest level: is Potential a result-phenomenon rather than a structural problem?

No. If Potential were only a result-phenomenon, we would only need to count: which path appears more; which direction occurs more commonly; which result repeats more easily. What this yields is only probability, frequency, or result-distribution.

But Potential is not after the result. Potential is before the result. Before water flows into a river channel, the field already contains structures that prevent flow from continuing in all directions simultaneously. Before electricity completes discharge, the electric field is already in an about-to-fracture Potential. Before desire forms action, the intention-domain has already formed a pressing-in Potential.

Therefore: Potential is not statistics about “what has already happened”—but the structural state of “flow is not yet complete, yet can no longer continue in all directions simultaneously.”

Definition 1: Potential

Potential is not result-tendency, not probability-preference, not optimal path—but: the unclosed continuation-tension formed in the system after old-hold can no longer maintain Original Continuation and before Re-Continuation has yet to close.

### 4.2 Continuation-Flow Structure F

Since Potential is not result-statistics, the next question: does the problem of Potential arise in all systems?

No. If the system contains no continuation-flow, only one-time changes, there is no problem of Continuation-Dynamics.

Therefore: Continuation-Dynamics first requires that continuation-flow exist within the system.

Definition 2: Continuation-Flow

Continuation-Flow is not a one-time change—but: change that can continue to propagate trans-temporally and maintain continuation-capacity in subsequent structures. If change can only occur instantaneously and cannot continue to propagate, then Continuation-Flow does not obtain and neither does Continuation-Dynamics.

First structure derived:

F: Continuation-Flow Structure. F’s function is to enable change to propagate trans-temporally. Without F, there is no flow; without flow, there is no Potential.

Symbol note: F is a structure name (uppercase), distinct from Field Chapter 7’s f (lowercase, Local Diffusion Coefficient f·∇²δ).

4.3 Continuation-Position Exclusion Structure O

Given continuation-flow exists, the next question: does continuation-flow alone necessarily produce biased flow?

Not necessarily. If all continuation-flows can simultaneously unfold, simultaneously maintain, simultaneously occupy the same structural position without mutual constraint, the system produces only all-direction parallel flow—not biased flow. Without biased flow, there is no “go here and not there”; without “go here and not there,” there is no Continuation-Dynamics.

Definition 3: Dominant Flow

Dominant Flow is not arbitrary parallel flow, not background flow, not residual flow—but: the continuation-flow that has obtained actual occupancy rights at a given Continuation-Position. Only between Dominant Flows does genuine continuation competition arise.

Definition 4: Continuation-Position

A Continuation-Position is not a simple spatial location—but: the structural position at which continuation-flow can continue to propagate, maintain, and form subsequent occupancy. Examples: a river-bed cross-section (water); a neural impulse pathway (discharge); the current-occupancy position (reality-continuation); a tuning center (desire-direction continuation); a threshold-gate focus (signal-entering-display).

Definition 5: Continuation-Position Exclusion

Continuation-Position Exclusion means: the same Continuation-Position cannot simultaneously sustain multiple Dominant Flows.

Note (the scope of biased flow). "Biased flow" 「偏流」 has several contextual uses in the Inner and Outer Chapters: prior traces guiding later flow (Inner Chapter 15, 「传导者，就势之流也，其偏流为顺势自成也」, main book: "Conduction is flow that follows tendency. Its biased flow forms of itself by following tendency"), differential output of the Experience-Field (Inner Chapter 7), intentional selection or alteration of the direction of flow (Inner Chapter 15, 「择之者，偏其流也」), and tinting that alters experiential biased flow (Outer Chapter 9); none may be taken as the sole meaning throughout the book. What this chapter treats is limited to the "go here and not there" of biased continuation arising when the same Continuation-Position cannot be occupied by several Dominant Flows at once, that is, biased continuation under co-position competition; O is a necessary structure of this biased continuation, not the cause of every biased flow. Statements below such as "without O, no biased flow" all mean biased continuation under co-position competition.

Second structure derived:

O: Continuation-Position Exclusion Structure. Without O, multiple flows can sustain in all directions; without biased continuation under co-position competition, there is no Potential.

### 4.4 Original-Continuation Structure L

Given Continuation-Positions are mutually exclusive, the next question: once continuation-flow enters a Continuation-Position, does it naturally stabilize?

No. Any continuation-flow may be interrupted, dissipated, or exit Original Continuation. If the system cannot make a continuation-flow maintain Original Continuation, then river channels, Familiar Routes, inertia, current continuity, desire-direction maintenance—none of these can obtain.

Definition 6: Original Continuation

Original Continuation is not stasis, not fixed unchangingness—but: the state in which existing continuation-flow continues to maintain its own continuation-relation.

Third structure derived:

L: Original-Continuation Structure. L’s function is to make continuation-flow continue to maintain Original Continuation. Without L, continuation-flow cannot sustain long-term; without Original Continuation, there is no Law.

Therefore: Law is not an abstract regularity, but the maintenance by continuation-flow of its own Original Continuation.

Note (the use of "Law" in this chapter). This chapter names the maintenance of Original Continuation 律, Law, after Inner Chapter 9, 「律者，持之势也」 ("Law holds"); this is a naming at the field layer. It is not the Law qualification adjudicated by the Law Group-Form (Established ∧ Govern, through the sufficient direction of The Necessity Form of Law, carrying its carry-over condition SC\_Hold): from L holding or ReCont holding, Law may not be certified directly. "New Law" below is to be read likewise. (In v1.2 the English rendering of 律 is changed from "Rule" to "Law," following the main book.)

### 4.5 Pressure-Displacement Structure P

But if the system has only Original Continuation, what happens?

If the system has only L, formed continuation-flows only maintain Original Continuation and never give way through Pressure-Displacement—river channels do not change course, consciousness does not shift (as illustration). In this respect the system rigidifies.

Therefore: a Continuation-Dynamics system cannot have only Original Continuation. It must also contain a structure that makes existing held-flows unable to continue Original Continuation.

Defining “pressing-in” first:

Press(f,p,t)=1 denotes that continuation-flow f is continuously pressing into Continuation-Position p at time t. Pressing-in does not equal completed occupancy; it only denotes that the flow is attempting to enter the Continuation-Position and continuously exerting continuation-pressure. Pressing-in belongs to continuation-motive-force; occupancy belongs to continuation-result.

Fourth structure derived:

P: Pressure-Displacement Structure.

Symbol note: P is a structure name, distinct from Field Chapter 12’s P\_Δ(f,t) (Boundary Differential Pressure, a function quantity) and Field Chapter 13’s P\_s(f,t) (Differential Stability Maintenance Pressure, a function quantity). All three relate to the concept of “pressure” but at different levels: P is at the Continuation-Dynamics structure level; P\_Δ is at the domain-boundary level; P\_s is at the differential-maintenance level.

Pressure-Displacement operator:

P : (𝔽\_old × 𝔽\_new × ℙₜ) → 𝒮ₜ

where: 𝔽\_old is the set of existing held-flows; 𝔽\_new is the set of continuously pressing-in new continuation-flows; ℙₜ is the set of Continuation-Positions; 𝒮ₜ is the continuation-flow state space.

If L(fᵢ)=fᵢ′ and fᵢ ∈ 𝔽\_old, while fⱼ ∈ 𝔽\_new and Press(fⱼ,p,t)=1 (new continuation-flow fⱼ continuously pressing into the same Continuation-Position p), then by Continuation-Position Exclusion the two cannot both be dominant; if the pressing-in of the new continuation gains dominance — which is precisely what the Pressure-Displacement Structure P bears — then the old hold thereby loses Original Continuation: ¬OrigCont(fᵢ′,p,t+Δt). This step follows from exclusion together with "Original Continuation presupposes dominance" (Section 9, A₁): if the new continuation dominates, the old hold does not dominate, and what does not dominate is not in Original Continuation. This is: Breaking-Hold. Exclusion alone yields only that co-dominance is impossible, not that the old hold must lose Original Continuation: pressing-in while the old hold still holds can also occur (see 14.4); and an old hold that loses Original Continuation for some other reason before the new continuation gains dominance is not Breaking-Hold either (Section 9, Lapse).

Breaking-Hold is not random disturbance but a structural outcome: exclusion makes co-dominance impossible, and Pressure-Displacement makes the old hold give way.

Therefore: Principle is not fracture at the result level, but the Pressure-Displacement that makes hold unable to continue Original Continuation.

Connection to Outer Chapter 3 · Number Theory: Outer Chapter 3 states 「分而有限，则可适其分，分而无限，则必压入一」, “differentiation that is finite can fit its differentiation; differentiation that is infinite must be pressed into one”—read by this chapter as meaning that in a finite field, Continuation-Positions cannot be co-occupied indefinitely, which is precisely the number-theoretic foundation of O (exclusion) and P (Pressure-Displacement). This chapter’s Boolean operators (Press, OrigCont, ReCont, Dom, CoDom) all operate on already-differentiated continuation structures, consistent with the position of Outer Chapter 3, 「可数，可指，可类，可比，其工于已成」 (number operates upon what has already been differentiated and categorized; the earlier quotation “number arises from what has already differentiated and been categorized” was a summary, not the original text of Outer Chapter 3); these operators are minimal computable approximations, with precise functional forms left to subsequent chapters of Field Mathematics.

### 4.6 Tuning Structure T

After Pressure-Displacement, can the system naturally continue to proceed?

No. If the system cannot re-establish continuation-relations, then no flow — the old hold included — can re-form sustainable Original Continuation at that Continuation-Position: the old hold can at most revert briefly, another flow can at most occupy briefly, and the system remains unclosed or keeps dissolving.

Therefore: the system must also contain Tuning Structure.

Definition 7: Tuning

Tuning is not simple coordination, not result-equilibrium—but: the structure that enables some flow after Pressure-Displacement — the new continuation, the old hold or another flow — to re-acquire continuation-relations at that Continuation-Position (Section 9, Tune).

Fifth structure derived:

T: Tuning Structure. T’s function is to enable some flow after Pressure-Displacement to re-acquire continuation-relations at that Continuation-Position; the old hold holding again also requires Tuning, and a brief reversion without Tuning does not constitute closure. In this chapter T is a functional definition, not a special mechanism independent of L and Rc. Corresponds to Field Chapters 8/9’s tuning field θ\_sep, L(t).

### 4.7 Re-Continuation Structure Rc

But even after Tuning, has the system truly formed new stable continuation?

Not yet. If the continuation-relations after Tuning cannot re-form sustainable Original Continuation, the Potential State remains unclosed (the Full-Fracture case).

Therefore: Continuation-Dynamics must also possess Re-Continuation Structure.

Re-Continuation operator:

Rc : 𝔽ₜ₊Δ → 𝔽ₜ₊₂Δ

ReCont(f,p,t)=1 denotes that continuation-flow f has re-formed sustainable Original Continuation at Continuation-Position p; its formal criterion is ReCont\_e of Section 9: Tuned, and dominating continuously from Tuning onward for no less than the declared window W\_R.

Rc’s action: ∃f\* ∈ 𝔽ₜ₊₂Δ satisfying ReCont(f\*,p,t+2Δt)=1.

Sixth structure derived:

Rc: Re-Continuation Structure. Note: the original v1.0 used K, but K is already defined in Field Chapter 13 as Compression Intensity K\_f(t); this chapter therefore uses Rc. Rc’s function is to make flows that have lost continuation re-form new sustainable Original Continuation. Without Rc, the Potential State cannot close and no new Law can form.

5\. General Formula of Continuation-Dynamics

At this point, the General Formula of Continuation-Dynamics has been derived step by step.

First, Continuation-Flow F. After continuation-flow enters the system, it must face Continuation-Position Exclusion O. If existing flows are to persist long-term, they must possess Original-Continuation Structure L. If the old hold could hold against any pressing-in, the system would rigidify; for the old hold to be able to lose Original Continuation under the pressing-in of new continuation, a Pressure-Displacement Structure P is required — pressing-in by itself does not guarantee Breaking-Hold (4.5). After Pressure-Displacement succeeds, if the system cannot re-establish continuation-relations, it can only revert or dissolve — thus deriving Tuning Structure T. After Tuning, if sustainable Original Continuation still cannot re-form, the Potential State cannot close — thus deriving Re-Continuation Structure Rc. The first four are required for the formation of Potential, the latter two for its closure.

General Formula of Continuation-Dynamics:

Continuation-Dynamics = (F, O, L, P, T, Rc)

where: F = Continuation-Flow Structure; O = Continuation-Position Exclusion Structure; L = Original-Continuation Structure; P = Pressure-Displacement Structure; T = Tuning Structure; Rc = Re-Continuation Structure.

Satisfying the ordering relations:

O ≺ P (without Continuation-Position Exclusion, no co-position pressing-in pressure forms)  
L ≺ P (without existing held-flows, no Original Continuation to be broken)  
P ≺ T (without Pressure-Displacement, no need for Tuning)  
T ≺ Rc (without Tuning, Re-Continuation cannot form)

6\. Master Theorem

Let continuation-flow exist, Continuation-Positions be mutually exclusive (O), existing held-flows be present, and new continuation-flows press into the same Continuation-Position, and adopt the declared premises A₁, A₂ and the Re-Continuation window W\_R \> 0 of Section 9. (i) Formation of Potential: if Ψ(p,t)=1 at some Continuation-Position p (Section 9), the system possesses F, O, L, P. (ii) Closure of Potential: if that Breaking-Hold event e is afterwards closed (t\_c(e) \< ∞), the system further possesses T and Rc. (iii) That the system merely continues to proceed does not entail the antecedent of (i): with pressing-in while the old hold holds throughout, the system proceeds as usual and no Potential State forms (the Full-Hold case, 14.4). (i) and (ii) are unfoldings of the definitions of Section 9: F, O, L, P are constituent conditions of a Breaking-Hold event, T and Rc constituent conditions of first closure. This theorem is therefore a definitional result; its content is that Section 9 places the six as constituent conditions of the formation and closure of Potential, and it does not separately prove the minimality of an independent mechanism for each of the six. The six together are called the Continuation-Dynamics structure; "minimum complete" is said with respect to the whole process "formation and closure of Potential"; with respect to the formation of Potential, only F, O, L, P are necessary. This theorem does not claim that the six together are sufficient.

Proof:

Without F: without trans-temporal continuation-flow there is no persisting OrigCont and Press on \[t₀,t\_b), and a Breaking-Hold event P(e) cannot hold.

Without O: "without O" in this clause means that within the working domain under consideration the candidate flows at p actually co-dominate, i.e., CoDom holds throughout. Then the ¬CoDom of Compete fails and P(e) does not hold; and the new continuation's dominance no longer entails that the old hold does not dominate. The system forms no biased continuation under co-position competition, and Potential does not hold. If co-dominance is merely possible but not actual, then at the times when it does not hold, competition and Breaking-Hold can still occur by definition; this clause does not claim that "possible co-dominance" excludes Potential. This is a definitional conclusion.

Without L: there is no OrigCont, Compete does not hold, there is no Original Continuation to lose, and no Potential State forms.

Without P: there is no Breaking-Hold event, Ψ(p,t)=0; the old hold does not lose Original Continuation through Pressure-Displacement — it may hold fully, or lose continuation for some other reason (Lapse, not Potential in this chapter's sense).

Without T (with respect to closure): no flow (the old hold included) acquires Tune at p, ReCont\_e never holds, t\_c(e)=∞; a brief reversion of the old hold or of another flow does not satisfy ReCont\_e and does not constitute closure. This necessity comes from Tune being contained in the definition of ReCont\_e (functional inclusion); no minimality of an independent tuning mechanism is claimed to be proven.

Without Rc (with respect to closure): with Tuning but without dominance lasting no less than W\_R, ReCont\_e does not hold, and the event remains unclosed (the Full-Fracture case).

Further: O ≺ P (no co-position pressing-in pressure); L ≺ P (no Original Continuation to be broken); P ≺ T (no continuation-relation needing re-establishment); T ≺ Rc (no genuine Re-Continuation formation). Hence the ordering relations are necessary. ∎

7\. Falsification Arguments

Seven most likely counterexamples examined:

Counterexample 1 (one-time change system): without F, no Continuation-Dynamics.

Counterexample 2 (all-direction parallel-flow system): without O, no biased continuation under co-position competition, no Potential.

Counterexample 3 (pure Original-Continuation system): without P, the old hold does not lose Original Continuation through Pressure-Displacement; if it does not lose continuation for any other reason either, that Continuation-Position rigidifies (the Full-Hold case).

Counterexample 4 (pure-fracture system): without L, all structures continuously dissolve.

Counterexample 5 (no-Tuning system): without T, no flow (the old hold included) is Tuned; the old hold can at most revert briefly, and the Potential State cannot close.

Counterexample 6 (no Re-Continuation system): without Rc, system remains in unclosed Potential state and cannot form new stable continuation.

Counterexample 7 (pure-optimization system): if the system is explained only as “choosing the optimal path,” without explaining how continuation-flow maintains Original Continuation, how new continuation continuously presses in, why old-hold loses Original Continuation, or how the system re-forms Re-Continuation—it remains only a result-level explanation insufficient to constitute the General Formula of Continuation-Dynamics.

9\. Potential-State Definition

This section gives this chapter's sole formal definition of the Potential State; wherever Section 6, Section 11 and Sections 12, 13, 14 speak of a Potential State, this is the standard. A Continuation-Position p is fixed first; the system layer is obtained from the position layer by existential quantification (9.6).

### 9.1 Basic Predicates

All are state predicates taking 0/1: Dom(f,p,t): f dominates p at t. CoDom(fᵢ,fⱼ,p,t): the two can co-dominate p at t. Press(f,p,t): f presses into p at t. OrigCont(f,p,t): f is in Original Continuation at p at t. Tune(f,p,t): f has re-acquired continuation-relations at p at t (Tuning; corresponding to the tuning fields of Field Chapters 8 and 9, whose computable criteria are supplied by those two chapters; this chapter uses only its indicator value).

### 9.2 Declared Premises

O (exclusion): fᵢ ≠ fⱼ ⇒ ¬(Dom(fᵢ,p,t) ∧ Dom(fⱼ,p,t)).  
A₁ (Original Continuation presupposes dominance): OrigCont(f,p,t)=1 ⇒ Dom(f,p,t)=1.  
A₂ (re-establishment of Original Continuation after Breaking-Hold is Re-Continuation): after t\_b of a Breaking-Hold event e and before its first closure t\_c(e), the Original Continuation of any flow at p can hold only through the Re-Continuation of 9.4; i.e., ∀t ∈ \[t\_b, t\_c(e)), ∀f, OrigCont(f,p,t)=0.  
W\_R \> 0: the declared Re-Continuation window, i.e., the minimum duration of "sustainable," declared with the implementation correspondence.  
Time domain: time in this chapter is taken in the discrete domain 𝕋 = {kΔt : k ∈ ℤ}, Δt \> 0 declared with the implementation correspondence, W\_R a positive integer multiple of Δt; every interval \[a,b), \[a,b\] in this section denotes its points in 𝕋. Hence every non-empty subset of 𝕋 bounded below has a least element, and "first" and "earliest" times are attained. For continuous time, conditions guaranteeing that the earliest time is attained would have to be declared separately; this chapter sets up none.

9.3 Pressing-In Competition and Breaking-Hold Events

Pressing-in competition: Compete(f\_old,f\_new,p,t) :⇔ f\_old ≠ f\_new ∧ OrigCont(f\_old,p,t)=1 ∧ Press(f\_new,p,t)=1 ∧ ¬CoDom(f\_old,f\_new,p,t). Competition is the stage before the Potential State and is not itself a Potential State.

Breaking-Hold event: e = (f\_old, f\_new, p, t₀, t\_b), f\_old ≠ f\_new. P(e) holds if and only if:  
(a) t₀ \< t\_b, and ∀τ ∈ \[t₀, t\_b): Compete(f\_old,f\_new,p,τ) (the old hold holds until t\_b, the new continuation presses in throughout);  
(b) Dom(f\_new,p,t\_b)=1 (the new continuation gains dominance at t\_b).

By f\_old ≠ f\_new, O and A₁: (b) ⇒ ¬Dom(f\_old,p,t\_b) ⇒ ¬OrigCont(f\_old,p,t\_b). Hence the old hold's loss of Original Continuation is derived from the new continuation's occupancy and occurs at t\_b — (a) guarantees that the old hold still held before, so it does not lose continuation earlier than t\_b.

What P(e) certifies and what it does not. It certifies: the old hold's loss of Original Continuation coincides with the new continuation's occupancy and is derived from the occupancy via O and A₁ (Breaking-Hold by occupancy). It does not certify: (i) that the new continuation's gaining of dominance is caused by its pressing-in — this is this chapter's mechanism reading M\_P (pressing-in is the route by which the new continuation gains dominance), declared and not entering the antecedent of any theorem; (ii) that occupancy is the sole cause of the old hold's loss — where an external cause acts at the same time (overdetermination), P(e) still holds.

Loss after competition: Lapse(f\_old,f\_new,p,t₀,t) :⇔ t₀ \< t ∧ Compete(f\_old,f\_new,p,t₀) ∧ ¬OrigCont(f\_old,p,t), a mere temporal marker carrying no causal attribution. Lapse holding without a corresponding P(e) — e.g., the old hold losing Original Continuation from an external cause before the new continuation gains dominance — is not a Potential State in this chapter's sense.

9.4 Re-Continuation of This Event and First Closure

For a Breaking-Hold event e: ReCont\_e(f,t) :⇔ ∃t′ ∈ \[t\_b, t−W\_R\]: Tune(f,p,t′)=1 ∧ ∀τ ∈ \[t′, t\]: Dom(f,p,τ)=1. That is: after Breaking-Hold some flow has been Tuned and, from Tuning onward, dominates continuously for no less than W\_R. f may be f\_new, f\_old (the old hold holding again) or another flow.

First-closure time: t\_c(e) := min{t ∈ 𝕋 : t ≥ t\_b, ∃f, ReCont\_e(f,t)}; when this set is non-empty its least element exists by the discrete time domain, and ReCont\_e indeed holds at t\_c(e); if the set is empty, t\_c(e)=∞. Hence for t ≥ t\_b: t \< t\_c(e) if and only if ∀t″ ∈ \[t\_b,t\], ∀f: ¬ReCont\_e(f,t″). After closure, the continuing dominance of the re-continuing flow f\* is its new Original Continuation: OrigCont(f\*,p,t)=1 until it loses dominance.

Brief reversion: the old hold (or another flow) dominating after Breaking-Hold without Tuning, or for less than W\_R, does not satisfy ReCont\_e and does not constitute closure.

9.5 Event Indicator and Position-Layer Potential State

J(e,t) := 1 if and only if P(e) and t\_b ≤ t \< t\_c(e); otherwise J(e,t) := 0.  
Ψ(p,t) := 1 if and only if there is a Breaking-Hold event e at p with J(e,t)=1; if there is none, Ψ(p,t) := 0.

Uniqueness of the witness: the unclosed segments of two Breaking-Hold events at the same p do not intersect. If the t\_b of one lies within the \[t\_b, t\_c) of the other, its (a) requires an old hold in Original Continuation within that segment, contradicting A₂; if the two have the same t\_b, then by O and A₁ their new continuations are the same and their old holds are the same. Hence, when Ψ(p,t)=1, the witnessing event is unique up to the choice of t₀, and the value of J does not depend on the choice of t₀.

Every Breaking-Hold opens an unclosed segment: since W\_R \> 0, t\_c(e) ≥ t\_b + W\_R \> t\_b, so J(e,t)=1 holds throughout \[t\_b, t\_b+W\_R).

### 9.6 System Layer

Unclosed(Ψₜ) := 1 if and only if ∃p ∈ ℙₜ: Ψ(p,t)=1. Closure at one position returns only that position's Ψ(p,·) to 0; where another position still has an unclosed event, the system layer remains 1.

### 9.7 Reopening

After event e closes at t\_c(e), a further Potential State at p requires a new Breaking-Hold event e′ = (f*, f′, p, t₀′, t\_b′), t₀′ ≥ t\_c(e), with the re-continuing flow f* as the old hold. After closure, a flow's disappearance or loss of dominance without a new Breaking-Hold event does not reopen a Potential State: it is loss of continuation without competition, or Lapse, outside this chapter's Potential State.

That is: Original Continuation holds before t\_b → fails at t\_b through the new continuation's occupancy → Re-Continuation is unclosed from t\_b until first closure. This is: the Unclosed Potential State. Original-Continuation competition (Compete) is its prior stage: where the old hold holds throughout, the new continuation presses in throughout and no Breaking-Hold event occurs, the system is in competition throughout and has no Potential throughout (consistent with 14.4).

10\. Final Compression

Law is the maintenance by continuation-flow of its own Original Continuation.  
Principle is the Pressure-Displacement that makes hold unable to continue Original Continuation.  
Potential is the continuation-tension between the failure of Original Continuation and the unclosed state of Re-Continuation.

11\. Theorems

Theorem 1: Continuation-Dynamics Minimum Completeness Theorem

Continuation-Dynamics = (F, O, L, P, T, Rc) is, with respect to "formation and closure of Potential," the minimum necessary structure-set: without any one, Potential either cannot form (F, O, L, P) or cannot close (T, Rc). This is a definitional result obtained by unfolding the definitions of Section 9; it claims neither sufficiency nor the minimality of an independent mechanism for each of the six.

Proof sketch: (i) and (ii) of the Master Theorem of Section 6; the ordering relations O ≺ P, L ≺ P, P ≺ T, T ≺ Rc follow Section 5. ∎

Theorem 2: Potential-State Necessary and Sufficient Conditions Theorem

Continuation-Position p is in the Unclosed Potential State at t (Ψ(p,t)=1) if and only if: ∃f\_old ≠ f\_new, ∃t₀ \< t\_b ≤ t (t₀, t\_b ∈ 𝕋) such that ∀τ ∈ \[t₀,t\_b): OrigCont(f\_old,p,τ)=1 ∧ Press(f\_new,p,τ)=1 ∧ ¬CoDom(f\_old,f\_new,p,τ); Dom(f\_new,p,t\_b)=1; and ∀t″ ∈ \[t\_b,t\], ∀f: ¬ReCont\_e(f,t″) (i.e., t \< t\_c(e)). The system layer Unclosed(Ψₜ)=1 if and only if this holds for some p ∈ ℙₜ.

Proof sketch: it is the unfolding of 9.3–9.6 of Section 9; both directions hold by definition. The old hold's loss of Original Continuation at t\_b is derived from f\_old ≠ f\_new, O and A₁; the equivalence of "no Re-Continuation up to t" with t \< t\_c(e) rests on the least element of 9.4; not from competition itself; Lapse does not constitute a Potential State. ∎

Theorem 3: Closure / Persistent-Unclosure Dichotomy Theorem (formerly "Potential Cannot Self-Stabilize Theorem," narrowed in v1.2)

For any Breaking-Hold event e at p: either t\_c(e) \< ∞ and the event closes at t\_c(e), or ∀t ≥ t\_b, J(e,t)=1 and it remains unclosed (the Full-Fracture case, 13.4). This is a dichotomy coming from the definition, not a separately proven dynamical conclusion. This chapter does not prove that an unclosed Potential State is dynamically unstable and does not exclude its long persistence; nor does it claim that the system must enter one of the four cases of Section 13: persistent competition (pressing-in while the old hold holds, no Potential State entered), repeated closure followed by reopening through a new Breaking-Hold event, alternation among several Continuation-Positions and the like all lie outside the four, and this chapter does not exclude them.

Proof sketch: t\_c(e) is either the least element of that set (finite, Re-Continuation indeed holding then) or ∞; the two branches follow directly from the definition of J. ∎

Theorem 4: Law-Principle-Potential Triad Theorem

Law (L), Principle (P), and Potential (Ψ) constitute the minimum dynamic triad: Law keeps flow maintaining Original Continuation; Principle makes hold unable to continue Original Continuation; Potential arises between the failure of Original Continuation and the unclosed state of Re-Continuation; the absence of any one means Potential cannot form.

Proof sketch: without L → Compete does not hold → no Breaking-Hold event → Ψ=0; without P → no Breaking-Hold event → Ψ=0 (the old hold may hold fully, or lose continuation for some other reason, the latter not being Potential in this chapter's sense); when L holds and P(e) holds, the old hold loses Original Continuation at t\_b; moreover t\_c(e) ≥ t\_b + W\_R \> t\_b, so J(e,t)=1 on \[t\_b, t\_b+W\_R) and Ψ obtains; T and Rc further determine whether the event can close (Master Theorem of Section 6). ∎

12\. Continuation-Dynamics Expressions

This section provides further expressions of Continuation-Dynamics at different levels—a further compression of the relations among Continuation-Flow, Original Continuation, Pressure-Displacement, Tuning, and Re-Continuation from Section 4’s Proof-Flow.

### 12.1 Total Potential-State Definition

Potential is not result: Potential ≠ Result

Definition: position layer Ψ(p,t) (Section 9, 9.5), system layer Unclosed(Ψₜ) (9.6): Original Continuation holds before Breaking-Hold, fails at t\_b through the new continuation's occupancy, and Re-Continuation is unclosed from t\_b until first closure.

i.e., Potential is not “where things have already headed” but “Original Continuation can no longer continue while Re-Continuation has not yet closed.”

12.2 Original-Continuation Failure Expression

¬OrigCont(f,p,t+Δt)

denotes: continuation-flow f can no longer maintain Original Continuation at Continuation-Position p. Note: Original-Continuation failure does not equal disappearance of flow. Flow remains but Original Continuation has failed (e.g., before a river changes course, residual water still flows in the old channel; before consciousness shifts, residual drive of the old desire-direction remains).

### 12.3 Pressing-In Expression

Press(f,p,t)=1 denotes that continuation-flow f is continuously pressing into Continuation-Position p at time t. Press does not denote completed occupancy—only continuation-motive-force. If Press=1 and OrigCont(fᵢ,p,t)=1, the system is in Original-Continuation competition (Compete, see 9.3), the stage before the Potential State, not itself a Potential State.

12.4 Continuation-Position Exclusion Expression

fᵢ ≠ fⱼ ⇒ ¬(Dom(fᵢ,p,t) ∧ Dom(fⱼ,p,t))

where Dom(f,p,t) denotes that continuation-flow f dominates Continuation-Position p at time t. This expression denotes that the same Continuation-Position cannot simultaneously sustain two distinct Dominant Flows; the restriction fᵢ ≠ fⱼ means it does not say that a flow cannot dominate together with itself.

### 12.5 Pressure-Displacement Expression

Pressing-in competition Compete and the Breaking-Hold event P(e) are given in 9.3 of Section 9 and are not set up again here. The essence of Pressure-Displacement: the new continuation presses in and gains dominance (occupancy), and by exclusion and "Original Continuation presupposes dominance" (A₁) the old hold thereby loses Original Continuation. Pressing-in while the old hold holds is only competition, not Pressure-Displacement (14.4); an old hold that loses Original Continuation for some other reason before the new continuation gains dominance is Lapse, not Pressure-Displacement either.

That the new continuation's gaining of dominance is caused by its pressing-in is this chapter's mechanism reading M\_P, declared and bearing no load: the theorems of Sections 6 and 11 use only the formal conditions (a)(b) of P(e).

### 12.6 Unclosed Potential-State Expression

Ψ(p,t)=1 if and only if ∃e: P(e) ∧ t\_b ≤ t \< t\_c(e); the system layer Unclosed(Ψₜ)=1 if and only if ∃p: Ψ(p,t)=1 (9.5, 9.6).

### 12.7 Re-Continuation Expression

ReCont\_e(f,t) (9.4) denotes that after Breaking-Hold event e continuation-flow f has been Tuned and, from Tuning onward, dominates p continuously for no less than W\_R, i.e., has re-formed sustainable Original Continuation. The termination condition of a Potential State is not the complete disappearance of the old continuation, but the completion of Re-Continuation by some flow — the new continuation, the old hold holding again, or another flow.

12.8 Total Continuation-Dynamics Expression

Ψ(p,t) holds if and only if (term by term as in 9.3–9.5):

∃f\_old ≠ f\_new, ∃t₀ \< t\_b ≤ t (in 𝕋): \[∀τ ∈ \[t₀,t\_b): OrigCont(f\_old,p,τ)=1 ∧ Press(f\_new,p,τ)=1 ∧ ¬CoDom(f\_old,f\_new,p,τ)\] ∧ Dom(f\_new,p,t\_b)=1 ∧ \[∀t″ ∈ \[t\_b,t\], ∀f: ¬ReCont\_e(f,t″)\]

Stage indicator:

Event level: J(e,t) = 𝟙\[P(e)\] · 𝟙\[t\_b ≤ t\] · 𝟙\[t \< t\_c(e)\] ∈ {0,1}, with e = (f\_old,f\_new,p,t₀,t\_b) fixed.  
Position level: Ψ\_ind(p,t) = 1 if there is a Breaking-Hold event e at p with J(e,t)=1; otherwise 0 (0 when there is no Breaking-Hold event). By 9.5 the witnessing event is unique up to the choice of t₀ and the value of J does not depend on t₀, so Ψ\_ind(p,t) is uniquely determined, and Ψ\_ind(p,t) = Ψ(p,t).  
System level: Ψ\_ind(t) = 1 if ∃p ∈ ℙₜ: Ψ\_ind(p,t)=1; otherwise 0.

Every quantity takes 0/1 and expresses only whether the system is in this stage, not pressing-in force, rate of Original-Continuation failure or degree of unclosed Re-Continuation; the earlier draft called it "Potential-State intensity," now changed. Should a continuous intensity be needed, a further quantity with a declared basis would have to be set up; this chapter sets up none.

### 12.9 Temporal Dynamic Expressions

Law: OrigCont(f,p,t) ⟶ OrigCont(f,p,t+Δt) (Original Continuation maintained trans-temporally)  
Principle (Breaking-Hold): P(e) — pressing-in competition on \[t₀,t\_b), occupancy by the new continuation at t\_b, whereby the old hold loses Original Continuation (9.3); pressing-in by itself does not entail Breaking-Hold (4.5, 14.4)  
Potential: J(e,t)=1, i.e., t\_b ≤ t \< t\_c(e) (Original Continuation has failed; this event's Re-Continuation not yet closed)  
Re-Continuation (closure): t = t\_c(e), i.e., some flow f first satisfies ReCont\_e(f,t) (not necessarily occurring)

13\. Continuation-Dynamics Limit Formulas

This section discusses what happens when Continuation-Dynamics structures are pushed to extremes. “Limit” here does not mean mathematical infinity but the typical degenerate cases the system may enter (declared, not exhaustive) after one structure persistently dominates the others in the General Formula of Continuation-Dynamics.

### 13.1 Total Limit Formula

The following are four typical degenerate cases when one structure persistently dominates the others (declared, not exhaustive). Persistent competition, repeated closure and reopening, alternation among several Continuation-Positions and the like lie outside them; this chapter does not claim that the system can ultimately enter only these four.

Lim₁: Flow-Null Limit (∅Flow)  
Lim₂: Full-Hold Limit (OrigCont-Dominance)  
Lim₃: Full-Fracture Limit (never Re-Continuation after Pressure-Displacement succeeds)  
Lim₄: Stable Re-Continuation Limit (Stable-ReCont)

### 13.2 Flow-Null Limit

F → 0: the system no longer contains trans-temporal continuation-flow; OrigCont, ReCont, Unclosed(Ψₜ)=0. The system degenerates into a set of one-time changes (instantaneous-flash systems: completely unsustainable random noise; memoryless perturbation; one-time discharge). Continuation-Dynamics completely disappears.

### 13.3 Full-Hold Limit

OrigCont(f,p,t) ⟶ OrigCont(f,p,t+nΔt) holds for all n ∈ ℕ. i.e., Original Continuation at that Continuation-Position holds persistently, there is no Breaking-Hold event, Ψ(p,t)=0. That position no longer forms a Potential State and rigidifies (as illustration: the river channel never changes course, consciousness never shifts there). Full-Hold at one position does not entail that reality or history as a whole stops updating.

### 13.4 Full-Fracture Limit

The old hold once held Original Continuation; after Breaking-Hold event e there is never Re-Continuation at p: t\_c(e)=∞, ∀t ≥ t\_b, J(e,t)=1, hence Ψ(p,t)=1 and Unclosed(Ψₜ)=1. That position permanently remains in an Unclosed Potential State and cannot form new stable continuation (permanently dissolving systems: completely unstabilizable fields; permanently conflicting structures; infinitely destabilizing systems). This is compatible with 14.3: 14.3 is the case in which Original Continuation never held, so there is no Breaking-Hold event and no Potential; this case is one in which Original Continuation once held, failed through Breaking-Hold, and Re-Continuation never comes.

### 13.5 Stable Re-Continuation Limit

Breaking-Hold event e closes at t\_c(e) \< ∞, and the re-continuing flow f\* thereafter dominates persistently with no further Breaking-Hold event: ∀t ≥ t\_c(e), Ψ(p,t)=0. Re-Continuation stabilizes as new Original Continuation; the Potential State at that position has closed; new Law is complete (new river channel stabilizes; new tuning fixes; new reality forms). Other positions may still have Potential, and the system layer does not thereby become 0.

### 13.6 Closure and Persistent Unclosure

With respect to a single Breaking-Hold event: never closing is the Full-Fracture case (by definition); after closure, either the new Original Continuation persists (the Stable Re-Continuation case) or a new Breaking-Hold event reopens (9.7; repetition, outside the four cases). The Potential State is the stage between the failure of Original Continuation and Re-Continuation; this chapter proves neither its dynamical stability nor its instability, and does not claim that the system must ultimately enter some one case. All this chapter can say is: what is stabilized in the Stable Re-Continuation case is the new Original Continuation after Re-Continuation, not the Potential State itself.

Continuation-Dynamics Limit Compression Formula:

Flow-Null: F → 0 ⟹ Unclosed(Ψₜ)=0  
Full-Hold: OrigCont-Dominance (at p) ⟹ Ψ(p,t)=0  
Full-Fracture: t\_c(e)=∞ ⟹ ∀t ≥ t\_b, Ψ(p,t)=1  
Stable Re-Continuation: t\_c(e) \< ∞ and no Breaking-Hold event thereafter ⟹ ∀t ≥ t\_c(e), Ψ(p,t)=0

Therefore: what Potential truly corresponds to is not stable structure but the unclosed phase before structures regenerate. The true limit of Continuation-Dynamics is not optimality, balance, or stability—but the process in which the system may cross between closed and unclosed states among Original Continuation, loss of continuation, and Re-Continuation. With respect to a single event—if Potential never closes, that is the Full-Fracture case; if Potential closes, there is Re-Continuation (named new Law at the field layer).

14\. Continuation-Dynamics Dissolution Formulas

This section does not discuss how Potential forms, but instead asks in reverse: if the necessary structures in the General Formula of Continuation-Dynamics are removed one by one, why can Potential not obtain? This is the reverse falsification of the General Formula of Continuation-Dynamics.

### 14.1 Dissolving F

F → 0: trans-temporal continuation-flow no longer exists; OrigCont/ReCont/Press no longer have sustained significance; Unclosed(Ψₜ)=0. Without flow, no Potential.

### 14.2 Dissolving O

If at p CoDom(fᵢ,fⱼ,p,t)=1 holds persistently for all candidate flows (the flows within the working domain actually able to co-dominate), then Compete does not hold, the new continuation's dominance does not entail the old hold's loss of Original Continuation, there is no Breaking-Hold event at p, Ψ(p,t)=0. The old hold may still lose continuation for some other reason, but that is not this chapter's Pressure-Displacement. Without exclusion, no Potential.

### 14.3 Dissolving L

Position layer: if at Continuation-Position p, ∀f, ∀t, ¬OrigCont(f,p,t) (no candidate flow at that position is ever in Original Continuation), then at p there is no Compete and no Breaking-Hold event, “Original-Continuation failure” does not exist, nor does the unclosed phase between Original Continuation and Re-Continuation; Ψ(p,t)=0. System layer: only when this holds for every p ∈ ℙₜ does Unclosed(Ψₜ)=0 follow. Without Original Continuation, no Potential.

### 14.4 Dissolving P

Press(f\_new,p,t)=1 but OrigCont(f\_old,p,t+Δt) holds persistently: perturbation/competition/pressing-in exists but old-hold continues to maintain Original Continuation; that position has not entered the loss-of-Original-Continuation phase, there is no Breaking-Hold event with f\_old as old hold, Ψ(p,t)=0 — by A₁ and O at most one flow is in Original Continuation at p at a time, so the old hold of any Breaking-Hold event at p can only be f\_old, and since f\_old holds throughout, (b) cannot hold. Other positions may still have unclosed events, and the system layer does not thereby become 0. Without Pressure-Displacement, no Potential.

### 14.5 Dissolving T

Breaking-Hold event e has occurred but ¬T: no flow (the old hold included) acquires Tune at p, ReCont\_e never holds, t\_c(e)=∞. A brief reverting dominance of the old hold or of another flow does not satisfy ReCont\_e and does not constitute closure. Without Tuning, Potential cannot complete closure (this is contained in the definition of ReCont\_e).

### 14.6 Dissolving Rc

With Tuning but ∀t, ∀f, ¬ReCont\_e(f,t) (no dominance lasting no less than W\_R): the event remains unclosed (the Full-Fracture case). Without Re-Continuation, no new Law.

### 14.7 Dissolution Chain

Dissolving F/O/L/P: these are Potential-Formation Dissolutions (the system cannot enter the unclosed continuation phase at all).

Dissolving T/Rc: these are Potential-Closure Failures (the system enters the unclosed continuation phase but cannot complete Re-Continuation closure).

### 14.8 Final Dissolution Formula

True Potential must simultaneously satisfy: continuation-flow exists; Original Continuation exists; Original Continuation fails through the new continuation's occupancy (a Breaking-Hold event); a phase in which this event's Re-Continuation is unclosed exists. The absence of any one condition means Potential cannot form; closure further requires Tuning and Re-Continuation. That the system merely continues to proceed does not suffice to constitute Potential.

Potential does not belong to change itself; it does not belong to randomness, perturbation, switching, or result-bias. True Potential exists only when Original Continuation has been lost through Pressure-Displacement while new Original Continuation has not yet completed Re-Continuation. Potential is not defined by stability or its absence—Potential is: the unclosed continuation phase between the failure of Original Continuation and the incompletion of Re-Continuation.
