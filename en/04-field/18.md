# Field Genesis and Dissolution

1\. Purpose

The preceding seventeen chapters established the internal dynamics of fields and Inter-Field Coupling. But an implicit assumption persisted throughout: the field already exists.

This assumption concealed two fundamental questions:

First: how does a field come to be?

In the absence of any Familiar Routes, any Co-Nuclei, any stable sedimentation structures—how does a new field emerge from a pre-field state?

Second: how does a field dissolve?

Under what conditions does a once-existing field reach its end? What does it leave behind after dissolution, and what does it not?

These two questions matter for the whole system (starting viewpoint, interpretation): genesis conditions bear on where reality structures come from, and dissolution conditions bear on how old structures are replaced and how new fields generate on the residues of old ones.

Therefore this chapter discusses: what the minimum conditions for field genesis are; the two paths of field dissolution and their different consequences; and how death residues become the initial sedimentation conditions for new field genesis.

1\. Core Proposition

A field is not given.

This chapter proposes: a field is a structure that gradually obtains only after sustained continuation-flow rushes into a finite space, sedimentation depth persistently accumulates, and differential structures begin to stably maintain themselves.

A field is not eternal.

This chapter proposes: after continuation-flow stops or compression saturates, a field proceeds toward termination via two different paths: Compression Death and Forgetting Death. Once a Co-Nucleus is separately certified, Compression Death may leave deep-nucleus residues; under the sustained conditions of Theorem 3, the residue of Forgetting Death tends to zero over time.

Note: “dissolution” is used broadly here for field termination. Compression Death is terminal rigidification rather than true disappearance—the field still exists but has lost the capacity to differentiate new differentials (a proposed reading); the criterion of Forgetting Death gives only that sedimentation depth is everywhere below δᵣ and not growing, and the return of structures to zero is the case under additional sustained conditions (Theorem 3).

This chapter proposes: these two dissolution paths leave different initial conditions, affecting the structural environment a new field faces when generating.

Therefore (interpretation): field genesis and dissolution are not externally imposed—they are the internal results of sedimentation dynamics.

1\. Field Genesis Conditions

Let Domain f not yet exist (∅ → f to be derived).

This chapter characterizes Domain f’s field-formation candidacy by the following three minimum conditions (for certification of field formation, see the end of this section):

Condition 1: Minimum Continuation-Flow Density Condition

In finite space Ω, there exists sustained pulse input I(x,t) (Field Chapter 7), such that there exists a sub-region Ω\_f ⊆ Ω in which local sedimentation depth δ(x,t) continuously and positively grows:

∃Ω\_f ⊆ Ω such that ∂δ(x,t)/∂t \> 0 for x ∈ Ω\_f, sustained duration ≥ τ\_stable

This is Criterion 5 of Field Chapter 7 v1.2 (growth condition for domain-formation candidacy, τ\_stable corresponding to its τc): sustained positive growth includes the joint contributions of effective input, neighborhood transfer, net migration, and diffusion, and can occur in structures without new boundaries; hence it is only a candidacy condition and cannot by itself certify a new Domain.

This chapter proposes: if continuation-flow is too sparse or the duration too short, sedimentation depth finds it hard to accumulate under the degradation terms (Field Chapter 7’s −q₀δ and others).

Condition 2: Minimum Differential Density Condition

Within region Ω, the number of differentiable differential entities in the sedimentation structure exceeds the minimum threshold:

N\_diff(Ω, t) ≥ N\_min

N\_min is a numerical threshold declared for this chapter’s screening of field-formation candidacy. N\_diff \< N\_min means that Condition 2 of this chapter is not met; it is not used to judge that differentials do not exist or that the Domain does not obtain; the obtaining of a Domain is checked separately by Field Chapter 1, Definition 8.

Note: Condition 2 is solely responsible for “whether sufficient differential entities exist”—it does not require that relations between differentials already be stable; that is the responsibility of Condition 3. The stable maintenance of differential relations is an independent additional condition. N\_min is a declared threshold, and the counting of N\_diff must be declared. The Numericology says: “What may be indicated is already divided; what may be compared is already classed. Thus number is born of what is already divided and already classed”—the prerequisite for countability is already having sufficiently many differentiable entities (interpretive correspondence).

Condition 3: Minimum Stable Closure Condition (“Stable Closure” in this chapter means stable interlocking among differentials, not Closure in Field Chapter 1, Definition 24 and Equation 16)

This chapter takes as the operational criterion of Condition 3 that the sedimentation depths of at least one differential pair (q\_i, q\_j) simultaneously reach the declared main-resolution-zone threshold δᵣ (Field Chapter 7 v1.2, Section 12; the operational use of δᵣ is to judge whether a path is in the main resolution zone, and it does not replace the general meaning of familiarity in Field Chapter 1, Definition 13), and that d(q\_i, q\_j) \> θ\_sep holds persistently within the declared window:

∃(q\_i, q\_j): δ(q\_i, t) ≥ δᵣ ∧ δ(q\_j, t) ≥ δᵣ

And the separability condition d(q\_i, q\_j) \> θ\_sep of Field Chapter 8 v1.2 holds persistently within the declared window. This criterion does not by itself certify structural interlocking, a self-maintaining boundary, or Closure in Field Chapter 1. v1.0 wrote “the differentiation relation M(q\_i, q\_j, t) between them (Field Chapter 7) is stably maintained”: Field Chapter 7’s M(x,t) is the overlap-match rate between input structure and existing sedimentation structure, not a differentiation relation between two differentials; this version follows the separability condition of Field Chapter 8 instead.

Note: Condition 3 is not a natural consequence of Condition 1. Condition 1 gives that sedimentation depth in some region continuously grows, but does not guarantee that a specific differential pair (q\_i, q\_j) persistently meets the separability condition; the two must be checked separately.

This chapter proposes: without stable interlocking, the sedimentation structure within the region finds it hard to self-maintain and will rapidly dissipate under tuning fluctuations.

Field Genesis Operator (operational delimitation of this chapter):

Genesis(f, t) = 1

if and only if: the three operational conditions of Section 3 hold together throughout the same declared window for not less than τ\_stable (definition).

Genesis(f, t) = 0 denotes that Domain f has not yet met the field-formation candidacy conditions.

Certification: Genesis = 1 is this chapter’s reading of field-formation candidacy; whether Domain f obtains is judged by Field Chapter 1, Definition 8 (a segment of a Line-Bundle that can stably maintain Retentive Containment, differentiation, differential accumulation, continuation, and bundle-boundary). The three conditions do not by themselves entail the five items of Definition 8.

1\. Field Genesis Dynamics

This chapter proposes: field genesis is not instantaneous but a gradual process of sedimentation accumulation.

Phase 1 (Pre-Field Phase, proposed): scattered pulse input exists in region Ω; sedimentation depth is low and unstable; degradation continuously erases sedimentation; no stable structure has formed. Genesis(f, t) = 0.

Phase 2 (Domain-Formation Threshold, proposed): pulse input density rises; local sedimentation depth begins to reach the main-resolution-zone threshold δᵣ; differential count approaches N\_min; but stable interlocking has not yet formed. Slight reduction of input may cause the sedimentation to dissipate again, while sustained input may drive toward field formation.

Phase 3 (Field-Formation Candidacy Obtains): all three conditions are simultaneously satisfied, Genesis(f, t) = 1; whether Domain f obtains is checked separately by Field Chapter 1, Definition 8; the field’s internal dynamics (resolution, tuning, Familiar Routes, compression) may begin to gradually build up (proposed). v1.0’s “Field Chapter 7’s master equation begins to operate effectively on Domain f” is withdrawn: the master equation of Field Chapter 7 v1.2 is an illustrative model and does not take the obtaining of a Domain as a condition of operation.

Relation to Field Chapter 17 v1.1: a newly generated field initially has no Familiar Route structure, FamOverlap ≈ 0; if φ\_f \> 0 and MatchTheta \> 0, then ε·φ\_f·MatchTheta \> 0, and hence a positive compatibility reading is obtained in the illustrative model of Field Chapter 17 (Condition 4’s new-anchor term); when MatchTheta = 0, the term is zero. This floor term is a model score and does not certify that actual new anchors, sedimentation nuclei, or Familiar Routes have formed (Field Chapter 17 v1.1).

1\. Field Dissolution Conditions

This chapter proposes: Domain f’s dissolution is not a single path but two distinct dynamic processes. The two deaths can be concurrent: when K\_f ≥ K\_dead while continuation-flow is simultaneously interrupted, the conditions of Death\_C and Death\_F can be met in succession. In concurrent death, if Core(f) has been separately certified, the residue may be dominated by the core residue δ\_res^C; under the conditions of Theorem 3, if input interruption persists long enough, core sedimentation depth ultimately falls below the main-resolution-zone threshold, turning into a Forgetting Death state.

### 5.1 Compression Death

Conditions: K\_f(t) ≥ K\_dead (K\_dead being the Death Compression Threshold declared in this chapter; K\_f is read per Field Chapter 13 v1.2 and counts only compressed sharing certified by Field Chapter 1, Definition 35); simultaneously the Independent Differential Count Ind\_f(t) tends to zero (Field Chapter 13 v1.2, counted directly by independent members); the Resolution Margin R\_f(t) does not exceed ε\_R (Field Chapter 12 v1.2, a margin under budget conditions).

Process (proposed): differentials in the field are continuously compressed into shared maintenance; independent structural space contracts to its limit. The field does not dissolve—it is compressed into an extremely small number of deep-nucleus structures; in the most extreme case, possibly tending toward a single deep nucleus. Whether what is compressed constitutes a Co-Nucleus is judged by Field Chapter 1, Definition 15 and Equation 7; compression is not Resolution Loss (Field Chapter 1, Definition 35).

Death operator (operational delimitation of this chapter):

Death\_C(f, t) = 1

if and only if: K\_f(t) ≥ K\_dead ∧ R\_f(t) ≤ ε\_R ∧ Ind\_f(t) ≤ ε\_N (definition)

where K\_dead ∈ (0,1) is the declared Death Compression Threshold (K\_f is not required to tend precisely to 1); ε\_R, ε\_N are declared nonnegative thresholds. “When Compression Intensity exceeds this threshold the field loses independent differentiation capacity” is a reading proposed in this chapter. Death\_C records only the declared conditions on compression, budget margin, and independent-maintenance count; it does not by itself entail the existence of a Co-Nucleus, sparse cores, or high sedimentation depth.

Core residue of Compression Death (after Core(f) is separately certified):

δ\_res^C(x, f, t) = δ(x, t)\|\_{x ∈ Core(f)} (Core judged by Field Chapter 1, Definition 15; its sedimentation depth checked separately; “residues of a few high deep nuclei” discussed as an additional case)

Characteristics of δ\_res^C (proposed; additional case: high core sedimentation depth): per Field Chapter 11 v1.2, the deep is called familiar (comparison of familiarity follows the path Sedimentation-Degree statistic δ̄) and comes out first under comparison conditions (Theorem 1, under H\_δ). New input entering this residual region may mostly be absorbed by existing Familiar Routes, making it hard to form independent differentials; coming out first gives only the order of outflow, not absorption shares, the latter being proposed in this chapter. v1.0’s “Familiarity extremely high”, “absorbed by existing Familiar Routes at the highest priority”, and “(referencing Field Chapter 11’s Familiar Route Priority Theorem)” are re-expressed per Field Chapter 11 v1.2.

Examples (interpretation): obsessions that cannot be forgotten in memory; core vocabulary that cannot be bypassed in language; core categories sedimented over millennia in civilization.

### 5.2 Forgetting Death

Conditions: all external inflow to f is interrupted—per Field Chapter 7 v1.2, effective input and neighborhood transfer are zero (I\_eff = B\_out = 0); the coupling of all external fields to f, Cpl(f\_ext → f, t), approaches zero (Field Chapter 17 v1.1; Cpl is a coupling intensity, not the amount of inflow, and their relation is not established in this chapter); simultaneously internal Re-Continuation Rc(f, t) ≈ 0 (Field Chapter 16; if internal Re-Continuation remains, the field has not truly died); and the baseline degradation coefficient q₀ \> 0 (Field Chapter 7). The Chinese v1.0 conditions included OutFlow(f, t) ≈ 0 (Field Chapter 17; f no longer has external output); f’s own output is unrelated to whether its sedimentation fades, so it is removed, consistent with the English v1.0.

Process: the field no longer receives any continuation-flow input, and sedimentation depth changes under the degradation terms. Under the exact conditions listed in Theorem 2 of Field Chapter 7 v1.2 (I\_eff = B\_out = 0, β·φ(ρ/Λ) = 0, ∇·J = 0, ∇²δ = 0), the decay is exponential and Theorem 3 holds:

δ(x,t) = δ(x,t₀)·e^{−q₀(t−t₀)} → 0 (t → ∞)

When the conditions hold only approximately, an error bound and a valid period for the omitted terms must be given separately; convergence to zero or satisfying Death\_F in finite time cannot yet be guaranteed thereby. For example, with an omitted positive source r, δ′ = r − q₀δ, whose limit r/q₀ may exceed δᵣ.

This chapter proposes: under the above exact conditions, all sedimentation structures gradually recede; Familiar Routes, Co-Nuclei, and differential relations dissolve one by one; ultimately Domain f’s sedimentation depth falls entirely below the main-resolution-zone threshold δᵣ. Field Quality and forgetting: the Numericology says: “If struck once yet never revisited, the trace-response gradually fades; this is called forgetting. Such is determined by the qualitative nature of the field.” q₀ is the baseline degradation coefficient of Field Chapter 7, read in this chapter as the shallowing component of Field Quality (Field Chapter 1, Definition 28) (proposed correspondence).

Death operator (operational delimitation of this chapter):

Death\_F(f, t) = 1

if and only if: ∀x ∈ f, δ(x,t) \< δᵣ ∧ ∂δ(x,t)/∂t ≤ 0 (sedimentation depth below the main-resolution-zone threshold and no longer growing; definition). v1.0 wrote ∂δ/∂t \< 0: where δ = 0, ∂δ/∂t = 0, so an empty Domain would never satisfy it as written; this version uses ≤ 0.

Residue of Forgetting Death:

When Death\_F = 1, the residue satisfies 0 ≤ δ\_res^F \< δᵣ and is not growing at the time of judgment. Under the sustained conditions of Theorem 3, the residue tends to zero exponentially over time. v1.0’s “δ\_res^F ≈ 0 (almost no residue)” is replaced by this statement: the criterion itself does not give δ ≈ 0—for example, a constant δ = 0.9δᵣ satisfies the criterion without tending to zero.

Examples (interpretation): skills that are no longer used completely disappear; languages that are thoroughly forgotten; the final result when a dissolved civilization’s body of knowledge is completely severed from its chain of transmission.

1\. Death Residues and New Field Genesis

This chapter proposes: death residue δ\_res is the initial sedimentation condition for new field genesis.

Case 1 (New field genesis after Compression Death):

This chapter proposes: in the additional case (Core separately certified, with high core sedimentation depth), when a new field generates on the basis of δ\_res^C, it faces a deep-nucleus attractor structure. New continuation-flow input may mostly be absorbed by the old deep nucleus, making it difficult to form independent differentials (FamOverlap high, φ\_f low).

Therefore (proposed): new fields generated on Compression Death residues tend to compress along the direction of old deep nuclei, finding it hard to break through old paradigms. Examples (interpretation): civilizational path dependence, cognitive preconceptions, and the absorption of new concepts by core vocabulary in language.

Breakthrough condition (proposed): OutFlow strong enough (the T\_replace exception path of Proposition 5 of Field Chapter 17 v1.1, proposed) may trigger fracture of the old deep nucleus; local sedimentation depth of the old nucleus falls; new differentials can then obtain independent sedimentation space.

Case 2 (New field genesis after Forgetting Death):

This chapter proposes: under an additional small-residue condition (δ\_res^F ≈ 0, for example after sufficient time under the sustained conditions of Theorem 3), the new field generates on a nearly empty field—no old structural constraints, no Familiar Route anchors of any kind.

New continuation-flow input begins accumulating sedimentation depth from nearly zero; once the three conditions are satisfied, field-formation candidacy obtains (Definition 8 checked separately). This chapter proposes: the new field’s generation direction is then mainly determined by current input structure, unaffected by old structures.

But the cost (proposed): generation speed is slower (no Familiar Route acceleration), and the field is prone to dissipating again at input interruption (early sedimentation depth is low, degradation pressure relatively high).

Examples (interpretation): rebuilding a new civilization after civilizational rupture; relearning after forgetting; generating new structures after cognitive reset.

1\. The Field Life Cycle

Synthesizing the preceding seven sections, the field life cycle proposed in this chapter is as follows:

Phase 1 (Pre-Field): scattered input in region Ω, δ \< δᵣ, Genesis = 0.

Phase 2 (Genesis Threshold): sustained input, δ locally reaches δᵣ, differential count approaching N\_min, stable interlocking not yet formed.

Phase 3 (Field-Formation Candidacy): Genesis = 1, the Domain checked separately by Field Chapter 1, Definition 8; the internal dynamics of Field Chapters 7–16 may begin to build.

Phase 4 (Field Development): Familiar Routes deepen, Co-Nuclei form, K\_f↑, φ\_f↓; the field may tend toward stability and also toward compression.

Phase 5 (Field Limit): K\_f → 1 or φ\_f → 0; the field enters the limit states discussed in Field Chapters 12, 13, and 15 (reaching them requires the conditions of each chapter’s illustrative model).

Phase 6 (Dissolution Path):

\- Path A (Compression Death): K\_f ≥ K\_dead, R\_f ≤ ε\_R, and Ind\_f ≤ ε\_N, hence Death\_C = 1; core residue is read after Core(f) is separately certified, and its sedimentation depth is checked separately;  
\- Path B (Forgetting Death): external inflow interrupted, Death\_F = 1, residue below δᵣ and not growing; under the sustained conditions of Theorem 3, δ → 0.

Phase 7 (New Field Genesis): on the basis of δ\_res, new continuation-flow rushes in; new field genesis conditions accumulate again.

Criterion 1: Field-Formation Candidacy Criterion

This chapter takes “the three operational conditions holding together throughout the same declared window for not less than τ\_stable” as the field-formation candidacy condition of Domain f, i.e., Genesis(f, t) = 1 (defined in Section 3).

Note: this is a declared operational delimitation, not a theorem derived from upstream chapters; since Genesis is defined by the three conditions, “Genesis = 1 if and only if the three conditions hold” restates the definition. Proposition 2: Difficulty of Reversing Compression Death (proposed)

If Death\_C(f, t) = 1, f may find it hard to recover differentiation spontaneously from within; external strong input exceeding T\_replace triggering old-nucleus fracture is one possible recovery path (Proposition 5 of Field Chapter 17 v1.1, proposed).

Note: this is proposed. Per Field Chapter 1, Definition 34, the field’s release of load does not necessarily restore stable differentiation; restoration requires re-forming stable differentials and other conditions.

Theorem 3: Forgetting Death Theorem (under the conditions of Theorem 2 of Field Chapter 7 v1.2)

Let every point of Domain f satisfy on \[t₀, ∞) the conditions of Theorem 2 of Field Chapter 7 v1.2: I\_eff = B\_out = 0, β·φ(ρ/Λ) = 0, ∇·J = 0, ∇²δ = 0, with q₀ \> 0 constant and δ ≥ 0, with δᵣ \> 0 a fixed threshold; let δ\_max = sup\_{x∈f} δ(x, t₀) \< ∞, and let internal Re-Continuation provide no input. Then δ(x,t) = δ(x,t₀)·e^{−q₀(t−t₀)}, let t\* = t₀ when 0 ≤ δ\_max ≤ δᵣ, and t\* = t₀ + ln(δ\_max/δᵣ)/q₀ when δ\_max \> δᵣ; then for all t \> t\*, Death\_F(f, t) = 1.

Proof sketch: under the listed conditions the master equation reduces to ∂δ/∂t = −q₀δ (Theorem 2 of Field Chapter 7 v1.2), whose solution is the exponential shown, so ∂δ/∂t = −q₀δ ≤ 0; for t \> t\*, δ(x,t) ≤ δ\_max·e^{−q₀(t−t₀)} \< δᵣ for all x ∈ f (when δ\_max = 0 the solution is identically zero and the definition is met from t₀; when 0 \< δ\_max ≤ δᵣ, δ \< δᵣ for t \> t₀); the result follows from the definition of Death\_F. ∎

Note: v1.0 Theorem 3 took “all external coupling Cpl(f\_ext→f) ≈ 0” as its condition: Cpl is a coupling intensity, not the same as zero inflow (Field Chapter 17 v1.1), and “≈ 0” yields only an approximate conclusion; this version states the exact conditions of Theorem 2 of Field Chapter 7 v1.2. When the conditions hold only approximately, an error bound and a valid period for the omitted terms must be given separately, and convergence to zero or satisfying Death\_F in finite time cannot yet be guaranteed thereby (see the positive-source example r in 5.2). The Numericology’s “If struck once yet never revisited, the trace-response gradually fades; this is called forgetting” is the original-meaning correspondence of this theorem.

Proposition 4: Non-Equivalence of the Two Death Residues (proposed)

The criteria of Death\_C and Death\_F differ, and so do their residue readings: Death\_C restricts only compression, budget margin, and independent-maintenance count, the core residue being read only after Core(f) is separately certified and its depth checked separately; Death\_F gives a residue below δᵣ and not growing, which tends to zero under the sustained conditions of Theorem 3. In the additional cases (high core sedimentation depth; forgetting residue already small), new fields generated on Death\_C residues may tend toward path dependence, while new fields generated on Death\_F residues may develop freely but are prone to early dissipation.

Note: this is proposed. The residue readings follow the two definitions, the separate certification of Core, and Theorem 3; “high deep core, small forgetting residue” is an additional case, and its consequences are proposed. The claim that the levels of the residues are read from the definitions does not hold: Death\_C does not restrict δ, and Death\_F gives only δ \< δᵣ. The proof chain of v1.0 is revised: (i) “Familiarity(new input direction) extremely high” is re-expressed per Field Chapter 11 v1.2 as deeper δ̄, coming out first giving only precedence; (ii) “Field Chapter 17’s coupling equation causes new field input to be absorbed by old nucleus”: the compatibility of Field Chapter 17 v1.1 determines only the coupling drive and gives no destination of absorption; (iii) on Death\_F residues, “only the ε·φ\_f·MatchTheta term \> 0” is a model score of Field Chapter 17 and does not certify new-anchor formation.

Proposition 5: Genesis-Dissolution Closed Loop (proposed)

Field genesis and dissolution may constitute a dynamic closed loop: Death\_C ∨ Death\_F → δ\_res → new Genesis initial conditions → new field development → new field limit → new Death; each death provides the initial structure for the next genesis.

Note: this is proposed. In the additional case (Core separately certified with core δ ≥ δᵣ), the positions preserved by δ\_res^C may provide sites for new input to re-satisfy Condition 3, but stable separability must be re-established by new input rather than persisting automatically. To regain field-formation candidacy after Death\_F, all three conditions must be checked for joint persistence within the same window; the initial sedimentation depth is the actual residue and is not presumed to be zero. v1.0’s “closed loop obtains” becomes a proposal; examples (interpretation): civilizational rise and fall, the formation and recession of memory, and the succession of cognitive paradigms.

Proposition 6: Field Genesis Speed (under H\_res)

Compare the two residue cases with the same starting point, declared protocol, and τ\_stable, and let hypothesis H\_res hold: the window qualification of Conditions 1 and 2 is the same in both cases, and every window qualifying for Condition 3 in the forgetting-residue case also qualifies in the compression-residue case. Let 𝒯\_C, 𝒯\_F be the sets of end points of qualifying windows in which the three conditions hold together for not less than τ\_stable in the two cases, and define τ\_gen^C = inf 𝒯\_C − t₀, τ\_gen^F = inf 𝒯\_F − t₀ (the infimum of the empty set being ∞). Then τ\_gen^C ≤ τ\_gen^F; but the former’s independence may be lower (Proposition 4).

Proof sketch: by H\_res, every window in which the three conditions jointly qualify in the forgetting-residue case also qualifies jointly in the compression-residue case, so 𝒯\_F ⊆ 𝒯\_C, hence inf 𝒯\_C ≤ inf 𝒯\_F; with the same starting point t₀, τ\_gen^C ≤ τ\_gen^F. ∎

Note: the substantive content of this proposition lies entirely in H\_res (inclusion of window qualification); satisfying Condition 3 earlier for the first time does not guarantee that the three conditions hold jointly earlier (Condition 3 may be satisfied earlier on compression residues yet cease before Conditions 1 and 2 hold); this proposition depends on the window-qualification inclusion hypothesis and cannot be inferred merely from preserved sites of deep sedimentation. v1.0 Theorem 6 stated that δ\_res^C has already preserved closure-compatible conditions so that only Conditions 1 and 2 are needed; Condition 3 requires stable separability of a differential pair, and residues preserve only sedimentation depth, not separability relations, so this is withdrawn; “there is a structural trade-off between speed and independence” is an interpretation.
