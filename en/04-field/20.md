# Observation-Domain Generation Conditions

I. Purpose

The preceding nineteen chapters have given the dynamic framework of the Sedimentation-Field: single-field dynamics, inter-field coupling, field genesis and dissolution, and the hierarchical structure of fields (the status of each conclusion is as recorded in its chapter, and Field Chapter 19 is still awaiting upgrade; starting viewpoint).

Inner Chapter 16, "Observation-Domain," gives the phenomenal-level usage of the Observation-Domain: “Hence when speaking of the Observation-Domain, it often refers to the set of opened Boundary-Gates.” This chapter writes:

Observation-Domain = the collection of opened Boundary-Gates.

“The outputs of circuits biased-flow toward Boundary-Gates. By Intent, the opening and closing of the Boundary-Gates are tuned, awaiting biased-flow to appoint the Experience-Target. Open and receive—Imagery steams forth within the circuits, and Lighting arises thereby.” And: “Becoming one—then thing arises; thus the observing of the Observation-Domain is completed.” (quotation from Inner Chapter 16, per the main-book English; v1.2 merged the two passages into one and altered their wording; this version quotes them separately as in the source)

Therefore (reading of this chapter, starting viewpoint): the establishment of the Observation-Domain depends on the simultaneous establishment of three mechanism-level conditions:

1\. the establishment of Boundary-Gates (the gate of the experience-target can open and close);  
2\. the establishment of manifestation circuits (the circuit can close upon the Observation-Domain and has Intent reverse-flow);  
3\. multiple circuits complete alignment under tuning (temporal tolerance, spatial mapping, and weight suppression) and tend toward becoming-one.

This chapter discusses what each of these three Inner-Chapter mechanism conditions requires at the level of Sedimentation-Field dynamics.

II. Core Proposition

The Inner Chapters indicate: signals pass through Boundary-Gates into the Observation-Domain; of the Boundary-Gate, “Its opening and closing are not pushed by A; only B can open or close it.” That is, the opening and closing of the Boundary-Gates is controlled by Intent (system B, the Observer Field), not determined by the signal itself (system A, the Content Field).

At the level of Field-Chapter dynamics, this means:

Modeling choice of this chapter: the opening and closing of the Boundary-Gate is judged by the Observer Field’s own receptivity readings (R\_{f\_B}, MatchTheta(f\_B), AnchorCapacity(f\_B)); content passage through the gate (Pass = 1) additionally involves the coupling conditions of Field Chapter 17 v1.1 (the A-field output OutFlow and the effective Familiar Route overlap FamOverlap\_eff). Pass is a same-instant predicate, whereas coupling intensity Cpl is a cumulative quantity; neither entails the other (note in Section III).

Reading of this chapter: the establishment of a manifestation circuit means that field coupling is not one-way (an automatic circuit), but forms a complete closed loop containing Intent reverse-flow; that is, the bidirectional coupling in Field Chapter 17 v1.1, with the upper → lower direction (Intent reverse-flow) maintained by the active pressing-in of the Intent Field (Section IV).

This chapter proposes: multi-circuit alignment means that, among multiple manifestation circuits that have already entered the Observation-Domain, their sedimentary structures reach sufficient interpenetration (Itr of Field Chapter 14, current version), and under the unified coordination of the Tuning-Field they tend toward Co-Nucleus alignment (Co-Nucleus judged by Field Chapter 1, Definition 15 and Equation 7).

Therefore (reading of this chapter): the establishment of the Observation-Domain is not an internal matter of a single field, but a structural emergence under a specific coupling configuration among multiple fields.

III. The Dynamic Substrate of Boundary-Gates

In the Inner Chapters, a Boundary-Gate is the gate of an experience-target: the B-system can open and close it; the A-system cannot determine it.

At the level of Field-Chapter dynamics, the opening and closing of the Boundary-Gate is divided into two layers:

Gate(f\_B, t) = 1 (Boundary-Gate open; determined only by the B-field; definition)

if and only if:

R\_{f\_B}(t) \> 0 ∧ MatchTheta(f\_B, t) \> 0 ∧ AnchorCapacity(f\_B, t) \> 0

Each of the three readings must be read from the state of f\_B alone:

R\_{f\_B}(t): the Resolution Margin (Field Chapter 12 v1.2, R\_f = SDC\_f − S\_f). R\_{f\_B} ≤ 0 is Margin Exhaustion, which is not Domain Saturation in Field Chapter 1, Definition 20. The compatibility of Field Chapter 17 v1.1 does not contain R\_{f\_B}; its entry into the Boundary-Gate is a modeling choice of this chapter.

MatchTheta(f\_B, t): a single-argument tuning-openness reading on the B-field side, ∈ \[0,1\], whose computation must be declared. MatchTheta(f\_A, f\_B, t) of Field Chapter 17 v1.1 is the pairwise match between f\_A’s output and f\_B’s separability threshold and depends on f\_A; since the Boundary-Gate must depend only on f\_B, this chapter enters a single-argument reading into the gate (for example, the supremum over a declared class of inputs, or the openness of f\_B’s current tuning focus), while the pairwise reading enters only gate-passage (through FamOverlap\_eff) and coupling.

Here AnchorCapacity(f\_B, t) indicates the B-field’s current capacity to receive new anchors (including existing Familiar Routes or the remaining capacity to spontaneously form new anchors), independent of the specific input structure of any A-field; its computation must be declared. Existing Familiar Routes follow Field Chapter 1, Definition 13 (comparison of familiarity follows the path Sedimentation-Degree statistic δ̄, Field Chapter 11 v1.2; the continuation-competition index CI is not a familiarity degree); new anchors follow the proposal of Condition 4 of Field Chapter 17 v1.1 (new-anchor formation is proposed, and its floor term ε·φ\_{f\_B}·MatchTheta is a model score). Hence AnchorCapacity \> 0 is a receptivity reading declared by this chapter and does not certify that actual new anchors or Familiar Routes have formed.

Pass(f\_A → f\_B, t) = 1 (gate-passage: the content of A-field enters B-field through its Boundary-Gate; definition)

if and only if:

Gate(f\_B, t) = 1 ∧ OutFlow(f\_A, t) \> 0 ∧ FamOverlap\_eff(f\_A, f\_B, t) \> 0

That is: the gate is open (Gate = 1), content comes (OutFlow \> 0), and the effective Familiar Route overlap is positive (FamOverlap\_eff \> 0). Per Field Chapter 17 v1.1: FamOverlap\_eff(f\_A, f\_B, t) = max(FamOverlap(f\_A, f\_B, t), ε·φ\_{f\_B}(t)·MatchTheta(f\_A, f\_B, t)); the Familiar Routes in FamOverlap follow Field Chapter 1, Definition 13, and comparison of familiarity follows δ̄; when φ\_{f\_B} \> 0 and MatchTheta(f\_A, f\_B) \> 0, FamOverlap\_eff is automatically positive through the floor term, and this positive value is a model score that certifies neither actual matching nor new-anchor formation. If Gate = 1 but OutFlow = 0, the gate is open but no one passes through: the Boundary-Gate is in an open state but has no manifestation input. This corresponds to Intent being ready while no content is available to enter (interpretation).

Note (gate-passage and coupling): Pass is a same-instant predicate; Cpl is a cumulative quantity. By Theorem 1 of Field Chapter 17 v1.1, if Cpl(f\_A → f\_B, t₀) = 0, then Cpl(f\_A → f\_B, t) \> 0 if and only if OutFlow(f\_A) \> 0, φ\_{f\_B} \> 0, and MatchTheta(f\_A, f\_B) \> 0 hold simultaneously on some positive-measure subset of \[t₀, t\]. Pass contains neither φ\_{f\_B} \> 0 nor the pairwise MatchTheta \> 0, so Pass holding on a positive-measure period does not by itself make Cpl turn positive from zero; the conditions for Cpl turning positive from zero contain neither R\_{f\_B} \> 0 nor AnchorCapacity \> 0, and do not entail Pass. Existing coupling can remain positive after gate-passage is interrupted (Theorem 4 of Field Chapter 17 v1.1).

Boundary-Gate closure condition (read off from the definition): if any of R\_{f\_B}(t) ≤ 0 (Margin Exhaustion), MatchTheta(f\_B, t) = 0 (tuning incompatibility), or AnchorCapacity(f\_B, t) = 0 holds, then Gate(f\_B, t) = 0. While the three tend to zero but remain positive, Gate is still 1; v1.2 wrote “→ 0” and called R\_{f\_B} → 0 “domain saturation”; this version uses taking zero or being nonpositive, and renames it Margin Exhaustion. This corresponds to the Inner Chapter’s “not entering—is mute” (interpretation).

Tuning control of Boundary-Gate opening and closing (proposed): the Inner Chapters state, “Opening and closing Boundary-Gates belong to tuning.” This chapter proposes that the Tuning-Field changes MatchTheta(f\_B), and thereby the open/closed state of the Boundary-Gate, by adjusting θ\_sep(f\_B, t) (Field Chapter 8 v1.2: the stronger the tuning capacity, the lower θ\_sep tends to be); the reading rule of MatchTheta(f\_B) with respect to θ\_sep must be declared. This is a proposed reading of the control mechanism by which Intent acts on the Boundary-Gates.

IV. The Dynamic Substrate of Manifestation Circuits

The Inner Chapters indicate: “Among them, those that can close upon the Observation-Domain are called manifestation circuits; those that cannot are called automatic circuits.” “Automatic circuits—those without reverse intent flow.” And: “Those that can close upon the Observation-Domain are full circuits.” That is, what can close upon the Observation-Domain is a full circuit (with Intent reverse-flow); what cannot close upon the Observation-Domain is an automatic circuit (without reverse-flow).

At the level of Field-Chapter dynamics, this corresponds to:

Circuit(f, t) = 1 (manifestation circuit established; definition. f denotes the field pair involved in the circuit: Content Field f\_A and Observer Field f\_B, to be declared; likewise for each fᵢ in ℒ\_cir)

if and only if:

Cpl(f\_A → f\_B, t) \> 0 (lower → upper coupling established)

∧ Cpl(f\_B → f\_A, t) \> 0 (upper → lower reverse-flow coupling established)

∧ Press(f\_B → f\_A, t) \> 0 (the Intent Field actively presses in to maintain reverse-flow: the active continuation-flow pressing of f\_B into f\_A, i.e., the aggregate Press reading of Field Chapter 17 v1.1, derived from the binary Press(f, p, t) of Field Chapter 16 v1.2, whose computation must be declared)

Reason for the third condition (reading of this chapter): bidirectional coupling alone is insufficient (two automatic controllers can also mutually feed back); there must also be the active Press of the Intent Field pressing in to maintain reverse-flow. Only then does it constitute what the Inner Chapters call “the countermand of intent.” If Press = 0, then even if reverse-flow exists, it is only passive echo and does not possess self-manifestation (interpretation). There is also a model reason: in the illustrative model of Field Chapter 17 v1.1, Press enters the β term of OutFlow(f\_B); when Press is zero, OutFlow(f\_B) may still contain other terms, and existing Cpl(f\_B → f\_A) evolves by the decay equation and can remain positive for some time (Theorem 4); hence Cpl(f\_B → f\_A) \> 0 does not entail Press \> 0, and the two must be checked separately.

The distinction between an automatic circuit and a manifestation circuit:

Automatic circuit: only Cpl(f\_A → f\_B) \> 0; there is no Cpl(f\_B → f\_A), that is, only one-way transmission. Content enters, but there is no Intent reverse-flow.

Manifestation circuit: bidirectional coupling is established, and Press(f\_B → f\_A) \> 0; a complete closed loop maintained by Intent is formed.

Note (scope of terms): one-way transmission is one case of a non-manifestation circuit, namely what the Inner Chapters call an automatic circuit; bidirectional coupling without active Press also fails the manifestation-circuit criterion of this chapter. Circuit = 0 also includes the case of no transmission at all, and this chapter does not call every Circuit = 0 an automatic circuit.

Note: Theorem 5 of Field Chapter 19 v1.0 states that the higher the level, the weaker the upper → lower coupling; that chapter is still awaiting upgrade, and the “Field Chapter 17’s Directional Asymmetry Theorem” on which its proof relies is now Proposition 2 of Field Chapter 17 v1.1, which gives only that the two directions are in general unequal, not the direction; hence it is not taken here as a proven premise (v1.2 miscited it as “Theorem 5 of Field Chapter 17”; corrected in this version). This chapter proposes: a manifestation circuit requires the upper → lower reverse-flow coupling to be maintained positive; this is not naturally given, but must be maintained by the active pressing-in of the Intent Field (Press of Field Chapter 17 v1.1). The Inner Chapter’s “Power-Ring” corresponds, at the level of Field-Chapter dynamics, to the following reading (interpretation): the Intent Field’s continuous Press output maintains the state Cpl(f\_B → f\_A) \> 0 and prevents reverse-flow from decaying to zero.

V. The Dynamic Substrate of Multi-Circuit Alignment

The Inner Chapters indicate that alignment within the Observation-Domain has three aspects: temporal tolerance (the coordination of delays among different circuits), spatial mapping (the positional correspondence among contents of different circuits), and weight suppression (which positional circuit is taken as primary).

At the level of Field-Chapter dynamics, multi-circuit alignment corresponds to the following:

Let the set of multiple manifestation circuits that have entered the Observation-Domain be:

ℒ\_cir = {f₁, f₂, …, f\_m}

where each satisfies Circuit(fᵢ, t) = 1, and each fᵢ is a field pair (f\_Aᵢ, f\_B) whose observer end is the declared Observer Field f\_B.

Align(ℒ\_cir, t) ∈ \[0,1\]

indicates the overall degree of alignment of multiple manifestation circuits under the coordination of the Tuning-Field.

The three components of Align are:

Component 1 (temporal tolerance): the degree of synchronization among the principal activation moments of δ(x,t) in the several circuits, written Sync ∈ \[0,1\], whose computation must be declared. This chapter proposes that it is related to the allocation state of the tuning load L(t) of Field Chapter 9 v1.2; L(t) in Field Chapter 9 is the total resource consumed in maintaining the tuning stability of all active boundaries, and a “multi-path equilibrium state” is not defined in that chapter; v1.2’s “determined by the multi-path equilibrium state of L(t)” is changed to a proposal.

Component 2 (spatial mapping): the degree of interpenetration among the circuits, Itr(fᵢ, fⱼ, t) (Field Chapter 14, current version: Itr ≥ 0, defined between continuation-potentials, with no strict upper bound; this chapter transfers it to circuits, and its computation and normalization must be declared). This chapter proposes: the higher Itr is, the more sufficient the correspondence among circuit contents is.

Component 3 (weight suppression): the Tuning-Field’s primary-secondary allocation among the several circuits. This chapter proposes: the Tuning-Field locally lowers the separability threshold involved in the dominant circuit (θ\_sep is a field-level quantity; reading it per circuit is proposed in this chapter, and its computation must be declared), causing the signal of that circuit to pass more easily through the Boundary-Gate, obtain higher weight, and become the dominant circuit. This corresponds to the Inner Chapter’s “Weighted suppression—whichever positional circuit is primary, that experiential dimension governs” (interpretation; v1.2’s quoted “Intent determines which positional circuit is taken as the base” is not a sentence of the source; this version follows the source).

This component is written as:

TunePriority(ℒ\_cir, t) ∈ \[0,1\]

It indicates the dominance actively granted by the Tuning-Field, namely the degree of advantage possessed by the highest-priority circuit relative to the other circuits. Note: φ\_f (residual capacity ratio) is not used here. φ\_f is a passive state quantity; it is not equal to the weight priority actively selected by the Tuning-Field. A field with high φ\_f is not necessarily the current tuning focus.

The overall alignment degree (illustrative reading, a model choice of this chapter) is:

Align(ℒ\_cir, t) = Sync(ℒ\_cir, t) · Itr\_mean(ℒ\_cir, t) · TunePriority(ℒ\_cir, t)

where Sync is the degree of temporal synchronization, ∈ \[0,1\]; Itr\_mean is the normalized mean of pairwise interpenetration readings among the circuits, ∈ \[0,1\] (citing Field Chapter 14, current version; the normalization must be declared); and TunePriority is the tuning priority of the dominant circuit (actively granted by the Tuning-Field, ∈ \[0,1\]). All three factors lie in \[0,1\], so the product lies in \[0,1\], consistent with Align ∈ \[0,1\]. v1.2 wrote “∝”: with an undeclared proportionality constant, Align ∈ \[0,1\] and the threshold Align\_min have no determinate meaning; this version uses the product equation.

Becoming-one criterion (operational delimitation of this chapter):

For \|ℒ\_cir\| ≥ 2: if there is a declared window ending at t of length not less than τ\_align, within which all circuits under consideration maintain Circuit = 1 and Align(ℒ\_cir, s) ≥ Align\_min for every moment s in the window, then becoming-one is recorded as established. This chapter reads this as the differentials of multiple circuits being unified, the object being established, and the Inner Chapter’s “Becoming one—then thing arises” occurring (interpretive correspondence).

Degeneracy note for the single-circuit case: Align and its components (Sync and Itr\_mean) are defined for \|ℒ\_cir\| ≥ 2. When \|ℒ\_cir\| = 1, there is no object of alignment; the becoming-one criterion degenerates into the sustained convergence of that circuit itself: its differentials stably form under tuning coordination and are maintained within a declared window ending at t of length not less than τ\_align (the reading of convergence must be declared; this corresponds to the completion-retention of the single-candidate case in the Observer Mathematical Form, Definition 47). Single-circuit manifestation (illumination through a single sensory channel) is therefore normally established and is not excluded by the alignment condition.

VI. Observation-Domain Generation Conditions

Synthesizing the preceding three sections, the operational delimitation of this chapter (definition): ObsRealm(t) = 1 (the Observation-Domain is established) if and only if the following four conditions are simultaneously satisfied. ObsRealm is a mechanism-layer predicate; for its correspondence with Form Chapter 22, see Section XII. The Observer Field f\_B under consideration is a declared single Observer Field; all four conditions are read for this f\_B: the gate-passage of Condition 1 is Pass(f\_A → f\_B); the circuits of Condition 2 are all of the form (f\_A, f\_B); ℒ\_cir in Condition 3 consists of circuits whose observer end is f\_B; and the continuation-flow of Condition 4 is the tuning continuation-flow of f\_B. Conditions 1 and 2 may use different Content Fields f\_A, but the Observer Field must be the same (observer individuation is outside the scope of this chapter; see item Sixth of Section XII).

Condition 1: At least one gate-passage is established.

∃ f\_A: Pass(f\_A → f\_B, t) = 1.

That is, there is at least one Content Field whose content has passed through the Boundary-Gate into the Observer Field according to the gate-passage criterion (Gate = 1, OutFlow \> 0, and FamOverlap\_eff \> 0).

Condition 2: At least one manifestation circuit with observer end f\_B is established.

∃ f\_A′: Circuit((f\_A′, f\_B), t) = 1.

That is, at least one Content Field has formed with f\_B a complete bidirectional coupling closed loop containing Intent reverse-flow and Press maintenance.

Note: Conditions 1 and 2 do not entail each other and must be checked separately. Pass = 1 only says that content has passed through the gate at this instant, whereas Circuit = 1 also requires positive coupling in both directions and Press \> 0; hence Pass = 1 does not entail Circuit = 1. Conversely, the definition of Circuit contains no same-instant condition on Gate, OutFlow, or FamOverlap\_eff, and existing Cpl can remain positive after gate-passage is interrupted (Theorem 4 of Field Chapter 17 v1.1), so Circuit = 1 does not entail Pass = 1 at the same instant; Cpl(f\_A → f\_B) turning positive from zero requires OutFlow(f\_A) \> 0, φ\_{f\_B} \> 0, and MatchTheta(f\_A, f\_B) \> 0 on a positive-measure period (Theorem 1), which contains neither R\_{f\_B} \> 0 nor AnchorCapacity \> 0, so it also does not entail Pass = 1 at some earlier moment. v1.2’s “Condition 1 (Pass = 1) is a necessary precondition of Condition 2 (Circuit = 1) … Circuit = 1 necessarily presupposes that Pass = 1 has already been established” is withdrawn. The Content Fields in Conditions 1 and 2 may differ (f\_A and f\_A′), but the Observer Field must be f\_B in both; requiring the same field pair, if declared, is a strengthened form of this chapter’s reading.

Condition 3: the becoming-one criterion is established (Section V).

If \|ℒ\_cir\| ≥ 2, then Align(ℒ\_cir, t) ≥ Align\_min persists within a declared window ending at t of length ≥ τ\_align.

If \|ℒ\_cir\| = 1, then the circuit's own convergence is sustained for ≥ τ\_align.

That is, the manifestation circuit or circuits already in the Observation-Domain complete sufficient convergence under the coordination of the Tuning-Field: in the multi-circuit case, by alignment; in the single-circuit case, by sustained convergence.

Condition 4: tuning continuation-flow is not in full fracture.

NoFrac(f\_B, p, t) = 1, where

NoFrac(f\_B, p, t) = 1 ⟺ OrigCont(f\_B, p, t) = 1 ∨ ∃ e \[e is a Breaking-Hold event ∧ p(e) = p ∧ ReCont\_e(f\_B, t) = 1\]

(Field Chapter 16 v1.2, Section 9; p is a declared continuation-position, and p(e) is the continuation-position at which Breaking-Hold event e occurs. ReCont\_e in Field Chapter 16 v1.2 is judged at the continuation-position carried by event e itself, so p(e) = p must be required; otherwise re-continuation of f\_B at another position p′ would make NoFrac at p equal 1. In the two predicates OrigCont and ReCont\_e, f\_B is read as one declared tuning continuation-flow of the Observer Field (a continuation-flow f in the sense of Field Chapter 16), and its correspondence to the Observer Field f\_B must be declared. Where NoFrac(f\_B) is written below, what is omitted is the same already fixed p and t.)

That is, the tuning continuation-flow of the Observer Field at p is either in Original Continuation or, after a Breaking-Hold event, has re-continued (has been Tuned and has dominated continuously since Tuning for no less than the declared window W\_R). v1.2 wrote ReCont(f\_B, p, t) = 1: ReCont in Field Chapter 16 v1.2 is defined only with respect to Breaking-Hold events, and a flow whose Original Continuation persists without any Breaking-Hold does not satisfy ReCont\_e, so it would literally be judged as collapsed; this version therefore writes NoFrac. NoFrac = 0 includes persistent non-closure after Breaking-Hold (the Full-Fracture case, Field Chapter 16 v1.2) and the case in which f\_B at p has neither Original Continuation nor Re-Continuation. This chapter proposes: when the continuation-flow is not in full fracture, the Observer Field can continuously coordinate the opening and closing of Boundary-Gates and the becoming-one of manifestation circuits.

The relation among the four conditions (reading): Condition 1 is the lowest precondition (content passes through the gate); Condition 2 is the core (only with reverse-flow is there self-manifestation; interpretation); Condition 3 is the object-formation condition (only when becoming-one occurs is there an object; multiple circuits by alignment, a single circuit by sustained convergence); Condition 4 is the dynamic continuation condition (continuation-flow not in full fracture).

VII. Dynamic Conditions of Observation-Domain Instability

Observation-Domain instability occurs in the following cases (the effect of each case on ObsRealm is read off from the definition; the corresponding phenomena are interpretation):

Case 1 (Boundary-Gate closure): if any of R\_{f\_B} ≤ 0 (Observer Field Margin Exhaustion), MatchTheta(f\_B) = 0 (tuning incompatible), or AnchorCapacity(f\_B) = 0 (no anchor and no new anchor can form; a reading) holds, then Gate(f\_B) = 0, all Pass(f\_A → f\_B) = 0, Condition 1 fails, and ObsRealm = 0. This corresponds to the Inner Chapter’s “not entering—is mute”: perception closes and Reality goes mute (interpretation).

Case 2 (reverse-flow interruption): if every circuit with observer end f\_B has Cpl(f\_B → f\_A) = 0 or Press(f\_B → f\_A) = 0 (Intent reverse-flow interrupted), then none of them satisfies the manifestation-circuit criterion (Circuit = 0; with one-way transmission it is what the Inner Chapters call an automatic circuit), Condition 2 fails, ObsRealm = 0, and the Observation-Domain loses self-manifestation. This corresponds to: the Power-Ring fails and consciousness cannot be initiated (interpretation). For the distinction between an instantaneous interruption of reverse-flow and the boundary-fringe, see item Third of Section XII.

Case 3 (becoming-one collapse): in the multi-circuit case, Align \< Align\_min (coordination is lost); or in the single-circuit case, convergence cannot be maintained. The becoming-one criterion fails, Condition 3 fails, and the unified structure of the object disintegrates. This corresponds to the Inner Chapter’s illusions: “seeing stillness as motion, small as large, … vision and hearing mismatched,” and other alignment-failure phenomena (interpretation).

Case 4 (tuning continuation-flow in full fracture): NoFrac(f\_B, p, t) = 0 (including the Full-Fracture case, Field Chapter 16 v1.2); Condition 4 fails and ObsRealm = 0. “the Tuning-Field loses the capacity for Re-Continuation and cannot maintain multi-circuit alignment” is proposed; correspondence to deep sleep or the disappearance of consciousness is interpretation.

Moreover, the Press condition is not redundant: Cpl(f\_B → f\_A) \> 0 does not entail Press(f\_B → f\_A) \> 0.

Proof sketch: the first part is read off from the definition of the manifestation circuit. XII. Interface Correspondence with the Form Chapters (added in v1.1, re-checked in this version)

This section gives the item-by-item correspondence between the field-level operators of this chapter and the Observer Group v1.0 (whose Form 4, the Observer Construction Form, is now v1.1) and Form Chapter 22 v1.2, in order to fix the conversion relation between the two vocabularies. Each item is a correspondence reading, not an entailment derived from the field-level definitions.

First, terminological stratification. ObsRealm in this chapter (the establishment of the Observation-Domain) is a mechanism-and-event-layer predicate: the Observation-Domain mechanism is operating and becoming-one has occurred (the appearing layer is non-empty). W(t), the Observation-Domain of Form Chapter 22, is a set-layer object: the manifestable domain, the set of what, under the current structure and parameters, can in principle be lit up. They are the same term with different referents and must be read together: by the correspondence reading of this section, ObsRealm = 1 corresponds to the appearing layer being non-empty, and hence to W(t) ≠ ∅ (Form Chapter 22 v1.2, proof of nonemptiness in Theorem 1: the occupant belongs to W); W(t) ≠ ∅ does not correspond to ObsRealm = 1 (manifestable but not yet manifest).

Second, field-level realization of the three gates. The gate of candidacy, Cand, corresponds to OutFlow(f\_A) \> 0 and the existence of matchable representation (FamOverlap\_eff can be satisfied, and AnchorCapacity provides anchor-position). The gate of admissibility, Adm, corresponds to Gate(f\_B) = 1 (the Boundary-Gate, consistent with the realization-correspondence section of Form Chapter 22 v1.2). The gate of reachability, Reach, corresponds to tuning reachability (MatchTheta, TunePriority, R\_{f\_B}, and NoFrac jointly give the field-level candidate content of the realizable allocation set 𝒳(t)). Per Definition 3 of Form Chapter 22 v1.2, Reach is meant in the complete-path sense: there exist a realizable allocation and a complete candidate–integration–maintenance path by which the item reaches (or is already at) the occupancy level within finite time and is maintained; the equilibrium admissibility condition Eq is listed separately, and Reach ⇒ Eq is not reversible. The field-level readings of this chapter give only candidate content for 𝒳(t) and do not by themselves decide Reach. Therefore: the eligibility domain E corresponds to gate-open and matchable; the manifestable domain W corresponds to eligibility plus a complete reachable path; the boundary-fringe M = E ∖ W corresponds to eligibility being present while no complete reachable path exists. A third referent must also be placed: the Inner Chapter's "Observation-Domain = the collection of opened Boundary-Gates" corresponds to the eligibility face (Gate open means that the gate of admissibility has passed); Form Chapter 22 then further distinguishes eligibility and reachability above it. Thus the definition in the Inner Chapter is refined rather than replaced. The joint reading of the three uses is: the Inner-Chapter Observation-Domain (eligibility face) ⊇ Form-Chapter W (manifestable domain), while ObsRealm is the mechanism-event predicate; the three are each given their place.

Third, the dynamic realization of the boundary-fringe. The criterion must be read modally, in harmony with the counterfactuality of Reach in Form Chapter 22 v1.2; the boundary-fringe is finally judged as E ∖ W (Definitions 5 and 6). In a corresponding model in which eligibility holds and reverse-flow is separately confirmed to be necessary for a complete path, Circuit being unestablishable under the current parameters and realizable allocation (the reverse-flow coupling or Press pathway is damaged, and is not within the realizable configuration) can serve as one source of Reach failing: content enters the field, can be acted on by the competition mechanism, and can gain partial progress, but cannot manifest no matter how long it is exposed (with parameters unchanged). Instantaneous Circuit = 0 does not decide band membership. Even if Circuit can be established, the complete Reach path must still be checked (Reach also contains integration and maintenance duties, Form Chapter 22 v1.2, Definition 3): what is eligible, has Reach, and is currently unmanifested belongs to W ∖ Cur; what is eligible but lacks Reach belongs to E ∖ W. Thus the field-level characterization of blindsight (a correspondence reading): the Pass channel is retained, reverse-flow coupling (Cpl(f\_B → f\_A) or Press) is structurally impaired, Circuit cannot be established, and hence Reach fails - a chronic case of boundary-fringe (Form Chapter 22, Counter-example 5). Subliminal unmanifestation, by contrast, is the case in which eligibility and complete Reach both hold but the content has not won the competition or exposure is insufficient. The two kinds of "having effect without manifestation" can, in the corresponding model above, be given one field-level source of distinction by whether Circuit can be established (not by its instantaneous value); band membership is finally judged by E ∖ W and W ∖ Cur.

Fourth, the correspondence between occupancy and becoming-one. Becoming-one - in the multi-circuit case, Align ≥ Align\_min sustained within a declared window for ≥ τ\_align; in the single-circuit case, the sustained convergence of that circuit itself, as given in the degeneracy note in Section 5 - corresponds to completed and maintained convergence (candidate completion per the Observer Mathematical Form, Definition 45, maintenance per the completion-retention of Definition 47; Form Chapter 22 v1.2: z reaching 1 is by itself insufficient to decide Occ). τ\_align corresponds to Δt. TunePriority corresponds to the active item of resource share x actively granted by the Tuning-Field within zero-sum allocation. The field-level candidate realization mechanism of exactly-one corresponds to the unique granting of dominance: the Tuning-Field's relative-advantage suppression of the highest-priority circuit. It must be recorded truthfully that synchronic double occupancy is not excluded — the Observer Mathematical Form, Theorem 8, has proven that a retention state of double completion can exist — so this item is no longer "the field-level realization face of Lemma 3" but one candidate field-level realization mechanism of the pool-level current single-value principle (a constitutive commitment, unproven); it does not prove exactly-one; at most one is limited by that commitment, and existence is to be verified separately (Form Chapter 22 v1.2, proof of nonemptiness in Theorem 1).

Fifth, the correspondence between instability and the four bands (band membership is judged by set eligibility: the boundary-fringe is E ∖ W and the unmanifested within the boundary is W ∖ Cur, Form Chapter 22 v1.2, Definition 6; not by instantaneous field-level values). Case 1 (Boundary-Gate closure) corresponds to contraction of the eligibility domain, and content becomes outside the boundary. Case 2 (reverse-flow interruption): if reverse-flow cannot be restored under the current parameters and realizable allocation (Circuit cannot be established), and reverse-flow is necessary for a complete path in the corresponding model, then Reach fails and content withdraws into the boundary-fringe; when reverse-flow can be restored, the band must still be judged by complete Reach eligibility, and the content cannot be judged unmanifested within the boundary merely by restorability. Case 3 (becoming-one collapse): the appearing layer is empty with respect to the content in question, and that content is unmanifested within the boundary or in the boundary-fringe according to whether a complete reachable path exists; becoming-one collapse alone does not decide the band. Case 4 (continuation-flow in full fracture) corresponds to the manifestation-vacancy zone (Observer Mathematical Form, Theorems 7 and 11; vacancy follows the general criterion, the non-existence of any realizable candidate–integration–maintenance path; r ≤ η, in the single-candidate, no-overlap, constant-coefficient linear model, only excludes new completion from below threshold, does not by itself decide vacancy, and does not exclude the maintenance of an existing occupancy, Form Chapter 22 v1.2, Theorem 3 (ii)): W contracts to emptiness while the eligibility domain may persist (Form Chapter 22 v1.2, Theorem 1(ii)), consistent with "candidate activity persists in deep sleep without manifestation." Whether full fracture of the continuation-flow makes every complete reachable path nonexistent is not proven in this chapter; this item is a correspondence reading.

Sixth, the division of labor with pool-boundaries (checked conclusion). The four conditions in this chapter all belong to the admission-and-manifestation mechanism (Conditions 1 through 3) and to the dynamic precondition of observer persistence (Condition 4). Observer individuation - who constitutes one Observer, and how many Observers there are in one system - is not within the scope of this chapter. Its criterion is Sep against Coact + Infilt + Reentry (Mathematical Form of Observer Individuation, Section 6: the layered pool-track definitions and the pre-identification protocol; for the field-level basis, see Field Chapter 14); note that this protocol presently stands at framework grade with its calibration open (Observer Adjudication Form H5, half-closed), and that the number of pools yields only the number of individuation-unit candidates, not directly an observer count. There is no overlap and no conflict between this chapter and pool-boundaries. Proposition 6 (necessary conditions of self-manifestational operation) describes the necessary configuration of self-manifestational operation after a single Observer has been established; it is not an individuation criterion.

Seventh, re-check of matter awaiting formalization (f) of Form Chapter 22 v1.2 (new in this version). That chapter requires this chapter, when upgraded, to re-check whether it actually provides the boundary, reachability, and integration-maintenance conditions that chapter needs. Conclusion of the re-check: this chapter provides field-level operational delimitations (Gate, Pass, Circuit, the becoming-one criterion, NoFrac) and the correspondence readings listed above. The gate of admissibility has a direct field-level landing point (Gate); for the existence of complete reachable paths in Reach, the integration and maintenance conditions, and the outside-existence condition H\_excl, this chapter provides no proof, and its field-level readings give only candidate content.
