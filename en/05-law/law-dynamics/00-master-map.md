Use, Nature, and Declaration of Nothing New

### 1.1 Use

The thirteen texts of The Law-Dynamics Group-Form have each been sealed. Each answers one question, and they are joined by interfaces; but what the thirteen together close into one theoretical object, which relations between texts are hard logical dependence, which only semantic inheritance or dependence in running, and which questions look alike but cannot stand in for one another — these can no longer be seen clearly from the cross-references of the texts alone.

This master diagram draws these relations clearly once, and gives the group-level boundary of closure of The Law-Dynamics Group-Form within the working domains currently declared.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;adds no proposition to be proved

Section Two

The Five-Zone Structure of the Thirteen Texts

The thirteen texts are not thirteen linear steps, but five functional zones.

2.1 Zone One　The Zone of Current Qualification (Chapters 1, 2, 3)

It answers: whether a standing Law is really acting at this moment; and if acting, what its degree profile is. The comparison of familiarity follows the familiarity-order domain separately; see below.

Chapter 1 proves the necessary direction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;LawAct\_{F,t}( L ; ξ\_t )　⇒　CurLawBear

where CurLawBear = CurrentLawOccurrence ∧ DutyConformance, the two layers constrained by the binding of one and the same current realization.

Chapter 2 supplies the sufficient direction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;CurLawBear　⇒　LawAct

The two texts combine into the two-way closure of current action.

Chapter 3 (v1.1) asks separately about degree, setting up two grading domains apart:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;familiarity-order domain　J\_δ = { δ̄ }  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Degree profile domain　J\_D = { δ̄, Reentry, 𝒵, FlowShare }

The familiarity-order domain compares "more familiar" in the original sense — the comparison of familiarity follows Sedimentation-Degree (Outer Chapter 3, 「可称深者为熟」; Inner Chapter 9, 「其深积而在顶，称之谓熟」; Field Chapter 1, Definition 13 and Operation 11), δ̄ being the path Sedimentation-Degree statistic. The four items of the profile domain are the four inputs of the Continuation Competition Index family CI = Φ( δ̄, Reentry, 𝒵, FlowShare ) of Familiar-Route Measurement Equation · Field Chapter 11 v1.2; 𝒵 is named continuation cost. In the profile, a decrease of 𝒵 and an increase of the other three are stipulated as favourable directions; this is not a proven actual competitive advantage, nor "more familiar," and the profile order may not be called more familiar. To give the four dimensions one direction, that text writes uniformly

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D̂( q ) = ( δ( q ), Reentry( q ), −𝒵( q ), FlowShare( q ) )

where δ denotes the path Sedimentation-Degree statistic δ̄; and in its Section Ten takes the comparison vector D( q ) with the same third component; the componentwise condition is written 𝒵\_A ≤ 𝒵\_B. Chapter 7 inherits these four coordinates, writing D( q, t ) = ( δ, Reentry, 𝒵, FlowShare ) and marking 𝒵↓ (Chapter 7 v1.1 notes that they correspond to J\_D, δ being δ̄). The two refer to the same thing in different notations; see Table B of 12.3 (v1.0 wrongly referred to 11.3).

What is obtained is a partial order, not an overall score, distinguishing strict dominance, Degree equality, and incomparability; non-totality on the legal Law domain presupposes the existence of a crossing pair of profiles, and the non-deletability and nondegeneracy of the four dimensions rest on G\_{D-Sep} (Chapter 3 v1.1). Degree is kept apart from the qualification of current action, and is not fed back as a condition of LawAct.

What this zone establishes is qualification and degree at one moment, not yet crossing into trajectories. So it is called the zone of current qualification, not the zone of dynamics.

Section Three

Three Kinds of Dependence and the Diagram of Dependence among the Texts

### 3.1 Three Kinds of Dependence

Dependence between texts is of three kinds:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;H　hard logical dependence — the proof of the downstream text takes the conclusion of the upstream text as premise; should the upstream be withdrawn, the conclusion of the downstream fails or becomes pending  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S　semantic or protocol inheritance — the downstream text carries over the terms, stratifications, boundaries, or protocols of the upstream, while its proof does not take the upstream conclusion as premise  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R　dependence in running realization — the objects of the downstream text can obtain witnesses only on the running substrate given upstream

An arrow of dependence is not an arrow of cause, nor an arrow of generation in time. An H arrow says "the proof of this text uses the conclusion of that text," not "the object of that text produced the object of this text."

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;the dynamics of the field　→　trajectory　→　the read-out of LawDyn

and it may not be drawn as

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;LawDyn　→　change of the field

### 5.3 Three Layers

One and the same LawDyn has its place in three layers: Chapter 4 gives its object qualification, Chapter 5 its formal system, Chapter 7 its running witness on a real Sedimentation-Field. Section Eleven

Open Boundaries, Candidates for Upgrade, and Downstream Interfaces

### 11.1 What Is Not Closed

The following questions are not among what this group closes; this master diagram lists them actively, to guard against misuse.

Identity. The definition and adjudication of SameLaw are not closed. Chapter 8 proves only that two rules of pairing can be distinguished, neither being a criterion of identity; its 13.3 states that no closed property of Law identity has yet been produced. Wherever the thirteen texts touch identity they do not cross the line, so this question's not being closed does not constitute a gap in the ontology of Law-dynamics.

Control. Chapter 6 proves only that there exists a path; it does not prove control, nor universality, nor probability one.

Networks of multi-level interaction. Chapters 10 through 12 deal only with pairwise, event-level, direct interaction; long cascades, graphs of causal ancestry, global interaction networks and their controllability are not within them, nor are they supplied by Chapter 13.

Probability. Of the six classes of event it can be adjudicated whether they occur, change, or can be reached; with what probability some event occurs, and how future events are distributed under competition among several Laws, is not the content of this group. The texts are explicit on this: Chapter 6 does not upgrade to probability one, what Chapter 7 proves is not a probabilistic theorem, Chapter 9 does not claim a probabilistic distribution of sources.

Intentional selection. This group answers how a Law acts, how it moves, how it interacts with another Law; it does not answer why some Law is selected by the present intent, nor how the Intent-Field is configured, driven, contested, and rerouted.

Computation. That this group has an operating substrate and formal systems is not the same as having answered what computation is, or what structure is only legal running and not computation. In the thirteen texts the word "computation" is used only in its ordinary sense — computing a profile, the finite steps of solution, and the like — and not one text establishes its definition; Chapter 5 further states expressly that it does not assert that each subroutine is computable and terminates on every field specification.
