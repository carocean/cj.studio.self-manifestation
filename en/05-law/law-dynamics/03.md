# The Grading Form of Law

Section One

Purpose

This text establishes, for Laws that already stand, a legitimate institution for comparing their degree of depth and familiarity.

That a Law stands shows only that it has acquired the qualification of really and stably bearing later Continuation through a relational organization in the field. Qualification is two-valued; depth and familiarity are not. Of two Laws that stand equally, one may have sedimented a deep and familiar route while the other has only just taken shape. The question this text answers is: under a comparison protocol declared in advance, when can the degree of sedimentation and familiarization of two standing Laws be adjudicated as one being no weaker than the other, strictly dominant, degree-equal, incomparable, incommensurable, or currently undecided?

The form-kind is the grading form, an auxiliary form, of the multi-dimensional partial-order type.

No single-valued Grade(L), overall score, or total order is established on the Degree profile. Comparison of familiarity, following the original meaning, takes sedimentation degree alone (the familiarity-order domain J\_δ, Section Seven); the Degree profile takes the four inputs of the Continuation Competition Index family of Familiar-Route Measurement Equation · Field Chapter 11 v1.2, for which the canonical source gives no unique function, so the strongest legitimate comparison available on the profile is a four-dimensional partial order, coordinate by coordinate, not a ranking.

This text takes on Degree only. Whether a Law stands, whether it is currently acting, how it is generated, whether it can stably persist, whether it is still the same Law, how several Laws compete, why Degree changes, and how a difference of Degree affects the amount of Continuation priority on the next beat — none of these is here.

The Necessity Form of Law has already sorted depth, familiarity, and ease of coming forth first into Degree, apart from current qualification; The Necessity Form of Law-Action has further excluded Degree from the qualification for current action. This text takes up that task of Degree, which has not yet been formalized.

Section Three

Three Questions That Force Out an Institution of Grading

3.1 Why Standing Laws Must Still Be Graded

Suppose two Laws A and B both stand in F at time t: each has a carrier organization, and each has acquired DynamicRole and ΩSupport. By The General Form of Law and The Necessity Form of Law, the two adjudications of qualification are exactly the same: both are one.

But in the field the two are not the same. The path A runs along has been passed thousands of times; its sedimentation is deep, and the greater part of later flow goes to it. B has only just taken shape and barely crossed the threshold of Law-formation, and every Continuation along it must contend with other paths.

The layer of qualification has nothing to say about this: it is two-valued, and asks whether something stands, not how far it has come.

The canonical source does have something to say. The Experience-Field · Inner Chapter 9 says:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"Like a road made by many feet, it settles as Law."

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"Flow sinks into Sedimentation; what is often passed becomes well-worn. Gradually it deepens. Thus Law clears beneath."

The order of the two sentences is one piece of evidence. The first, 「自而沉积为律」 ("it settles as Law," of itself), gives the point at which law-standing comes to rest; the "gradually it deepens" of the second comes after it: deepening continues after a Law has come to stand, and the degree of depth is not fixed at the moment of law-standing, so the degree of depth is not a threshold of Law-formation. The order of the sentences does not by itself exclude a minimal depth threshold; that the degree of depth is not part of the qualification for law-standing rests on the source text giving no threshold d\* of law-standing independent of sedimentary formation (The Necessity Form of Law, Section Six, step two). This analysis was carried out by The Necessity Form of Law, and this text inherits it.

What "gradually it deepens" and "Law clears beneath" describe is exactly the dimension that goes on changing after a Law stands. If the institution of qualification does not take up this dimension, it has nowhere to go.

So an institution of grading must be set up separately.

3.2 Why the Familiarity Order Takes Sedimentation Depth Alone, While the Degree Profile Takes Four Dimensions

To compare familiarity, take a single quantity: sedimentation depth. This is the original meaning: the deeper may be called familiar (Outer Chapter 3); deep sediment rises to the top, and this is called the well-worn (Inner Chapter 9); Field Chapter 1, Definition 13 and Operation 11 fix that “more familiar” is compared by sedimentation degree.

This is not a matter of convenience but of the original meaning.

Take two paths. The first is very deeply sedimented but has long had no flow through it — δ high and FlowShare near zero; later flow has long since turned elsewhere. The second is still shallowly sedimented but is being passed repeatedly by a large volume of returning flow — δ low, and both Reentry and FlowShare high.

Judged by sedimentation depth, the first is more familiar than the second. That judgment holds by the original meaning: the first is deeper, and so more familiar.

But the first has long had no flow through it and may no longer have Familiar Route qualification: a Familiar Route further requires a frequent-travel record within a declared window (Field Chapter 1, Definition 13; the path-level operational qualification of Field Chapter 11 v1.2). The second is higher on the two items of re-entry and flow share; this is not greater familiarity, nor does it amount to an overall advantage in competition — where the four dimensions cross, the profile is incomparable. Field Chapter 11 v1.2 has made the four items inputs of the Continuation Competition Index family CI, no longer the definition of familiarity or of a degree of familiarity.

So this text sets up two grading domains: the familiarity-order domain J\_δ = { δ̄ }, comparing familiarity by the original meaning; and the Degree profile domain J\_D = { δ̄, Reentry, 𝒵, FlowShare }, comparing the degree profile of continuation competition. In this example they give different results: the familiarity order judges the first more familiar; on the profile the four dimensions cross, and by what Section Ten fixes the two are incomparable. They do not contradict each other; they answer different questions: the former answers which is deeper and more familiar, the latter which is no weaker on each item of continuation competition.

The familiarity order may not be replaced by the profile order, and the profile order may not pass itself off as the familiarity order.

3.3 Why a Law Cannot Be Given an Overall Score on the Degree Profile

One step back: take the four coordinates, weight them, and sum, obtaining an overall score.

That will not do either, for two reasons.

First, the weights have no source. Familiar-Route Measurement Equation v1.2 gives the Continuation Competition Index family

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;CI(q, t) = Φ( δ̄(q,t), Reentry(q,t), 𝒵(q,t), FlowShare(q,t) )

and states: Φ is not fixed as a unique function; every Φ meeting the construction requirements belongs to the family; in a concrete comparison the same Φ and input protocol must be fixed.

The canonical source gives only the four coordinates and their construction directions; it gives no unique Φ. Were the weights fixed here, the resulting order would be an invention of this text, not a conclusion of the canonical source. For any two Laws that cross on the four dimensions, which ranks higher would be decided by the weights chosen — another set of weights, another answer. That is not grading; it is making up a score.

Second, a Law is not a path. One duty can have more than one legal RolePath, and one Law can have more than one legal realization. To press these into an overall score, they must first be pressed into a single number, and the manner of pressing likewise has no canonical source.

So this text sets no overall score on the Degree profile. The strongest legitimate comparison available is a coordinate-by-coordinate comparison along the four directions the canonical source gives. When things are comparable and when they are not is settled from there. The familiarity-order domain has one dimension, and its order follows the original meaning; it is not an overall score.

Section Four

Freezing the Comparison Protocol

### 4.1 Γ\_D

Comparison cannot proceed in a vacuum. The Degree read-outs of one and the same Law can differ under different duties, different entry points, and different contexts; if two Laws each took their read-outs on whatever basis favoured them, the result of comparison would mean nothing.

So the comparison protocol is frozen first, denoted Γ\_D. It is a purely auxiliary object: not a field quantity, not an ontological predicate, and bearing no independent truth value.

Γ\_D declares at least ten items:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;1\. the Laws being compared;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;2\. the fixed F, t, I, or the range of variation allowed;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;3\. the rule of correspondence for the same duty and the same evaluation unit;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;4\. Type;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;5\. EvalMode;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;6\. the basis on which Degree coordinates are read (including the fixed common aggregation protocol of the path Sedimentation-Degree δ̄ and the time protocol of Field Chapter 11 v1.2);  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;7\. the direction of comparison;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;8\. the precision of comparison;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;9\. the correspondence of carriers across states;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;10\. which quantities are fixed and which are allowed to vary.

### 4.2 The Discipline of Freezing

The protocol must be frozen before comparison.

It may not be modified after the result is seen. The most favourable mapping may not be chosen after the fact in order to make two Laws comparable. Shrinking the dimension set or changing the criteria in passing so as to manufacture comparability is prohibited — this is expressly prohibited by the specification of the grading form in Canon Chapter 1, and this text inherits the prohibition.

Wherever the protocol is changed after comparison, that comparison is void and must be made again under the new protocol.

Section Five

The Load-Bearing Path

5.1 Degree Is Not Read Off an Abstract Law

Degree is a quantity on paths. A Law is a relational qualification, not a path. Between the two there must be a complete load-bearing chain, with no step skipped:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Law　→　Law witness　→　realization　→　RolePath　→　Hosts　→　SupportTrace　→　physical familiar route　→　Degree read-out

The defect of skipping steps is this: were Degree allowed to be read directly off an abstract Law, there would be no telling whose δ and which components' Flow were being read, and comparison would have no object.

The Mathematical Form of Law already has the institutions RolePath, Hosts, Comp, and Supp, which guarantee that the generative paths really bearing a duty are supported by one and the same realization, and which exclude free riding on shared segments and joint nodes. No separate structure of bearing is created here; read-outs are only taken on these.

### 5.2 SupportTrace

By Definition 3.9 of The Mathematical Form of Law, the nodes of the generation graph fall into four classes, and each node z has Hosts(z) ⊆ 𝔘\_t, the set of structural components hosting that node: for a structural-state node, Hosts = {u}; for an intermediate generated quantity with a host, Hosts = {u}; for a joint node Z\_τ = (z₁,…,z\_n), Hosts = ⋃\_k Hosts(z\_k); for pure computational quantities without a structural host, pure output read-outs, and Read(d), Hosts = ∅.

For a legal RolePath

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;π = ( z\_0, …, z\_n )

define the auxiliary load-bearing trace

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Σ(π) = ( Hosts(z\_0), …, Hosts(z\_n) )

with empty Hosts removed, and with the original order of generation and the structure of joint hosts preserved.

Lemma One (legitimacy of the load-bearing trace)

Σ(π) marks which structures in the field actually bear π, and all its components fall within Comp(π).

Proof. By The Mathematical Form of Law, Comp(Z) = ⋃\_{z∈Z} Hosts(z); taking a path as its set of nodes, Comp(π) is a special case of this definition. Σ(π) takes Hosts node by node, and removing empty items adds no component; hence ⋃ Σ(π) ⊆ Comp(π), and once the empty items are removed the non-empty parts of the two are the same. ∎

Σ(π) has only this one duty: to mark who the bearers are.

Σ(π) may not be identified directly and unconditionally with a physical familiar route q. The two lie on different levels: Σ(π) is a sequence of bearing structures; q is a directed path in the sense of the Field Chapters. Getting from the former to the latter requires a legitimate rule of reduction; see the next section.

Section Six

Degree Readability and the Discipline of Joint Hosts

### 6.1 PathReadable

If Σ(π) can be legitimately reduced, according to the existing path definitions of the Field Chapters, to a physical familiar route q\_π, and all four Degree coordinates can be read on q\_π, then

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;PathReadable(π) = 1

### 6.2 The Hard Discipline for Joint Hosts

If joint hosts, hyperpaths, or other structures occur and upstream gives no legitimate rule for reducing them to a single path, then

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;PathReadable(π) = ⊥

In that case one may not: pick one branch at will; take an average; take the maximum; take the minimum; invent a joint path; invent a hyperpath; let the most favourable branch represent the joint bearing.

The reason lies in the load-bearing chain itself. The Hosts of a joint node are the union of the Hosts of its branches — by Definition 3.9 (c) of The Mathematical Form of Law, the bearer is the whole, not any one branch. Letting one branch represent the whole, the δ and Flow read are not the bearing this RolePath actually receives; taking an extreme or a mean, the read-out obtained corresponds to no path in the field. Neither is reading; both are fabrication.

Lemma Two (the boundary of Degree readability)

Law\_{F,t}(L, I) = 1 and PathReadable(π) = 1 are mutually independent.

Proof. On the one hand, the standing of a Law is given by Established ∧ Govern, which contains no requirement about whether Hosts can be reduced to a single path; so there exist cases with Law = 1 in which Σ(π) of some legal RolePath contains joint hosts that cannot legitimately be reduced to a single path, and then PathReadable = ⊥ while Law = 1. On the other hand, PathReadable asks only whether a read-out can be obtained, not whether that path bears a duty of a standing Law. ∎

So Degree = ⊥ may never be read as Law = 0. This is the formal side of the gate set up in 2.2.

Should the Field Chapters later formally establish a familiarity for joint paths, this text is upgraded. Until then, ⊥ is the only legitimate output.

### 6.3 Readability at the Level of the Unit

PathReadable governs only one RolePath. What comparison needs, however, is all the bearing on an evaluation unit. A layer must be added between the two, or there is a danger: if a unit contains one read witness and one legal but unreadable witness, and only the read one is admitted to comparison, the unreadable one may in fact lie on the true non-dominated front — and an unknown profile would pass for a known one.

So the full set of bearing on the unit is fixed first. Denote by

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;𝒲^Γ\_u(L)

all the legal complete Law-bearing witnesses of L on evaluation unit u under Γ\_D. All of them are taken here, not those that enter comparison: if the boundary were whether a witness enters comparison, an unfavourable witness could be excluded by claiming it did not enter comparison, and readability could be produced artificially. The full set is fixed by the third and tenth items of Γ\_D, not delimited ad hoc by whoever is comparing.

Finiteness premise (declared in this version): for each evaluation unit u, the full set of legal bearings 𝒲^Γ\_u(L) is a non-empty finite set. Every conclusion of Sections Thirteen through Fifteen involving fronts (Theorem Seven, Theorem Eight, the Proposition of 15.3, and 15.5) takes this as its premise. An arbitrarily truncated finite subset of witnesses may not replace the full set of legal bearings; if the full set of legal bearings of some unit is not a non-empty finite set, this version gives no front for it, and the unit is recorded as ⊥. This is written into the definition of readability below.

Hence

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;UnitReadable^Γ\_u(L) = 1　⟺　0 \< \|𝒲^Γ\_u(L)\| \< ∞　∧　∀ w ∈ 𝒲^Γ\_u(L), DegreeRec(w) ≠ ⊥

As soon as one legal bearing in 𝒲^Γ\_u(L) has DegreeRec equal to ⊥, the unit is ⊥; this may not be silently ignored.

6.4 Readability at the Level of the Whole Law

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DegreeReadable^Γ(L) = 1　⟺　∀ u ∈ 𝒰\_Γ, UnitReadable^Γ\_u(L) = 1

The state of readability set up in 2.2 has exactly this content. DegreeReadable^Γ(L) takes one or ⊥, never zero: it does not adjudicate qualification, only whether the current profile can be read completely.

This closure agrees with the three-valued determination of direction in Section Fourteen: an unreadable bearing is not treated as non-existent, but makes its unit ⊥; a unit that is ⊥ makes the direction either ⊥ or, because of a determined failure elsewhere, zero. The discipline that zero takes precedence over ⊥ is thereby carried through to the layer of read-outs — a determined failure is not washed back into undecidedness because something is unread elsewhere, and an unread bearing is not treated as non-existent because it is silent.

The familiarity-order domain requires only δ̄ to be readable. Write DegreeReadable^Γ\_δ(L), defined as above (including the condition that the bearing set of the unit is non-empty and finite, 0 \< \|𝒲^Γ\_u(L)\| \< ∞), with DegreeRec(w) ≠ ⊥ replaced by the δ̄ read-out of w ≠ ⊥. A Law may be readable for the familiarity order but not for the profile; the two are determined separately and propagate separately.

Section Seven

Declaration of the Grading Domain

### 7.1 Type

This text takes the grading form of the multi-dimensional partial-order type, not of the single-axis stratification type.

By the specification of the grading form in Canon Chapter 1, one type must be declared; the two may not be mixed. For the multi-dimensional partial-order type, the grading domain must first be declared: the set of dimensions fixed, the criterion of each dimension explicit, the direction of order on each dimension explicit, and the value range of each dimension explicit. They are declared item by item below.

### 7.2 The Dimension Set

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;J\_D = { δ̄, Reentry, 𝒵, FlowShare }    J\_δ = { δ̄ }

The canonical source of J\_D is the Continuation Competition Index family of Familiar-Route Measurement Equation · Field Chapter 11 v1.2:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;CI(q, t) = Φ( δ̄(q,t), Reentry(q,t), 𝒵(q,t), FlowShare(q,t) )

The four items are the four arguments of that expression, frozen as the dimension set of the Degree profile domain. J\_δ is the familiarity-order domain: following Outer Chapter 3, “the deeper may be called familiar,” Inner Chapter 9, “deep sediment rises to the top; this is called the well-worn,” and Field Chapter 1, Definition 13 and Operation 11, “more familiar” is compared by sedimentation degree, realized at the path level by δ̄. The familiarity order has its own basis in the original meaning and is declared in advance as a separate task; its read-out coincides with the projection onto the δ̄ coordinate of the four-dimensional profile. Familiarity-order results answer only the comparison of depth and do not pass themselves off as an overall four-dimensional competition grade (12.2).

7.3 The Criterion and Direction of Order of Each Dimension

δ̄(q, t): the Sedimentation-Degree statistic of path q, per Field Chapter 11 v1.2: obtained from the Sedimentation-Degrees of the Structural Positions along the path by a declared fixed common aggregation protocol (item six of Γ\_D). The larger δ̄, the more familiar (the sole coordinate of the familiarity-order domain, the first coordinate of the profile domain).

Reentry(q, t): the re-entry ratio of path q, per the definition of Field Chapter 10 v1.1 adopted by Field Chapter 11 v1.2: the total subsequent flow re-entering ÷ the direct new input, defined only when the direct new input is greater than 0; the path value is a weighted sum with declared weights. In this text’s profile order, an increase of Reentry is stipulated as the favourable direction (with other inputs fixed, members of the CI family are non-decreasing in this direction); this is not a proved actual advantage in competition, nor greater familiarity.

𝒵(q, t): the continuation cost of path q, citing Field Chapter 4 v1.2’s 𝒵(q,t) (formerly named continuation impedance). In this text’s profile order, a decrease of 𝒵 is stipulated as the favourable direction (with other inputs fixed, members of the CI family are non-decreasing in this direction); this is not a proved actual advantage in competition.

FlowShare(q, t): the flow share of path q,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;FlowShare(q, t) = Flow(q, t) / Σ\_j Flow(q\_j, t)

It is defined only when the total flow of the declared finite set of paths satisfies 0 \< Σ\_j Flow(q\_j, t) \< ∞; then FlowShare ∈ \[0, 1\] and Σ\_i FlowShare(q\_i) = 1; when the total flow is 0 it is marked ⊥ and the normalization identity is not used. When paths overlap, it is a share normalized over the declared path flows and is not interpreted as resource occupation. In this text’s profile order, an increase of FlowShare is stipulated as the favourable direction (with other inputs fixed, members of the CI family are non-decreasing in this direction); this is not a proved actual advantage in competition. The predictive power of CI remains to be proved (Field Chapter 11 v1.2).

Profile read-outs follow the time protocol of Field Chapter 11 v1.2: only readings at or before t are used; δ̄ and 𝒵 take end values, Reentry takes the window mean, FlowShare takes the ratio of cumulative flows over the window; window readings must be finite and defined, otherwise they are ⊥. Field Chapter 11 v1.2 does not offer “using the flow-share proportion” as the reason for avoiding circularity; same-beat self-reference is blocked by the time protocol, see Section Seventeen.

### 7.4 Unifying the Favourable Direction

To give the four dimensions one direction, write uniformly

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D̂(q) = ( δ(q), Reentry(q), −𝒵(q), FlowShare(q) )

All four components are such that larger means more favourable (the stipulated direction of the profile order, not a proved actual advantage in competition). The minus sign on 𝒵 is only a notation for unifying direction; it does not change its physical meaning and introduces no new quantity.

For brevity below, every δ among the Degree coordinates means the path Sedimentation-Degree statistic δ̄. The two notations must be made clear together, so that they are not mixed later. The third component of D̂ and of D(q) is −𝒵, so that the four dimensions share one direction; Φ, following the canonical general expression, takes 𝒵 itself as its third argument. The two correspond by

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;−𝒵\_A ≥ −𝒵\_B　⟺　𝒵\_A ≤ 𝒵\_B

and are two ways of writing the same thing. Below, every component condition involving ⪰\_D is written as 𝒵\_A ≤ 𝒵\_B, and every argument of Φ is written as 𝒵.

### 7.5 The Standing of Φ

The canonical source (Field Chapter 11 v1.2) states expressly: Φ is not fixed as a unique function; every Φ meeting the construction requirements belongs to the Continuation Competition Index family.

So it is prohibited to stipulate on J\_D a linear weighting or any other overall function, and prohibited to fix weights for the four dimensions. The robust order proved in Section Eleven is exactly the strongest conclusion obtainable while no unique Φ is fixed.

Theorem One (the theorem of the Degree grading domain)

Under Γ\_D, J\_δ and J\_D each constitute a legitimate grading domain: J\_δ of one dimension, J\_D of four; the criterion of each dimension is given by existing quantities of the Field Chapters; the direction of order on each dimension is fixed; and the value range of each dimension is explicit — δ̄ and 𝒵 take the continuous domains fixed by the Field Chapters, Reentry takes nonnegative reals where defined, and FlowShare takes \[0,1\] (when defined).

Proof. All items cite existing quantities of the Field Chapters directly and are not newly created here; the directions are given item by item by Familiar-Route Measurement Equation v1.2 (the larger δ̄, the more familiar, by the original meaning; the other three are construction directions of the Continuation Competition Index family); the value ranges have the same source. The sixth item of Γ\_D has already fixed the basis of reading, and the tenth has already fixed which items vary and which do not; so the read-outs within one grading domain are comparable. ∎

Section Eight

The Non-Redundancy of the Four Dimensions

### 8.1 What Non-Redundancy Means

The four items are the current Degree coordinates, none of which can be deleted.

By the specification of the grading form in Canon Chapter 1, the non-redundancy of each dimension must be argued: by exhibiting a counterexample pair — two objects agreeing on all other dimensions and differing on this one — or by an equivalent rigorous reasoning.

What 8.2 through 8.5 give dimension by dimension below are directions of separation — routes of construction by which that dimension can be separated from the other three. A direction of separation is not the certificate of non-redundancy the Canon requires; the latter must be given by instances on the legal domain of Law Degree, and its strength is declared in 8.6 and in Theorem Two. Keep the δ, Reentry, 𝒵, and Flow(q) of the target path unchanged, and change only the total flow Σ\_{j≠i} Flow(q\_j) of the other competing paths that have no coupling with the target Law witness.

Whereas the other three local quantities of the target path, δ, Reentry, and 𝒵, are all quantities of that path itself, and do not vary with the total flow of other paths.

Section Ten

The Partial Order on Degree Vectors

10.1 The Partial Order Is Built on Vectors, Not on Paths

For a Degree-readable familiar route q, take

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D(q) = ( δ(q), Reentry(q), −𝒵(q), FlowShare(q) )

The comparison relation is defined on four-dimensional vectors, not on paths:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D\_A ⪰\_D D\_B

if and only if the four items are each no weaker:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;δ\_A ≥ δ\_B  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Reentry\_A ≥ Reentry\_B  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;𝒵\_A ≤ 𝒵\_B  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;FlowShare\_A ≥ FlowShare\_B

Paths enter the domain of comparison only by way of D(·).

It must be explained why the order is not built directly on the set of paths. Two different paths q\_A ≠ q\_B can happen to have exactly the same four-dimensional read-outs. Were the order built on paths, then q\_A ⪰ q\_B and q\_B ⪰ q\_A would both hold while q\_A = q\_B does not — antisymmetry fails on the set of paths, and what is obtained is only a preorder, whose equivalence classes must be treated separately. By the specification of the grading form in Canon Chapter 1, for the multi-dimensional partial-order type, where antisymmetry fails, it must be declared that what is established is a preorder, and the treatment of the equivalence classes given.

Built on vectors, this problem does not arise: the coordinatewise order on four-dimensional real vectors is a true partial order. The relation between paths is given by their images, not by the order itself.

### 10.2 Degree Equality

Define

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;q\_A ∼\_D q\_B　⟺　D(q\_A) = D(q\_B)

This is Degree equality, not identity of paths. ∼\_D is the equivalence relation induced by the preimages of D(·): that two paths are Degree-equal shows only that their read-outs within this grading domain are the same, not that they are one and the same path in the field.

Identity of paths is not in this text; it is handled by the Law-identity texts.

### 10.3 Properties

Theorem Four (the theorem of the partial order on Degree vectors)

⪰\_D is a partial order on the set of four-dimensional Degree vectors: reflexive, antisymmetric, and transitive.

Proof.

Reflexivity. For any vector D, each of the four components equals itself, all four no-weaker relations hold, and so D ⪰\_D D.

Antisymmetry. Suppose D\_A ⪰\_D D\_B and D\_B ⪰\_D D\_A. Then on every dimension no-weaker holds in both directions, the four components are equal one by one, and so D\_A = D\_B. What is obtained here is equality of vectors: true antisymmetry, with no need to set up equivalence classes.

Transitivity. Suppose D\_A ⪰\_D D\_B and D\_B ⪰\_D D\_C. The real no-weaker relation on each of the four components is transitive; dimension by dimension A is no weaker than C, and so D\_A ⪰\_D D\_C. ∎

By the specification of the grading form in Canon Chapter 1, for the multi-dimensional partial-order type, reflexivity and transitivity must be proven; where antisymmetry fails, it must be declared that what is established is a preorder. All three properties hold here on the set of vectors, so this is a partial order and no preorder clause is needed.

### 10.4 Incomparability

If the four coordinates cross, no strict ordering can be imposed before Φ is fixed.

Example: δ\_A \> δ\_B while Reentry\_A \< Reentry\_B, with no coordinatewise dominance; then

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D\_A ∥\_D D\_B

and correspondingly the preimage paths are written q\_A ∥\_D q\_B.

Incomparability here is a determined conclusion, not an undecided one. The read-outs have all been obtained and the comparison has been completed; the conclusion is that neither dominates the other. Writing it as undecided would describe a result already computed as one not yet computed.

Section Eleven

The Robust Order

### 11.1 The Theorem

Theorem Five (the theorem of the robust profile order)

Let Φ be any member of the Continuation Competition Index family (Field Chapter 11 v1.2) — that is, monotone non-decreasing in δ̄, Reentry, and FlowShare, and monotone non-increasing in 𝒵. Then

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D(q\_A) ⪰\_D D(q\_B)　⇒　Φ(D(q\_A)) ≥ Φ(D(q\_B))

Proof. Suppose ⪰\_D holds. By the component conditions of 10.1, δ\_A ≥ δ\_B, Reentry\_A ≥ Reentry\_B, FlowShare\_A ≥ FlowShare\_B, and 𝒵\_A ≤ 𝒵\_B — the last being the equivalent form of −𝒵\_A ≥ −𝒵\_B for the third component of D(q); see 7.4. Below, wherever Φ is evaluated, its third argument is always 𝒵.

Replace component by component. Starting from Φ(δ\_B, Reentry\_B, 𝒵\_B, FlowShare\_B), first replace δ\_B by δ\_A: since Φ is monotone non-decreasing in δ, the value does not decrease. Then replace Reentry, then FlowShare; likewise the value does not decrease at either step. Finally replace 𝒵\_B by 𝒵\_A: since 𝒵\_A ≤ 𝒵\_B and Φ is monotone non-increasing in 𝒵, the value still does not decrease. After four steps we have Φ(D(q\_A)); hence Φ(D(q\_A)) ≥ Φ(D(q\_B)). ∎

### 11.2 The Converse Does Not Hold

If the four items cross, the current canonical source alone cannot guarantee that every Φ in the family gives the same order. No assertion is made here; a construction is given directly. The construction below is carried out on the abstract four-dimensional vector domain; whether the domain of legal Law-bearing read-outs actually contains such a crossing pair is a separate matter (Theorem Six).

Take two four-dimensional vectors on the abstract vector domain:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;δ\_A = 9, Reentry\_A = 2, 𝒵\_A = z₀, FlowShare\_A = s₀  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;δ\_B = 2, Reentry\_B = 9, 𝒵\_B = z₀, FlowShare\_B = s₀

The two have the same value on 𝒵 and FlowShare and cross on δ and Reentry, so by 10.1 neither dominates the other.

Take two functions:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Φ₁( δ, r, z, s ) = 2δ + r − z + s  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Φ₂( δ, r, z, s ) = δ + 2r − z + s

Both are members of the Continuation Competition Index family: their partial derivatives with respect to δ, r, s are 2, 1, 1 and 1, 2, 1 respectively, none negative, so both are monotone non-decreasing; their partial derivative with respect to z is −1 in both cases, so both are monotone non-increasing in z. All four construction directions are satisfied.

Substituting:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Φ₁(A) = 18 + 2 − z₀ + s₀ = 20 − z₀ + s₀  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Φ₁(B) = 4 + 9 − z₀ + s₀ = 13 − z₀ + s₀

So Φ₁ ranks A higher.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Φ₂(A) = 9 + 4 − z₀ + s₀ = 13 − z₀ + s₀  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Φ₂(B) = 2 + 18 − z₀ + s₀ = 20 − z₀ + s₀

So Φ₂ ranks B higher.

Two Φ in the family give opposite orders on the same pair of abstract vectors. So where the coordinates cross there is no robust order to speak of. In general, for any two mutually non-dominating and unequal vectors, take a coordinate i on which A is larger and a coordinate j on which B is larger; Φ = D̂\_i and Φ = D̂\_j (each taking a single coordinate and constant in the others, hence meeting the construction requirements) give opposite strict orders.

This construction has two further uses below: the proof of Theorem Six takes it as a counterexample, and the breach K-D3 uses it for contrast.

### 11.3 The Standing of the Conclusion

The coordinatewise partial order is the robust profile order available while no unique Φ is specified: every relation of dominance it adjudicates is agreed to by every Φ in the family; for every pair it adjudicates as incomparable, different Φ in the family give different answers.

It is not the unique CI function and may not be upgraded into an overall function; nor is it the familiarity order, which follows δ̄ (7.2). It is all that can be obtained while no unique Φ is fixed — no more, and no less.

Section Twelve

No Overall Score, and the Rule of Projection

### 12.1 No Overall Score

Theorem Six (the theorem of no overall score)

Under Γ\_D, suppose the domain of legal Degree read-outs contains a pair of read-outs crossing on the profile (mutually non-dominating and unequal). Then there is no single-valued function g, determinable from the current canonical source alone, such that g gives a total order on that domain of read-outs and that total order is agreed to by every Φ in the family. Whether the legal domain of read-outs actually contains a crossing pair is not proved in this text; Theorem Six takes its existence as a premise (the pending non-redundancy of the four dimensions cannot replace this step).

Proof. Suppose there is such a g. Take the crossing pair D\_A, D\_B of the premise, neither of which dominates the other under ⪰\_D. By the general construction at the end of 11.2, the family contains Φ₁ ranking A strictly higher and Φ₂ ranking B strictly higher.

Since g is a total order, it must rank one strictly above the other. If g ranks A higher, take Φ₂ of 11.2 — it ranks B higher and has been verified as a legitimate Φ; if g ranks B higher, take Φ₁ — it ranks A higher and has likewise been verified as a legitimate Φ. In either case there is a legitimate Φ that disagrees with g, contrary to g being agreed to by every legitimate Φ. ∎

Corollary. Whoever gives an overall score has already attached to the four dimensions weighting information the canonical source does not give. That weighting is an attachment, not a derivation.

So no LawStrength is set, no overall score is set on the Degree profile, Φ is not fixed, and no weights are fixed. The familiarity order follows δ̄; it is a one-dimensional order fixed by the original meaning, not the overall score prohibited here.

### 12.2 The Rule of Projection

The absence of an overall score blocks pressing the four dimensions into one number. An adjacent route must be blocked with it: comparing on only some of the four dimensions.

Let K ⊊ J\_D be a proper subset of the dimensions, and compare item by item on K in the manner of 10.1, obtaining

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D\_A ⪰\_K D\_B

This is a legitimate local comparison — what it says is that on the dimensions contained in K, A is no weaker than B.

The rule of projection: where a subset of dimensions is used for a local comparison, it must be declared a projection and may not be raised to an overall grade.

That is: ⪰\_K may not be written as ⪰\_D; A ≻\_D B, A ∼\_D B, or A ∥\_D B may not be adjudicated on its basis; it may not enter the unit directions of Section Fourteen; and it may not enter the final relation of Section Fifteen.

The reason shares its source with the absence of an overall score and is more direct. Take K = { δ, Reentry }, on which A dominates B, while on the two dimensions 𝒵 and FlowShare A is weaker than B. The projection adjudicates A as no weaker; the full dimensions adjudicate the pair as incomparable. Letting the projection pass for the whole is to assume tacitly that the two omitted dimensions have weight zero — a weighting that likewise has no canonical source.

Among the ways of shrinking dimensions, the one most to be guarded against is shrinking after the fact. Shrinking the dimension set or changing the criteria in passing so as to make two Laws comparable is already prohibited by the discipline of freezing in 4.2; it is stated once more here: the dimension set is frozen at four items by 7.2, and none may be added or removed after comparison begins. If the read-out on some dimension is ⊥, the treatment is that the unit is ⊥ (6.3), not that the dimension is removed from the grading domain.

A projection has only one legitimate use: as an intermediate step of analysis, to show on which dimensions dominance holds and on which it does not. Such use must be marked as a projection wherever it occurs, and may not be output alone as a conclusion.

The familiarity-order domain J\_δ differs from this: mathematically it coincides with the projection onto the δ̄ coordinate of the profile, but it has its own basis in the original meaning and is declared in advance as a separate task (7.2). What the rule of projection prohibits is letting a projection pass for conclusions of ⪰\_D; the familiarity order is output in its own name as the familiarity-order relations ≻\_δ, ∼\_δ, ∥\_δ, ⊥\_δ (15.5), answers only the comparison of depth, may not be written as relations of ⪰\_D, and does not pass itself off as an overall four-dimensional competition grade; conversely, relations of J\_D may not be written as the familiarity order.

Section Thirteen

### 13.1 The Problem

One duty can have more than one legal RolePath. One Law can have more than one legal realization and witness.

So one and the same Law, on one and the same evaluation unit, can have more than one Degree vector. The institution of comparison must deal with this multiplicity first.

### 13.2 Prohibited Practices

One may not pick the most familiar path at will to represent the whole Law. One may not pick the strongest witness. One may not assemble, coordinate by coordinate from different witnesses, a Degree vector that does not exist. One may not take a simple mean, a single maximum, or a single minimum.

The defect of assembling coordinates most needs explaining. Suppose two witnesses have read-outs (9, 2) and (2, 9). What the assembled (9, 9) describes is a bearing that does not exist in the field — no realization has both an accumulated depth of 9 and a return of 9. Letting a non-existent object take part in comparison, the comparison loses its object.

### 13.3 The Front

For a fixed legal evaluation unit u, if UnitReadable^Γ\_u(L) = 1, take the Degree read-outs of its full set of bearing 𝒲^Γ\_u(L):

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;V^Γ\_u(L) = { D(w) \| w ∈ 𝒲^Γ\_u(L) }

If UnitReadable^Γ\_u(L) = ⊥, the unit does not enter the computation of the front, and its Front is recorded as ⊥ — the front of the part already read may not pass for the front of the unit.

Keep its non-dominated front:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Front^Γ\_u(L) = Max\_{⪰\_D} V^Γ\_u(L)

that is, all members of V^Γ\_u(L) not strictly dominated by another member.

Theorem Seven (the theorem that the front preserves order)

Let the finiteness premise of 6.3 hold. (a) Every member of V^Γ\_u(L) either lies within the front or is dominated by some member of the front. (b) For Degree comparison, dominated members can be deleted from V^Γ\_u(L) without losing any comparison information.

Proof. (a) If v is not in the front, it is strictly dominated by some v₁; if v₁ is not in the front, take v₂ strictly dominating v₁, and so on. By transitivity and antisymmetry the members of this chain of strict domination are pairwise distinct; V is finite, so the chain must end at some member of the front, which dominates v. (On an infinite set the front may be empty — for example { (n, 0, 0, 0) : n = 1, 2, … } has no maximal element — so the finiteness premise is needed here.) (b) In two parts.

The dominating side (side A). Suppose v ∈ V^Γ\_u(A) is dominated by w ∈ V^Γ\_u(A), that is, D(w) ⪰\_D D(v) and D(w) ≠ D(v). For any vector x, if D(v) ⪰\_D x, then by transitivity D(w) ⪰\_D x. So whatever v can dominate, w can dominate, and deleting v does not reduce the dominating power of side A.

The dominated side (side B). Suppose b ∈ V^Γ\_u(B) is dominated by b′ ∈ V^Γ\_u(B), that is, D(b′) ⪰\_D D(b). Suppose a ∈ Front\_u(A) covers b′, that is, D(a) ⪰\_D D(b′). By transitivity D(a) ⪰\_D D(b), so a also covers b. That is, whatever covers b′ covers b, and deleting the dominated b on side B does not loosen the requirement of covering.

Taking the two parts together, and by (a): checking the Max-set is equivalent to checking the whole. So deleting dominated members changes no adjudication of ⪰\_cov. ∎

13.4 The Front Is Not the Set of Stability Witnesses

A dominated witness can be deleted from the front of Degree comparison, but it may not be said on that account that it does not exist in the sense of stability.

If the strong witness later exits while the Law still stands, then when the current profile is computed again, the formerly weak witness can re-enter the front.

Hence

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Degree Front　≠　Stability Witness Set

The former is the non-dominated set used for current comparison; the latter is the set bearing qualification that falls under The Stability Form of Law. Their members can differ, and so can the meaning of their changes: a change of the front is only a change of the Degree profile and implies no change of qualification.

Section Fourteen

### 14.1 Definition

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;G\_D^Γ(L) = ( Front^Γ\_u(L) )\_{u ∈ 𝒰\_Γ}

where 𝒰\_Γ is the index set of duties and evaluation units that must be covered completely under one and the same comparison protocol.

An evaluation unit is not a fifth dimension, nor a new ontological object. It only keeps duty, entry point, context, Type, and EvalMode from being covertly swapped by narrowing the domain: were it permitted, in comparing, to take only the units favourable to oneself, what is being compared would no longer be two Laws but two fragments of two Laws.

What is guarded against here is of the same kind as the rule of projection in 12.2, at a different level: 12.2 guards against shrinking dimensions — taking only some of the four; this guards against shrinking units — taking only some of 𝒰\_Γ. Theorem Eight (the theorem of the completely readable Law Degree profile)

If

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DegreeReadable^Γ(L) = 1, and the finiteness premise of 6.3 holds,

then G\_D^Γ(L) preserves all the necessary Degree information of the Law under Γ\_D: the Degree read-out of any legal complete record of bearing either lies within the front of its unit or is dominated by some member of that front.

Proof. By the antecedent, ∀ u ∈ 𝒰\_Γ, UnitReadable^Γ\_u(L) = 1, so the Front of every unit is obtained as the non-dominated set of the image of the read-outs of 𝒲^Γ\_u(L), and no unit is ⊥. By 13.3, the read-out of any record either lies within the front of its unit or is dominated by some member of the front. 𝒰\_Γ covers all the units that must be compared, so no unit is left out. ∎

If DegreeReadable^Γ(L) = ⊥, then G\_D^Γ(L) is a partial profile containing ⊥ items: it preserves only the fronts of the units already legitimately obtained, and does not claim to be a complete Degree profile.

In that case a partial profile may not pass for a complete profile in comparison. Units that are ⊥ propagate by 14.2 and 14.3 and may not be silently omitted — omitting them would pass the unknown off as known, exactly the gap 6.3 closes.

14.2 The Covering Relation at the Level of the Unit

The covering relation is evaluated only when the unit is readable on both sides. That is, it is first required that

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;UnitReadable^Γ\_u(A) = UnitReadable^Γ\_u(B) = 1

otherwise the covering result for that unit is ⊥.

Under that premise, define for one and the same evaluation unit u

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Front\_u(A) ⪰\_cov Front\_u(B)

if and only if

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;∀ b ∈ Front\_u(B), ∃ a ∈ Front\_u(A): D(a) ⪰\_D D(b)

Note that the a taken may vary with b, but each comparison must have one and the same a dominate one and the same b item by item. Different coordinates of different a may not be put together to dominate one b — that would be the assembling of coordinates prohibited in 13.2, in a variant form at the layer of covering.

Hence three values for the direction of a unit:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;both sides readable and covering succeeds　→　the unit's direction is 1;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;both sides readable and covering fails　→　the unit's direction is 0;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;at least one side unreadable　→　the unit's direction is ⊥.

Failure of covering is a determined conclusion with the read-outs complete; it belongs to a different class from what cannot be evaluated because it is unreadable, and the two may not be recorded as one.

### 14.3 The Direction of a Whole Law

The directions of the units obtained by 14.2 are combined:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dir\_D(A, B) = 1　when the directions of all common evaluation units are 1;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dir\_D(A, B) = 0　when the direction of at least one unit is 0;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dir\_D(A, B) = ⊥　when no unit's direction is 0 and the direction of at least one unit that must be evaluated is ⊥.

Theorem Nine (the theorem of three-valued determination in both directions)

Dir\_D is a well-defined three-valued function, and zero takes precedence over ⊥.

Proof. The three cases are mutually exclusive and exhaustive: either there is a determined failure, or there is no failure but something unread, or everything is determined to pass. The order is fixed by the rule of precedence: as soon as one unit has determinately failed, A can no longer cover B on that unit, and whatever the read-outs elsewhere, the direction has already failed; so it is adjudicated zero. An unread unit cannot wash a determined failure back into undecidedness. ∎

That zero takes precedence over ⊥ is a hard discipline. If one unknown were allowed to wash away one known failure, any comparison could escape defeat by producing an unreadable unit.

Section Fifteen

The Final Relation

### 15.1 Combinations

Dir\_D takes three values, and the two directions give nine combinations, listed one by one.

The four in which both directions are determined:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1, 0)　⇒　A ≻\_D B  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(0, 1)　⇒　B ≻\_D A  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1, 1)　⇒　A ∼\_D B  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(0, 0)　⇒　A ∥\_D B

The five containing ⊥, whose final relation is ⊥\_D:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1, ⊥), (⊥, 1), (0, ⊥), (⊥, 0), (⊥, ⊥)

⊥\_D is not knowing nothing. Among the five containing ⊥, the side already determined still carries information, which must be recorded along with ⊥\_D and may not be erased:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1, ⊥)　A has indeed covered B, so ¬(B ≻\_D A) and ¬(A ∥\_D B); what remains undecided is between A ≻\_D B and A ∼\_D B.  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(0, ⊥)　A has indeed not covered B, so ¬(A ≻\_D B) and ¬(A ∼\_D B); what remains undecided is between B ≻\_D A and A ∥\_D B.  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(⊥, 1), (⊥, 0)　as above, with the two sides exchanged.  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(⊥, ⊥)　both sides undecided; none of the four final relations is excluded.

So beneath ⊥\_D there are four further grades, which exclude different things. Downstream citations of ⊥\_D must inherit the grade along with it: reading (0, ⊥) as knowing as little as (⊥, ⊥) loses an adjudication already obtained.

This does not conflict with the precedence of zero over ⊥ in 14.3; the two govern different things. 14.3 governs the inside of one direction: once a unit has determinately failed, that direction does not return to undecidedness because other units are unread. Here what is governed is the relation between the two directions: when one side is determined and the other is not, the final relation is indeed undetermined, but the determined side is not voided on that account.

15.2 Distinguishing Three Kinds of "Cannot Be Compared"

Theorem Ten (the theorem distinguishing incomparability, incommensurability, and undecidedness)

∥\_D, incommensurability, and ⊥\_D are mutually distinct, and may not be lumped together as "cannot be compared."

Proof. The three hold under different conditions.

Incomparability ∥\_D: a common complete domain of comparison exists, the read-outs have been obtained, the comparison has been completed, and the conclusion is that each is determinately unable to dominate the other. This is a determined result of comparison.

Incommensurability: no legitimate common complete comparison protocol exists. The two Laws cannot establish, by the rule of correspondence in the third item of Γ\_D, a correspondence of the same duty and the same evaluation unit — for example, their duty structures have no corresponding evaluation unit at all. Here the comparison has not yet been able to begin, and there is no result to speak of.

Undecidedness ⊥\_D: the protocol could hold and a common domain of comparison exists, but the necessary facts or read-outs are currently insufficient, and at least one unit that must be evaluated is ⊥. Here the comparison has begun and has not been completed.

The three lie at three stages of comparison: incommensurability before comparison, undecidedness during it, incomparability after it. Lumping them under one name confuses the three stages, and downstream texts could not then tell whether to supply a protocol, supply read-outs, or accept the conclusion. ∎

15.3 Degree Equality Is Equality of the Fronts Unit by Unit

A ∼\_D B is obtained from (1, 1), that is, from mutual covering. Mutual covering is weaker than equality of the fronts, so it must first be proved that the two in fact coincide.

Proposition (Degree equality is equality of the fronts)

Suppose DegreeReadable^Γ(A) = DegreeReadable^Γ(B) = 1. If A ∼\_D B, then

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;∀ u ∈ 𝒰\_Γ: Front\_u(A) = Front\_u(B)

that is, G\_D^Γ(A) = G\_D^Γ(B).

Proof. Fix a unit u. A front is a non-dominated set, so Front\_u(A) and Front\_u(B) are each an antichain — any two distinct members of either do not dominate each other.

Take a ∈ Front\_u(A). By Front\_u(B) ⪰\_cov Front\_u(A), there exists b ∈ Front\_u(B) with D(b) ⪰\_D D(a). By Front\_u(A) ⪰\_cov Front\_u(B), there exists a′ ∈ Front\_u(A) with D(a′) ⪰\_D D(b).

By transitivity D(a′) ⪰\_D D(a). Since a′ and a both lie in the antichain Front\_u(A), D(a′) = D(a).

Hence D(a) ⪰\_D D(b) ⪰\_D D(a). By the antisymmetry of Theorem Four, D(a) = D(b). So the read-out of a lies in Front\_u(B); that is, Front\_u(A) ⊆ Front\_u(B).

By symmetry, Front\_u(B) ⊆ Front\_u(A). So the two are equal. u was arbitrary, so they are equal unit by unit. ∎

Antisymmetry is available here precisely because Section Ten builds the partial order on Degree vectors: were the order still built on paths, this step would yield only that D(a) and D(b) dominate each other, not that they are equal, and the proposition would fail.

### 15.4 Degree Equality Is Not Identity

From the above, A ∼\_D B means

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;G\_D^Γ(A) = G\_D^Γ(B)

This is equality of the Degree profiles unit by unit, not identity of the Laws.

A = B may not be inferred from it.

Degree identity and Law identity are two different matters. Two Laws with entirely different duty contents can happen to be profile-equal on the four read-outs — profile equality says that their fronts of four read-outs are the same unit by unit, not that they are equally familiar (being equally familiar is familiarity-order equality ∼\_δ), still less that they are the same Law.

Law identity belongs to the later Law-identity texts (at present only Chapter 8, The Distinction Form of Law-Identity Pairings, is sealed, and no closed criterion of Law identity has yet been established).

15.5 The Familiarity Order and the Profile Order

The institution of Sections Thirteen through Fifteen holds for any frozen grading domain (under the finiteness premise of 6.3). Taking the familiarity-order domain J\_δ, the Degree vector is the one-dimensional δ̄ (coinciding with the projection onto the δ̄ coordinate of the profile), and ⪰ is the real ≥; the front of each unit is the set of all its legal bearings at which δ̄ attains its maximum; readability follows DegreeReadable^Γ\_δ (6.4). The resulting direction and final relations are written Dir\_δ and ≻\_δ, ∼\_δ, ∥\_δ, ⊥\_δ — the familiarity-order relations: A ≻\_δ B reads that under Γ\_D A is more familiar than B (unit by unit, by the maximal δ̄ of each unit). The familiarity order is one-dimensional, so within one unit there is no crossing; ∥\_δ arises only when the directions on different units are opposite. Comparing two Laws by the maximal-δ̄ fronts of the units is this text’s operational rule at the whole-Law level; “more familiar by sedimentation degree” at the path level has its basis in the original meaning, but the sufficiency of taking the maximal δ̄ at the whole-Law level is not directly guaranteed by the original meaning of the Inner and Outer Chapters.

Proposition (profile coverage implies familiarity-order coverage)

Let A and B be readable on unit u both for the profile and for the familiarity order, let the finiteness premise of 6.3 hold, and let the two domains use the same full set of bearings and the same δ̄ protocol. If Front\_u(A) ⪰\_cov Front\_u(B) (on the profile domain), then A also covers B on the familiarity-order domain. Hence when Dir\_D(A, B) = 1 and both are fully readable for the familiarity order, Dir\_δ(A, B) = 1.

Proof. Take a bearing b of B on u with maximal δ̄ (the bearing set is non-empty and finite, so a maximum exists). By Theorem Seven (a), b either lies in the profile front of B or is dominated by some member of it, so there is b″ ∈ Front\_u(B) with D(b″) ⪰\_D D(b). By profile coverage there is a ∈ Front\_u(A) with D(a) ⪰\_D D(b″). Then δ̄\_a ≥ δ̄\_{b″} ≥ δ̄\_b, so the maximal δ̄ of A on u is not less than that of B, i.e., familiarity-order front coverage holds. This holds on every unit, so the direction is 1. ∎

The converse fails: familiarity-order coverage does not imply profile coverage (the example of 3.2 has the familiarity order judging A more familiar while the profile is incomparable).

The Ruling on "Clears"

### 16.1 The Canonical Source

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"Flow sinks into Sedimentation; what is often passed becomes well-worn. Gradually it deepens. Thus Law clears beneath."

The Necessity Form of Law has already placed 澄, "clears," under Degree. But that shows only that it is semantics of the Degree layer, not that it is an independent coordinate.

### 16.2 The Ruling

Read the context of the canonical source closely: often passed and well-worn, gradually deepening, and thereby clearing — this is a chain of results. Clearing is a result of depth and familiarity; the sentence links it with 由是, "thus," and does not set it up as an item parallel to depth and familiarity.

Ruling One ("clears" is not an independent coordinate)

"Clears" is kept as resultant canonical semantics of the Degree layer, and is not raised to a fifth dimension.

Reason. By the specification of the grading form in Canon Chapter 1, a new dimension must have an independent readable quantity, an explicit direction of order, a certificate of non-redundancy, and a certificate of nondegeneracy. Under the existing theory "clears" has none of the four: no readable quantity independent of δ, Reentry, 𝒵, and FlowShare; no definition of its own direction of order; no possibility of constructing a counterexample pair agreeing on the other four dimensions and differing in clearing; and no proof that it has two attainable values under the entry conditions.

So no Clarification(q,t) is added, and no fifth coordinate is set.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Clarification = δ

nor that

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Clarification = f(δ, Reentry, 𝒵, FlowShare)

has been proved. Both pronounce on something the canonical source does not give. The present state is: the placement of clearing in the Degree layer is settled; its exact relation to the four dimensions is not.

Section Seventeen

The Ruling on "The Well-Worn Appears First," and Its Handing Down

### 17.1 The Canonical Source

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"Deep sediment rises to the top; this is called the well-worn. Thus the well-worn appears first."

The Necessity Form of Law has already placed this under Degree, not under the current qualification of Law.

### 17.2 What This Text Can Bear

It can bear two things. First, the semantics of Degree includes priority for succession; this is part of the canonical gloss of the well-worn. Second, precedence already has an upstream formalization: Outer Chapter 3, “the familiar coming out first is also what is meant by the deeper coming out first”; Field Chapter 1, Law 5 and Theorem 17: under the comparison conditions C₌, the deeper satisfies the outflow condition no later than the shallower, and strictly first when P₁₇ also holds. At the path level, Field Chapter 11 v1.2, Theorem 1 gives: under Bridge Assumption H\_δ (a common threshold function or a verified cross-path threshold order, plus path-level comparison conditions), the path with larger δ̄ satisfies the outflow condition no later than the other; strictly first further requires a strict threshold order and the path-level P₁₇. Hence: for specified, correspondable bearing paths satisfying H\_δ and the path comparison conditions, the coming-out-first conclusion is carried over. Whole-Law familiarity-order coverage or being strictly more familiar (15.5) does not by itself imply the precedence of current action: the whole-Law familiarity order compares the maximal-δ̄ fronts of the units, does not specify which path actually bears the current event, and does not guarantee that the actually acting paths of the two Laws satisfy H\_δ, a common entrance, the pressure conditions, and P₁₇. An interface for coming out first at the whole-Law level further requires a bridge selecting the actually bearing paths and establishing correspondence across units; this text has not established it, and it is registered as open. Nor does precedence imply occupying more flow, or the amount of Continuation priority on the next beat.

### 17.3 What This Text May Not Prove

A same-beat read-back may not pass for a causal proof; that is, one may not take

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Degree\_t ⇒ FlowShare\_t

as a causal proof. The Degree profile contains FlowShare, and reading that coordinate back from the complete profile is a legitimate descriptive operation, not a logical circle. What cannot be established in this way is: taking a description that contains FlowShare as an independent proof of the cause of FlowShare — such an argument takes what is to be explained as its own evidence.

Field Chapter 11 v1.2 deals with this separately: read-outs of the Continuation Competition Index family use only readings at or before t, FlowShare takes the ratio of cumulative flows over a past window, and testing uses a later disjoint window; the same reading may not serve both as a basis and as the outcome of a test. It does not offer “using the flow-share proportion” as the reason for avoiding circularity.

Separating past and future windows avoids the same result serving both as a basis and as the outcome of a test; but it does not by itself prove the direction of causation, independence, or that a dynamical bridge holds.

So this text registers only this: any claim of a causal effect of Degree on flow occupation requires a separate dynamical argument (17.4), and testing may not use the same reading both as a basis and as the outcome.

17.4 The Correct Form, and Its Handing Down

The testing form of the interface must separate time:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Degree\_t　─\[ C\_dyn \]→　Priority\_{t→t+1}

Any claim that the Degree profile of the present beat affects the amount of Continuation priority on the next beat (flow and share) must be proved separately by a dynamical bridge; separating time is only the form of testing, not a proof.

Ruling Two (the interface of “the well-worn appears first”: path-level precedence carried over; whole-Law level and flow treated separately)

Path-level precedence has been carried over by Field Chapter 1, Law 5 and Theorem 17, and by Field Chapter 11 v1.2, Theorem 1 (under H\_δ, for specified, correspondable bearing paths); see 17.2. The dynamical bridge Degree\_t → Priority\_{t→t+1} for the amount of Continuation priority on the next beat (flow, share) is not proved in this text; it is formally handed down to Chapter 4, The General Form of Law-Dynamics, and Chapter 5, The Mathematical Form of Law-Dynamics.

Section Eighteen

Degree Is Not a New Dynamical Cause

Degree is an ordered reading of existing quantities of field state.

The four coordinates δ̄, Reentry, 𝒵, and FlowShare are all existing quantities of the Field Chapters; this text only prescribes how their read-outs are taken on the load-bearing path and how legitimate comparison is established between read-outs. The institution of comparison adds nothing to the field.

So Degree is not a new force, potential, weight, or causal variable in the field.

It is therefore prohibited to write

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;CI → κ

or

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Degree → Flow

unless a later dynamical text proves it separately and at the same time deals with the problem of double counting — the four coordinates are themselves already determined by the dynamical equations of the Field Chapters, and if Degree were in turn made to drive its own input quantities, one and the same dynamics would be counted twice.

Section Nineteen

Failure Worlds

The following five examples support the disciplines above, given one by one.

Counterexample One: assembling coordinates

On one evaluation unit there are two witnesses with Degree read-outs (9, 2) and (2, 9) (only the first two dimensions are written here; the other two are the same).

They may not be assembled into (9, 9). No realization in the field has both an accumulated depth of 9 and a return of 9; the bearing (9, 9) refers to does not exist. A non-existent object entering comparison leaves the comparison without an object.

Correct treatment: neither dominates the other; both enter the front, and the Front has two members.

Counterexample Two: crossing across several duties

Law A is stronger than B on duty d\_1, and Law B is stronger than A on duty d\_2.

By 14.3, Dir\_D(A,B) determinately fails on d\_2 and is adjudicated zero; Dir\_D(B,A) determinately fails on d\_1 and is also adjudicated zero. The combination is (0,0), so

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A ∥\_D B

The failures in the two directions may not be offset against each other into a single ranking by an overall score. This is exactly what the theorem of no overall score in Section Twelve prohibits.

Counterexample Three: the strong witness exits

A strong witness w and a weak witness v exist together, w ⪰\_D v, so the Front contains only w.

Later w exits while the Law still stands — by The General Form of Law, it suffices that there still exists some R with Established ∧ Govern; qualification is not lost because one witness exits.

When the current profile is computed again, v enters the new Front.

So the Degree profile can change while Law identity need not. This example also proves 13.4: the front is not the set of stability witnesses.

Counterexample Four: joint hosts may not be split covertly

A RolePath has a joint node joint(A, B); by Definition 3.9 (c) of The Mathematical Form of Law, its Hosts = Hosts(A) ∪ Hosts(B), and both hosts belong to the support of that path.

One may not choose A or B alone to compute the Degree read-out. If upstream has no legitimate rule for reducing to a single path, the output is

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;PathReadable = ⊥

not the read-out of a branch picked at will.

Counterexample Five: a read-back passed off as a cause

If one writes both

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D\_t = ( δ̄\_t, Reentry\_t, −𝒵\_t, FlowShare\_t )

and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D\_t → FlowShare\_t (as its cause)

then: reading back the fourth coordinate FlowShare\_t from the complete profile D\_t is a legitimate coordinate projection; but using D\_t (which already contains FlowShare\_t) to prove the cause of FlowShare\_t takes what is to be explained as evidence and is not a causal proof. Note further: the scalar CI\_t = Φ(D\_t) in general cannot recover its inputs — the family allows Φ to be constant in some coordinate, and even allows constant functions, in which case FlowShare\_t cannot be recovered from CI\_t; whether it can be recovered must be proved separately.

A causal claim requires a separate dynamical argument; testing must use a disjoint future window, but separating windows does not by itself prove causation; see 17.3 and 17.4.
