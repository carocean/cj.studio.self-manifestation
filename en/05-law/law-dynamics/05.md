# The Mathematical Form of Law-Dynamics

Section One

Purpose

The structural input S = The General Form of Law-Dynamics v1.1 (sealed), together with the three preceding texts it inherits from (Chapter 3, The Grading Form of Law, currently v1.1; Chapters 1 and 2 currently v1.0). The formal output M = the formal system of Law-dynamics of this text. This text is the mapping

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S\_LawDyn ⟼ M\_LawDyn

The structure S gives is

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;LawDyn^η\_F(L; I, Γ\_dyn) = 1  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;⟺　LegalLawSegment^η\_F(L; I, Γ\_dyn) = 1　∧　DynActual^η\_F(L; Γ\_dyn) = 1

where DynActual = ActDyn ∨₃ DegDyn.

Its components are: the legal Law-dynamics segment with its five items — one and the same legal trajectory segment, the target reference fixed, qualification preserved within the segment, updates following the protocol, the evaluation protocol frozen in advance; dynamical actuality; the two projections of dynamics of action and dynamics of degree; the three-valued disjunctive composition; and the three-valued propagation together with the line of the layer of qualification.

On the existing formal base of The Mathematical Form of Law and on the Sedimentation-Field of the Field Chapters, this text establishes for these components an exact representation, a state space, two clocks, operators of running and of succession, a solving procedure, and rules of coupling and read-back; and it takes on the formalization of the items handed down by Chapter 4 — the attribution of contribution, the cut set of loss of bearing, and the gain and loss of advantage.

This text does not prove again the minimal necessary structure of Chapter 4. That text forces out its two items one by one through failure worlds, and the Status of both its necessary and its sufficient direction is Established; this text does not walk that road again, and does not in any way ask anew "what must a Law minimally have in order to have dynamics." What this text proves is formal realization: that M gives the components already established in S a realization that is runnable and solvable, its determinate outputs reliably readable back item by item (undecided outputs assign no value to the object layer; see 7.2), and that its formal auxiliary objects add no ontological condition, add no field quantity, and change neither the theorem directions nor the Statuses of Chapter 4.

This text takes on no independent task of a determination form. As a mathematical system, it must be able to execute a solution and return a formal read-out when given a legal input — SolveLawDyn of Section Five is exactly this. But the completeness of a protocol of adjudication, the classification into PASS and FAIL, the duty of identification, and qualification for the domain of adjudication are not set up separately in this text; they belong to the layer of adjudication.

Section Two

Core Thesis

The structure of S has two layers: the layer of qualification and the layer of read-out.

The layer of qualification is LegalLawSegment, the conjunction of five items, which decides whether a candidate segment enters evaluation; at the object layer it takes two values.

The layer of read-out is DynActual and its two projections: along the frozen T\_η, pair by adjacent pair of evaluation points, one side reads the rest, Start, Continue, and Stop of current action, the other compares the Rise, Fall, Degree equality, and Migration of the Degree profile; each is aggregated in three values, and the two are then composed by three-valued disjunction. What the side of degree compares is the Degree profile domain J\_D of The Grading Form of Law v1.1, not familiarity order: a profile rise is not called more familiar, and a profile fall is not called growing shallower; "more familiar" and growing shallower are adjudicated separately by δ̄ on the familiarity-order domain J\_δ, outside the two projections (The General Form of Law-Dynamics v1.1).

This text must express seven matters exactly.

First, the separation of two indices. The underlying execution has its own order of steps, and the evaluation protocol has its own set of points; the two may not share one index, or the separation Chapter 4 draws specifically — "the scale of evaluation is not the scale of underlying execution" — would be merged back at the level of symbols.

Second, the stratification of the state space, and read-out quantities not entering the state. The three kinds of state — running, sedimentation, structure — bear different phase roles; Law-side read-out quantities may not be persistent components of the state.

Third, two clocks. Running time and sedimentation time may not be pressed into a single t, and their increments may not be stipulated in advance as single-valued functions of the mode of running.

Fourth, the standing of the running operators. 𝓡 and Φ are not new dynamical laws assumed out of nothing by this text, but a formal factorization of legal transitions already existing upstream.

Fifth, machine read-out of the layer of qualification. Each of the five items must have an executable read-out, and they are then composed by a zero-absorbing three-valued conjunction; machine unreadability and failure of qualification must be kept strictly apart.

Sixth, two institutions of three values. The existence of dynamical events uses a one-absorbing disjunction; certificates of contribution and the conjunction of qualification use a zero-absorbing conjunction; the two may not be mixed.

Seventh, multiple solutions under a fixed η. This text seeks the dynamics of a given segment and generates no new η; multiple solutions mean only that formal completion under one and the same η is not unique, and do not touch reachability.

The formal directions are two, forward solution and backward read-back; both must be complete, with no dangling symbol.

### 3.1 Two Indices

The index of underlying execution

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;r = 0, 1, 2, …

is used for underlying states and single-step records: X\_r, Y\_r, u\_r, m\_r, σ(r), ζ\_r.

The index of evaluation points

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;k = 0, 1, …, n

is used only for the T\_η = ( t₀ \< t₁ \< ⋯ \< t\_n ), n ≥ 1, frozen by Chapter 4.

The two are not shared. Whatever concerns underlying execution uses r throughout; whatever concerns evaluation points uses k throughout.

### 3.2 The Anchoring Map

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;a : { 0, 1, …, n } → ℕ,　a(0) \< a(1) \< ⋯ \< a(n)

a(k) is the underlying execution index corresponding to the k-th evaluation point t\_k; that is, the underlying state read at that evaluation point is X\_{a(k)}.

a is strictly increasing, which guarantees that evaluation points are neither inverted nor coincident in the underlying order.

### 3.3 The State Space

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;X\_r = ( X^run\_r, X^sed\_r, X^str\_r )　∈　𝒳 = 𝒳\_run × 𝒳\_sed × 𝒳\_str

X^run: the fast state needed for current running — the running state of the field, current flow, continuation cost 𝒵 (formerly named continuation impedance, renamed per Field Chapter 4 v1.2), current availability, tuning, and the like, all objects already existing upstream.

X^sed: the slow state on the side of sedimentation — Sedimentation-Degree δ, the re-walk history and historical support, and the like. The state is not named "accumulated depth" here: per The Mathematical Form of the Field v2.7, the state quantity of depth is Sedimentation-Degree δ (Definition 9), and deepening is its process (Dτ in Equation 2); the re-walk history must be kept within X^sed, because the recurrence facts checked by Ω\_W cannot be uniquely recovered from current δ (see 6.4).

X^str: structural facts — the relational organization, RolePath, carriers, the current state of legal carrier structures.

No fourth component X^read is set up. Law-side read-out quantities may not be persistent components of the dynamical state — were it set up, read-outs could enter successor states through Φ, and read-outs would become causes, contrary to the declaration at the end of 3.0, triggering K-MD7.

### 3.4 Two Clocks

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;σ(r) = ( t\_run(r), t\_sed(r) )

with increments written

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;σ(r+1) − σ(r) = ( Δt^run\_r, Δt^sed\_r ),　Δt^run\_r ≥ 0,　Δt^sed\_r ≥ 0

The pair of increments is required to be compatible with Prot, m\_r, and ζ\_r — that is, under that protocol and that mode, this pair of increments is permitted.

It is not required that t\_run = t\_sed, nor that Δt^run\_r = Δt^sed\_r, nor are the increments stipulated in advance as single-valued functions of the mode of running. If a concrete specification stipulates that they are uniquely determined by the mode, they degenerate into functions within that specification; the degeneration is a property of the specification, not an assumption of this text.

Three cases must all be representable: running advances while sedimentation is frozen (Δt^run \> 0, Δt^sed = 0); sedimentation ages with no actual current action (Δt^sed \> 0 while the segment has no Start and no Continue); both advance together.

Forcing t\_run = t\_sed, or forcing the two increments to be equal, triggers K-MD9.

3.5 The Legal Single Step and the Factorization into 𝓡 and Φ

The upstream The Mathematical Form of Law and the mathematical forms of the Field Chapters already give legal trajectories

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;τ ∈ Traj( Spec\_F, Init, Prot )

in which a legal step is a quadruple ( X\_r, u\_r, m\_r, X\_{r+1} ), m\_r ∈ 𝓜 being the mode of running.

First write the upstream one-step transition relation

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Trans\_Prot ⊆ 𝒳 × 𝒰 × 𝓜 × 𝒳

whose members are the legal steps Prot permits.

This text sets up no separate dynamical law; it only factorizes this step formally, inserting a formal transition witness Y\_r in the middle and decomposing the step into two stretches

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;X\_r　──𝓡──▶　Y\_r　──Φ──▶　X\_{r+1}

On that basis define

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;𝓡 : 𝒳 × 𝒰 × 𝓜 ⇉ 𝒴,　Y\_r ∈ 𝓡( X\_r, u\_r, m\_r )  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Φ : 𝒳 × 𝒴 × 𝓜 ⇉ 𝒳,　X\_{r+1} ∈ Φ( X\_r, Y\_r, m\_r )

and fix it as a factorization, not merely the insertion of an intermediate symbol, by

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Trans\_Prot( X, u, m, X′ )　⟺　∃Y \[ Y ∈ 𝓡( X, u, m ) ∧ X′ ∈ Φ( X, Y, m ) \]

Write the set of all Y satisfying this as WitTrans( X, u, m, X′ ), the set of witnesses of the formal decomposition of that legal transition.

The components are written Φ = ( Φ\_run, Φ\_sed, Φ\_str ).

The standing of Y must be made clear, and the minimal requirement on its type fixed with it — otherwise 3.7, in taking Y to write sedimentation, has nothing to rest on.

This text does not claim that the field naturally contains a currently produced object. Y is a witness of the formal decomposition of an existing legal transition; but it is not an arbitrary intermediate symbol. Its minimal requirement is:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Y ∈ 𝒴　must carry the actual execution information, within that legal step, sufficient for successor updating to draw on and sufficient for the upstream Trace to read.

That is, Y is a package witnessing execution, not an empty placeholder. Whatever does not carry this information does not enter 𝒴, and a decomposition it takes part in is not a factorization this text recognizes.

The part Trace can read is given a separate projection:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;π\_tr : 𝒴 → 𝒴\_trace

where 𝒴\_trace is the space of actual execution records the upstream Trace permits. π\_tr only extracts and adds no content; the writing branch of 3.7 takes π\_tr(Y\_r), not the whole of Y\_r.

The more concrete content of Y still awaits a concrete field specification — if some specification makes it concrete as current flow, a package of output, or some other object, that is the business of that specification; but however it is made concrete, the minimal requirement above and the existence of π\_tr may not be omitted.

Single-valuedness is not stipulated: set-valued maps are taken, without presupposing that the underlying dynamics is universally determinate; if a concrete specification satisfies \|𝓡(X, u, m)\| = 1 and \|Φ(X, Y, m)\| = 1, they degenerate into single-valued functions.

Neither Φ = 𝒢 ∘ 𝒰 nor Φ = 𝒰 ∘ 𝒢 is stipulated in advance. Sedimentation updating and structural updating each have their own place, and no universal assumption is made about the order in which they are carried out.

### 3.6 The Single-Step Record

What upstream Prot permits is a quadruple transition, which does not contain the formal witness Y. So this text's set of witnessed single steps must be set up separately:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Step^M\_Prot = { ( X, u, m, Y, X′ ) : Trans\_Prot( X, u, m, X′ ) ∧ Y ∈ WitTrans( X, u, m, X′ ) }

Hence

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ζ\_r = ( X\_r, u\_r, m\_r, Y\_r, X\_{r+1} )　∈　Step^M\_Prot

The two must be kept apart; one name may not carry both senses:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Trans\_Prot　　the upstream legal transition, a quadruple, without Y  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Step^M\_Prot　 this text's single-step record with the formal witness added, a quintuple

ζ\_r is a purely formal record and adds no field quantity; its five components have all been defined in 3.3 and 3.5.

3.7 The Protocol of Sedimentation Updating

The read-out of the protocol is defined on the whole space of legal single steps:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ς : Step^M\_Prot → { WRITE, DECAY, HOLD }

That is, ς(ζ\_r) reads the whole legal single-step record and its protocol semantics, and is not decided by the name of the mode alone. The mode space also contains Mode\_exo, Mode\_endo, Thaw, do(Mask), and do(Avail), whose consequences for sedimentation must be fixed by the protocol semantics of the step; written merely as a function of m\_r, these modes would have nowhere to land.

Accordingly

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;X^sed\_{r+1} =  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sediment( Trace( Decay(X^sed\_r), π\_tr(Y\_r) ) )　　if ς(ζ\_r) = WRITE  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Decay( X^sed\_r )　　　　　　　　　　　　　if ς(ζ\_r) = DECAY  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;X^sed\_r　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　if ς(ζ\_r) = HOLD

This is written as a statement about the state rather than as an equation for Φ\_sed: Φ is a set-valued map, while its sedimentation component, once ζ\_r is given, takes one definite successor state; the two differ in type and may not be written interchangeably.

Use: a legal actual execution that the protocol requires to be written into sedimentation takes WRITE; IdleStep takes DECAY; Freeze takes HOLD, or keeps the corresponding components according to the upstream protocol of freezing.

"Legal actual execution" is not the same as "takes WRITE": Freeze itself can be an actual execution, yet by the protocol it expressly writes no sedimentation. The criterion lies in the protocol, not in whether the execution is actual.

None of the following may enter Trace and Sediment automatically: candidate evaluations; hypothetical branches; re-walk probes; non-actual execution; unpermitted rewriting by hand. Whatever violates this triggers K-MD8.

3.8 Evaluation Steps and Underlying Execution Segments

The evaluation step

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;E^eval\_k = ( t\_k, t\_{k+1} ),　k = 0, …, n−1

has as its underlying execution segment

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Exec( E\_k ) = ( ζ\_{a(k)}, ζ\_{a(k)+1}, …, ζ\_{a(k+1)−1} )

of length a(k+1) − a(k) ≥ 1.

Since a is strictly increasing, this length is always positive, so every evaluation step contains at least one legal single step.

It is not assumed that evaluation steps correspond one to one with underlying single steps. The Continue of Chapter 4 is a conclusion relative to the evaluation protocol; that its two ends are the same does not guarantee that only one step was walked between them, nor that there is no interruption between them at a finer scale; the attribution of contribution must be able to drill down into Exec(E\_k) — see Section Eight.

3.9 The Read-Back Operator of Law Qualification

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;𝒬 : 𝒳 × L × I → { 1, 0, ⊥\_Q }

It serves only as the formal read-back of the upstream Law and does not adjudicate qualification again. ⊥\_Q means that the machine cannot complete this read-back, not a failure of qualification.

Note (conditions carried by the read-out of Law, corresponding to The Necessity Form of Law v1.4, The General Form of Law v2.6, and The Construction Form of the Law-Dynamics Operating Substrate v1.1). The content of 𝒬 is the formal criterion of The Mathematical Form of Law (6.1); what it adjudicates at the program level is the formal solution R ∈ 𝒮. To read this as Law = 1 at the object layer, Established ∧ Govern is obtained through the read-back of The Mathematical Form of Law, carrying its read-back antecedents; Law is then obtained through the sufficient direction of The Necessity Form of Law, carrying the carry-over condition SC\_Hold; and if the Ω read by the criterion is the engineering qualification Ω\_e, the supplementary checks of facts two and three of Ω\_W are further carried (K13 of The Construction Form of the Law Operating Substrate v1.3). Thus 𝒬 = 1 read as "R ∈ 𝒮" does not carry the above conditions, and read as "Law holds" carries them. The positive and the negative read-outs rest on different grounds. 𝒬 = 1: there is a formal witness R ∈ 𝒮 meeting the criterion, and the conditions listed above for reading as Law are satisfied. 𝒬 = 0: there must be a reliable ground sufficient to negate Law at the object layer; that some candidate R does not belong to 𝒮, or that no witness has been found, is not by itself sufficient to adjudicate Law = 0 — the read-back is then not closed, and ⊥\_Q is returned. SC\_Hold and the like account only for positive carry-over and do not stand in for a ground of negative adjudication. The antecedent "the read-outs are reliable" (3.12) assumed in Theorem Zero below includes, for the item R\_hold, both of these grounds.

This stratification is set up precisely to keep non-adjudicability at different levels from contaminating one another, so no symbol may be used for more than one purpose.

Reliable read-out and complete readability. The two must be kept apart:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;reliable read-out　whenever the machine returns a determinate value (1, 0, or a determinate step kind or comparison value), that value is the value of the corresponding object at the object layer  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;complete readability　the machine returns no ⊥ whatever within the range in question

The read-back theorems of this text take only reliable read-out as antecedent; complete readability is not assumed in general. Under reliable read-out, a ⊥ returned by the machine means only that this reading is undecided, and does not presume that the object layer is likewise non-adjudicable — the object layer may determinately hold while the machine cannot read it. To make the machine's ⊥ correspond value for value to non-adjudicability at the object layer, a further antecedent H\_Exact (the non-adjudicability of each machine layer corresponds exactly to that of the corresponding object layer) must be set up and argued; this text does not claim H\_Exact.

3.13 The Object Layer and the Machine Layer of Preservation of Qualification within the Segment

At the object layer,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;LawHold^η ∈ { 1, 0 }

is the preservation of qualification within the segment required by the third item of Definition 2 of Chapter 4; two-valued, inherited from Chapter 4, and not changed by this text.

At the machine layer a separate read-out is set up; see 3.14. The two may not be written interchangeably: that the machine cannot read it out does not mean that qualification is not preserved.

3.14 Machine Read-Out of Preservation of Qualification within the Segment

The range of quantification must be pinned down first. The underlying execution indices covered by the working segment are

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_η = { r ∈ ℕ : a(0) ≤ r ≤ a(n) }

that is, from the underlying state anchored by the first evaluation point to that anchored by the last.

If the underlying legal trajectory is a discrete execution sequence, then

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ReadLawHold^η =  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;0　　　　if ∃r ∈ R\_η: 𝒬( X\_r, L, I ) = 0  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;1　　　　if ∀r ∈ R\_η: 𝒬( X\_r, L, I ) = 1  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;⊥\_hold　 if there is no 0 and at least one r ∈ R\_η has 𝒬 = ⊥\_Q

If the underlying specification is in continuous time, the upstream institution of continuous preservation is invoked to obtain the corresponding three-valued read-out on \[t₀, t\_n\]; this text creates none of its own. This interval is the whole of the working segment η, symmetrical with R\_η in the discrete case; writing \[t₀, t₁\] would cover only the first evaluation step and, when n \> 1, would miss the rest.

Quantification may not run outside R\_η. What Chapter 4 requires is preservation of qualification within this dynamical segment, not permanent standing over the whole history of the system. A Law may not yet stand at X\_0, come to stand somewhere later, and be preserved throughout this segment — the preservation of qualification on this segment is then flawless, and to adjudicate that this segment does not preserve it because 𝒬 = 0 at some point outside the segment would charge this segment with what happened elsewhere.

The resolution, however, must run over the whole of R\_η: T\_η is responsible for dynamical evaluation, and the institution of underlying trajectories for preservation of qualification within the segment. Substituting the finite evaluation points of T\_η for preservation within the segment would take the resolution of evaluation for the resolution of qualification. So the quantification of this clause runs over r, not over k, and its range is exactly R\_η.

3.15 Machine Read-Out of the Legal Segment

At the object layer LegalLawSegment^η ∈ { 1, 0 }, two-valued, inherited from Chapter 4, and not changed by this text.

At the machine layer the five items are read out separately:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_traj　　one and the same legal trajectory segment: whether η really is the restriction to an interval of some τ ∈ Traj(Spec\_F, Init, Prot)  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_target　the target reference fixed: whether L and I have not been swapped within the segment  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_hold　　preservation of qualification within the segment: takes ReadLawHold^η (3.14)  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_prot　　updates following the protocol: whether every ζ\_r within the segment belongs to Step^M\_Prot, and whether there is any rewriting from outside the segment  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_freeze　the evaluation protocol frozen in advance: whether T\_η satisfies the condition of well-formedness, was frozen before the segment began, and has not since had points added, deleted, or changed

The types must be fixed definitively, and no new non-adjudicability symbol is set up.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_traj, R\_target, R\_prot, R\_freeze ∈ { 1, 0, ⊥\_Seg }

These four take the non-adjudicable value of the layer of the legal segment directly.

R\_hold, by contrast, is lifted from the read-out of 3.14:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_hold = Lift\_{hold→Seg}( ReadLawHold^η )

where

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Lift(1) = 1,　Lift(0) = 0,　Lift(⊥\_hold) = ⊥\_Seg

### 7.2 The Group of Read-Back Theorems

The theorems below set up no separate formal quantities with superscripts. Read-back means asserting that the determinate value the solving procedure obtains within its domain equals the value of the object of the same name in Chapter 4; a ⊥ obtained by the procedure reports only an undecided reading and is not read back as a value of the object layer (3.12). The theorems take reliable read-out as antecedent, not complete readability.

Theorem Zero (reliability of the machine read-back of the legal segment)

Suppose the five machine read-outs of 3.15 are all reliable (3.12), that is, whenever an item returns a determinate value, that value agrees with the truth value of the corresponding conjunct of Definition 2 of Chapter 4; the reliability of the item R\_hold includes the positive and negative grounds of read-out of 𝒬 listed in the note to 3.9. Then

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ReadLegalSeg^η = 1　⇒　LegalLawSegment^η = 1  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ReadLegalSeg^η = 0　⇒　LegalLawSegment^η = 0

When ReadLegalSeg^η = ⊥\_Seg, the truth value of the object layer is not presumed.

If it is further assumed that the five items are completely readable on this segment (3.12), the first formula may be strengthened to ReadLegalSeg^η = 1 ⟺ LegalLawSegment^η = 1.

Proof. Definition 2 of Chapter 4 is the conjunction of five items: one and the same legal trajectory segment, the target reference fixed, qualification preserved within the segment, updates following the protocol, the evaluation protocol frozen in advance. R\_traj, R\_target, R\_hold, R\_prot, R\_freeze of 3.15 correspond to them one to one, each reading one.

The route of 1: ReadLegalSeg = 1 if and only if all five items return 1 (the only case in which the zero-absorbing conjunction takes one); by reliability, all five conjuncts hold, so LegalLawSegment = 1.

The route of 0: ReadLegalSeg = 0 if and only if at least one item returns 0; by reliability, the corresponding conjunct does not hold, so LegalLawSegment = 0.

The route of ⊥: when there is no 0 and there is ⊥\_Seg, at least one item has not been read out as a determinate value. Reliability constrains only determinate values, not ⊥, so the machine layer does not presume the object-layer truth value on that basis: all five items may hold at the object layer while some item cannot be read by the machine; this is the distinction set up in 3.13 between failure of qualification and machine unreadability.

The strengthened route: with complete readability further assumed, all five items return determinate values, and ReadLegalSeg ∈ { 1, 0 }. If LegalLawSegment = 1, then ReadLegalSeg ≠ 0 (otherwise the route of 0 would give LegalLawSegment = 0), so ReadLegalSeg = 1; together with the route of 1 this gives both directions. ∎

In v1.0 this theorem took as antecedent "each R takes 1 if and only if the corresponding conjunct holds" and on that basis wrote a two-way formula; that antecedent in fact contains complete readability, whereas this text allows the object layer to hold while the machine cannot read it; so this version makes the theorem one-way, the two-way form holding only when complete readability is further assumed.

This theorem is the bridge between the layer of qualification and the layer of read-out. Theorem Four takes ReadLegalSeg^η = 1 as an explicit antecedent and obtains LegalLawSegment^η = 1 from this theorem; legality at the object layer does not by itself imply that the machine reads qualification as 1.

Theorem One (reliable read-back of determinate outputs on the side of action)

Given ReadLegalSeg^η = 1, every A\_k obtained, and the read-outs of 𝒜 reliable (3.12): if the ActDyn^η obtained by 5.1 is in { 1, 0 }, it equals the ActDyn^η of Definition 4 of Chapter 4; if what 5.1 obtains is ⊥\_A, it reports only that the reading on the side of action is undecided, and does not on that basis fix ActDyn at the object layer.

Proof. Definition 4 of Chapter 4 aggregates, in three grades, the four ways of the adjacent steps within T\_η, existential polarity first. StepAct of 5.1 corresponds to its four ways one to one; both take one and the same frozen T\_η as parameter, and neither deletes points.

The route of 1: there is a k with StepAct\_k ∈ { Start, Continue, Stop }, that is, A\_k and A\_{k+1} are both determinate values; by reliability, they are the values of current action at those two points at the object layer, so step k at the object layer is the same way, and by existential polarity first ActDyn = 1 at the object layer. This route needs only the two ends of that one step to be readable; whether the other read-outs are readable does not matter.

The route of 0: every step is Idle, that is, every A\_k is determinately 0; by reliability, current action at every point of the object layer is 0, every step is at rest, so ActDyn = 0 at the object layer.

The route of ⊥: when the machine read-outs contain ⊥\_A and no determinate dynamical step, the points not read out at the object layer may be 0 or may be 1, so nothing is presumed. ∎

Theorem Two (reliable read-back of determinate outputs on the side of degree)

Under the same conditions, with the read-outs of 𝒟 and Cmp\_D reliable: if the DegDyn^η obtained by 5.2 is in { 1, 0 }, it equals the DegDyn^η of Definition 5 of Chapter 4; if it is ⊥\_D, it reports only that the reading on the side of degree is undecided.

Proof. Definition 5 of Chapter 4 aggregates, in three grades, the five relations of the adjacent steps within T\_η under one and the same Γ\_D. Cmp\_D of 5.2 is the comparison system of Chapter 3; its five values correspond to those five relations one to one; in both places Migration takes ∥\_D at the level of the profile.

The route of 1: there is a k with StepDeg\_k determinately Rise, Fall, or Migration; by reliability, this is the relation of that step at the object layer, so DegDyn = 1 at the object layer. The route of 0: every step is determinately Equal; by reliability, every step at the object layer is of equal degree, so DegDyn = 0. The route of ⊥ is as in Theorem One: nothing is presumed. ∎

Theorem Three (reliable read-back of determinate outputs of the composition)

Under the antecedents of Theorems One and Two: if the machine-side ActDyn^η ∨₃ DegDyn^η is in { 1, 0 }, it equals the DynActual^η of Definition 6 of Chapter 4; if it is ⊥\_Dyn, it reports only an undecided reading.

Proof. The disjunction table of 3.17 is the three-valued rule of Definition 6 of Chapter 4, unchanged. The machine side takes 1: at least one side takes 1, and by Theorem One or Theorem Two that projection is 1 at the object layer, so DynActual = 1 at the object layer. The machine side takes 0: both sides take 0, and by Theorems One and Two both projections are 0 at the object layer, so DynActual = 0. The machine side takes ⊥: nothing is presumed. ∎

Note (value-for-value equality in three values). If H\_Exact (3.12) is further set up and argued, the routes of ⊥ in Theorems One through Three are also equal value for value, returning to v1.0's statement "equal value for value"; this text does not claim H\_Exact.

Theorem Four (reliable read-back of determinate outputs over the whole segment)

Suppose that for every ρ ∈ CompatSol( 𝕀\_η ) the first step of SolveLawDyn( 𝕀\_η\[ρ\] ) gives ReadLegalSeg^η = 1, that the read-outs of the five items of 3.15, 𝒜, 𝒟, and Cmp\_D are all reliable (3.12), and that the necessary formal readings can be executed. &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;v ∈ { 1, 0 }　⇒　v = LawDyn^η  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;v = ⊥\_Dyn　⇒　only an undecided reading is reported, and the value of LawDyn^η at the object layer is not fixed

Proof.

By the antecedent and the route of 1 of Theorem Zero, LegalLawSegment^η = 1, (η, L, I, Γ\_dyn) ∈ Dom\_Legal, and on every 𝕀\_η\[ρ\] the procedure enters its fourth step.

The various ρ are different formal completions of one and the same segment, and their read-outs may differ; but by reliability every determinate read-out on any 𝕀\_η\[ρ\] is a value of the object layer, so the differences among the ρ can only be differences in where things can be read and where they cannot, not differences of two distinct determinate values at the same place.

Let v ∈ { 1, 0 }. Take any ρ; its DynActual output is v; by Theorem Three, v equals DynActual^η at the object layer. A determinate conclusion needs only the support of sufficient witnesses already read — when it takes 1, one determinate dynamical step suffices — and does not require every read-out on that completion to be determinate. And by 3.16, when LegalLawSegment^η = 1, LawDyn^η = DynActual^η; hence v = LawDyn^η.

Let v = ⊥\_Dyn. No completion has read out witnesses sufficient to fix a value, while the object layer may still be determinate: for example, the values of action at some adjacent step of the object layer are in fact ( 0, 1 ), constituting a Start, no completion reads out these two points, and every step on the side of degree is of equal degree; then all completions jointly return ⊥\_Dyn, while DynActual at the object layer is 1. So the value at the object layer is not presumed. ∎

When CompatSol( 𝕀\_η ) is a singleton, this theorem says: when SolveLawDyn( 𝕀\_η\[ρ\] ) ∈ { 1, 0 }, it equals LawDyn^η.

Illegal candidates do not enter the domain of this theorem: by 3.16, Dom\_Legal contains only those with LegalLawSegment^η = 1. Where the object layer is legal but the machine reads qualification as ⊥\_Seg, the procedure returns EligibilityUnresolved, which also lies outside this theorem.

In v1.0 this theorem contained two invalid inferences, withdrawn in this version: first, "if v ≠ ⊥, then on at least one completion every read-out can be adjudicated" — with existential polarity first, one determinate Start makes the side of action take 1 while other read-outs may remain unreadable; second, "if every completion gives ⊥, the object layer is also non-adjudicable" — see the counterexample above.

Theorem Five (conservativeness under several solutions)

If the solutions of CompatSol( 𝕀\_η ) give, under SolveLawDyn( 𝕀\_η\[ρ\_i\] ), one and the same value v, then Agg\_Sol takes v: when v ∈ { 1, 0 }, LawDyn^η = v may be read back under the antecedents of Theorem Four; when v = ⊥\_Dyn, the solver returns ⊥\_Dyn, reporting only an undecided reading, and LawDyn at the object layer is not read back. If the values of the solutions do not agree, the solver returns BranchUnresolved, and in that case LawDyn is not read back, nor may one solution be picked at will.

Proof. The route of agreement: by the aggregation rules of 5.7, a set of a single value takes that value; when v ∈ { 1, 0 }, by Theorem Four it is LawDyn^η; when v = ⊥\_Dyn, by Theorem Four nothing is presumed.

The route of disagreement: by 5.7, a set containing different values takes ⊥\_branch; by its declaration of type, ⊥\_branch is an undecided subclass at the solver layer, not a value of LawDyn, so in that case there is no read-back. Picking one solution at will violates the end of 5.7 and also triggers K-MD10. ∎

Note. Under the reliability antecedent of Theorem Four, the solutions cannot give 1 in one case and 0 in another (both would have to equal the one value of the object layer); a set of branch values containing { 1, 0 } indicates that some read-out is unreliable, a lead toward K-MD1 or K-MD2. Where it contains { 1, ⊥\_Dyn } or { 0, ⊥\_Dyn }, this text still conservatively returns BranchUnresolved per 5.7 and does not take the determinate value directly.

### 8.0 The Standing of the Module

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;M\_Contrib is an attached module of attribution of this text, not a necessary antecedent of the main solution.

Delete the whole of Section Eight, and the main solution and read-back of LawDyn in Sections Three through Seven still close — none of the nine steps of SolveLawDyn invokes an object of this section. This ruling is fixed definitively.

What this module answers is: to whose account the source of a dynamical event that has already occurred should be credited. Chapter 4 has proved that DynContrib = 0 does not imply LawDyn = 0; this module does not change that ruling, nor does it take contribution as a qualification for dynamics. Whatever violates this triggers K-MD11.

### 8.1 Type

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DynContrib : 𝓑 × 𝓔\_η → { 1, 0, ⊥\_C }

where 𝓑 is the set of all candidate sources and 𝓔\_η is the set of dynamical events encoded in 5.4. It is taken as a three-valued read-out map, not written as a subset relation.

### 8.2 The Unified Certificate

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;CertDyn( B, e ) = SourceQual( B, e ; L, I ) ∧₃ ActualMechanism( B, e ) ∧₃ GenReach( B, e ) ∧₃ SameDynBind( B, e )

All four items take the event e as a parameter, with uniform signatures.

The conjunction takes the zero-absorbing rule of 3.17.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DynContrib( B, e ) = CertDyn( B, e )

The two institutions of three values may not be mixed: the existence of dynamical events uses the one-absorbing disjunction (5.1, 5.2, 5.3); the composition of qualification and certificates of contribution use the zero-absorbing conjunction (3.15, 8.2). The former asks "did it happen?" — one determined case settles it; the latter asks "do they all hold?" — one item failing settles it.

### 8.3 The Actual Sub-Segment of an Event

Before the four certificates are given, the stretch on which the witnesses of each must fall is fixed.

For an event e, its evaluation step is E\_k. Fix the actual sub-segment

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Z\_e = ( ζ\_p, ζ\_{p+1}, …, ζ\_q )　⊆　Exec( E\_k )

required to be contiguous, with a(k) ≤ p ≤ q ≤ a(k+1) − 1.

Z\_e is the stretch of execution records that the actual occurrence of e occupies at the underlying level. The actual witnesses of the three items SourceQual, ActualMechanism, and GenReach below must all fall within one and the same Z\_e — these three are the antecedents bound by SameDynBind; they differ in reference from the four certificates of 8.2, and are not called "the three certificates" for short.

### 8.4 SourceQual

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SourceQual\_r( B, L, I ) ∈ { 1, 0, ⊥\_C }

carries the underlying index r, and takes one if and only if

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;B ∈ 𝒮( F\_r, L, I )

where F\_r is the symbol for the current Sedimentation-Field state corresponding to X\_r, and adds no state object: the complete underlying state X\_r = ( X^run\_r, X^sed\_r, X^str\_r ) is already given by 3.3, and F\_r is its reading on the field side, written formally F\_r ≔ π\_F( X\_r ), π\_F being the projection taking the current Sedimentation-Field state from the complete underlying state. This convention serves only to connect with the notation of the upstream solution set, and opens no separate object.

Here 𝒮 is the formal solution set given by Definition 3.47 and Formula (4.7) of The Mathematical Form of Law,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;𝒮( F\_t, L, I ) = { R ⊆ Existing\_t \| EstWitness^M = 1 ∧ Govern^M = 1 }

that is, the upstream solution set reused directly, with no separate assembly of witnesses and no second system of bearing. If the read-out of this criterion cannot be completed, it takes ⊥\_C.

The index may not be omitted. The contributions to events such as Start, Continue, and Fall can occur at different r; without the index, "the current state" has nothing to rest on.

SourceQual at the level of the event is thereby defined:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SourceQual( B, e ; L, I )　≔　SourceQual\_{r\_s}( B, L, I )

where r\_s is the index of the starting point of the actual generating chain recorded in 8.5.

The way r\_s is taken may not be relaxed. One may not write

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;∃ r ∈ Z\_e: SourceQual\_r( B, L, I ) = 1

or the qualification at some later moment of the sub-segment could be used to make up the evidence for a generating source earlier in it — proving a source at this time by a qualification at that time. So r\_s must be the starting point of the same actual generating chain that GenReach uses; the two may not each take their own.

### 8.5 ActualMechanism and GenReach

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ActualMechanism( B, e ) ∈ { 1, 0, ⊥\_C }

takes its value according to the type of e; see the type table of 8.7.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;GenReach( B, e ) ∈ { 1, 0, ⊥\_C }

The endpoint must be stated exactly first. For Start and Continue one can still say intuitively that "the chain reaches the event," but Rise, Fall, and Migration are themselves comparison relations between two profiles, not a state node at the underlying level; to say that the chain ends at one of them does not hold in type.

So an object of witness is set up between the event and the chain. It takes one if and only if: within the actual sub-segment Z\_e of e there exists an actual execution chain whose starting component belongs to B and whose endpoint is some object of event witness w\_e, and that witness produces e through the corresponding rule of event read-out. This is written

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;B ⤳ w\_e ⤳ e

and not B ⤳ e.

The object of witness is taken according to the type of event:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Start, Continue　w\_e is the witness of actual occurrence or the witness of Continuation of that step  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Stop　　　　　　 w\_e = C\_e, where C\_e ∈ CutActual⁻\_k( L, e )  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Rise　　　　　　 w\_e ∈ GainAdv^Γ\_k(L)  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fall　　　　　　 w\_e ∈ LostAdv^Γ\_k(L)  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Migration　　　　w\_e ∈ Cross⁺\_k ∪ Cross⁻\_k

For Stop, the witness is the whole actually effective cut set C\_e, not one exiting component of it. The reason lies in the fact itself: what a Stop requires is that every legal current-bearing arrangement be actually cut, and the exit of a single component is not enough to witness it — under redundant bearing, remove one and the rest still bear, and no Stop has occurred. So the witness must be the set sufficient to cut all candidates.

As for the withdrawal contribution of some B to a Stop, ActualMechanism adjudicates whether the exit of B actually enters that C\_e; see 8.8 and the type table.

The actual execution chain here means the chain linked by the outputs and succession relations of the ζ\_r within Z\_e, not a walkable chain at the structural layer, nor a re-walk chain.

Write the underlying index at which that chain starts as r\_s ∈ Z\_e; 8.4 uses it.

A line must be drawn: GenReach is not the reachability of Chapter 6.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This text: given η, ask about the generating chain of what has already happened within it.  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Chapter 6: given an initial state and a target, ask whether there exists some η that reaches it.

It is strictly prohibited to write GenReach as an existential quantification over η searching for future target states.

### 8.8 The Cut Set of Loss of Bearing

Write the legal current-bearing candidates corresponding to the successor end t\_{k+1} of the evaluation step E\_k as

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;𝓑\_{k+1}(L) = { B : B is a legal current-bearing candidate of L at t\_{k+1} }

The subscript is k+1, not k: what is asked is whether, after this step, any candidate can still bear; its object is at the successor end, the same end as CurLawBear\_{k+1} in the formula below. k is an index of evaluation, not a step of underlying execution.

The family of structural cut sets is defined as:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;C ∈ CutStruct⁻\_k(L)  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;⟺　∀ B ∈ 𝓑\_{k+1}(L): CurLawBear^{do(C)}\_{k+1}( B ) = 0

that is: after C is legally deleted or disabled, no candidate on the next beat can still jointly satisfy CurrentLawOccurrence and DutyConformance.

For a minimal cut set, add:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;C minimal　⟺　C ∈ CutStruct⁻\_k(L) and ∀ C' ⊊ C: ∃ B ∈ 𝓑\_{k+1}(L): CurLawBear^{do(C')}\_{k+1}( B ) = 1

where CurLawBear is the object of the sealed Chapter 1, and the superscript do(C) denotes its value after C is legally deleted or disabled. This text sets up no name CurBear of its own.

It must be explained why the criterion is "sufficient to cut all candidates" rather than "the union of the necessary load-bearing points of the candidates." The latter gives only a large deletion set; it can neither express redundant arrangements of bearing nor answer which set really suffices to cut all legal bearing solutions; in a world of redundant realization, taking the union counts in components that are not necessary, while the true minimal cut set may be much smaller. So the criterion is "after deletion, every candidate fails."

An actual cut set is one that really occurred and took effect in this actual stop. Its type must be kept apart from the family of cut sets: CutStruct⁻\_k(L) is a family of cut sets, and only its elements are cut sets C.

So, for a stop event e, define the family of actually effective cut sets

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;CutActual⁻\_k( L, e ) ⊆ CutStruct⁻\_k(L)

whose members C must satisfy: every component of C has indeed exited or been disabled within Z\_e.

If a stop corresponds to only one actual cut set, the family is a singleton, and its member is written C^actual\_{k,e} ∈ CutStruct⁻\_k(L); if there are several equivalent actually effective cut sets, the family contains several members. Both are cases of a family; it is no longer said on one side "one cut set" while using, on the other, the type of a subset of the family.

WithdrawContrib may bind only to CutActual⁻, not to CutStruct⁻ — "in theory sufficient to make it stop" and "what really made it stop this time" are two different matters. This follows the first item of the Appendix of Chapter 4.

### 8.9 Gain and Loss of Advantage

Section Eleven

Breach

This text is a mathematical form, and its breach is as the Canon Chapter prescribes: exhibit a contradiction internal to the formal system; or a conflict at the read-back point between a formal result and an established structural proposition; or a load-bearing symbol missing from the correspondence table; or that the actual properties of some relation in the definition sequence do not match those declared.

Accordingly sixteen are listed. The first twelve fall under the first two ways; the last four fall under the four: K-MD13 and K-MD14 are concrete forms of internal contradiction, K-MD15 is an omission from the correspondence table, and K-MD16 a mismatch of the properties of a relation.

K-MD1 Internal contradiction. One and the same fixed input and one and the same formally completed solution yield both LawDyn = 1 and LawDyn = 0.

K-MD2 Conflict in upstream read-back. The formal output disagrees with the definitions of Chapter 4, that is, one of Theorems One through Five has a counterexample.

K-MD3 An illegal segment written as zero dynamics. LegalLawSegment = 0 and yet LawDyn = 0 is returned, violating the domain of the partial function of 3.16.

K-MD4 Compression to first and last. The frozen T\_η has interior points, and the solution skips evaluation at the interior points and compares only the two ends.

K-MD5 Deleting unknown points. When an evaluation point cannot be read, the point is deleted directly before computing, so as to manufacture two values.

K-MD6 Scalarization of Degree. Replacing the profile partial order with an overall score or a single scalar; or compressing Migration into a crossing of coordinates within a single vector; or calling the profile order more familiar, or a profile fall growing shallower.

K-MD7 Read-outs fed back into dynamics. LawDyn, ActDyn, DegDyn, or DynContrib enters the arguments of 𝓡 or Φ directly, without independent proof.

K-MD8 Sedimentation written wrongly. Freeze, candidate evaluations, or non-actual execution written into Trace and Sediment.

K-MD9 The two clocks covertly identified. Forcing t\_run = t\_sed, or forcing the two increments to be equal, or stipulating in advance that the increments are single-valued functions of the mode of running.

K-MD10 Covert selection among several solutions. The outputs of the formally completed solutions disagree and one is picked at will; or the one of one solution is made to absorb the unknown of another.

K-MD11 Contribution fed back into qualification. Inferring LawDyn = 0 from DynContrib = 0.

K-MD12 Same-beat circle of Degree. Setting up a same-beat closed loop between flow share and Degree.

K-MD13 Mixing indices. Using one and the same index for both underlying execution steps and evaluation points, or substituting "the length of Exec(E\_k) is always one" for the anchoring map.

K-MD14 Cut sets fixed wrongly. Substituting the union of the necessary load-bearing points of the candidates for CutStruct⁻, so that a world of redundant bearing is adjudicated wrongly; or binding withdrawal contribution with CutStruct⁻ in place of CutActual⁻.

K-MD15 Omission from the correspondence table. Exhibiting a symbol used in a load-bearing way in the main text and listed neither in the three classes of 3.0 nor in the list of 7.1; or exhibiting some component of S with no route of read-back in the correspondence table of 7.3. The former is the counterexample provided for by the last sentence of 7.1; the latter is a failure of completeness of read-back.

K-MD16 Mismatch of the properties of a relation. Exhibiting that some property declared in 3.18 does not match the actual properties of the object — for example, a function said to be total has points on its declared domain at which it is undefined, a map said to preserve values does not, two sets said to be disjoint intersect, a relation said to be of contiguous sub-segment is in fact an arbitrary subset; or exhibiting that the definition sequence sets up a new object whose properties 3.18 does not declare, which is the omission provided for by the last sentence of 3.18.

If any holds, the corresponding claim of this text fails or must be upgraded.
