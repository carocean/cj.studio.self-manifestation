# The Distinction Form of Law-Identity Pairings

Section One

Purpose, Form-Kind, and the Single Task

### 1.1 The Single Task

For two occurrences that have each separately acquired formal Law qualification: are the pairing declared on the side of Law and the pairing by physical root in fact only one and the same cross-end relation?

What is proved is not that the two are written differently in definition, but this: there exists a legal observation on which the outputs of the two necessarily differ.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS ≠ OccPair\_B

and directly

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS ⇏ OccPair\_B

Both candidates are rules of pairing. What they say is "by this rule, these two occurrences are paired," not "these two occurrences are the same Law." Which pairing bears on identity, and in what relation, is not within this text.

### 1.2 Form-Kind

The distinction form, an auxiliary form, set up as an independent text.

By Canon Chapter 1, Part Four, Chapter Twelve, the purpose of the distinction form is to prove that an observation exists which separates two candidate structures or hypotheses; its entry conditions are that the definitions of the two candidates are already separately closed and that the forward mappings of both candidates are already given; its standard composition is declaration of the candidate pair; construction of the distinguishing observation; proof of distinguishing validity …; distinction theorem; interfaces; its completion conditions are that the distinguishing observation is explicit and its validity proven; its breach method is to exhibit a case in which the two candidates' outputs coincide under this observation; its prohibited writing is passing off differences of statistical tendency as distinction; an observation that cannot be executed; candidate definitions not closed.

The distinction form does not ask about the minimal structure of an object; it asks only whether there is an observation that separates the two. So the recursive writing of the general form is not used.

Section Two

Upstream Input and Working Domain

### 2.1 Upstream Inheritance

The Law Group-Form and The Determination Form of Law (sealed) — the formal Law qualification of the two ends. Law = 1 must be obtained along the formal adjudication of the sealed system; an end may not be taken for a Law because its structural specification looks like one.

The Construction Form of the Law-Dynamics Operating Substrate (sealed) — three things: Law occurrences run for real, with their formal certificates; the PathMap bridge between formal witnesses and physical paths, a rule of bridging and not a dynamical variable; and that one and the same specification can have several legal instances, different capabilities need not all be pressed into one state.

The Mathematical Form of Law (sealed) — the formal solution set 𝒮( F, L, I ).

### 2.2 The Two Ends

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;λ\_a = ( F\_a, t\_a, L\_a, I\_a )  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;λ\_b = ( F\_b, t\_b, L\_b, I\_b )

### 2.3 Entry Conditions

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Law\_{F\_a, t\_a}( L\_a, I\_a ) = 1  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Law\_{F\_b, t\_b}( L\_b, I\_b ) = 1

The entry conditions of the objects are these two only. It is not required that F\_a = F\_b; not required that the two ends lie on one trajectory; not required that there be any historical succession between them; not required that there be a relation of source; still less required that they be the same.

Whether some observation can enter the admissible domain 𝒴\_pair^adm must further satisfy the readability conditions of each of the two candidates; see 6.2. The two belong to different layers and are not mixed.

2.4 The Two Ends Can Have No Traffic with Each Other

The two ends can come from different running instances. Precisely because no connection between them is required, the distinguishing observation of Section Six can take two independent instances.

Copying, migration, replacement, splitting, merging, recovery, reappearance after Law = 0, and the questions of source and of the boundary of identity all lie outside this text, and their answers may not be borrowed.

Section Three

Candidate One: Pairing Declared on the Side of Law

### 3.1 Protocol

Freeze

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Γ\_LS^can

It reads only formal declarations already existing on the side of Law, and excludes the namespace of physical instances.

### 3.2 The Canonical Declaration Signature

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DeclSig^can( λ ) ≔ Canon\_{Γ\_LS}( L, I, Rel(L), … )

It consists of the Law-side content specified in advance by Γ\_LS^can from the formal candidate declaration of that occurrence — for example, selected items among Rel(L), Ante, Anchor, Type, Read, Cov, Trig, and I.

What it reads is the formal declared structure on the side of Law, not the sedimented structure that the occurrence realizes in the field. The two must be kept apart: upstream takes the candidate L and Rel(L) as given objects in advance, while a running instance proves, by a further chain of qualification through the formal solution and the field state, that the candidate really becomes a Law in the current field. That one declaration is the same and that the actual structures in two instances are the same are not one thing.

None of the items above is an ontological constituent of identity; they are only fields that this rule of pairing reads. Listing them does not claim that they constitute identity.

### 3.3 Definition

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS^can( λ\_a, λ\_b ) =  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;1　　if DeclSig^can( λ\_a ) ≡\_{Γ\_LS} DeclSig^can( λ\_b )  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;0　　if the two are comparable and the canonical correspondence fails  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;⊥　　if the required Law-side declaration is unreadable or incomparable

### 3.4 It Is Not Identity

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS = 1　⇏　SameLaw = 1

It means only that, by the frozen rule of declared pairing, these two Law occurrences are paired.

3.5 The Protocol Takes the Narrowest Reading

Γ\_LS^can compares only the fields it specifies in advance, and does not rule on whether changes outside those fields can still correspond.

This restriction is deliberate: widening it would touch the boundary of identity, which is the business of other texts.

Section Four

Candidate Two: Pairing by Physical Root

4.1 A Formal Witness Is Not a Physical Carrier

The most important discipline here:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R　≠　physical bearer

R is a formal witness; the physical carrier is the actual path in the field. The two must be kept in separate layers. Chapter 7 uses PathMap precisely as the bridge from the carrier on the side of Law to the actual physical path, and notes that this bridge is not a new dynamical variable.

### 4.2 Canonical Selection of the Witness

The formal solution is the solution set

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;𝒮( F, L, I )

which may contain more than one legal witness. If the value one were fixed by "there exists a pair of witnesses whose footprints share a root," then the value zero would have to exhaust every pair of legal witnesses; whereas the distinguishing observation of this text takes only one pair of footprints, and the two do not fit.

So this candidate is not set up by an existential quantifier; instead a selection function is frozen in advance:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sel\_B^can( F, L, I ) ∈ 𝒮( F, L, I ) ∪ { ⊥ }

Its requirements are four: the rule is frozen in advance; it does not read SameLaw; for one and the same input it returns exactly one R; where there is no selectable witness it returns ⊥.

The rule of selection may not be left to be fixed later. This text v1.0 freezes it in advance as part of Γ\_B^can:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Γ\_B^can = ( rule of selection, rule of root comparison )

What is taken is

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sel\_B^can = min\_lex( 𝒮\_listed )

that is, the first item of 𝒮\_listed in the frozen lexicographic order.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;𝒮\_listed ⊆ 𝒮( F, L, I )

is the set of legal witnesses the implementation lists for that occurrence, the manner of listing being frozen together with Γ\_B^can. The difference between the two must be stated exactly: 𝒮 is the theoretical solution set, not necessarily enumerable item by item; 𝒮\_listed is what has really been listed in the implementation. The selection function takes from the latter.

If 𝒮\_listed is empty, Sel\_B^can returns ⊥, and the read-out of this side is ⊥ with it.

One may not write "with another rule satisfying the four conditions above, the conclusion is unchanged." That sentence is unproved, and there is a counterexample to fear: if some occurrence has two legal witnesses R\_1 and R\_2 that fall by PathMap on different roots, changing the rule of selection can change the value of OccPair\_B.

So it must be stated exactly: other rules of selection satisfying the conditions of admission above can form other legal candidates of physical-root pairing; and this text proves neither that the candidates generated by different rules of selection are equivalent to one another, nor that the conclusion of distinction of this text is preserved under any rule of selection.

Only with the rule of selection frozen into Γ\_B^can is this candidate closed.

This method does not require \|𝒮\| = 1. The solution set may have several witnesses, while the pairing looks only at the one selected by the frozen rule.

Write

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_B^can( λ ) ≔ Sel\_B^can( F, L, I )  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Foot\_B^can( λ ) ≔ PathMap( L, R\_B^can( λ ) )

### 4.3 Root Pairing

Freeze the narrowest protocol of physical pairing Γ\_B^can, comparing only roots:

where Root( q ) is the root identifier of the running instance in which the footprint q lies, read from the namespace frozen by Γ\_B^can; it identifies only the instance, and carries neither Law-side content nor history across states.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;RootPair\_B^can( q\_a, q\_b ) = 1　⟺　Root( q\_a ) = Root( q\_b )

Readable and unequal is 0; unreadable is ⊥.

Carrier lineage is not involved here. Tracking across states through replacement, migration, copying, splitting, or merging is not within this protocol — introducing it would bring back the problem of tracking across states, which the distinguishing observation does not need.

### 4.4 Definition

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_B^can( λ\_a, λ\_b ) =  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;1　　if Root( Foot\_B^can( λ\_a ) ) = Root( Foot\_B^can( λ\_b ) )  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;0　　if both are readable and the roots differ  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;⊥　　if either canonical witness or its footprint is unreadable

All three values are determined uniquely by the frozen rule, with no existential quantifier.

### 4.5 It Is Not Identity Either

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_B = 1　⇏　SameLaw = 1

Section Five

Each Candidate Closed

### 5.1 What Each Reads

OccPair\_LS reads only the formal declarations on the side of Law.

OccPair\_B reads only the witness obtained by the selection function frozen in Γ\_B^can, the footprint it falls on by PathMap, and the root of its instance.

### 5.2 Each Single-Valued

Each returns one determinate value for one and the same observation package.

The side of Law: DeclSig^can is read field by field from the fields specified by Γ\_LS^can, and the result of its comparison is single-valued.

The physical side: its single-valuedness rests on the selection function frozen in 4.2. Were it not frozen and an existential quantifier taken instead, then taking the value one would require traversing pairs of witnesses and taking the value zero would require exhausting them, and that side would not be a single-valued read-out. Sel\_B^can returns exactly one witness for one and the same input, so the footprint and root are unique with it, and the value is single-valued.

### 5.3 What Each Does Not Invoke

Neither invokes SameLaw, nor LawSource, nor NoFork, nor the output of the other.

### 5.4 So the Candidates Are Closed

By 5.1 through 5.3, the two are closed separately as the forward mappings of two candidates: each has what it reads, each is single-valued, neither is circular. The entry conditions of the distinction form are met.

5.5 There Is No Ontological Hierarchy between the Two

One may not write anything like "what the structural layer corresponds to is the Law itself, while the bearer is only its realization." Such a sentence would rank the two ontologically, and this text neither proves nor intends to prove any conclusion of that kind.

The two are rules of pairing on two different surfaces of reading, and no more.

Section Six

The Common Observation Package and the Distinguishing Observation

### 6.1 The Common Observation Package

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Y\_pair = ( λ\_a, λ\_b, Cert\_a, Cert\_b,  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DeclSig\_a, DeclSig\_b,  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_a^can, R\_b^can, Foot\_a^can, Foot\_b^can, Root\_a, Root\_b,  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Γ\_LS^can, Γ\_B^can )

where Cert\_a and Cert\_b are the formal certificates of Law qualification.

### 6.2 The Admissible Working Domain

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;𝒴\_pair^adm

requires: Cert\_a = PASS, Cert\_b = PASS; the structural fields can be evaluated by Γ\_LS; the physical footprints can be evaluated by Γ\_B.

Hence two forward mappings

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Y\_pair ⟼ OccPair\_LS( Y\_pair )  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Y\_pair ⟼ OccPair\_B( Y\_pair )

6.3 Construction of the Distinguishing Observation

Take two independent legal instances permitted by one and the same sealed running specification

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ι\_A, ι\_B ∈ Inst( Spec\_LDB )

This manner of construction already has an institution upstream: Inst( Spec\_LDB ) is the set of legal instances and trajectories produced by that fixed specification under common rules, and different legal instances of one and the same specification can serve separately as witnesses, without requiring every capability to appear on one and the same state.

The two instances adopt the same frozen Law-side candidate declaration

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;L*,　I*

and one and the same canonical Law-side formal schema.

In ι\_A an occurrence λ\_A is formed, and by formal adjudication Law( λ\_A ) = 1.  
In ι\_B an occurrence λ\_B is formed, and by formal adjudication Law( λ\_B ) = 1.

The qualification of the two ends is obtained by the formal upstream system; this text takes its certificates as interface antecedents for input, and does not re-run the upstream dynamics of adjudication. In the reference implementation of Appendix A, this antecedent is represented by law\_pass = True.

6.4 The Observation Lies within the Admissible Domain

The certificates of both ends are PASS; the Law-side declarations of both ends can be evaluated by Γ\_LS — the two use the same schema; the canonical witnesses of both ends can be obtained by Sel\_B^can, and their footprints and roots evaluated by Γ\_B — each has an actual carrier path in its own instance.

Hence

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Y\* ∈ 𝒴\_pair^adm

### 6.5 The Observation Can Be Executed

This observation depends on no unobservable quantity. What it needs is: two formal Law certificates; two declaration signatures obtained by the frozen schema; two witnesses obtained by the frozen selection function, and the footprints and roots they fall on by PathMap.

Each item can be looked up in the declared implementation environment.

Section Seven

Distinguishing Validity

### 7.1 The Output on the Side of Law

The two use one and the same frozen canonical declaration, whose fields specified by Γ\_LS^can have the same values item by item:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DeclSig^can( λ\_A ) = DeclSig^can( λ\_B )

By 3.3,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS^can( λ\_A, λ\_B ) = 1

What is obtained here is sameness at the layer of declaration, not sameness of the structures realized in the two instances; see 3.2.

### 7.2 The Output by Physical Root

Sel\_B^can takes one canonical witness at each end, which fall by PathMap on the footprints q\_A and q\_B.

The two belong to two independent running instances, whose root namespaces differ:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Root( q\_A ) ≠ Root( q\_B )

By 4.3 and 4.4, both are readable and the roots differ, so

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_B^can( λ\_A, λ\_B ) = 0

This value is determined uniquely by the frozen selection function, and does not involve the question "whether there exists some pair of witnesses sharing a root" — 4.2 has not set up this candidate by an existential quantifier.

### 7.3 Distinction

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Y\*:　( OccPair\_LS, OccPair\_B ) = ( 1, 0 )

On this observation, the outputs of the two candidates necessarily differ.

7.4 Counter-Check: The Difference of Root Really Decides the Physical Output

The OccPair\_B = 0 obtained in the previous subsection must be shown not to be a hard-coded constant, but really triggered by the difference of root.

Keep three things unchanged: the Law-side declaration; the canonical witness; the PathMap schema. Change only the root of the second end's instance to be the same as the first's; then

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_B^can　0 → 1

This is a control of the sensitivity of that side's read-out: with the other inputs kept unchanged, and only the equality of roots changed from false to true, its output changes from 0 to 1 accordingly. So the value of that read-out is really decided by the declared relation of roots, not a hard-coded constant.

Its strength must be stated exactly. This control does not replace the external verification of the reality of the root namespace by H\_PAIR2 and K-PAIR4: whether two roots merely differ in label while actually sharing one physical root or one state object must be looked into by the implementation environment itself, and cannot be tested by manually changing the labels to be the same. The former tests the sensitivity of the read-out to its input; the latter tests whether the input itself is true; the two lie at different layers.

The reference implementation of Appendix A executes this control by assertion. A failed assertion aborts, so the PASS on the last line of Appendix B is itself the proof that it has passed.

7.5 This Is Not a Difference of Statistical Tendency

One of the cases the Canon prohibits is passing off differences of statistical tendency as distinction. No tendency is involved here: the two outputs are each obtained determinately by their frozen protocols on one and the same observation package, one being 1 and the other 0, not a shift in some distribution.

Section Eight

Theorems of Distinction

Theorem One (closure of the candidates)

Under Γ\_LS^can and Γ\_B^can,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS^can,　OccPair\_B^can

are both non-circular forward mappings 𝒴\_pair^adm → { 1, 0, ⊥ }.

The proof has two items.

Single-valued. By 3.3, the value on the side of Law is read field by field from the fields specified by Γ\_LS^can. By 4.2 and 4.4, the witness on the physical side is obtained uniquely by the frozen Sel\_B^can, its footprint and root are unique with it, and the value is unique too. So each returns one determinate value for one and the same observation package; see 5.2.

Non-circular. By 5.3, neither invokes SameLaw, LawSource, NoFork, or the output of the other.

With both items established, both are the mappings claimed. ∎

Theorem Two (an explicit distinguishing observation)

There exists a legal

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Y\* ∈ 𝒴\_pair^adm

such that

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS( Y\* ) = 1,　OccPair\_B( Y\* ) = 0

Proof. 6.3 gives the construction of the observation, 6.4 verifies that it lies within the admissible domain, 6.5 that it can be executed; 7.1 and 7.2 give the evaluation of the outputs on the two sides respectively. ∎

Theorem Three (distinction of the pairings)

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS ≠ OccPair\_B

and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS ⇏ OccPair\_B

Proof. By Theorem One, both are mappings defined on 𝒴\_pair^adm; their domains are the same. By Theorem Two, Y\* ∈ 𝒴\_pair^adm, and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS( Y\* ) = 1　≠　0 = OccPair\_B( Y\* )

Two mappings with the same domain that take different values at some point of their common domain are not one and the same mapping. Y\* is that point, so

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS ≠ OccPair\_B

Further, at that point, within the common domain, the former takes 1 and the latter 0; this is an instance of the former holding while the latter does not, so

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS ⇏ OccPair\_B

The converse non-implication does not follow from this; for the reason see 10.2. ∎

It stops here.

Section Nine

Blocking Three Covert Substitutions

None of the three misreadings follows from the theorems of the previous section, yet each might be taken for their conclusion. They are sealed off one by one.

9.1 Different Laws May Not Be Inferred from Independence

One may not write: the two instances are independent, so they are two different Laws.

In this construction "independent" bears only one thing: Root\_A ≠ Root\_B. This is a fact in the observation.

OccPair\_B = 0 is not a further thing it bears, but the evaluation of that fact by 4.3 and 4.4 — the fact is the fact, the read-out is the read-out, and between them stands the frozen protocol.

And neither bears SameLaw = 0. To adjudicate that two occurrences differ ontologically requires a criterion of identity, and there is none here.

9.2 The Same Law May Not Be Inferred from the Same Declaration

One may not write: the two instances use one and the same declaration, so they are the same Law.

One and the same canonical declaration bears only one thing: DeclSig^can( λ\_A ) = DeclSig^can( λ\_B ). This too is a fact in the observation, and OccPair\_LS = 1 is its evaluation by 3.3.

Neither bears SameLaw = 1, nor that the structures realized in the two instances are the same.

9.3 An Ontological Ranking May Not Be Drawn from the Ability to Cross Instances

One may not write: declared pairing can cross instances while root pairing cannot, so the declaration is closer to identity than the carrier.

Theorem Three proves only OccPair\_LS ≠ OccPair\_B. It contains no conclusion of superiority.

This one especially needs attention. The observation taken has exactly the shape Law side 1 and root side 0, and reading it this way very easily produces the impression that "the declaration is more fundamental" — yet that is only the shape of the observation, not what is proved. Should the reverse separating world be constructed later, its shape will be root side 1 and Law side 0, and then too it will not follow that "the carrier is more fundamental."

Section Ten

Minimality

### 10.1 What Is Meant

The distinction form does not prove that constituent items cannot be deleted. Minimality here is the minimality of the distinguishing observation: to prove OccPair\_LS ≠ OccPair\_B, only one Y\* is needed on which the outputs of the two differ.

So a single certificate ( 1, 0 ) suffices. No reverse world ( 0, 1 ) is needed, no exhaustion of the space of candidates, no component of source or historical continuity added, no ontology of identity constructed.

10.2 The Reverse World May Not Be Added for the Sake of Completeness

Adding it would write an unproved matter as a precondition of sealing. The reverse non-implication

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_B ⇏ OccPair\_LS

is not among what this text proves; the separating world it requires is written G\_Pair-RevSep and left for future construction.

### 10.3 What Is Not Proved

The definition and adjudication of SameLaw; any implication in either direction between the two candidates and SameLaw; the reverse non-implication above.

Copying, migration, replacement, splitting, merging, recovery, reappearance after Law = 0, and the boundaries of source and identity are none of them in this text; see 2.4.

Section Eleven

Conditional Items and Status

### 11.1 The H Group

Two items only.

H\_PAIR1. The antecedent of the qualification of the ends. The Law certificates of the two ends depend on the upstream sealed system of Law adjudication, whose antecedents continue to propagate. Should its antecedents be withdrawn, the entry conditions of this text fail.

H\_PAIR2. The antecedent that the physical instance root can be audited. The root of a physical instance has a unique and auditable namespace. This is an interface of implementation, not an H of the ontology of identity.

### 11.2 Items That May Not Enter H

Structural identity, source, no forking, copying, and migration have nothing to do with the theorems of the previous section and do not enter H. Writing them in would look like a commitment about the ontology of identity.

Section Twelve

Breach

By Part Four, Chapter Twelve of the Canon Chapter, the breach method of the distinction form is to exhibit a case in which the two candidates' outputs coincide under this observation. Accordingly one core breach and three auxiliary breaches are listed.

K-PAIR1. Core breach.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OccPair\_LS( Y\* ) = OccPair\_B( Y\* )

If on the same declared legal observation Y\* the two outputs are the same, the core certificate of distinction fails, and Theorems Two and Three fail with it.

K-PAIR2. Candidates not independently closed. It has two forms. First, exhibit that either candidate in fact invokes SameLaw, LawSource, or the output of the other; then the item of non-circularity fails. Second, exhibit that either candidate can return more than one value for one and the same observation package — for instance, the witness on the physical side not obtained uniquely by the frozen rule; then the item of single-valuedness fails. With either, the definitions of the candidates are not closed, this text loses the entry conditions of the distinction form, and Theorem One fails.

K-PAIR3. An end without qualification. Exhibit that either end has not in fact obtained Law = 1 by formal adjudication. Then Y\* ∉ 𝒴\_pair^adm, and it cannot serve as a distinguishing observation.

K-PAIR4. The difference of root only a label. Exhibit that the so-called two independent carrier roots differ only in label, while in fact sharing one physical root or one state object. Then OccPair\_B( Y\* ) = 0 has no certificate, and the observation fails.

All four can be verified directly by a reviewer: K-PAIR1 by checking the evaluation on the two sides; K-PAIR2 by checking what the definitions of the two candidates invoke, and whether their returns for one and the same observation package are unique; K-PAIR3 by checking the two certificates; K-PAIR4 by checking the roots of the two instances.

Not breaches: the reverse world not constructed — the reverse non-implication is not among what is proved; see 10.2; the relation of the two candidates to identity undetermined — not among what is asked; the upstream fldb\_v1\_12.py not re-run end to end — 11.3 has recorded this separately, and the PASS of the pairing-layer harness does not take that re-run as its condition of completion.

from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Any, Dict, Tuple, Optional

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class LawOccurrence:
    instance_root: str
    field_id: str
    time_id: int
    law_id: str
    domain: Tuple[str, ...]
    law_pass: bool
    formal_witnesses: Tuple[str, ...]
    law_side_spec: Tuple[Any, ...]

def sel_b_can(occ: LawOccurrence) -> Optional[str]:
    if not occ.formal_witnesses:
        return None
    return sorted(occ.formal_witnesses)[0]

def path_map(law_id: str, carrier_id: str) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    table = {
        ("L1", "R1"): {
            "branch": "A",
            "depth_nodes": ("m1", "c"),
            "carrier_edges": (("a1", "m1"), ("m1", "c")),
        }
    }
    return table[(law_id, carrier_id)]

def foot_b_can(occ: LawOccurrence) -> Optional[Dict[str, Any]]:
    r = sel_b_can(occ)
    if r is None:
        return None
    pm = path_map(occ.law_id, r)
    return {
        "root": occ.instance_root,
        "carrier_edges": pm["carrier_edges"],
        "branch": pm["branch"],
    }

def occ_pair_ls_can(a: LawOccurrence, b: LawOccurrence):
    if not (a.law_pass and b.law_pass):
        return None
    if a.law_side_spec is None or b.law_side_spec is None:
        return None
    return 1 if a.law_side_spec == b.law_side_spec else 0

def occ_pair_b_can(a: LawOccurrence, b: LawOccurrence):
