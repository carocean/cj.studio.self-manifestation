# The Necessity Form of Law Interaction

Section One

Purpose

### 1.1 What This Text Answers

Chapter 10 has proved: wherever there is direct inter-Law interaction, there is necessarily LawInteractionBear. That is the necessary direction.

This text asks the reverse: with the common working domain and antecedents holding, and the three items of Bear all present, does there still exist a legal world in which this interaction nonetheless does not hold?

If the answer is no, then with Bear complete the interaction cannot but occur, and the sufficient direction holds with it.

### 1.4 Form-Kind and Version

The necessity form, a principal form, set up as an independent text, first edition.

Section Two

Core Proposition

### 2.1 The Central Proposition

Under the frozen working domain and antecedents, once the three items established by Chapter 10 are all present, this direct inter-Law interaction cannot but hold.

The reason is twofold. First, the current qualification for interaction permitted by the canonical source is completely covered by these three items, with no fourth item of qualification outside them. Second, with the three items all held, there is in the domain of the canonical source no independent disqualifier that could by itself make the interaction not hold.

With the two established, the interaction's not holding has nowhere to be placed — it can neither lack an item of qualification nor be stopped by a disqualifier.

2.2 Two Things Must First Be Stated Exactly

First, the covering of qualification is not the identity of definitions. The three items are the structure forced out by Chapter 10 from failure worlds; the qualification of the canonical source is semantics on the side of the object; what this text must prove is that the latter falls within the former, not that the two share a name.

Second, the disqualifiers being empty is not their not having been found. This text gives a criterion, examines class by class, and explains why every candidate is not an independent disqualifier — either it destroys one of the three items, or with the three items held it does not cancel interaction.

### 4.5 The Two-Sided Criterion

The grounds for assigning regions are two, in different directions.

Positive　Whether the atom directly bears "this interaction holds at this moment." If so, it enters 𝒜\_Q.

Negative　Take the negation of the atom, and ask whether the interaction then fails to hold. This direction authorizes only exclusion, not inclusion: what is negated with the interaction still holding can be adjudicated not a current qualification; what is negated with the interaction not holding only shows that it is not excluded by this direction, and does not thereby prove that it is a current qualification. Inclusion in 𝒜\_Q is borne by the positive side.

Special examination of what looks like a veto　With the antecedents held, ask whether the atom could by itself make the interaction not hold. If its occurrence necessarily makes an antecedent fail, it is not independent, and goes to its own class rather than to 𝒜\_D.

Section Five

Exhaustion of the Current Qualification

### 5.1 Two Layers of Examination

Any proposed fourth item of current qualification, if its semantics has ground in 𝓜\_INT and it bears the current qualification for interaction, has as its carrier either a single atom or an irreducible conjunction of several atoms. The two are examined separately: 5.1 has completed the layer of single atoms by the assignment of 4.6, and 5.2 examines the layer of conjunctions by the authorization of the canonical source.

The conclusion of the layer of single atoms is 4.6: each of the twelve atoms has its place; those entering 𝒜\_Q are all gathered by Q\_S^母, Q\_T^母, and B\_INT^母, and those entering 𝒜\_O do not bear standing. No atom falls outside the three while still bearing current qualification.

5.2 Examination of Conjunctive Candidates by the Authorization of the Canonical Source

An irreducible conjunction means that removing any member, the candidate is no longer the condition originally claimed.

The criterion is the authorization of the canonical source. A conjunction is not in itself a new atom of the canonical source — the twelve atoms are already all the carriers of the semantics of interaction in the three sentences, and writing two registered atoms together adds no sentence of the canonical source. For a conjunction to be raised into a new qualification, the canonical source must explicitly bear the relation of that combination itself as a semantic fact.

Accordingly, conjunctive candidates fall into three classes, and the three exhaust all candidates.

First class　The candidate contains a member of 𝒜\_Q. That member is already gathered by Q\_S^母, Q\_T^母, or B\_INT^母, and its other members come from 𝒜\_O without authorization from the canonical source to confer a new current qualification; so what the conjunction bears does not go beyond the three items.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Example: A\_3 ∧ A\_9 — the Driving-Factor that may reverse. A\_3 is already in Q\_S^母, A\_9 is a shape; the canonical source does not bear "the Driving-Factor must be able to reverse" as a fact, so this conjunction is not raised.

Second class　The candidate comes entirely from 𝒜\_O. The frozen canonical source does not authorize that conjunction to bear a newly added current qualification for interaction, so it cannot be raised by conjunction into a fourth item.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Example: A\_9 ∧ A\_12 — may reverse one another, and makes Causal-Response transform without cease. The former is a shape, the latter an aftermath; together they still do not go beyond the layers of shape and aftermath, and the canonical source does not take this as a ground of standing. Take it as a qualification, and the type of the result is used to adjudicate standing at this moment.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Example: A\_6 ∧ A\_11 — coming from outside, and pursuit. The former belongs to the specialization of Principle, the latter is a shape of continuance; together they still do not go beyond the two layers of specialization and shape, and the canonical source does not take this as a newly added current qualification of ordinary interaction.

Third class　The candidate is claimed to have new semantics emerging. That semantics, being not within the twelve atoms, is already outside the frozen domain of the canonical source, and not permitted by this text.

### 5.3 Conjunctions across Sentences

It must further be examined whether joint readings across sentences have the authorization of the wording of the canonical source.

Between M1 and M2 there is a direct joint reading: "the Driving-Factor must come from outside" in M2 speaks precisely of "the Driving-Factor" of M1, and what their joint reading confers is "there is that which takes charge, and it stands in the position of coming from outside." The sense of standing outside goes to 𝒜\_O / the relational restriction of the specialization of Principle, and the sense of there being one in charge is already in Q\_S^母, so no fourth item arises.

Within M2, "made to go against tendency" joins "disturbing one another." With the specialization of Principle in its "against" removed, what their joint reading confers is still "the going of one end actually acts upon the going of the other," that is, what A\_4 and A\_8 bear in common, which goes to Q\_T^母.

Between M1 and M3 the canonical source has no statement of direct joint reading; to force a connection belongs to the third class.

So joint readings across sentences produce no fourth item either.

Theorem One　Exhaustion of the Current Qualification for Interaction

Given that C\_scope holds, within the frozen domain of the canonical source 𝓜\_INT, the current qualifications a direct inter-Law interaction must have in order to hold at this moment are all among Q\_S^母, Q\_T^母, and B\_INT^母, with no fourth item. That is,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Q\_4 = ∅

where Q\_4 refers to a qualification that is not the same as any of Q\_S^母, Q\_T^母, and B\_INT^母, while being a current qualification every direct LawInteraction must additionally have.

Proof.

Suppose there is a Q\_4 as described. If its semantics has ground in 𝓜\_INT and it bears current qualification for interaction, its carrier is either a single atom or an irreducible conjunction authorized by the canonical source.

Where the carrier is a single atom: by 4.6, each of the twelve atoms has its place. Those entering 𝒜\_Q are all gathered by Q\_S^母, Q\_T^母, and B\_INT^母; if Q\_4 were one of them, this would contradict "not the same as any of the three." Those entering 𝒜\_O do not bear standing; if Q\_4 came from there, it would not be a current qualification. Neither route works.

Where the carrier is an irreducible conjunction: by the three exhaustive classes of 5.2. In the first class, its members from 𝒜\_Q are already absorbed by the three items, and its other members have no authorization from the canonical source to confer a new current qualification. In the second class, what comes entirely from 𝒜\_O is not authorized by the canonical source to be raised. In the third class, the semantics claimed to emerge is already outside the frozen domain. None of the three produces Q\_4. Joint readings across sentences are examined separately by 5.3, and produce none either.

If its semantics has no ground in 𝓜\_INT, it already goes beyond the freezing of 4.1, and is not permitted by this text.

A\_5 is explained separately: it belongs to the specialization of Principle, its requirement of identity borne by P ≠ A, while ordinary LawInteraction does not require CounterHold and does not study SameLaw. So it does not enter the current qualification of ordinary interaction, and does not thereby produce Q\_4.

So under C\_scope and within 𝓜\_INT, neither single atoms nor irreducible conjunctions authorized by the canonical source have an independent fourth item of qualification. ∎

Proof.

What all three formulas prove is that a strong structure implies the weaker qualification of the canonical source, not that the two share a name.

First item. What Q\_S^母 requires stops at "this disturbance has an attributable source of action" — the canonical source says only that it must have that which takes charge, called the Driving-Factor, and does not touch whether what it issues comes from a duty. While the SourceLawContribution of Section Five of Chapter 10 contains three examinations: qualification at the source point, the actual occurrence of contribution, attribution on the side of Law. &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SourceLawContribution = 1　⇒　Q\_S^母 = 1

The converse does not hold: what has an attributable source of action does not necessarily issue from a duty — the physical influence of a bearer is precisely what has a source and is not a duty. So the formula is one-way, which also shows it to be strong implying weak.

Second item. What Q\_T^母 requires stops at "the going of one end acts upon the going of the other" — the canonical source says only made to go and disturbing one another, and does not touch whether what is acted on enters the generation of some declared event. While the TargetDynGeneration of Section Seven of Chapter 10 requires that the declared event fall within Θ\_LD, and that this contribution actually enter its generation. Having actually entered the generation of some event, the going of one end acting upon the going of the other is contained in this. Hence

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;TargetDynGeneration = 1　⇒　Q\_T^母 = 1

The converse does not hold: there are cases in which the going of one end acts upon the other without entering the generation of any declared event. So this formula too is one-way.

Third item. What B\_INT^母 requires stops at "what is spoken of is two ends of one and the same matter" — the canonical source bears this sense with the "two tendency-systems" and "the two" of "the pursuit of the two." While the SameInteractionBind of Section Eight of Chapter 10 requires every item to fall on one and the same set β\_int: one and the same contribution, one and the same stretch of execution, one and the same event. &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SameInteractionBind = 1　⇒　B\_INT^母 = 1

All three formulas set contents of the object layer against each other, not readings of the formal layer: the reading of Chapter 11 only proves that these structures have an exact formal realization, and does not fix the qualification of the canonical source; see 3.2. ∎

Section Seven

The Independent Disqualifiers Are Empty

### 7.1 The Criterion

A candidate X is an independent disqualifier if and only if, while

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;C\_scope = 1　and　LawInteractionBear = 1

hold — that is, with

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SourceLawContribution = TargetDynGeneration = SameInteractionBind = 1

all three held — X's holding still makes LawInteraction = 0.

The criterion binds directly to Bear, not to the qualifications of the canonical source. What this text is to prove is that with Bear complete the interaction cannot but hold, so what must be excluded is exactly the case "Bear entirely true and still stopped by X."

Hence, if X's holding makes one of the three items drop out, the antecedent of this text is not satisfied to begin with, and X is not a fourth condition of disqualification — there is no need to say further that it corresponds to the negation of some atom of the canonical source.

This comes from the same principle as the special examination of what looks like a veto in 4.5: if its occurrence necessarily makes the antecedent fail, it is not independent, and goes to its own class rather than to 𝒜\_D.

The two lines are distinct here: the line of the domain of the canonical source proves that the semantics of interaction has been exhausted; the line of Bear proves that when the three items are all true there is no X that can disqualify; the two meet in Theorem Four.

7.2 Examination of Single Atoms on the Side of Disqualification

Whatever has the potential of negating, excluding, blocking, or making something not hold must be registered as a candidate of 𝒜\_D and examined one by one. Within the frozen domain of the canonical source, what has this potential comes from two places.

First, the negations of the twelve atoms. Take the negation of each item of 𝒜\_Q, and its occurrence necessarily makes the corresponding qualification of the canonical source fail — take the negation of A\_2 or A\_3, and this disturbance has none in charge, and Q\_S^母 fails with it; take the negation of A\_4, and the going of the other end has not received this action; take the negation of A\_8, and there is no matter reaching each of the two; and Q\_T^母 fails with them; take the negation of A\_10, and what is spoken of is not one and the same matter, and B\_INT^母 fails with it.

And with the qualification of the canonical source failing, take the contrapositive of Theorem Two: Q\_S^母 = 0 implies SourceLawContribution ≠ 1, Q\_T^母 = 0 implies TargetDynGeneration ≠ 1, B\_INT^母 = 0 implies SameInteractionBind ≠ 1. If one of the three fails, Bear is not held, and the antecedent of this text is not satisfied to begin with. By the criterion of 7.1, these are all not independent.

Theorem Two comes before this section for exactly this reason: the criterion of disqualifiers binds to Bear, and inferring from the negation of a qualification of the canonical source that some item of Bear is not held must go through the contrapositive of that theorem.

Take the negation of A\_1, that is, no disturbance. It is not an independent disqualifier, but first makes the antecedent fail: by 4.6, Q\_T^母 = 1 implies A\_8 (disturbing one another), and A\_8 implies A\_1; so no disturbance conflicts with Q\_T^母 = 1, and by the contrapositive of Theorem Two, if Q\_T^母 fails then TargetDynGeneration is not held. By the criterion of 7.1, this is not independent.

Take the negations of the items of 𝒜\_O: not against; the Driving-Factor not coming from outside; not two ends; not able to reverse one another; no pursuit; Causal-Response not transforming — none of the six bears standing, and their negation cannot make an interaction whose antecedents are already present not hold. What stops after once is still one interaction, and one whose reading does not change is still one interaction; this was established in Section Ten of Chapter 10.

Of these, A\_5 and A\_6 must further be stated exactly: by 4.6, both belong to the specialization of Principle, and do not enter the current qualification of ordinary interaction; this text neither requires CounterHold nor studies SameLaw, so their negation is not among what this text can adjudicate, still less an independent disqualifier.

The negation of A\_7 is not two ends, which conflicts with the pairwise working domain set by C\_scope, and is not within the working domain of this text.

Second, the negative semantics within the three sentences of the canonical source. What the three sentences state is disturbance, reaching each other, and pursuing and transforming — all positive statements of relation; not one of them authorizes the veto "though there is one in charge whose going as a duty acts on the other, and the other moves by it, it still does not amount to interaction."

So at the layer of single atoms, the candidates of 𝒜\_D all come to nothing:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;𝒜\_D = ∅

7.3 Examination of Conjunctions on the Side of Disqualification

As on the side of qualification, it must further be examined whether irreducible conjunctions authorized by the canonical source form a disqualifier.

The criterion is still the authorization of the canonical source: a conjunction is not in itself a new atom of the canonical source, and to serve as a condition of disqualification, the canonical source must explicitly bear the relation of that combination itself as a ground of veto.

First class　Candidates containing the negation of a member of 𝒜\_Q. Their occurrence already makes the corresponding qualification fail, so by 7.1 they are not independent, whether conjoined or not.

Second class　Candidates composed entirely of negations of members of 𝒜\_O. These bear no standing, and together still bear none; the canonical source does not take "its shape is thus, its consequence is so" as a ground of veto.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Example: the negation of A\_9 ∧ the negation of A\_12 — not able to reverse one another and Causal-Response not transforming. This is the case where the shape is not mutual reversal and the result has not unfolded, and 7.2 of Chapter 10 has established: having really entered generation, the interaction holds.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Example: the negation of A\_11 ∧ the negation of A\_12 — stopping after once and transformation not yet unfolding. Events of the class of Continue belong exactly here, and the interaction holds.

Third class　Candidates claimed to have new semantics of veto emerging. That semantics is not within the twelve atoms, and is already outside the frozen domain.

The three classes exhaust all conjunctive candidates on the side of disqualification, and none forms an independent disqualifier.

### 7.4 Six Groups of Pressure Worlds

The two subsections above give the conclusion from a finite domain; this section confirms it with six classes of concrete case, and shows where each falls.

First, an extremely weak contribution. Let the quantity issued by the source be minute. So long as it has entered the actual generating mechanism of this event, the second item holds — this text sets no threshold of magnitude. If it is so weak that it never entered that mechanism, then TargetDynGeneration = 0, Bear already does not hold, and the antecedent is not satisfied.

Second, a change of background state. A bare change of environment, even if it has one in charge, does not belong to L\_S's duty-bearing, so SourceLawContribution = 0 and Bear already does not hold — what is lacking here is attribution on the side of Law, and there is no need to assert that the "having one in charge" of the canonical source is false. While if the change of that state itself is what the source Law's duty carries out, and is actually read by this mechanism of the target, then all three items hold, and the interaction does not fail to hold because it is a "state."

Third, enabling. Mere enabling is not actually taken up by this mechanism, so TargetDynGeneration = 0 and Bear already does not hold — what is lacking here is entry into this generation, and there is no need to assert that the "made to go" of the canonical source is absolutely false:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MereEnable　⇏　TargetDynGeneration

Only when this enabling is actually read by the current mechanism does it qualify to enter the second layer:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Enable ∧ ActualMechanismUse　only then

Fourth, suppression. Contributions to Stop and Fall can be realized by cutting off, withdrawing, loss of bearing, or increasing resistance. Generation may not be narrowly understood as positive injection — the six classes of event of Chapter 4 include stopping and falling, and their witnesses of mechanism include cut sets and loss of advantage. If the going of suppression has entered the actual mechanism of that event, the second item holds; whether it may reverse belongs to A\_9, goes to 𝒜\_O, and bears no standing.

Fifth, a distant ancestor. Ordinary media, gates, and non-Law transformations on the way do not cut off directness, as 7.3 of Chapter 10 has permitted. But if on the way another Law acquires a new source anchor on the side of Law and takes over the suffix leading to the present target witness, the directness of the original source is broken, TargetDynGeneration = 0, and Bear already does not hold.

Only the layer of directness is broken here: the original source may still be a distant causal ancestor, only it no longer constitutes this direct interaction. This text does not on that basis assert that the "made to go" of the canonical source is false.

Sixth, delay. The length of time belongs to A\_11, goes to 𝒜\_O, and bears no standing. What crosses storage and delay with provenance continuous still has the second item hold; where provenance is broken, TargetDynGeneration = 0, and Bear already does not hold.

The common shape of the six is:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;each candidate either destroys one item of Bear, or does not cancel Interaction when Bear is held

Theorem Three　The Independent Disqualifiers Are Empty

With C\_scope = 1 and LawInteractionBear = 1 held — that is, with the three items SourceLawContribution, TargetDynGeneration, and SameInteractionBind all held — there is no independent disqualifier within the frozen domain of the canonical source 𝓜\_INT. That is,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IndependentDisqualifier\_INT = ∅

Proof.

Suppose a candidate X is an independent disqualifier. By the criterion of 7.1, X must make the interaction not hold while C\_scope and the three items of Bear are all held, and its route to this result may not be first making one of the three items drop out.

If X's semantics has ground in 𝓜\_INT, its carrier is either a single atom and its negation, or an irreducible conjunction authorized by the canonical source.

Where the carrier is at the layer of single atoms: by 7.2, the negation of each item of 𝒜\_Q makes the corresponding qualification of the canonical source fail. But the criterion binds to the three items of Bear, not to the qualifications of the canonical source, so one more step must be taken — the contrapositive of Theorem Two:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Q\_S^母 = 0　⇒　SourceLawContribution ≠ 1  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Q\_T^母 = 0　⇒　TargetDynGeneration ≠ 1  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;B\_INT^母 = 0　⇒　SameInteractionBind ≠ 1

If one of the three fails, Bear is not held, the antecedent is lost, and by the criterion it is not independent.

This step cannot be omitted: from the failure of a qualification of the canonical source to some item of Bear not being held must be carried by Theorem Two; Theorem Two comes before this section for exactly this reason.

The negations of the items of 𝒜\_O bear no standing, and cannot make an interaction whose antecedents are present not hold; nor is there in the three sentences of the canonical source any semantics authorizing a veto. So 𝒜\_D = ∅.

Where the carrier is a conjunction: by the three exhaustive classes of 7.3. Candidates containing the negation of a member of 𝒜\_Q are not independent as above, also through the contrapositive of Theorem Two; candidates composed entirely of negations of members of 𝒜\_O have no authorization from the canonical source; those claimed to have new semantics of veto emerging are already outside the frozen domain.

If its semantics has no ground in 𝓜\_INT, it already goes beyond the freezing of 4.1, and is not permitted by this text.

And the six classes examined in 7.4 each fall into one of these, none meeting the criterion.

So there is no such X. ∎

This conclusion is confined to the frozen domain of the canonical source. Should that domain be added to or reduced, this theorem must be proved again; see 10.1.

### 8.3 This Proof Is Not Circular

Each of the three theorems has its own ground and none cites minimality: Theorem One does not rely on the minimality of Chapter 10; Theorem Two only establishes the strong-implies-weak mapping of Bear onto the qualifications of the canonical source; Theorem Three inherits the contrapositive of Theorem Two to complete the examination of disqualifiers.
