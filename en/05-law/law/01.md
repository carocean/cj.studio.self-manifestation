# The General Form of Law

This text attempts a general proposition:

What is ordinarily called a law, once it stands in the Sedimentation-Field, cannot be merely one occasion on which a relation happened to match, one present response, or one trace left behind; it must already have acquired the necessary qualification for later Continuation to be borne by the field's own present structure.

The word "law" here means what is ordinarily called a regularity, a law of nature, or a rule. It is not some special object newly fabricated by *On Self-Manifestation*.

Physics itself is only flux. Flux may or may not possess a stable continuation-tendency. With no Observer present, physics fluxes all the same; but "regularity," taken as a relation that is discerned and articulated, does not thereby become of itself a logical object suspended above physics.

External Trace-Response carries its signatures into the Sedimentation-Field, where Trace-Response occurs again; through repeated action, the relation gradually enters the present structure and, when similar conditions arrive later, bears the subsequent Continuation. Once the Observer has arisen, this relational organization — already formed and already bearing later Causal-Response — can then be discerned, distinguished, and articulated, and is called a regularity, a law of nature, or a rule.

*On Self-Manifestation* therefore neither redefines "law" as some apparatus inside the Sedimentation-Field, nor claims that external flux and the law in the field have nothing to do with one another. External Trace-Response and the in-field relation can answer to one another precisely because the latter is shaped by the repeated action of the former; but the latter does not thereby become a logical entity existing a priori in the external world.

Law stands at a pivotal turning point in this generative process.

A Sedimentation-Field may first have traces; it may respond immediately to a trace; it may also, through repeated action, leave sediment of differing Depths. But only when the relation already sedimented begins to enter the present, and is able to bear Continuation under similar conditions later, does the field truly acquire the qualification to generate law. At that step, sediment is no longer merely a trace left by past action, but begins to be the structure that organizes later Causal-Response.

By the existing account in *On Self-Manifestation*, law is further the basis of what is generated afterwards. Principle does not occur with nothing to rest upon; it takes what has already formed and holds as the object of its selecting, biasing, and breaking. Nor can the further generation of Self-Manifestation begin out of nothing in a field that has only immediate Trace-Response and cannot yet organize later Causal-Response by means of formed relations. Only once the Observer has arisen can these Causal-Response relations, already occurring in the field, be further discerned, distinguished, and articulated.

Further: law is not an abstract pattern generated apart from the object. What *On Self-Manifestation* calls Object-Transformation generates object and law together: the object bears the material expression, the law bears the pattern expression — that is, the motion-pattern of the object; within the Sedimentation-Field this pattern is further exhibited as a relational organization already formed and able to bear Continuation. The object is not material given in advance by the external world, but a generated outcome within Object-Transformation; nor is the law an explanation later affixed to the object.

　　The joint generation of an object and its law is Object-Transformation.

Object-Transformation takes place within the Self-Manifestation-Field, and acquires within it differing sedimentary expressions: the Imagery-Field sediments the imagery of the object; the Experience-Field sediments law; Principle, generated further upon laws already in place, sediments in the Intent-Field. Thus, although object and law are alike outcomes of Object-Transformation, they acquire respectively the material expression and the pattern expression; and law further becomes the basis for the generation of Principle, and the basis for the further generation of Self-Manifestation.

The "basis" here remains only a relation of precedence in the order of generative qualification; it does not mean that once law stands, Principle or Self-Manifestation is generated automatically.

In the subsequent running of Self-Manifestation, Principle may further drive laws already in place to take part in concrete generation; Perceptual Experience is but one example of this, and is not developed here.

Object-Transformation states the constitutive relation by which thing and law are generated together; what this text asks below is, of these, by what "law" — this pattern expression — has already acquired its present qualification to stand, on the side of the Sedimentation-Field. The two describe different question-levels of one and the same generative fact; they are not two generations of law.

What this text asks is therefore not an isolated question of "criteria for regularity." It stands at a generative juncture of this kind: how a Sedimentation-Field passes from leaving traces to being able to organize later Causal-Response by means of formed relations.

Following the existing account in *On Self-Manifestation* concerning external flux, Trace-Response, and sedimentary generation, this text adopts the following higher-level ontological position:

Law is not suspended outside the field as a logical entity; it is generated within the Sedimentation-Field. When the relation that has sedimented is able to enter the present and to bear later Continuation, the field acquires the qualification to generate law. Law thereby becomes the prior basis by which a Sedimentation-Field passes from the accumulation of Trace-Response into the organization of Causal-Response, and continues toward the generation of Principle and of Self-Manifestation. The precedence here is precedence in the order of generative qualification, not a sufficient causal relation: it must not be read as saying that once law stands, Principle and Self-Manifestation necessarily follow. What this text does is to put law back in its own place.

The above — Object-Transformation, the division of office among the three fields, and the higher-level relation of law to Principle and to Self-Manifestation — comes from the existing theory of *On Self-Manifestation* and is not reproved here. This text takes up only the part of it that concerns law and goes on interrogating: given that a law already stands in the Sedimentation-Field, what structural qualification must it minimally have acquired?

That is to say:

If external physics is only flux, and what is ordinarily called a law is generated within the Sedimentation-Field, then once a law stands in the field, what must it minimally possess? Lacking what, can it not stand as a law already formed?

In this text we shall, under continuous interrogation, find where the proof ought to go, and derive the general form of law.

Our aim is:

to obtain the minimal necessary structure for a law to stand in a Sedimentation-Field, together with a proof of minimality and the paths of breakdown.

We give the result first.

Under the source-text semantics of "holding-and-recurrence" inherited by C6, and when C0, C2 and H1—H3 hold: if a candidate law L stands in a Sedimentation-Field F relative to a non-empty domain of application I, then there exists at least one in-field bearing organization R such that:

　　⇔ DynamicRole\_F,t(L;R,I)  
　　 ∧ ΩSupport\_F,t(L;R,I)

That is:

　　C0 ∧ C2 ∧ H1 ∧ H2 ∧ H3  
　　⇒ \[  
　　　 Law\_F,t(L,I)  
　　　 ⇒ ∃R \[  
　　　　 Established\_F,t(R)  
　　　　 ∧ Govern\_F,t(L;R,I)

This text proves only this necessary direction.

The converse:

　　Established ∧ Govern ⇒ Law

is not proven here; the sufficient direction belongs to the Necessity Form of Law.

I. Working Domain

Let:

　　F　be a Sedimentation-Field;  
　　t　be the present moment;  
　　L　be the present candidate law;  
　　I　be the non-empty domain of application of L.

Write:

　　Law\_F,t(L,I)

for: the candidate law L stands at moment t, in the Sedimentation-Field F, relative to the domain of application I.

L fixes only the law now being adjudicated.

Rel(L) denotes the relational content to which L refers. It serves only as a propositional-semantic index; it is not a newly added field quantity, and it does not presuppose that the relation already holds.

I must satisfy:

　　I ≠ ∅

I delimits only the domain of application of the present candidate law. It does not require that a second actual inflow has already occurred, nor that two or more alternative paths exist.

In proving or adjudicating a candidate law, I must be declared before Govern is tested; I may not be shrunk after the fact because a counterexample has appeared.

\**The semantics of I: the coverage domain of application*\*

I is the coverage domain of application predeclared for the candidate law L. It is not a pool of candidates from which "any one good entry will do." For the conditions to which L claims actual application, one successful bearing somewhere in I may not stand in for the qualification of the remaining applicable conditions; nor may I be shrunk, once a failing condition is seen, in order to preserve Law.

\**Applicability-coverage guard*\*　Coverage does not require that every bare condition in I bear the same office; it imposes the coverage obligation upon those conditions in I that \**belong to the actual applicable coverage of Rel(L)*\* — that is, those to which L, by its relational content, really does claim application, and which constitute a legitimate case of application in F. Bare conditions that do not belong to the applicable office of L are not brought under the coverage requirement. At the structural layer this text makes only this abstract declaration; the specific technical characterization of the coverage domain (the triggering family, the applicability constraint, the legitimate re-entry evaluations, and so forth) is assigned to the Mathematical Form of Law and is not developed here.

\**The quantifier polarity of the domain of application*\*　The structural predicates of this text do not have a uniform quantifier polarity with respect to I, and they must not be forced into uniformity for the sake of tidiness:

　CurrentCarrier\_F,t(R) — carries no I; no domain-quantifier question arises.  
　CarrierRealizes\_F,t(L;R,I) — coverage polarity. R forms the complete in-field bearing of the relational content that L actually claims to cover within I; it is not enough that "there exists some i at which it can be realized."  
　CarrierNonInstant\_F,t(L;R,I) — coverage polarity. That complete bearing, for all the applicable content actually claimed to be covered within I, depends on no necessary bearing link still maintained solely by the present instantaneous inflow.  
　DynamicRole\_F,t(L;R,I) — existential polarity. It answers whether R really enters the generation of what follows in Rel(L), rather than being pure record, pure correlation, or a bystanding structure; it does not require that each condition in I severally furnish an independent dynamical witness. This polarity must not be changed to a universal one: were it so changed, the legitimate cases of redundant bearing, joint bearing, and multiple bearing would be misjudged (the office borne by H3).  
　StableReplay\_F,t(L;R,I) — coverage polarity. For the legitimate re-entry conditions that L actually claims to cover within I, the already formed structure can in each case recur stably; it is not enough that "there exists some condition at which recurrence is possible."  
　ΩSupport\_F,t(L;R,I) — coverage polarity. See the third interrogation.

The ground of this division of polarities: DynamicRole is a \**qualification of reality*\* — it asks whether the bearing is real; StableReplay and ΩSupport are \**qualifications of applicable coverage*\* — they ask whether the whole claimed range of application is supported. Reality needs only one non-trivial witness to establish that this is "not mere record"; coverage, by contrast, must extend to all the applicable content claimed, for otherwise L's very claim of application falls through.

R denotes an in-field organization of F that may bear L.

R may be a single, distributed, redundant, jointly acting, or composite structure. R is not an object designated in advance in the antecedent of Law; it is the bearing organization whose existence the general form finally requires.

II. A Relation Has Occurred Once — Is That Already a Law?

Take first the weakest case.

An inflow arrives within I, and F presently exhibits a subsequent relation matching Rel(L).

This one occasion may be perfectly exact.

Write the actual running trajectory as:

　　ρ

Even if:

　　ρ ⊨ Rel(L)

this shows only that the trajectory that actually occurred on this occasion matched the candidate relation.

Now separate three layers.

The first layer is the concrete inflow.

An inflow may trigger the present run, and may also go on to alter the field.

The second layer is the bearing organization already formed in F at present.

It answers:

　　what, out of the past, has entered the present.

The third layer is the present actual running trajectory ρ.

It answers:

　　how this one occasion actually ran.

These three layers must not be conflated.

That one ρ matched Rel(L) does not amount to a law having been formed in F.

If we withdraw this assumption:

　　that F already contains some formed relational organization;

then the present relation may come entirely from:

　　the instantaneous compulsion of the present inflow;  
　　the direct stipulation of a structure outside the field;  
　　or an observer's description of the before-and-after relation on this occasion.

In such a case there is an event, there is a response, and there is a describable before-and-after relation.

But once this occasion is past, there is in F no formed organization that carries this relation forward to what follows as part of its own present structure.

*Information-Transformation*, Outer Chapter 7, says:

　　「信号之变化入沉积而律焉。」  
　　the transformation of signal enters sediment and is regulated therein.

This text takes this higher-level judgment as an antecedent of inheritance awaiting verification:

\**C0　Inheritance: law enters sedimentation*\*

Within the working domain of this text, if a relation acquires the qualification of a law already formed in F, then its relational bearing cannot remain merely at the level of stipulation outside the field, instantaneous response, or observational description; it must enter the in-field sedimentary generative chain of F.

C0 at present serves only to exclude the possibility that "law hangs entirely outside the field, or at the instantaneous or descriptive layer"; it does not directly presuppose Established.

But what this text discusses is not "a relation now entering sedimentation and possibly becoming a law in future"; it is:

　　Law\_F,t(L,I)

standing already at moment t.

If the structure that bears the relation still remains wholly at the stage of being shaped instantaneously by the present inflow, then at most a process of law-formation exists here; one cannot yet say that a law stands at moment t.

Therefore, under C0, the first interrogation narrows the question further:

For a Law that already stands, there must exist in the field a present relational bearing organization R; and the present standing of this complete relational bearing organization cannot still rely solely on the present instantaneous inflow for its maintenance.

Two intermediate predicates are introduced here:

　　CurrentCarrier\_F,t(R)

signifies that R already exists in F as a present relational bearing organization, and is not a mere stipulation outside the field, an observational description, or an abstract relation existing only in a proposition.

Further:

To avoid importing "whether it already bears what follows" into the first interrogation prematurely, no notion of role realization or of any dynamical office is used here.

Write:

　　CarrierRealizes\_F,t(L;R,I)=1

to signify that R is itself the complete in-field organization by which F, at moment t, bears the relational content Rel(L); this bearing must extend to the whole of the relational content that L actually claims to cover within I (coverage polarity, see Section I), and bearing at one place among them may not stand in for the rest.

"Bearing" here signifies only that the relational content has acquired a present structural position through this in-field organization. It does not signify that R has influenced what follows, that R possesses DynamicRole, or that R already Governs.

Further, write:

　　CarrierNonInstant\_F,t(L;R,I)=1

to signify that R, as this complete relational bearing organization, for the applicable content actually claimed to be covered within I, in each case stands without depending on any necessary bearing link still maintained solely by the present instantaneous inflow (coverage polarity).

What the first interrogation really needs to obtain is therefore:

　　CurrentCarrier\_F,t(R)  
　　∧ CarrierRealizes\_F,t(L;R,I)  
　　∧ CarrierNonInstant\_F,t(L;R,I)

But Established still cannot be written down directly here.

One must still answer:

why must an organization that has acquired a complete in-field relational bearing position, and whose present complete realization of that bearing has already detached from purely instantaneous maintenance, belong to the sedimentary bearing state?

An upstream physical bridge is needed here:

\**H1　Bridge from relational-bearing realization to sedimentary state*\*

　　CurrentCarrier\_F,t(R)  
　　∧ CarrierRealizes\_F,t(L;R,I)  
　　∧ CarrierNonInstant\_F,t(L;R,I)  
　　⇒ Established\_F,t(R)

H1 serves only to bring the "present complete in-field relational bearing + non-instantaneous standing" obtained by the first interrogation down to Established; it does not bear the question whether R really enters the generation of what follows.

Only thus does the first interrogation formally obtain:

　　Established\_F,t(R)

\**The meaning of Established*\*

Established\_F,t(R) signifies:

R, as the whole organization required to bear the present candidate relation, has already become the present sedimentary bearing organization of F, and is not a response state maintained solely by the present inflow.

Established does not require that R come from F's own lived history.

If some relational organization enters F by inheritance, transplantation, or external writing-in, then so long as it has really become the present sedimentary bearing state of F, it does not lose the qualification of Established on account of its differing provenance. ΩSupport is judged separately by the recurrence records within the declared window in F; see Sections IV and VII.

Established likewise does not require that R continue to exist in the future.

Having been formed at present, and whether it is preserved thereafter, are two distinct questions.

\**The freeze test*\*

C1 is used here:

\**C1　Instantaneous and non-instantaneous states are distinguishable.*\*

C1 is used only for the freeze test and for operational adjudication; it is not an antecedent of the general necessity theorem.

One may freeze the field state at one and the same moment, withdrawing only the instantaneous drive of the present inflow, without letting subsequent Shallowing, updating, or new inflow go on altering the field.

The freeze test is directed at the complete relational bearing organization R of the candidate relation L itself.

If some bearing link necessary to R as a complete bearing organization at present disappears together with the instantaneous drive, then:

　　CarrierNonInstant\_F,t(L;R,I)=0

and Established cannot be obtained from H1 in that case.

If there exists another complete redundant bearing organization R′ with:

　　CarrierRealizes\_F,t(L;R′,I)=1  
　　∧ CarrierNonInstant\_F,t(L;R′,I)=1

then R′ enters the existential search as another legitimate candidate bearer; the formation qualification of R′ is not transferred to a larger R.

Accordingly, this text does not infer Established of an arbitrary superstructure from a qualified substructure, nor does it judge whether a complete bearing organization has been formed by asking whether some single element is globally irreplaceable.

At this point the original question has been narrowed for the first time:

A law cannot consist of one occurrence only.

It must have a bearer already formed within the field.

So we go on asking:

　　Once a relational organization has been left in the field, is that already a law?

III. The Relation Has Been Left Behind, but It Does Not Govern What Follows — Is It Still a Law?

Keep the very same R obtained at the previous step.

Now let:

　　Established\_F,t(R)=1

that is, this relational organization has indeed entered the present sedimentary bearing state of F.

But let it be an abandoned old Groove-Line.

It may preserve traces of the past.

It may be measurable.

It may correlate highly with past results.

It may even help an observer to predict what happened in the past.

Only: when similar conditions arrive again within I,

　　how the subsequent state is generated has nothing to do with the present state of R.

Now ask again:

　　Once a relation has been left behind, yet no longer influences what comes after, can it still be called a law standing at present?

It cannot.

*The Experience-Field*, Inner Chapter 9, says:

　　「律者，持之势也，理者，破持之势也。」  
　　Law holds. Principle breaks the hold.

The first item of inheritance from C6 is used here:

\**C6a　Semantic inheritance of "holding"*\*

*The Experience-Field* uses "holding" to state that between a law and later Continuation there is a relation for which pure preservation, pure record, or static description cannot substitute.

In this text C6a stipulates only the direction of further interrogation:

Between an already formed relational structure and later Continuation, what actual relation is still lacking, such that it is not merely a trace left behind?

C6a does not directly presuppose DynamicRole, nor stipulate in advance what dynamical form this relation must take. The necessary standing of DynamicRole is still forced out by the degenerate world of this round.

Hence "having been left behind" settles only:

　　whether the past has entered the present.

It does not settle:

　　whether what has now been left behind begins, in turn, to bear what follows.

So Established is still not enough.

The second interrogation forces out further:

This already formed R must really enter the generation of the subsequent states involved in Rel(L).

Write it as:

　　DynamicRole\_F,t(L;R,I)

\**The meaning of DynamicRole*\*

C5 is used here:

\**C5　The present field state of F can enter the generation of subsequent states.*\*

DynamicRole\_F,t(L;R,I) requires:

The relevant states of R really enter the generation of the subsequent structure involved in Rel(L).

The subsequent reachable set, tendency, ordering, transition weights, distribution, or other dynamical structure connected with Rel(L) depends non-trivially and dynamically on the present state of R.

It excludes:

　　pure record;  
　　pure correlation;  
　　pure prediction;  
　　concomitant traces;  
　　structures already formed but idle with respect to the present relation.

DynamicRole does not require:

　　that R alone determine the result;  
　　that R be the sole cause;  
　　that R be the principal cause;  
　　that the result necessarily change when R is deleted.

Under redundancy, joint action, standby, or multiple bearing, simply deleting one R may leave the result maintained by another structure.

Therefore this text does not take:

　　remove(R) ⇒ result changes

as the definition of DynamicRole.

\**Correlation is not dynamical bearing*\*

Suppose there exists a hidden state Z which brings about both:

　　some state of R;

and:

　　the subsequent relation.

Then R and what follows may correlate highly, and may even have strong predictive power.

But if R itself has not entered the dynamics of Continuation, and R is only a concomitant trace, then:

　　DynamicRole=0

So statistical correlation cannot substitute for DynamicRole.

\**Full determination is not a necessary condition either*\*

If what follows is also jointly influenced by inflows, noise, other field structures, or competing relations, then so long as the present state of R really enters the generation of what follows in connection with Rel(L), DynamicRole may still hold.

Accordingly Govern will not thereafter require that R fully determine every actual outcome.

\**Redundant bearing*\*

Under multiple bearing, a particular R may really bear L, and yet, once it is simply deleted, another redundant structure may go on maintaining the same result.

Hence what is required is:

\**H3　Existence bridge for the redundant causal office of DynamicRole*\*

Under redundancy, joint action, standby, or multiple bearing, whether a particular R really belongs to the dynamics generating what follows in Rel(L) has an objective truth value; this truth value does not take the simple but-for deletion counterfactual as a necessary condition.

H3 bears only the ontological truth value of the redundant causal office; it does not bear the task of proving in advance the necessity of DynamicRole for Law.

That necessity has already been obtained by the second interrogation.

At this point we have:

　　the same R already formed;  
　　and the same R really entering the generation of what follows.

So we go on asking:

　　Once it begins to influence what follows, is that already a law?

IV. It Has Influenced What Follows Once — Is That Already a Law?

Keep the very same R.

Now require:

　　Established\_F,t(R)=1

and:

　　DynamicRole\_F,t(L;R,I)=1

Construct one strong impulse.

Once a strong impulse has entered F, it can leave a conspicuous groove mark.

This new Groove-Line may already have entered the present field state, so it need not be a purely instantaneous response.

At the same time, it may really alter the next stretch of what follows.

Hence one may have:

　　Established=1  
　　DynamicRole=1

If one asks only:

　　has this structure influenced what follows;

the answer is already yes.

But now ask further:

　　A Groove-Line just left by one strong impulse — provided it can alter the next step, is it thereby no different from a "law"?

If the answer were yes, then any single strong action, so long as it leaves a sufficiently deep mark and alters the next step, could immediately be called a law.

That would conflate:

　　a short continuation following one formation;

with:

　　an already formed stable recurrence.

Yet "law" means neither mere occurrence nor merely one action.

*The Experience-Field*, Inner Chapter 9, says:

　　「律者，复也，深也……」  
　　Law—repetition and depth …

The second item of inheritance from C6 is used here:

\**C6b　Semantic inheritance of "recurrence"*\*

*The Experience-Field* uses "recurrence" to state that a law cannot be understood from one actual occurrence alone; between one instance of bearing and the formation of a law there remains a difference that must be interrogated further.

In this text C6b stipulates only the direction of further interrogation:

Is a relational structure that has really influenced what follows once already sufficient to acquire the qualification of a law, or is there still lacking some formational difference bearing on bearing it again?

C6b does not directly presuppose StableReplay, nor stipulate in advance the specific form of that difference. The necessary standing of StableReplay is still forced out by the degeneration of the strong impulse in this round together with the continuous interrogation.

　　StableReplay\_F,t(L;R,I)

For each class of legitimate re-entry condition within I belonging to the actual applicable coverage of L, when a similar condition arrives again, the later Continuation required by Rel(L) is in every case not manufactured afresh by one external compulsion, but can go on being borne by the structure R has already formed at present (coverage polarity).
