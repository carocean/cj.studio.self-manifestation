# The Stability Form of Law

## Section I　Purpose

Once a law has acquired formal qualification in the Sedimentation-Field, it still faces another independent question: as the field state continues to change, can that qualification be preserved; after the original bearing organization withdraws, is there any other organization that goes on bearing; when do all bearing qualifications vanish at once; and can a qualification once lost be reacquired by the Sedimentation-Field's own subsequent dynamics.

*The Mathematical Form of Law* gives the formal solution set of a law:

　𝒮(F\_t,L,I) = {R ⊆ Existing\_t \| EstWitness^M\_F,t(L;R,I) ∧ Govern^M\_F,t(L;R,I)}.

where:

　EstWitness^M ⇔ CurrentCarrier^M ∧ CarrierRealizes^M ∧ CarrierNonInstant^M.

　Govern^M ⇔ DynamicRole^M ∧ ΩSupport^M ∧ Bind^M.

And it has further been proved that:

　CarrierNonInstant^M ⇒ CarrierRealizes^M.

　Bind^M ⇒ ΩSupport^M ⇒ StableReplay^M.

\**Upstream quantification compatibility check.*\*　From v1.2 onward the Mathematical Form of Law quantified these predicates (∀^{Role}, ∀^{Replay}, in the two modes set / measure); from v1.3 onward it further narrowed the quantification domain to the applicability-coverage guard domain I\_cov(L,I); and it introduced the three-valued convention. This check asserts one thing only: \**the directions of the implications above are unchanged*\* — CarrierNonInstant^M takes CarrierRealizes^M = 1 as a definitional antecedent (3.36); Bind, ΩSupport and StableReplay share the ReplayReady antecedent and the ∀^{Replay} polarity, and pointwise (ΩWit ∧ Scheme) ⇒ ΩWit ⇒ ReplayWit (3.41, 3.44), while the ∀ of both modes preserves pointwise implication.

This check does \**not*\* assert that the fibre equivalences of Section IV hold as they stand, under ordinary set-theoretic universals and existentials, in measure mode. Those have been rewritten to the current regime; see 4.0.

Therefore:

　R ∈ 𝒮(F\_t,L,I) ⇔ CurrentCarrier^M ∧ CarrierNonInstant^M ∧ DynamicRole^M ∧ Bind^M.

This equivalence gives the direct entrance to the stability problem. What stability preserves is not some fixed realization path, some particular Π, ρ or W, but the formal qualification already defined by *The Mathematical Form of Law*. Accordingly this text divides stability into two levels: whether one and the same R continues to belong to 𝒮, and whether 𝒮 continues to be non-empty when R may be replaced.

The former treats fixed-solution stability, the latter solution-set stability. The difference between them is precisely the formal locus at which substitution of bearers can absorb perturbation.

## Section II　Stability Instances

### 2.1　Stability Input

\**Definition 2.1*\*〔Stability instance〕

A stability instance is written:

　Σ = (F\_t,L,I,R,Δ,H).

where:

　F\_t is the present Sedimentation-Field;  
　L is the candidate law;  
　I is the non-empty domain of application given before adjudication;  
　R is the bearing solution in the fixed-solution task;  
　Δ is the permitted state-perturbation class;  
　H is the dynamic stability protocol.

In the fixed-solution task, L, I, Rel(L) and R are fixed.

In the solution-set task, L, I and Rel(L) are fixed, while R may be solved afresh by M at each state.

This fixity is of great importance. If, after destabilization, one alters I, rewrites Rel(L), or designates a new candidate law, what is obtained is already another formal instance, and it may not be conflated with the recovery of the original instance.

### 2.2　State Perturbation

\**Definition 2.2*\*〔State perturbation〕

For η ∈ Δ:

　η: F\_t ↦ F\_t^η.

η acts upon the present state of the Sedimentation-Field. After the perturbation, M adjudicates F\_t^η afresh with the original L, I and Rel(L).

\**Definition 2.3*\*〔Admissible perturbation〕

　AdmPert(η) = 1

if and only if F\_t^η, and all objects M reads from that field state, satisfy the types, domains of definition and formal interfaces of M.

Define:

　Δ^adm = {η ∈ Δ \| AdmPert(η) = 1}.

\**Definition 2.4*\*〔Decidable perturbation〕

　DecPert(η) = 1

if and only if:

　AdmPert(η) = 1

and that instance satisfies M1–M6 together with the corresponding SM conditions of this text.

Define:

　Δ^dec = {η ∈ Δ^adm \| DecPert(η) = 1}.

A perturbation that is admissible but not presently computable belongs to the domain of the stability problem, but its present adjudicated value is undecidable; this differs from stability being 0.

## Section III　Fixed-Solution Qualification

### 3.1　The Minimal Adjudication Core

\**Definition 3.1*\*〔Fixed-solution qualification〕

　Qual\_M(F\_t,L,I,R) = 1 ⇔ R ∈ 𝒮(F\_t,L,I).

\**Theorem 3.2*\*〔Minimal adjudication theorem for the fixed solution〕

For any legitimate F\_t, L, I, R:

　R ∈ 𝒮(F\_t,L,I) ⇔ CurrentCarrier^M ∧ CarrierNonInstant^M ∧ DynamicRole^M ∧ Bind^M.

*Proof.*

From:

　R ∈ 𝒮 ⇔ EstWitness^M ∧ Govern^M,

together with:

　CarrierNonInstant^M ⇒ CarrierRealizes^M,

we obtain:

　EstWitness^M ⇔ CurrentCarrier^M ∧ CarrierNonInstant^M.

And from:

　Bind^M ⇒ ΩSupport^M,

we obtain:

　Govern^M ⇔ DynamicRole^M ∧ Bind^M.

Conjoining gives the result. □

\**Three-valued note.*\*　Under the three-valued convention of the Mathematical Form of Law at v1.2 (a decisive 0 overrides ⊥; where there is no 0 but there is ⊥, then ⊥ propagates), this theorem holds by value: both sides are alike 1, alike 0, or alike ⊥. The ⊥ of adjudicated values at the M layer follows the convention of the Mathematical Form of Law; the ⊥ of the dynamic layer follows the DynDom adjudication-domain regime of this text. The two layers of ⊥ are of separate semantics and may not be conflated.

This minimal adjudication core is responsible only for adjudicating whether a fixed R remains a formal solution. CarrierRealizes^M, StableReplay^M and ΩSupport^M, though absorbed in truth value by stronger conditions respectively, still bear the office of destabilization diagnosis, and therefore may not be deleted from stability analysis.

### 3.2　Core Responsibilities

\**Definition 3.3*\*〔Core qualification set〕

　Core = {CC,CNI,Dyn,Bnd},

where:

　CC = CurrentCarrier^M,  
　CNI = CarrierNonInstant^M,  
　Dyn = DynamicRole^M,  
　Bnd = Bind^M.

\**Definition 3.4*\*〔Core failure set〕

For η ∈ Δ^adm:

　FailCore\_R(η) = {P ∈ Core \| P takes the value 0 on (F\_t^η,L,I,R)}.

Hence:

　R ∈ 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I) ⇔ FailCore\_R(η) = ∅.

The core failure set answers "which top-level necessary qualification has been severed." To answer "why it was severed," one must go further into the witness structure internal to each qualification.

## Section IV　Witness Fibres and Destabilization Diagnosis

4.0　Quantification Domain and Evaluation Mode of the Fibres

The witness fibres used in this section are all defined for a \**single*\* (i,ν); the quantification of the structural predicates over these fibres follows throughout the current regime of *The Mathematical Form of Law*, and does not use ordinary set-theoretic universals or existentials:

　(a) \**Domain.*\*　For the coverage-type criteria (CarrierRealizes, CarrierNonInstant, ReplayReady, StableReplay, ΩSupport, Bind) and the existential-type criterion (DynamicRole) alike, the quantification domain of i is the applicability-coverage guard domain I\_cov(L,I) = {i ∈ I \| Trig(i) ≠ ∅}, not the whole of I; AdmPair and ReplayPair are constructed over the whole of I and are notations for object sets only, bearing no quantification of criteria (Definition 3.16 of M).

　(b) \**Mode.*\*　For each i ∈ I\_cov, the quantification of ν is borne by the Type-sensitive quantifiers ∀^{Role}\_{L,i}, ∃^{Role}\_{L,i}, ∀^{Replay}\_{L,i}, ∃^{Replay}\_{L,i}, whose interpretation is determined by the unique EvalMode(L,i) of that i: in set mode they read as ordinary universals and existentials; in measure mode they read as almost-everywhere and positive-measure existence under the conditional applicability measure (3.16′ of M). When MixedMode(L,i) ∈ {1,⊥}, the corresponding quantifiers are ⊥.

　(c) \**Consequences.*\*　In measure mode, emptiness of a fibre on a null-measure evaluation does \**not*\* make a coverage-type criterion fail; conversely, non-emptiness of a fibre on null-measure evaluations alone does \**not*\* make an existential-type criterion hold. Hence every equivalence of this section is written in the form I\_cov + Type-sensitive quantifier; only when EvalMode(L,i) = set do they degenerate into ordinary set-theoretic universals and existentials over Adm(L,i) and ReplayAdm(L,i), thereby agreeing with the formulations of v1.1 of this text.

　(d) \**The diagnostic office of the fibres is unchanged.*\*　The change of quantification regime does not affect the diagnostic value of the fibres themselves: a fibre still gives, for each (i,ν), "which legitimate witnesses remain at that obligation point," and destabilization diagnosis is still traced along the fibres. What has changed is only the reading of the quantifier in the step from the state of the fibres to the truth value of the predicate.

### 4.1　Formation-State Joint Realizations

For a = (i,ν) ∈ AdmPair(L,I), define:

　𝓦\_CR(a;R) = JointReal\_R^{RoleCtx(i,ν)}(D\_{i,ν},ν).

Define the non-instantaneous sub-fibre:

　𝓦\_NI(a;R) = {Π ∈ 𝓦\_CR(a;R) \| Q(Π) ⊆ Estab\_t}.

Hence:

　𝓦\_NI(a;R) ⊆ 𝓦\_CR(a;R).

\**Theorem 4.1*\*〔Fibre criterion for CarrierRealizes〕

　CarrierRealizes^M = 1  
　⇔ I\_cov(L,I) ≠ ∅  
　　∧ ∀i ∈ I\_cov(L,I): \[the role domain is non-empty ∧ ∀^{Role}\_{L,i}\[D\_{i,ν} ≠ ∅ ∧ 𝓦\_CR((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅\] = 1\].

Non-emptiness of the role domain is an explicit antecedent: Adm(L,i) ≠ ∅ in set mode, RoleMass(L,i) \> 0 in measure mode.

In set mode this degenerates to: ∀ν ∈ Adm(L,i): D\_{i,ν} ≠ ∅ ∧ 𝓦\_CR((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅. In measure mode it becomes that condition holding almost everywhere under μ̄^Role\_{L,i}; emptiness of a fibre on a null-measure evaluation does not make the criterion fail.

\**Theorem 4.2*\*〔Fibre criterion for CarrierNonInstant〕

　CarrierNonInstant^M = 1  
　⇔ CarrierRealizes^M = 1  
　　∧ ∀i ∈ I\_cov(L,I): ∀^{Role}\_{L,i}\[𝓦\_NI((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅\] = 1.

*Proof.*

From Definitions 3.35 and 3.36 of M. □

The stability of CarrierNonInstant does not require that one and the same joint realization Π exist forever. At each present obligation point, so long as the non-instantaneous sub-fibre remains non-empty, the disappearance of the original Π may be replaced by another legitimate Π. Genuine destabilization occurs when all non-instantaneous joint realizations at that obligation point disappear together.

### 4.2　DynamicRole

\**Definition 4.3*\*〔Role witness fibre and total set〕

The role witness fibre at each (i,ν):

　𝓦\_D((i,ν);R) = {(d,ρ) \| d ∈ D\_{i,ν}, ρ ∈ RoleReal\_R^{RoleCtx(i,ν)}(d)}.

The total set of role witnesses (a diagnostic notation, bearing no quantification of criteria):

　𝓦\_D(R) = {(i,ν,d,ρ) \| (i,ν) ∈ AdmPair(L,I), (d,ρ) ∈ 𝓦\_D((i,ν);R)}.

\**Theorem 4.4*\*〔Witness criterion for DynamicRole〕

　DynamicRole^M = 1  
　⇔ ∃i ∈ I\_cov(L,I): ∃^{Role}\_{L,i}\[𝓦\_D((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅\] = 1.

*Proof.*

From Definition 3.38 of M. □

\**Mode note.*\*　In set mode this is equivalent to: there exist i ∈ I\_cov and ν ∈ Adm(L,i) with 𝓦\_D((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅, that is, the part of 𝓦\_D(R) over I\_cov is non-empty. In measure mode it requires instead that {ν \| 𝓦\_D((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅} have positive measure: where role witnesses exist only on null-measure evaluations, 𝓦\_D(R) ≠ ∅ while DynamicRole^M = 0. Hence 𝓦\_D(R) ≠ ∅ may not by itself serve as this criterion.

DynamicRole is an existential-type qualification. Hence the withdrawal of one original role witness does not suffice to prove that the dynamical role has failed; only when its existential quantifier takes 0 as a whole does Dyn pass from 1 to 0. What stability preserves here is the qualification "there is still an actual role entering subsequent generation," not the identity-continuity of some given role witness.

### 4.3　Replay, Ω and Bind

For r = (i,ν) ∈ ReplayPair(L,I), define:

　𝓦\_RP(r;R) = {Π \| ReplayWit\_R(L,i,ν,Π)}.

　𝓦\_Ω(r;R) = {Π \| ΩWit\_R(L,i,ν,Π)}.

　𝓦\_B(r;R) = {Π \| ΩWit\_R(L,i,ν,Π) ∧ Π ∈ Scheme\_R(L,i,ν)}.

By definition:

　𝓦\_B(r;R) ⊆ 𝓦\_Ω(r;R) ⊆ 𝓦\_RP(r;R).

\**Theorem 4.5*\*〔Fibre criterion for StableReplay〕

　StableReplay^M = 1 ⇔ ReplayReady = 1 ∧ ∀i ∈ I\_cov(L,I): ∀^{Replay}\_{L,i}\[𝓦\_RP((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅\] = 1.

\**Theorem 4.6*\*〔Fibre criterion for ΩSupport〕

　ΩSupport^M = 1 ⇔ ReplayReady = 1 ∧ ∀i ∈ I\_cov(L,I): ∀^{Replay}\_{L,i}\[𝓦\_Ω((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅\] = 1.

\**Theorem 4.7*\*〔Fibre criterion for Bind〕

　Bind^M = 1 ⇔ ReplayReady = 1 ∧ ∀i ∈ I\_cov(L,I): ∀^{Replay}\_{L,i}\[𝓦\_B((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅\] = 1.

The mode reading of these three is as in 4.0(b): in set mode an ordinary universal over ReplayAdm(L,i), in measure mode almost everywhere under the conditional measure.

Hence:

　Bind^M ⇒ ΩSupport^M ⇒ StableReplay^M.

This chain of inclusions shows that re-entry stability is not three mutually parallel Boolean conditions. ReplayWit gives a realization capable of re-entry; ΩWit adds upon it the requirements of reach and of stable continuation; Bind further requires that the Ω witness belong at the same time to one and the same realization family. At each further layer the witness set is tightened.

4.4　Destabilization on the Formation Branch

\**Theorem 4.8*\*〔Failure decomposition of CurrentCarrier〕

With R fixed, CurrentCarrier^M = 0 if and only if at least one of the following holds:

　(1) R = ∅;  
　(2) R ⊄ Existing\_t;  
　(3) there is in R a constituent not of legitimate type, or whose necessary field quantities are undefined;  
　(4) R cannot be expressed as a union of a legitimate family of organization blocks.

The absence of an Org\_t relation between distinct organization blocks does not by itself constitute a failure of CurrentCarrier; M permits distributed bearing organizations.

\**Theorem 4.9*\*〔Failure decomposition of CarrierRealizes〕

CarrierRealizes^M = 0 if and only if at least one of the following holds:

　(1) I\_cov(L,I) = ∅;  
　(2) ∃i ∈ I\_cov: the role domain is empty (Adm(L,i) = ∅ in set mode; RoleMass(L,i) = 0 in measure mode);  
　(3) ∃i ∈ I\_cov: ∀^{Role}\_{L,i}\[D\_{i,ν} ≠ ∅ ∧ 𝓦\_CR((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅\] = 0.

Mode reading of (3): in set mode it suffices that one ν ∈ Adm(L,i) have D\_{i,ν} = ∅ or 𝓦\_CR((i,ν);R) = ∅; in measure mode the failure event must have positive measure, a null-measure counterexample not sufficing for an adjudication of 0.

Here, the several offices each possessing a Real cannot substitute for JointReal being non-empty. Proof from Definition 3.35 of M and Theorem 4.3 of M. □

\**Theorem 4.10*\*〔Failure decomposition of CarrierNonInstant〕

CarrierNonInstant^M = 0 if and only if:

　CarrierRealizes^M = 0

or:

　∃i ∈ I\_cov(L,I): ∀^{Role}\_{L,i}\[𝓦\_NI((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅\] = 0 (mode reading as in 4.9(3)).

Hence the failure of the formation branch can be distinguished at least into failure of bearing qualification, failure of joint-realization coverage, and failure of non-instantaneous qualification. All three make EstWitness^M fail, but the structures they destroy are different.

4.5　Destabilization of the Dynamical Role

\**Theorem 4.11*\*〔Failure decomposition of DynamicRole〕

　DynamicRole^M = 0 ⇔ ∀i ∈ I\_cov(L,I): ∃^{Role}\_{L,i}\[𝓦\_D((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅\] = 0.

In set mode this is emptiness of the part of 𝓦\_D(R) over I\_cov; in measure mode it is that {ν \| 𝓦\_D((i,ν);R) ≠ ∅} is null for every i.

For a given ρ, the withdrawal of RoleReal may be brought about by at least the following two direct conditions:

　(1) ρ ∉ ExtReal;  
　(2) ρ ∈ ExtReal and RolePath\_ρ = ∅.

Lemma 4.7 of M gives the temporal-pre-emption type; Lemmas 4.8 and 4.9 of M give the support-closure-failure type.

Theorem 4.14 of M shows further that StableReplay^M and ΩSupport^M may be retained while DynamicRole^M = 0. Hence "being able to re-enter," "having Ω support," and "presently really entering subsequent generation" are distinct qualifications, and stability adjudication may not let one branch substitute for another.

4.6　Destabilization on the Re-entry Branch

\**Theorem 4.12*\*〔Failure decomposition of ReplayReady〕

ReplayReady = 0 if and only if at least one of the following holds:

　(1) I\_cov(L,I) = ∅;  
　(2) ∃i ∈ I\_cov: the re-entry domain is empty (ReplayAdm(L,i) = ∅ in set mode; ReplayMass(L,i) = 0 in measure mode);  
　(3) ∃i ∈ I\_cov: ∀^{Replay}\_{L,i}\[D\_{i,ν} ≠ ∅\] = 0 (mode reading as in 4.9(3)).

*Note.* For conditions in I ∖ I\_cov, Trig(i) = ∅ is precisely the reason why they bear no coverage obligation, and ReplayReady may not be adjudicated 0 on that ground. This accords with the I\_cov compatibility check of 14.1.

\**Theorem 4.13*\*〔Three-level failure chain of re-entry〕

For any r ∈ ReplayPair:

　𝓦\_B(r;R) ⊆ 𝓦\_Ω(r;R) ⊆ 𝓦\_RP(r;R).

Hence one may distinguish:

　failure of re-entry realization: 𝓦\_RP(r;R) = ∅;

　failure of Ω support: 𝓦\_RP(r;R) ≠ ∅ ∧ 𝓦\_Ω(r;R) = ∅;

　failure of family binding: 𝓦\_Ω(r;R) ≠ ∅ ∧ 𝓦\_B(r;R) = ∅.

The one-strong-impulse world of Theorem 4.13 of M falls at the layer of failure of Ω support; the cross-realization chain-witnessing world of Theorem 4.17 of M falls at the layer of failure of family binding; and the dead-end and Ω-elsewhere worlds of Theorem 4.15 of M show that the presence of Ω elsewhere cannot substitute for actual reach to the present office.

These three layers allow the destabilization of Bind to be traced further downwards, rather than stopping at a black-box conclusion Bnd = 0.

## Section V　Instantaneous Stability

### 5.1　Preservation of the Fixed Solution

\**Definition 5.1*\*〔Instantaneous preservation domain of the fixed solution〕

　K\_fix(R) = {η ∈ Δ^adm \| R ∈ 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I)}.

By Theorem 3.2:

　η ∈ K\_fix(R) ⇔ CC\_η(R) ∧ CNI\_η(R) ∧ Dyn\_η(R) ∧ Bnd\_η(R).

Preservation of the fixed solution requires that after the perturbation the qualification of formal solution still be borne by one and the same R. Internal Π, ρ, W may be replaced, provided R itself continues to satisfy the complete criteria of M.

### 5.2　Preservation of the Solution Set

\**Definition 5.2*\*〔Instantaneous preservation domain of the solution set〕

　K\_set = {η ∈ Δ^adm \| 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I) ≠ ∅}.

\**Theorem 5.3*\*〔Solution-set preservation theorem〕

　η ∈ K\_set ⇔ ∃R′ ⊆ Existing\_t^η \[CC\_η(R′) ∧ CNI\_η(R′) ∧ Dyn\_η(R′) ∧ Bnd\_η(R′)\].

*Proof.*

From Theorem 3.2 and the definition of 𝒮. □

\**Theorem 5.4*\*〔Preservation of the fixed solution entails preservation of the solution set〕

If R₀ ∈ 𝒮(F\_t,L,I), then:

　K\_fix(R₀) ⊆ K\_set.

*Proof.*

R₀ ∈ 𝒮\_η yields directly 𝒮\_η ≠ ∅. □

Preservation of the fixed solution and preservation of the solution set thereby form two levels of stability. After the original R withdraws, so long as there exists R′ ≠ R newly satisfying the complete qualification, fixed-solution stability has already failed while solution-set stability still holds. This region is precisely the formal locus at which redundancy among solutions absorbs perturbation.

### 5.3　Global Collapse

\**Definition 5.5*\*〔Global destabilization domain〕

　F\_global = {η ∈ Δ^adm \| 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I) = ∅}.

Hence:

　F\_global = Δ^adm ∖ K\_set.

\**Definition 5.6*\*〔Core failure domains〕

　F\_CC^η = {R′ \| CC\_η(R′) = 0}.

　F\_CNI^η = {R′ \| CNI\_η(R′) = 0}.

　F\_Dyn^η = {R′ \| Dyn\_η(R′) = 0}.

　F\_Bnd^η = {R′ \| Bnd\_η(R′) = 0}.

\**Theorem 5.7*\*〔Solution-set collapse theorem〕

　𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I) = ∅

if and only if:

　∀R′ ⊆ Existing\_t^η \[¬CC\_η(R′) ∨ ¬CNI\_η(R′) ∨ ¬Dyn\_η(R′) ∨ ¬Bnd\_η(R′)\].

Equivalently:

　𝒫(Existing\_t^η) ⊆ F\_CC^η ∪ F\_CNI^η ∪ F\_Dyn^η ∪ F\_Bnd^η.

*Proof.*

Negate Theorem 5.3 and apply De Morgan's laws. □

Failure of the fixed solution requires only that one R fall into a failure domain; global collapse requires that every candidate R be covered by the failure domains. The former is therefore a local withdrawal of qualification, the latter an exhaustion of the entire candidate bearing space by the failure sets.

\**Definition 5.8*\*〔Single-cause blockade〕

If there exists P ∈ {CC,CNI,Dyn,Bnd} such that:

　∀R′ ⊆ Existing\_t^η: P\_η(R′) = 0,

then P constitutes a single-cause global blockade for that perturbed state.

\**Definition 5.9*\*〔Multi-cause covering collapse〕

If there is no single core qualification covering all candidate failures, yet:

　𝒫(Existing\_t^η) ⊆ F\_CC^η ∪ F\_CNI^η ∪ F\_Dyn^η ∪ F\_Bnd^η,

this is called a multi-cause covering collapse.

The former has one and the same qualification killing every candidate; the latter permits different candidates to withdraw for different qualifications. Both make 𝒮 vanish, but the mechanisms of destabilization are not the same.

### 5.4　The Three States

\**Definition 5.10*\*〔The three states of fixed solution and solution set〕

Given an original solution R₀ ∈ 𝒮(F\_t,L,I), after perturbation exactly one of the following states obtains:

　Original-solution state: R₀ ∈ 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I).

　Substitute-solution state: R₀ ∉ 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I) ∧ 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I) ≠ ∅.

　No-solution state: 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I) = ∅.

\**Theorem 5.11*\*〔Completeness of the three states〕

The above three states are pairwise exclusive and jointly exhaustive.

*Proof.*

From R₀ ∈ 𝒮 ⇒ 𝒮 ≠ ∅ together with the law of excluded middle. □

The three states separate "failure of the original bearer" from "failure of the formal solution as a whole." A stability process may therefore pass from the original-solution state to the substitute-solution state without immediately entering the no-solution state.

## Section VI　Dynamic Stability

### 6.1　Temporal Structure

\**Definition 6.1*\*〔Protocol time domain〕

Dynamic stability inherits the temporal structure of Field Chapter 1, written 𝕋. For the finite observation intervals used in this text, it is required that:

　(1) 𝕋 have a total order ≤;  
　(2) the duration Dur(s₁,s₂) be defined for any two moments s₁ ≤ s₂ within the protocol;  
　(3) every non-empty failure time set have an infimum in the protocol time domain;  
　(4) T\_obs, T\_rec, h be positive durations.

Duration is written:

　Dur(s₁,s₂) ≥ 0.

This notation does not presuppose that time must be continuous or discrete. Both continuous and discrete time may enter this text from the time type of Field Chapter 1 itself.

### 6.2　Legitimate Successor Trajectories

\**Definition 6.2*\*〔Legitimate successor trajectory〕

A legitimate successor trajectory obtained from F\_t^η by the existing state-update rules of Field Chapter 1 is written γ.

γ has a legitimate time index set 𝕂\_γ ⊆ 𝕋; for each s ∈ 𝕂\_γ there is a corresponding field state F\_s^{η,γ}.

\**Definition 6.3*\*〔Family of all legitimate successor trajectories〕

Under protocol H, the family of all legitimate successor trajectories issuing from F\_t^η and satisfying the successor rules of Field Chapter 1 is written:

　TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H).

TrajLegal does not filter out legitimate trajectories by observation-window coverage or by present decidability.

\**Definition 6.4*\*〔Dynamic adjudication domain for the fixed solution〕

For γ ∈ TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) and a given original solution R₀, define:

　DynDomFix(γ,R₀,H) = 1

if and only if:

　(1) Cover\_γ(T\_obs) = 1;  
　(2) the M values required by the fixed-solution task on J\_obs are all computable;  
　(3) when FailFix\_γ(R₀;H) is non-empty, its infimum exists;  
　(4) all SM conditions required by the dynamic fixed-solution task are satisfied.

If DynDomFix(γ,R₀,H) ≠ 1, then the values of KeepFix, RecoverFix and StableFix for that γ are ⊥.

\**Definition 6.5*\*〔Dynamic adjudication domain for the solution set〕

For γ ∈ TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H), define:

　DynDomSet(γ,H) = 1

if and only if:

　(1) Cover\_γ(T\_obs) = 1;  
　(2) the M values required by the solution-set task on J\_obs are all computable;  
　(3) when FailSet\_γ(H) is non-empty, its infimum exists;  
　(4) all SM conditions required by the dynamic solution-set task are satisfied.

If DynDomSet(γ,H) ≠ 1, then the values of KeepSet, RecoverSet and StableSet for that γ are ⊥.

\**Definition 6.6*\*〔Decidable trajectory family for the fixed solution〕

　TrajDecFix(F\_t^η,R₀,H) = {γ ∈ TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) \| DynDomFix(γ,R₀,H) = 1}.

\**Definition 6.7*\*〔Decidable trajectory family for the solution set〕

　TrajDecSet(F\_t^η,H) = {γ ∈ TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) \| DynDomSet(γ,H) = 1}.

\**Definition 6.8*\*〔Declared trajectory families〕

The fixed-solution task takes:

　∅ ≠ 𝒢\_η^Fix ⊆ TrajDecFix(F\_t^η,R₀,H).

The solution-set task takes:

　∅ ≠ 𝒢\_η^Set ⊆ TrajDecSet(F\_t^η,H).

Trajectory-family stability answers only for the decidable trajectory family declared in each case; guaranteed stability over all legitimate futures requires that all legitimate trajectories of the corresponding task be decidable.

6.3　Time Protocol and Observation-Window Coverage

\**Definition 6.9*\*〔Time protocol〕

　H = (T\_obs,T\_rec,h),

where:

　T\_obs \> 0: the total observation duration;  
　T\_rec \> 0: the longest recovery duration permitted after the first failure boundary;  
　h \> 0: the minimal duration of sustained verification after recovery.

\**Definition 6.10*\*〔Observation-window coverage〕

　Cover\_γ(T\_obs) = 1

if and only if γ covers all protocol time points at a duration from t not exceeding T\_obs.

Formally, let:

　J\_obs = {s ∈ 𝕋 \| t ≤ s ∧ Dur(t,s) ≤ T\_obs}.

Then:

　Cover\_γ(T\_obs) = 1 ⇔ J\_obs ⊆ 𝕂\_γ.

Cover\_γ(T\_obs) is a necessary condition of DynDomFix and of DynDomSet respectively; where coverage is insufficient or undecidable, the corresponding dynamic stability value is ⊥.

### 6.4　Preservation

\**Definition 6.11*\*〔Trajectory preservation of the fixed solution〕

When DynDomFix(γ,R₀,H) = 1:

　KeepFix\_γ(R₀;H) = 1 ⇔ ∀s ∈ J\_obs: R₀ ∈ 𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I).

If DynDomFix(γ,R₀,H) ≠ 1, then:

　KeepFix\_γ(R₀;H) = ⊥.

\**Definition 6.12*\*〔Trajectory preservation of the solution set〕

When DynDomSet(γ,H) = 1:

　KeepSet\_γ(H) = 1 ⇔ ∀s ∈ J\_obs: 𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I) ≠ ∅.

If DynDomSet(γ,H) ≠ 1, then:

　KeepSet\_γ(H) = ⊥.

Preservation of the fixed solution requires one and the same R₀ throughout the observation window; preservation of the solution set requires only that at each moment there exist at least one formal solution. Hence:

　∃R ∀s∈J\_obs \[R∈𝒮\_s\] ⇒ ∀s∈J\_obs ∃R \[R∈𝒮\_s\].

The converse does not follow by quantifier logic. That quantifier structure permits different R to furnish the existential witness at different moments; whether there exists a concrete legitimate instance of relay in bearing must be proved separately from the corresponding field states.

### 6.5　Recovery of the Fixed Solution

\**Definition 6.13*\*〔Failure time set of the fixed solution〕

　FailFix\_γ(R₀;H) = {s ∈ J\_obs \| R₀ ∉ 𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I)}.

If FailFix\_γ ≠ ∅, define the first failure boundary:

　b\_fix = inf FailFix\_γ(R₀;H).

\**Definition 6.14*\*〔Trajectory recovery of the fixed solution〕

When DynDomFix(γ,R₀,H) = 1:

　RecoverFix\_γ(R₀;H) = 1

if and only if:

　FailFix\_γ ≠ ∅,

and there exists t\_r ∈ J\_obs satisfying:

　b\_fix \< t\_r,

　Dur(b\_fix,t\_r) ≤ T\_rec,

　∃s\_h ∈ J\_obs \[t\_r ≤ s\_h ∧ Dur(t\_r,s\_h) ≥ h\],

　∃s\_f ∈ FailFix\_γ: b\_fix ≤ s\_f \< t\_r,

　∀s ∈ J\_obs \[t\_r ≤ s ⇒ R₀ ∈ 𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I)\].

b\_fix is the infimum of the failure time set and does not automatically serve as an actual moment of failure; the actual failure is given by s\_f. s\_h guarantees that after recovery there exists at least a verifiable sustained length h; and the last item guarantees that the qualification thereafter holds continuously throughout the remainder of the observation window. The recovery deadline is computed from the first failure boundary, so a later subsequent failure cannot restart T\_rec.

If DynDomFix(γ,R₀,H) ≠ 1, then RecoverFix\_γ(R₀;H) = ⊥.

The requirement of persistence after recovery makes recovery "reacquiring the qualification after failure and holding it to the end of the observation window," rather than its holding again at some single instant.

### 6.6　Recovery of the Solution Set

\**Definition 6.15*\*〔Collapse time set of the solution set〕

　FailSet\_γ(H) = {s ∈ J\_obs \| 𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I) = ∅}.

If FailSet\_γ ≠ ∅, define the first collapse boundary:

　b\_set = inf FailSet\_γ(H).

\**Definition 6.16*\*〔Trajectory recovery of the solution set〕

When DynDomSet(γ,H) = 1:

　RecoverSet\_γ(H) = 1

if and only if:

　FailSet\_γ ≠ ∅,

and there exists t\_r ∈ J\_obs satisfying:

　b\_set \< t\_r,

　Dur(b\_set,t\_r) ≤ T\_rec,

　∃s\_h ∈ J\_obs \[t\_r ≤ s\_h ∧ Dur(t\_r,s\_h) ≥ h\],

　∃s\_f ∈ FailSet\_γ: b\_set ≤ s\_f \< t\_r,

　∀s ∈ J\_obs \[t\_r ≤ s ⇒ 𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I) ≠ ∅\].

If DynDomSet(γ,H) ≠ 1, then RecoverSet\_γ(H) = ⊥.

Recovery of the fixed solution and recovery of the solution set are two distinct events. If there exists a legitimate trajectory along which, throughout the failure of R₀, some other R′ keeps 𝒮 non-empty, and R₀ subsequently returns, then that trajectory exhibits recovery of the fixed solution without recovery of the solution set.

6.7　Trajectory Stability and Trajectory-Family Stability

\**Definition 6.17*\*〔Fixed-solution stability within the protocol〕

When DynDomFix(γ,R₀,H) = 1:

　StableFix\_γ(R₀;H) ⇔ KeepFix\_γ(R₀;H) ∨ RecoverFix\_γ(R₀;H).

If DynDomFix(γ,R₀,H) ≠ 1, then StableFix\_γ(R₀;H) = ⊥.

\**Definition 6.18*\*〔Solution-set stability within the protocol〕

When DynDomSet(γ,H) = 1:

　StableSet\_γ(H) ⇔ KeepSet\_γ(H) ∨ RecoverSet\_γ(H).

If DynDomSet(γ,H) ≠ 1, then StableSet\_γ(H) = ⊥.

\**Definition 6.19*\*〔Trajectory-family stability of the fixed solution〕

For ∅ ≠ 𝒢\_η^Fix ⊆ TrajDecFix(F\_t^η,R₀,H):

　FamilyStableFix(R₀;η,H,𝒢\_η^Fix) = 1 ⇔ ∀γ ∈ 𝒢\_η^Fix: StableFix\_γ(R₀;H) = 1.

　FamilyStableFix(R₀;η,H,𝒢\_η^Fix) = 0 ⇔ ∃γ ∈ 𝒢\_η^Fix: StableFix\_γ(R₀;H) = 0.

Where the declared trajectory family is empty, or where it cannot be confirmed to be a subset of TrajDecFix, FamilyStableFix = ⊥.

\**Definition 6.20*\*〔Trajectory-family stability of the solution set〕

For ∅ ≠ 𝒢\_η^Set ⊆ TrajDecSet(F\_t^η,H):

　FamilyStableSet(η,H,𝒢\_η^Set) = 1 ⇔ ∀γ ∈ 𝒢\_η^Set: StableSet\_γ(H) = 1.

　FamilyStableSet(η,H,𝒢\_η^Set) = 0 ⇔ ∃γ ∈ 𝒢\_η^Set: StableSet\_γ(H) = 0.

Where the declared trajectory family is empty, or where it cannot be confirmed to be a subset of TrajDecSet, FamilyStableSet = ⊥.

\**Definition 6.21*\*〔Guaranteed fixed-solution stability〕

　GuaranteedStableFix(R₀;η,H) = 1

if and only if:

　TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) ≠ ∅,

　TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) = TrajDecFix(F\_t^η,R₀,H),

and:

　∀γ ∈ TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H): StableFix\_γ(R₀;H) = 1.

　GuaranteedStableFix(R₀;η,H) = 0

if and only if:

　TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) ≠ ∅,

　TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) = TrajDecFix(F\_t^η,R₀,H),

and:

　∃γ ∈ TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H): StableFix\_γ(R₀;H) = 0.

In all other cases:

　GuaranteedStableFix(R₀;η,H) = ⊥.

\**Definition 6.22*\*〔Guaranteed solution-set stability〕

　GuaranteedStableSet(η,H) = 1

if and only if:

　TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) ≠ ∅,

　TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) = TrajDecSet(F\_t^η,H),

and:

　∀γ ∈ TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H): StableSet\_γ(H) = 1.

　GuaranteedStableSet(η,H) = 0

if and only if:

　TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) ≠ ∅,

　TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H) = TrajDecSet(F\_t^η,H),

and:

　∃γ ∈ TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H): StableSet\_γ(H) = 0.

In all other cases:

　GuaranteedStableSet(η,H) = ⊥.

FamilyStable answers only for the declared trajectory family; GuaranteedStable answers for all legitimate successor trajectories. Any legitimate but undecidable trajectory makes the corresponding GuaranteedStable presently ⊥.

### 6.8　Temporal Relations

\**Theorem 6.23*\*〔Persistence of a fixed bearer entails persistence of the solution set〕

For any legitimate trajectory γ and any J ⊆ 𝕂\_γ:

　∃R ∀s ∈ J \[R ∈ 𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I)\] ⇒ ∀s ∈ J ∃R \[R ∈ 𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I)\].

*Proof.*

A fixed R holding for all s furnishes pointwise an existential witness. □

\**Theorem 6.24*\*〔The recovery segment of the fixed solution entails non-emptiness of the solution set〕

If the recovery witness of RecoverFix\_γ(R₀;H) is t\_r, then:

　∀s ∈ J\_obs \[t\_r ≤ s ⇒ 𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I) ≠ ∅\].

*Proof.*

R₀ ∈ 𝒮 throughout that interval. □

## Section VII　Stability Boundaries

### 7.1　Perturbation Paths

Let an admissible state-perturbation path be:

　q ↦ η\_q,

where q belongs to a totally ordered parameter domain Q and η\_q ∈ Δ^adm.

Given the original solution:

　R₀ ∈ 𝒮(F\_t,L,I).

\**Definition 7.1*\*〔Failure parameter set of the fixed solution〕

　A\_fix = {q ∈ Q \| R₀ ∉ 𝒮(F\_t^{η\_q},L,I)}.

\**Definition 7.2*\*〔Failure parameter set of the solution set〕

　A\_set = {q ∈ Q \| 𝒮(F\_t^{η\_q},L,I) = ∅}.

From:

　𝒮 = ∅ ⇒ R₀ ∉ 𝒮,

we have:

　A\_set ⊆ A\_fix.

This shows that the withdrawal of the fixed solution is never later than the collapse of the solution set as a whole; but the two may coincide, or may be separated.

### 7.2　Infimum Boundaries

If A\_fix ≠ ∅ and has an infimum in Q, define:

　q\_fix^∂ = inf A\_fix.

If A\_set ≠ ∅ and has an infimum in Q, define:

　q\_set^∂ = inf A\_set.

From A\_set ⊆ A\_fix:

　q\_fix^∂ ≤ q\_set^∂.

q\_fix^∂ denotes only the infimum of the boundary of the failure set of the original solution; q\_set^∂ denotes the infimum of the boundary of the no-solution set. Whether there is a continuous substitution region between them is still to be adjudicated from the actual parameter states.

### 7.3　Actual First Failure Point

If A\_fix has a least element, define:

　q\_fix^\* = min A\_fix.

If A\_set has a least element, define:

　q\_set^\* = min A\_set.

Only when q\_fix^\* exists is the following defined:

　FirstCoreFail(R₀) = FailCore\_R₀(η\_{q\_fix^\*}).

Infimum and actual failure point are thereby kept apart. Where the boundary is not attained, core failure responsibility is not read off at q\_fix^∂.

### 7.4　Substitute-Solution Parameter Set

\**Definition 7.3*\*〔Substitute-solution parameter set〕

　A\_sub = {q ∈ Q \| R₀ ∉ 𝒮(F\_t^{η\_q},L,I) ∧ 𝒮(F\_t^{η\_q},L,I) ≠ ∅}.

Hence:

　A\_fix = A\_sub ∪ A\_set,

and:

　A\_sub ∩ A\_set = ∅.

A\_sub gives the precise parameter domain in which the original bearer has already failed while a formal solution still exists. It is the direct manifestation, along the perturbation path, of redundancy among solutions.

7.5　Analytic Boundaries and Solved Boundaries

\**Definition 7.4*\*〔Analytic boundary〕

If some admissible perturbation subclass possesses a proved monotone relation or an explicit threshold, so that the condition of qualification transition can be obtained directly from that relation, this is called an analytic boundary.

\**Definition 7.5*\*〔Solved boundary〕

If a perturbation simultaneously alters evaluations, witness fibres, generation chains or realization structures, and there is no proved monotone relation, then M is run state by state, and the boundary is determined by K\_fix, K\_set, A\_fix, A\_set.

A stability boundary therefore need not always be a single numerical threshold. Only where the concrete perturbation parameter possesses sufficient order and monotone structure is it possible to compress a set boundary into a directly computable critical value.

## Section VIII　Comparison of Declared Parameters

This section compares two M instances with different declared parameters. It does not belong to the preservation or recovery of one and the same state perturbation η, but serves to study the response of formal qualifications to changes in the declared domain.

### 8.1　Pure I Extension

\**Definition 8.1*\*〔Pure I extension〕

Compare:

　(F\_t,L,I₁,R)

with:

　(F\_t,L,I₂,R).

If:

　I₁ ⊆ I₂,

and for every i ∈ I₁ the RoleEval, ReplayEval, Adm, ReplayAdm, D, Inst, EvalMode(L,i) and their witness qualifications remain the same before and after the extension, only the obligations brought by I₂ ∖ I₁ being added, then I₁ → I₂ is called a pure I extension.

\**Monotonicity of the guard domain.*\*　From I₁ ⊆ I₂ together with the definition of I\_cov (3.15′ of M: i ∈ I\_cov ⇔ Trig(i) ≠ ∅, independently of the size of I and of success or failure of realization) we obtain directly:

　I\_cov(L,I₁) ⊆ I\_cov(L,I₂).

The three back-inferences and the preservation under extension below all rest upon this; the quantification of ν in each remains the Type-sensitive reading of 4.0(b), and pure extension guarantees that for every i ∈ I\_cov(L,I₁) the EvalMode and the conditional measure are unaltered by the extension.

\**Theorem 8.2*\*〔Coverage back-inference for CR/CNI once initiated〕

Under a pure I extension, if I\_cov(L,I₁) ≠ ∅ and the role domain of each of its i is non-empty (Adm(L,i) ≠ ∅ in set mode; RoleMass(L,i) \> 0 in measure mode), then:

　CarrierRealizes^M(I₂) ⇒ CarrierRealizes^M(I₁).

　CarrierNonInstant^M(I₂) ⇒ CarrierNonInstant^M(I₁).

*Proof.*

By monotonicity of the guard domain, the coverage obligations of I₂ contain, i by i over I\_cov(L,I₁), the original obligations of I₁; pure extension guarantees that the structure of the old obligations and the EvalMode are unchanged, so the value of ∀^{Role}\_{L,i} at each i is unaltered by the extension; and the non-emptiness initiation gate of the antecedent guarantees that the smaller domain does not fall into a vacuous universal over an empty domain. □

CR/CNI therefore have a two-layer structure: first the initiation qualification of I\_cov being non-empty together with non-emptiness of the role domain, then the coverage qualification over all obligation points. Only once initiation already holds does the coverage back-inference hold — otherwise the smaller domain might acquire the criterion by a vacuous universal, and the back-inference would be without content.

\**Theorem 8.3*\*〔Coverage back-inference for the Replay series〕

Under a pure I extension, if I\_cov(L,I₁) ≠ ∅ and the re-entry domain of each of its i is non-empty (ReplayAdm(L,i) ≠ ∅ in set mode; ReplayMass(L,i) \> 0 in measure mode), then:

　StableReplay^M(I₂) ⇒ StableReplay^M(I₁).

　ΩSupport^M(I₂) ⇒ ΩSupport^M(I₁).

　Bind^M(I₂) ⇒ Bind^M(I₁).

*Proof.*

By monotonicity of the guard domain, the coverage obligations of ReplayReady and of the three re-entry criteria on I₂ contain, i by i over I\_cov(L,I₁), the original obligations of I₁; pure extension guarantees that the ReplayAdm, the EvalMode and the conditional measure of each i are unaltered, so the value of ∀^{Replay}\_{L,i} at each i is unaltered by the extension; the non-emptiness initiation gate of the antecedent is as in 8.2. □

\**Theorem 8.4*\*〔Preservation of DynamicRole under extension〕

Under a pure I extension:

　DynamicRole^M(I₁) ⇒ DynamicRole^M(I₂).

*Proof.*

Let i₀ ∈ I\_cov(L,I₁) be such that ∃^{Role}\_{L,i₀} takes the value 1. By monotonicity of the guard domain, i₀ ∈ I\_cov(L,I₂); and by pure extension the Adm, EvalMode and conditional measure of i₀ are unaltered, so that existential quantifier still takes the value 1 at i₀ under I₂ (in set mode by the same legitimate evaluation, in measure mode by the same positive-measure event). By Definition 3.38 we obtain DynamicRole^M(I₂) = 1. □

The existential quantifier of DynamicRole and the coverage quantifiers of CR/CNI and Replay/Ω/Bind therefore run in different directions. This quantifier difference alone does not yet yield that the overall Qual\_M must be non-monotone in both directions; but it makes clear that enlarging the domain of application is not simply "adding one more condition": different qualifications bear different logical burdens in the face of extension.

### 8.2　Local Ω

\**Theorem 8.5*\*〔Preservation of Ω under fixed ReqSeg〕

Fix ρ, d, ν, ReqSeg, with ReqSeg ≠ ∅.

If originally ΩOK = 1, and after the change one still has:

　∀e ∈ ReqSeg: Ω(e) \> 0,

then that witness does not fail on account of the Ω item.

If:

　∃e ∈ ReqSeg: Ω(e) ≤ 0,

then ΩOK = 0 for that witness.

This threshold is first of all a witness-level boundary. After some ΩWit fails, so long as there are still other ΩWit at the same obligation point (i,ν), ΩSupport^M may still be preserved; only when all Ω witnesses at that obligation point disappear together does one enter predicate-level destabilization.

### 8.3　Fixed Reach Paths

\**Theorem 8.6*\*〔Preservation of a fixed Reach path〕

If E, Bear and the original Reach path are fixed, and the change only adds legitimate structural relations without deleting the original path, then the original Reach witness continues to hold.

This conclusion applies only to the fixed path itself. If Bear, E, X^Replay or RolePath change at the same time, M must be run afresh.

### 8.4　Equivalent Formulations of Ante

\**Theorem 8.7*\*〔Invariance under equivalent formulations of Ante〕

Under the antecedents of Proposition 3.1 of M, a change in the equivalent formulation of Ante(d) does not alter the Bear of a fixed ρ.

Changes of this kind belong to invariants of representation and do not constitute structural destabilization of Bear.

### 8.5　Monotone Relations Not Yet Closed

For the following changes no general monotone conclusion is presently laid down:

　alteration of Anchor;

　overall enlargement of Existing\_t, Estab\_t or the relevant base;

　alteration of Cov.

These changes enter Bear, RolePath, ReqSeg, the evaluation domains or the realization relations, and there are at present no sufficient theorems to compress them into a uniform "the larger the more stable" or "the larger the less stable." The concrete qualifications are recomputed by M.

## Section IX　Running Procedures

9.1　Instantaneous Fixed-Solution Stability

Given an original solution R₀ and η ∈ Δ:

　1\. Adjudicate AdmPert(η);  
　2\. Construct or read F\_t^η;  
　3\. Run M in full;  
　4\. Adjudicate R₀ ∈ 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I);  
　5\. If it holds, enter the original-solution state;  
　6\. If it does not, obtain FailCore\_R₀(η);  
　7\. Trace the direct mechanism of failure along the corresponding witness fibres and definitional chains.

### 9.2　Instantaneous Solution-Set Stability

After the fixed-solution adjudication, solve afresh for:

　𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I).

If:

　R₀ ∉ 𝒮 ∧ 𝒮 ≠ ∅,

enter the substitute-solution state.

If:

　𝒮 = ∅,

enter the no-solution state, and prove global collapse by single-cause blockade or by multi-cause covering.

### 9.3　Dynamic Stability

Given η and H:

　1\. Obtain TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H);  
　2\. For the fixed-solution task adjudicate DynDomFix(γ,R₀,H) for each legitimate trajectory, forming TrajDecFix; for the solution-set task adjudicate DynDomSet(γ,H) for each legitimate trajectory, forming TrajDecSet;  
　3\. For the fixed-solution task designate 𝒢\_η^Fix ⊆ TrajDecFix; for the solution-set task designate 𝒢\_η^Set ⊆ TrajDecSet;  
　4\. For each included trajectory run M moment by moment on J\_obs, obtaining 𝒮\_s;  
　5\. For the fixed-solution task obtain FailFix\_γ and b\_fix;  
　6\. For the solution-set task obtain FailSet\_γ and b\_set;  
　7\. Where the failure set is empty, adjudicate Keep;  
　8\. Where the failure set is non-empty, look for a recovery moment t\_r within T\_rec after the first failure boundary;  
　9\. Confirm that an actual failure or collapse exists before recovery;  
　10\. Confirm that after recovery it persists to the end of the observation window, with a persistence duration not less than h;  
　11\. Summarize FamilyStableFix or FamilyStableSet respectively;  
　12\. If TrajLegal = TrajDecFix or TrajLegal = TrajDecSet, adjudicate GuaranteedStable by the corresponding three-valued table; otherwise the corresponding GuaranteedStable = ⊥.

## Section X　Conditions of Solvability

\**SM1*\*　Δ and AdmPert can be effectively characterized.

\**SM2*\*　Each F\_t^η can be handed over to M1–M6 for running.

\**SM3*\*　The time domain 𝕋 used in dynamic stability satisfies the order, duration and infimum conditions of Section VI.

\**SM4*\*　Every γ included in the dynamic adjudication satisfies Cover\_γ(T\_obs) = 1, and the field states within J\_obs are computable or effectively describable.

\**SM5*\*　The ranges of TrajLegal(F\_t^η,H), TrajDecFix(F\_t^η,R₀,H), TrajDecSet(F\_t^η,H) and the actually declared trajectory families 𝒢\_η^Fix, 𝒢\_η^Set can be effectively characterized; to adjudicate GuaranteedStableFix one must be able to confirm TrajLegal = TrajDecFix; to adjudicate GuaranteedStableSet one must be able to confirm TrajLegal = TrajDecSet.

\**SM6*\*　H = (T\_obs,T\_rec,h) is given before adjudication, and all three are positive durations.

Where any condition required by the present task is unsatisfied, that instance is undecidable.

## Section XI　Breakdown Items

11.1　Spurious preservation of the fixed solution

It is claimed that η ∈ K\_fix(R₀), yet after running M in full:

　R₀ ∉ 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I).

11.2　Spurious preservation of the solution set

It is claimed that η ∈ K\_set, yet:

　𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I) = ∅.

11.3　A single witness passing itself off as a qualification

Some Π, ρ or W exists, yet the complete quantifier condition of the corresponding predicate is not satisfied.

11.4　A single office passing itself off as JointReal

Each d severally possesses a Real, yet there is no JointReal.

11.5　Death of the original witness passing itself off as failure of the predicate

The original witness has disappeared, yet the corresponding witness fibre is still non-empty.

### 11.6　Cross-realization chain-witnessing

ΩWit and Scheme belong to different Π, yet Bind^M is claimed to hold.

11.7　Spurious proof of instantaneous bearing on re-entry

Some Π has:

　Bear ∩ Inst(i,ν) ≠ ∅,

yet that Π is claimed to satisfy ReplayWit.

11.8　Spurious proof of the non-instantaneous qualification

No joint realization satisfying:

　Q(Π) ⊆ Estab\_t

can be found, yet CarrierNonInstant^M is claimed to hold.

11.9　Ω elsewhere passing itself off as ΩSupport

The necessary bearing is unreachable, or ΩOK fails on ReqSeg, yet ΩSupport^M is claimed to hold on the ground that Ω \> 0 exists elsewhere in R.

11.10　A local threshold passing itself off as a global boundary

A single ΩWit or a single ρ fails, and it is thereupon claimed that R withdraws from 𝒮 or that 𝒮 = ∅.

11.11　Failure of the original solution passing itself off as global collapse

Only:

　R₀ ∉ 𝒮

is proved, and it is thereupon claimed that:

　𝒮 = ∅.

11.12　Splicing qualifications across time

CC, CNI, Dyn and Bnd hold at different times respectively, yet never hold together at one and the same moment, and it is claimed that R ∈ 𝒮.

11.13　A substitute solution passing itself off as recovery of the fixed solution

R₀ has not re-entered 𝒮, only some R′ ≠ R₀ has become a solution, yet RecoverFix = 1 is claimed.

11.14　Spurious recovery by change of declaration

The qualification holds again after altering I, Rel(L) or other declared content of the original instance, yet the original stability instance is claimed to have recovered.

### 11.15　Resetting T\_rec by a later failure

An early failure has already exceeded T\_rec, and a later failure is then taken instead in order to recompute the recovery deadline.

11.16　Vacuous truth by trajectory truncation

Cover\_γ(T\_obs) ≠ 1, yet the universal condition is checked only at the time points already available and stability is claimed.

11.17　Trajectory-family stability passing itself off as guaranteed stability

Only FamilyStable is proved, or there remain within TrajLegal legitimate trajectories not belonging to the corresponding TrajDecFix / TrajDecSet, yet GuaranteedStable is claimed.

11.18　Reachability passing itself off as stability

There exists only one legitimate trajectory capable of recovery, yet the stability quantifier of the declared trajectory family is not satisfied, and trajectory-family stability or guaranteed stability is claimed.

11.19　Undecidability passing itself off as destabilization

M1–M6 or SM1–SM6 are unsatisfied, and stability is thereupon adjudicated 0.

## Section XIII　Readback

### 13.1　Formal Layer

Fixed-solution stability yields directly:

　R₀ ∈ 𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I)

or, along a trajectory:

　R₀ ∈ 𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I).

Solution-set stability yields directly:

　𝒮(F\_t^η,L,I) ≠ ∅

or, along a trajectory:

　𝒮(F\_s^{η,γ},L,I) ≠ ∅.

These two classes of conclusion must be kept distinct. The former retains one and the same R; the latter retains only that there exists some R.

### 13.2　Structural Layer

　R ∈ 𝒮(F\_t,L,I) ⇒ Established\_F,t(R) ∧ Govern\_F,t(L;R,I).

　𝒮(F\_t,L,I) ≠ ∅ ⇒ ∃R \[Established\_F,t(R) ∧ Govern\_F,t(L;R,I)\].

Fixed-solution stability reads back the Established and Govern of a fixed R; solution-set stability reads back only that there exists some bearing organization. The two retain different quantifier strengths at the structural layer.

### 13.3　Law Layer

That sufficient direction has been established by *The Necessity Form of Law* (sealed), with principal sufficient direction Status = Established.
