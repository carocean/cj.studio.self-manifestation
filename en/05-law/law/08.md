# The Boundary Form of Law

## Section I　Purpose and Form-Kind

This text is a Limit Form (*Canon Chapter 1*, Part Two, Chapter Four, first group, "auxiliary forms of boundary discrimination"; Part Four, Chapter One, the norms of the Limit Form: after a principal form has given a structure, to determine the boundary of that structure's standing). The "limit" of this text is the boundary of standing, within a predeclared comparison domain, of the structure given by *The General Form of Law*; the direction and conditions of the limit are declared by the comparison protocol Γ, and are not asymptotic in the sense of tending to infinity. This text does not take the form of a Grading Form (it gives no hierarchy or comparison relation), nor of a Dissolution Form (it dissolves no apparent contradiction), nor of a Stability Form: it does not study preservation, substitution and recovery as processes of stability, nor seek their parameters of occurrence, their widths of stability, or their positions of recovery (those three belong to *The Stability Form of Law*); but in adjudicating whether Law has really crossed a boundary, it is permitted to read the structural fact "whether a substitute witness exists at the right-hand end" (Theorems 4.3, 4.4, 7.1) — reading a set-level fact and studying its process are two different things.

The single central question of this text:

　　When, in a predeclared comparison, does one and the same law pass from standing to not standing, or conversely?

That is: the identity of the candidate law L and of its relational content Rel(L) is fixed (otherwise there is no boundary of "one and the same law"); the comparison protocol Γ predeclares, among F, t, I and the remaining quantities of comparison, which are fixed and which are permitted to vary; and one studies the position and structural conditions at which the truth-value crossing

　　Law\_Γ(X⁻) ≠ Law\_Γ(X⁺)

occurs for one and the same L between the several legitimate comparison states.

This text does not study how boundaries are formed. Why a law is generated, why it is replaced, why laws compete with one another, how Ω grows or decays with time, how a law migrates, splits or merges — all belong to the future *Law Dynamics*, and this text touches none of them. Neither does this text carry out parameter scans, threshold solving, widths of stability or numerical algorithms (these belonging to *The Stability Form of Law*), nor the enumeration of candidates, the measurement of operability, or the PASS / FAIL / ⊥ execution procedure (these belonging to *The Determination Form of Law*).

This text adds no ontological predicate, no field quantity, and no bridge awaiting verification.

## Section II　Core Theses

A boundary is a \**crossing of the truth value of Law*\*, and not a crossing of any lower-layer quantity.

Three negative propositions constitute the discipline of this text:

　Structural failure is not automatically a Law boundary — that some R loses its qualification says only that that witness has withdrawn; Law may go on being borne by another witness;  
　Formal failure is not automatically a Law boundary — the formal solution set 𝒮 becoming empty must pass through the corresponding conditions of representation before it turns into the falsity of Law; the failure of Bind^M belongs to the layer of formal coupling alone;  
　A critical parameter is not automatically a Law boundary — the fixed-witness boundary q\_fix^∂ defined in *The Stability Form of Law* is a witness-level event, and must be separately proved to be exactly the extinction of the last witness before it can be upgraded.

Positively there are three layers of criteria: the structural-witness layer (Section IV), the formal-solution layer (Section V), and the domain-of-application layer (Section VI). Each of the three has its own crossing conditions and its own condition bundle.

One particular result of this text is: \**asymmetry of the boundary endpoints*\* — to certify one concrete boundary, one need not first establish Law ⇔ 𝒮 ≠ ∅ over the whole comparison domain; it suffices that the positive end and the negative end each bear their own distinct burden of proof (Theorem 5.2).

## Section III　Basic Objects

Marking: 〔S〕 an object of the structural layer; 〔aux〕 a purely formal auxiliary object, with no ontological counterpart, not read back.

### 3.1　The Comparison Protocol

\**Definition 3.1*\*〔aux〕(comparison protocol)　Γ is the predeclared comparison protocol of one boundary investigation, prescribing:

　(i) the fixed quantities — those held unchanged throughout the comparison (containing at least L and Rel(L): sameness of law is the premise of the comparison);  
　(ii) the variable quantities — those permitted to vary, and their legitimate modes of variation;  
　(iii) the comparison resolution — at what precision two comparison states count as separable;  
　(iv) the direction of comparison — along what order or what parameter the comparison runs;  
　(v) identity recognition — how, should the field structure change, "the same bearing organization" is to be recognized.

Γ must be declared in full before the comparison begins, and may not be modified after the fact on account of a counterexample. Write X for a comparison state under Γ (its content comprising F, t, I and the remaining variations permitted by Γ).

\**Definition 3.2*\*〔aux〕(carrier transport)　τ\_Γ is the carrier transport given by item (v) of Γ: for an organization R in X⁻, τ\_Γ(R) is its corresponding organization in X⁺; if Γ adjudicates that there is no correspondent, then τ\_Γ(R) does not exist. τ\_Γ is an 〔aux〕 object bearing the bookkeeping of identity alone; it is not a new ontological relation, and it does not presuppose the preservation of structure.

### 3.2　Boundary Objects

\**Definition 3.3*\*〔aux〕(truth-value crossing)　Cross\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺) ⇔ Law\_Γ(X⁻) ≠ Law\_Γ(X⁺).

\**Definition 3.4**〔aux〕(adjacency)　Adjacent\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺) ⇔ at the comparison resolution declared by Γ, X⁻ and X⁺ are not further separable — that is, the resolution of Γ does not permit a third separable comparison state to be inserted between them. This is a notion **relative to resolution*\*: upon continuously variable quantities it is not required that a covering relation with "no intermediate element" exist, only that no further subdivision be possible at the declared precision.

\**Definition 3.5*\*〔aux〕(boundary pair)　Boundary\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺) ⇔ Adjacent\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺) ∧ Cross\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺).

　Exit　Boundary⁻\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺) ⇔ Boundary\_Γ ∧ Law(X⁻)=1 ∧ Law(X⁺)=0.  
　Entry　Boundary⁺\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺) ⇔ Boundary\_Γ ∧ Law(X⁻)=0 ∧ Law(X⁺)=1.

The difference between Cross and Boundary: the former says only that the truth values differ, the latter requires in addition that the location have been fixed to within the limit of separability. Whatever is called a "located boundary" must be the latter.

### 3.3　The Structural Witness Set

\**Definition 3.6*\*〔S-derived〕(structural witness set)　For a comparison state X:

　　𝒲\_S(X) = { R \| Established\_X(R) ∧ Govern\_X(L;R,I) },  
　　Govern\_X(L;R,I) ⇔ DynamicRole\_X(L;R,I) ∧ ΩSupport\_X(L;R,I) (*The General Form of Law*).

𝒲\_S is constituted by the existing structural components of *The General Form of Law* and is not a new object.

### 3.4　The Failure Spectrum

\**Definition 3.7*\*〔aux〕(witness failure spectrum)　For R ∈ 𝒲\_S(X⁻) with τ\_Γ(R) existing:

　　Fail\_S(R) = { A ∈ {E, D, Ω} \| A takes the value 0 on τ\_Γ(R) in X⁺ },

where E = Established, D = DynamicRole, Ω = ΩSupport. If τ\_Γ(R) does not exist, write Fail\_S(R) = ⟂τ (loss of correspondence), listed separately and not entering {E,D,Ω}.

The failure spectrum records only \**which qualifications have been voided**, and not their causes; causes belong to *Law Dynamics\*. Neither does this text further decompose Established into failures of CurrentCarrier / CarrierRealizes / CarrierNonInstant — that is upstream structural diagnosis, and not a necessary office of the Law boundary.

\**Definition 3.8*\*〔aux〕(minimal failure cover)　Let 𝒲\_S(X⁻) ≠ ∅ while 𝒲\_S(X⁺) = ∅. First divide the old witnesses into two classes:

　　𝒲^τ = { R ∈ 𝒲\_S(X⁻) \| τ\_Γ(R) does not exist }　(loss of carrier correspondence)  
　　𝒲^q = 𝒲\_S(X⁻) ∖ 𝒲^τ　　　　　　　　　　　　(correspondent existing, qualification failing)

MinFailCover treats the qualification-failure type only: call C ⊆ {E,D,Ω} a \**failure cover*\* if and only if, for every R ∈ 𝒲^q, Fail\_S(R) ∩ C ≠ ∅. MinFailCover\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺) is the totality of the minimal such covers; when 𝒲^q = ∅ the expression has no content (it is undefined), the withdrawal then being borne entirely by loss of carrier correspondence.

Loss of carrier correspondence takes no part in the minimal cover over E, D, Ω, and may not be rewritten as Established = 0: "there is no traceable same correspondent" and "the correspondent exists but is not Established" are not the same proposition, and τ\_Γ is an 〔aux〕 bookkeeping object (3.2), not a fourth ontological qualification.

### 3.5　The Boundary Frontier

\**Definition 3.9*\*〔aux〕(boundary frontier)　Let the direction of comparison of Γ be given by an order ≺.

　　ℬ⁻\_Γ = { (X,Y) \| X ≺ Y ∧ Boundary⁻\_Γ(X,Y) },  
　　ℬ⁺\_Γ = { (X,Y) \| X ≺ Y ∧ Boundary⁺\_Γ(X,Y) },  
　　ℬ\_Γ = ℬ⁻\_Γ ∪ ℬ⁺\_Γ.

That is, the elements of the frontier are throughout \**located boundary pairs*\* (containing Adjacent\_Γ, Definition 3.5). An unlocated crossing pair belongs only to Cross\_Γ and does not enter the frontier. Write further the set of crossing pairs 𝒞\_Γ = { (X,Y) \| X ≺ Y ∧ Cross\_Γ(X,Y) }; then ℬ\_Γ ⊆ 𝒞\_Γ, generally without equality.

The general Law boundary is this ℬ\_Γ, a \**set of boundary pairs*\* (a frontier), not a unique boundary point. This text develops no general topological theory of boundaries.

## Section IV　Structural Boundary Theorems

4.1　The Positive Direction: a Witness Existing, the Law Stands

\**Theorem 4.1 (sufficiency of a witness)*\*　𝒲\_S(X) ≠ ∅ ⇒ Law\_Γ(X) = 1.

*Proof.* By the law necessity theorem of *The Necessity Form of Law*: ∃R\[Established ∧ Govern\] ⇒ Law, whose principal sufficient direction has Status = Established (carrying neither the C nor the H antecedents of this text or of *The General Form of Law*). 𝒲\_S(X) ≠ ∅ is precisely that existential statement. □

\**Corollary 4.1.1*\*　Law\_Γ(X) = 0 ⇒ 𝒲\_S(X) = ∅. (The contrapositive of 4.1, of the same strength.)

4.2　The Negative Direction: Only upon Witness Extinction May a Law Be Adjudicated Not to Stand

\**Theorem 4.2 (necessity of a witness, Conditional)*\*　Under C0 ∧ C2 ∧ H1 ∧ H2 ∧ H3: 𝒲\_S(X) = ∅ ⇒ Law\_Γ(X) = 0.

*Proof.* The necessary direction of *The General Form of Law*: under those five conditions, Law ⇒ ∃R\[Established ∧ Govern\]. The contrapositive gives the result. □

The conditions of the two directions are asymmetric: the positive (4.1) is unconditional, the negative (4.2) carries all the Conditional antecedents of *The General Form of Law*. This asymmetry runs through the whole text and is the structural-layer source of the endpoint asymmetry of Section V.

4.3　The Failure of a Single Witness Is Not Enough

\**Theorem 4.3 (a bearer boundary does not entail a Law boundary)*\*　Let R ∈ 𝒲\_S(X⁻), while τ\_Γ(R) does not exist or τ\_Γ(R) ∉ 𝒲\_S(X⁺). This says only that the witness R has withdrawn. If ∃R′ ∈ 𝒲\_S(X⁺), then Law(X⁺) = 1 (Theorem 4.1) and Cross\_Γ does not hold.

*Proof.* The existential quantifier of 4.1 requires some one witness only; the withdrawal of R does not affect the qualification of R′. □

　　Hence: a bearer boundary ⇏ a Law boundary.

This is the point most often misused in this text: the failure of the Established, DynamicRole or ΩSupport of some R may not be taken as ground for certifying a Law boundary directly.

### 4.4　Decomposition of Withdrawal

\**Theorem 4.4 (decomposition of structural-witness withdrawal)*\*　Let 𝒲\_S(X⁻) ≠ ∅ and 𝒲\_S(X⁺) = ∅ (call this a structural-witness collapse). The necessary and sufficient decomposition of that collapse is that the following two hold together:

　(a) \**all original witnesses withdraw*\*　∀R ∈ 𝒲\_S(X⁻): τ\_Γ(R) does not exist ∨ τ\_Γ(R) ∉ 𝒲\_S(X⁺);  
　(b) \**no substitute witness*\*　there exists no R′ ∈ 𝒲\_S(X⁺) outside the image τ\_Γ(𝒲\_S(X⁻)) — that is, no newly qualified witness takes its place at the right-hand end.

*Proof.* ⇐: by (a) no image of an original witness lies in 𝒲\_S(X⁺), and by (b) there is no new witness outside the image, hence 𝒲\_S(X⁺) = ∅. ⇒: if (a) fails then some image still lies within 𝒲\_S(X⁺); if (b) fails there is a witness outside the image; either makes 𝒲\_S(X⁺) ≠ ∅. □

　　(a) ∧ (b) together are called \**witness extinction*\*. (a) alone is not enough — which is the other side of Theorem 4.3.

　　\**Upgrade conditions*\*　A structural-witness collapse is by itself only a set-theoretic fact and is not yet Boundary⁻. To be upgraded into Boundary⁻\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺), two things are further required: first, Law(X⁺) = 0 — obtained from 𝒲\_S(X⁺) = ∅ by Theorem 4.2, carrying C0 ∧ C2 ∧ H1 ∧ H2 ∧ H3 (the left end, Law(X⁻) = 1, being obtained unconditionally by Theorem 4.1); second, Adjacent\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺) — given by the resolution of Γ. Only with both is Boundary⁻ obtained. The present theorem itself carries no upstream condition (8.4).

### 4.5　Types of Boundary

\**Definition 4.5*\*〔aux〕(types of structural boundary)　Let witness extinction have occurred. First divide by the two classes of 3.8:

　𝒲^q = ∅ and 𝒲^τ ≠ ∅　　\**correspondence-loss*\* — the carrier correspondence of all the old witnesses has vanished under Γ, and there is no qualification failure to be classified;  
　𝒲^q ≠ ∅ and 𝒲^τ = ∅　　\**qualification-loss*\* — typed by the minimal cover below;  
　both non-empty　　　　\**correspondence-loss + qualification-loss mixed*\* — both sides must be reported together.

In the case 𝒲^q ≠ ∅, take C ∈ MinFailCover\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺):

　C = {E}　　\**Established-critical*\* — the last batch of witnesses were all voided in their carrier qualification;  
　C = {D}　　\**DynamicRole-critical*\* — all voided in their real dynamical bearing;  
　C = {Ω}　　\**ΩSupport-critical*\* — all voided in their stable recurrence support;  
　\|C\| ≥ 2　　\**mixed boundary*\* — several items striking together are required to cover all the old witnesses;  
　\|MinFailCover\| ≥ 2　\**non-unique mechanism*\* — one and the same withdrawal admits description by different minimal mechanisms, the boundary mechanism not being unique.

The types describe only \**upon which qualifications the voiding occurred*\*, and not its causes.

### 4.6　The Standing of Bind

\**Proposition 4.6 (Bind^M does not enter the types of structural boundary)*\*　At the S layer Govern ⇔ DynamicRole ∧ ΩSupport, with no Bind component; Bind^M is an 〔aux〕 formal coupling constraint of *The Mathematical Form of Law*. Hence Bind^M: 1 → 0 may be called only a \**failure of formal coupling*\*, and may not be listed as a kind of ontological boundary mechanism of Law.

　　For a failure of formal coupling to be upgraded into a Law boundary, it must pass through: the failure of Bind^M making 𝒮 = ∅ (the formal-solution layer, Section V), and the negative-end condition bundle H⁻\_M holding; only then is Law = 0 obtained.

Should the upstream rewrite that proof so as to alter the order of the three interrogations or the conditions they use, this lemma must be re-checked in synchrony (Breakdown K-B10).

### 5.3　The Theorem of Endpoint Asymmetry

\**Theorem 5.2 (asymmetry of the boundary endpoints)*\*　To certify one concrete formal exit boundary, one need not first establish Law ⇔ 𝒮 ≠ ∅ over the whole comparison domain. If

　　𝒮(X⁻) ≠ ∅ ∧ 𝒮(X⁺) = ∅,

then it suffices that H⁺\_M hold at X⁻ and H⁻\_M hold at X⁺, to obtain

　　Law\_Γ(X⁻) = 1 ∧ Law\_Γ(X⁺) = 0,

and hence Cross\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺); and if further Adjacent\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺), then Boundary⁻\_Γ(X⁻,X⁺).

*Proof.* Apply one branch of Theorem 5.1 at each end: (i) at the left end, (ii) at the right. The two branches require different condition bundles, and each is required only upon its own comparison state. □

　　Symmetrically, an entry boundary requires only that H⁻\_M hold at X⁻ and H⁺\_M at X⁺.

　　The significance of this theorem: the certification of a boundary is a \**local obligation endpoint by endpoint*\*, not a global obligation of equivalence over the whole domain. The global equivalence Law ⇔ 𝒮 ≠ ∅ would require H⁺\_M ∧ H⁻\_M to hold over the whole domain, at far higher cost; a single boundary uses only one bundle at each of two endpoints.

### 5.4　The ⟂ Discipline

　　⟂ ≠ 0.

A ⟂ arising in adjudication or in solving (M1–M6 of *The Mathematical Form of Law* being unsatisfied; OperableDo of *The Determination Form of Law* being insufficient, or the protocol being undecidable) does not constitute the falsity of Law:

　(i) an adjudicative output of ⟂ may not be recorded as Law = 0 for that comparison state;  
　(ii) if undecided candidates remain in the search, "no PASS found" may not be reported as a proof that 𝒮 = ∅;  
　(iii) once 𝒮 = ∅ has really been obtained by complete formal proof, and H⁻\_M holds, Law = 0 may still be inferred by Theorem 5.1(ii).

This text makes these three declarations only. The characterization of adjudicative capacity and the formalization of search states belong to *The Determination Form of Law*, and are not newly fabricated here.

## Section VI　The Boundary of the Domain of Application

This section fixes F, t, L and the remaining quantities of Γ, letting only the domain of application I vary. By *The General Form of Law* and *The Mathematical Form of Law*: DynamicRole has existential polarity, while CarrierRealizes, CarrierNonInstant, StableReplay and ΩSupport have coverage polarity, the coverage domain being the applicability-coverage guard domain I\_cov(L,I) (3.15′ of *The Mathematical Form of Law*); whether I\_cov grows as I grows depends upon whether Rel(L) predeclares an office for the new conditions.

Write 𝔍\_Law = { I \| Law\_Γ(F,t,L,I) = 1 }.

### 6.1　Non-Monotone in Both Directions

\**Theorem 6.1 (upward closure not guaranteed)*\*　There exist I ⊊ J with Law(I) = 1 and Law(J) = 0.

*Construction.* Take J = I ∪ {i\_new}, where Rel(L) predeclares an office for i\_new (so that i\_new ∈ I\_cov(L,J)) while R has at i\_new no reachable stable structure bearing Rel(L). Then the coverage obligation of ΩSupport fails at i\_new (the fifth case of "not enough" in *The General Form of Law*), and Govern(J) = 0; if there is no substitute witness, 𝒲\_S(J) = ∅, and under C0 ∧ C2 ∧ H1 ∧ H2 ∧ H3, Law(J) = 0. Whereas at I the coverage obligation does not include i\_new, 𝒲\_S(I) ∋ R, and Law(I) = 1. □

　　Mechanism: extending the domain adds new obligations to the predicates of coverage polarity.

\**Theorem 6.2 (downward closure not guaranteed)*\*　There exist I ⊊ J with Law(I) = 0 and Law(J) = 1.

*Construction.* Suppose R satisfies all the coverage obligations of J, while its unique existential witness for DynamicRole falls at i\_\* ∈ J ∖ I. Take I = J ∖ {i\_\*}: the coverage obligations upon I are only diminished, never increased, and still hold; but the existential quantifier of DynamicRole has no witness available upon I\_cov(L,I), so DynamicRole(I) = 0 and Govern(I) = 0; if there is no substitute witness then Law(I) = 0 (under the same conditions as above). Whereas Law(J) = 1 by Theorem 4.1. □

　　Mechanism: contracting the domain may delete the unique existential witness.

　　The two mechanisms run in opposite directions: coverage polarity makes \**extension*\* dangerous, existential polarity makes \**contraction*\* dangerous. Hence 𝔍\_Law is in general neither upward nor downward closed.

6.2　The Domain of Application May Have Holes

\**Theorem 6.3 (domain hole)*\*　There exist I₁ ⊊ I₂ ⊊ I₃ with Law(I₁) = 1, Law(I₂) = 0, Law(I₃) = 1.

*Construction (single-step chain).* Take I₁ = {a}, I₂ = {a,b}, I₃ = {a,b,c}, all three lying within the legitimate domain family 𝔄\_I declared by Γ; and let Rel(L) predeclare offices for a, b and c alike (so that I\_cov(L,I) = I for all three). Take two organizations:

　R\_A　its coverage qualification extends to a only, failing coverage at b; its existential witness for DynamicRole falls at a. Hence R\_A ∈ 𝒲\_S(I₁), while R\_A ∉ 𝒲\_S(I₂) and ∉ 𝒲\_S(I₃).  
　R\_B　its coverage qualification extends to the whole of a, b, c; its unique existential witness for DynamicRole falls at c. Hence R\_B ∈ 𝒲\_S(I₃); upon I₂ its coverage suffices, yet within I\_cov(L,I₂) = {a,b} there is no existential witness, so DynamicRole(I₂) = 0 and R\_B ∉ 𝒲\_S(I₂).

There is no third witness. Then Law(I₁) = 1 and Law(I₃) = 1 (Theorem 4.1); 𝒲\_S(I₂) = ∅, and under C0 ∧ C2 ∧ H1 ∧ H2 ∧ H3, Law(I₂) = 0 (Theorem 4.2). □

　　Each step of the chain is the addition of a single condition (I₁ ≺ I₂ ≺ I₃), so under the locating convention of 6.5 both steps are located boundary pairs, and no separate proof that "a minimal-step subdivision exists" is required.

　　Hence 𝔍\_Law is in general not an interval.

6.3　Domain Convexity for a Single Witness

\**Theorem 6.4 (single-R domain convexity)*\*　Fix R and write 𝔍\_R = { I \| R ∈ 𝒲\_S(I) }. If I₁ ⊆ I₂ ⊆ I₃ and I₁, I₃ ∈ 𝔍\_R, then I₂ ∈ 𝔍\_R.

*Proof.* This section fixes F, t and R; the three components of 𝒲\_S are respectively Established\_F,t(R), DynamicRole(L;R,I) and ΩSupport(L;R,I).

　Established\_F,t(R)　This predicate carries no I (*The General Form of Law*: CurrentCarrier has no I polarity, and Established is a function of F, t and R). F, t and R being fixed, its value is unchanged among I₁, I₂ and I₃; and from I₁ ∈ 𝔍\_R it is 1.  
　DynamicRole　Existential polarity. From I₁ ∈ 𝔍\_R, an existential witness falls at some condition within I\_cov(L,I₁); I₁ ⊆ I₂ gives I\_cov(L,I₁) ⊆ I\_cov(L,I₂), so that witness is still within the domain, and DynamicRole(I₂) = 1. \**An existential witness is preserved from the smaller domain to the larger.*\*  
　ΩSupport　Coverage polarity. From I₃ ∈ 𝔍\_R, R satisfies the coverage obligation upon the whole of I\_cov(L,I₃); I₂ ⊆ I₃ gives I\_cov(L,I₂) ⊆ I\_cov(L,I₃), so the coverage obligation of I₂ is a subset of it and is still satisfied. \**A coverage qualification is preserved from the larger domain to the smaller.*\*

The conjunction of the three is R ∈ 𝒲\_S(I₂). □

　*Note 1.* The coverage obligations of CarrierRealizes and CarrierNonInstant do vary with I, but the two are structural antecedents of Established (through H1) and not independent components of 𝒲\_S; this proof argues only over the three components of 𝒲\_S and does not treat their antecedents a second time.

　　*Note 2.* The proof uses only the monotonicity of I ↦ I\_cov (I ⊆ J ⇒ I\_cov(L,I) ⊆ I\_cov(L,J)), which follows directly from the definition of I\_cov, {i ∈ I \| Trig(i) ≠ ∅}, independently of success or failure of realization (3.15′(a) of *The Mathematical Form of Law*).

\**Corollary 6.4.1 (a domain hole must contain a switch of witness)*\*　The domain hole of Theorem 6.3 cannot be produced by one and the same R: were R a witness for both I₁ and I₃, then by 6.4 it would also be a witness for I₂, contradicting Law(I₂) = 0. Hence for any domain hole 1 → 0 → 1 the witnesses at the two ends must differ — \**a Law domain hole must contain a switch of witness*\*.

　　This text records only that the switch occurs, and does not explain its cause (which belongs to *Law Dynamics*).

6.4　Several Mutually Incomparable Maximal Domains

\**Theorem 6.5 (no maximum domain of application)*\*　Let U be the predeclared top domain (the upper bound of the comparison range declared by Γ). One may construct within U two domains I\_A, I\_B ∈ 𝔍\_Law that are mutually incomparable (I\_A ⊄ I\_B ∧ I\_B ⊄ I\_A) and each \**maximal*\* in 𝔍\_Law, while Law(U) = 0. Hence 𝔍\_Law in general has no \**maximum*\*.

*Construction (coatoms).* Take U = {a,b,c}, the legitimate domain family 𝔄\_I ⊆ 𝒫(U) declared by Γ containing U together with its two coatoms

　　I\_A = U ∖ {c} = {a,b},　I\_B = U ∖ {b} = {a,c}.

Let Rel(L) predeclare offices for a, b and c alike (so that I\_cov(L,I) = I for all three). Take a witness R\_A for which coverage and existence both suffice upon {a,b} while coverage fails at c; and R\_B symmetrically, sufficing upon {a,c} while coverage fails at b; and let there be no third witness upon U. Then

　　Law(I\_A) = 1, Law(I\_B) = 1 (Theorem 4.1);　Law(U) = 0 (𝒲\_S(U) = ∅, by Theorem 4.2 under C0 ∧ C2 ∧ H1 ∧ H2 ∧ H3).

Maximality is given directly by the lattice structure: the unique strict superdomain of each of I\_A and I\_B within 𝔄\_I is U, and Law(U) = 0, so neither has a qualifying proper extension and both are maximal. The two are mutually incomparable (c ∈ I\_B ∖ I\_A, b ∈ I\_A ∖ I\_B). Were there a maximum, it would have to contain I\_A ∪ I\_B = U, and U ∉ 𝔍\_Law; hence no maximum exists. □

　　This construction does not depend upon an assertion requiring separate proof, such as "every proper extension touches a failing condition": above a coatom there is only the top domain as candidate, and it suffices to check them one by one.

　　\**Maximal versus maximum*\*　*Maximal* means having no proper extension still within 𝔍\_Law; *maximum* means containing every element of 𝔍\_Law. This theorem asserts that there may be several of the former, mutually incomparable, and hence that the latter in general does not exist. The two may not be used interchangeably.

　　\**Restriction to the top domain*\*　This construction proceeds within the predeclared top domain U; apart from U there is no speaking of maximality, and hence the declaration of U is a necessary part of the construction.

6.5　The Boundary Frontier (Domain of Application)

By Definition 3.9, taking ≺ as the adjacency relation of set inclusion (at the resolution of Γ):

　　ℬ⁻\_I = { (I,J) \| I ≺ J, Law(I)=1, Law(J)=0 },  
　　ℬ⁺\_I = { (I,J) \| I ≺ J, Law(I)=0, Law(J)=1 }.

By 6.1–6.3 both are in general non-empty, and neither can be characterized by a single threshold.

　　\**Locating convention*\*　Below, the resolution of Γ upon domains of application is taken to be \**single-condition addition or deletion*\*: I ≺ J if and only if J = I ∪ {i} with i a single predeclared atomic condition (or conversely one deleted), both lying within the legitimate domain family 𝔄\_I. At this resolution Adjacent\_Γ(I,J) holds. The constructions of 6.1, 6.2, 6.3 and 6.5 have all been taken in single steps (each adding or deleting one atomic condition only, with all the domains involved declared within 𝔄\_I), so their crossing pairs are at once located boundary pairs, and no separate proof of the existence of a subdivision is required. Should Γ take a coarser resolution, or should 𝔄\_I not contain the required intermediate domain, the corresponding constructions yield Cross\_Γ only, and further subdivision is required before locating.

\**Theorem 6.6 (the frontier is not a single point)*\*　At the above resolution of single-condition addition and deletion, the general Law boundary upon domains of application is the set of boundary pairs ℬ\_I = ℬ⁻\_I ∪ ℬ⁺\_I, and not a unique boundary point.

*Proof.* By 6.1 (taking J = I ∪ {i\_new}, a single step) ℬ⁻\_I ≠ ∅; by 6.2 (taking I = J ∖ {i\_\*}, a single step) ℬ⁺\_I ≠ ∅; by the single-step chain I₁ ≺ I₂ ≺ I₃ of 6.3 there are upon one and the same inclusion chain two located boundary pairs, (I₁,I₂) ∈ ℬ⁻\_I and (I₂,I₃) ∈ ℬ⁺\_I; and by 6.5 several mutually incomparable maximal domains each contribute their own exit pair (I\_A ≺ U and I\_B ≺ U both being single-condition additions). Hence ℬ\_I is in general a set of several elements, and is not given by a single "boundary point." □

## Section VII　Interfaces with *The Stability Form of Law* and *The Determination Form of Law*

7.1　The Fixed-Witness Boundary and the Law Boundary

*The Stability Form of Law* gives the boundary parameter of a fixed witness, q\_fix^∂ (the boundary parameter at which that R loses its fixed-solution qualification), together with the solution-set boundary parameter (its own notation; this text does not redo its method of solution). Write q\_W^∂ for the boundary parameter of witness extinction, that is, the point at which 𝒲\_S becomes empty.

This section treats the parameter interface of \**exit*\* (1 → 0) only: q\_W^∂ is by definition the boundary parameter of the extinction of the structural witness set, the point at which 𝒲\_S passes from non-empty to empty, and hence its object is by nature a candidate for exit only and may not serve also for entry. Entry (0 → 1) is certified by 𝒲\_S passing from empty to non-empty together with Theorem 4.1, and the solving of its parameters still belongs to *The Stability Form of Law*; this text sets up no notation for it.

\**Theorem 7.1 (the fixed-witness boundary is not later than the witness-extinction boundary)*\*　Suppose: upon one and the same predeclared unidirectional comparison path of Γ, parameterized by one and the same order; the witness R being tracked is a structural witness at the starting end (R ∈ 𝒲\_S at the start of the path) and retains a traceable correspondence under τ\_Γ along the path; and both q\_fix^∂(R) (the boundary parameter at which that R loses its witness qualification) and q\_W^∂ (the boundary parameter of the extinction of 𝒲\_S) exist and are \**attained*\* (not merely having an infimum that the boundary does not reach). Then

　　q\_fix^∂(R) ≤ q\_W^∂.

*Proof.* When R loses its qualification, 𝒲\_S need not be empty (Theorem 4.3); when 𝒲\_S is empty, every witness within it has lost its qualification, that R in particular. Hence along the same path, R's loss of qualification is not later than the extinction. □

　(i) \**Strict inequality*\*　If, \**after*\* R first loses its qualification and \**before*\* witness extinction, there exists a strictly succeeding comparison state X′ (upon the same path, q\_fix^∂(R) ≺ X′ ≺ q\_W^∂) with 𝒲\_S(X′) ≠ ∅, then q\_fix^∂(R) \< q\_W^∂. This antecedent is stronger than "a substitute witness still exists after the failure": in the continuous case, if the relay occurs at a single point only while 𝒲\_S is again empty at its strict successor, the strict inequality may not be certified.  
　(ii) \**Equality*\*　If that failure coincides exactly with the extinction of the last witness and there is no substitute ((a) and (b) of Theorem 4.4 both holding at the same comparison state), then q\_fix^∂(R) = q\_W^∂.  
　(iii) \**Qualification of the domain of definition*\*　If 𝒲\_S never becomes extinct along the path (q\_W^∂ not existing), or the tracked R loses its traceable correspondence under Γ (τ\_Γ(R) not existing), or the boundary has only an infimum and is not attained, or the two criticalities come from different paths or different resolutions, then the two have no comparable meaning, this theorem does not apply, and no comparison may be forced.

　　\**Upgrade conditions*\*　For q\_W^∂ to be certified as the Law boundary parameter q\_Law^∂ in the exit direction, it must further pass through: Law(X⁺) = 0 obtained by Theorem 4.2 (carrying C0 ∧ C2 ∧ H1 ∧ H2 ∧ H3) or by Theorem 5.1(ii) (carrying H⁻\_M); the left end Law = 1 obtained unconditionally by Theorem 4.1; and Adjacent\_Γ further being required for a located boundary (the upgrade conditions of Theorem 4.4).

　　This text proves the above relations only, and tells *The Stability Form of Law* under what conditions the fixed boundary it computes may be interpreted as a Law boundary. Parameter scans, threshold solving, widths of the stable region, the position of first failure, post-recovery checking and numerical algorithms all belong to that text and are not done here.

### 8.5　Breakdown

　\**K-B1*\*　To construct a world with 𝒲\_S(X) ≠ ∅ and Law\_Γ(X) = 0 — breaking down Theorem 4.1 (which is in effect to break down *The Necessity Form of Law*).

　\**K-B2*\*　Under C0 ∧ C2 ∧ H1 ∧ H2 ∧ H3, to construct 𝒲\_S(X) = ∅ with Law\_Γ(X) = 1 — breaking down Theorem 4.2 (which is in effect to break down the necessary direction of *The General Form of Law*).

　\**K-B5*\*　To prove that I ↦ I\_cov(L,I) is not monotone — the whole argument of 6.4 depends upon it, and if it breaks, 6.4 and 6.4.1 both fall.

　\**K-B6*\*　To obtain Law(X) = 0 where H⁺\_M holds and 𝒮(X) ≠ ∅, or Law(X) = 1 where H⁻\_M holds and 𝒮(X) = ∅ — breaking down Theorem 5.1.

　\**K-B7*\*　To prove that H⁻\_M still lacks an item (for instance that H\_RepR or H1 is really required) — breaking down the division of the condition bundles at 5.2.

　\**K-B8*\*　To certify a Law boundary directly from the failure of the qualification of some single R, or to certify a type of structural boundary directly from the failure of Bind^M — this does not break down this text but violates its discipline (4.3, 4.6); and if a conclusion contrary to this text is thereby reached, that conclusion is void.

　\**K-B9*\*　To take "witness extinction" as an unconditional characterization of the Law boundary — witness extinction ⇒ Law = 0 carries C0 ∧ C2 ∧ H1 ∧ H2 ∧ H3 (Theorem 4.2), and covers the exit direction only; entry (0 → 1) is not characterized by extinction. Whoever asserts this in unconditional terms, that assertion is void.

　\**K-B10*\*　To prove that Lemma 5.1.1 (PreH1Witness) does not hold — for instance by pointing out that the proof of the necessary direction of *The General Form of Law* invokes H1 before obtaining DynamicRole or StableReplay, or that its three interrogations use conditions beyond C0 ∧ C2 ∧ H3. This breaks down H1 ∉ H⁻\_M and requires H1 to be added to the negative-end condition bundle of 5.1(ii) and 5.2.

Not constituting breakdown: upstream H being unclosed; this text not explaining the causes of boundaries; this text not giving numerical methods for boundaries; this text not treating the characterization of adjudicative capacity for ⟂.
