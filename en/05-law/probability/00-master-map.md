## Section One　Group-Form Identity and Why These Texts Must Compose a Group

### 1.1 Why these texts must compose a group

The sixteen texts must compose one group because they treat one and the same object: Probability. Qualification (Chapters 1–3) answers "what a Probability is"; current action (Chapters 4–6) and cross-time dynamics (Chapters 11–13) are the unfolding of one and the same Probability in current reality and in cross-time running; the remaining seven texts are reviews and establishment proofs carried out on one and the same Probability candidate. The texts cite and attach to one another: the conclusions of the auxiliary forms rest on structures the principal forms have already established, and the interfaces of later texts are handed over by earlier ones. From the cross-references within the texts alone, the membership boundary, the directions of dependence, the range affected by an upgrade and the degree of answer of each proposition can no longer be seen clearly; therefore a group diagram must be drawn (Canon Chapter, standard constitution of the group form, piece (5): once membership reaches four texts, a group diagram is required).

### 1.2 Identity

The Probability Group-Form is a bearing group form, aggregated along the object axis; every member text belongs to this bearing group form alone, and upgrade propagation, sealing roll-up and the execution of attachment all run through it. It is an organizational group form: it has no group-level proposition to be proven and sets up no group-charter form-text.

This is the group diagram of the Probability Group-Form and belongs to the group-level integrative forms:

it is not a seventeenth chapter; it is not a principal form, not an auxiliary form, not a proof form;

it adds no proposition to be proven, no ontological predicate and no H;

it carries no local proof; every load-bearing conclusion is marked, item by item, with its source chapter and version (the source table of Section Twelve);

the group diagram produces no independent theoretical proposition, and therefore admits no independent breach of a theoretical proposition: each theoretical conclusion gathered here can be breached only by breaching its source chapter. But the organizational statements of the group diagram can be breached: if the basis of aggregation does not hold, if two axes are mixed in one aggregation, if the registration of members or relations does not agree with the member texts, if the group diagram bears load in place of a member form-text, or if a member acquires two bearing memberships — any one of these makes this Master Diagram invalid or requires its upgrade; this does not of itself breach any member chapter.
