# The Inversion Form of Probability

Under a legal observation protocol frozen in advance, given an actual observation y, to determine what at most can be known from y about whether a candidate is a Probability: when it can be discerned, when it has been proven indiscernible, and when it can only be left pending because the preimage has not been checked in full or the qualification of the candidates themselves has not been settled.

Observation does not restore the internal system to one answer; it only narrows the possible internal systems into a preimage. Whether probability can thereby be recognized lies not in how much the outcome looks like probability, but in whether this preimage can still contain both probability systems and non-probability systems at once.

## Section One　Declaration of the Observation Set and the Forward Mapping

### 1.1 Frozen upstream conclusions

The probability qualification core:

N\_P := MultiLawDimension ∧ CommonContinuationBind ∧ DomainRelativeRateBearing

The sufficient direction (The Necessity Form of Probability): N\_P ⇒ Probability, Status = Established, carrying no H.

The necessary direction (The General Form of Probability): H2 ∧ H3^red ⇒ \[ Probability ⇒ N\_P \].

So:

N\_P = 1 ⇒ Probability = 1, carrying no H;

N\_P = 0 ⇒ Probability = 0, carrying H2 ∧ H3^red if obtained by the contrapositive of the necessary direction.

The sources of the conditions of the two differ; this difference is not washed out below.

Existing prohibited read-backs (The Mathematical Form of Probability, Section Seven): StableFrequency ⇏ DomainRelativeRateBearing; RateRead ≠ ⊥ ⇏ RateBearing; ρ(v\_i) = 0 ⇏ v\_i ∉ V\_P. A readable numerical rate, positive rates of the dimensions, normalization, full coverage, ontological randomness and stable empirical frequency are none of them conditions of probability qualification.

### 1.2 Observation protocol

Π\_obs = (D\_Π, Y\_Π, H\_Π, Prot\_Π)

D\_Π: the domain of legal candidate internal systems. Each member X is a completely specified candidate internal system, including its Law organization, generation structure and environment.

Y\_Π: the observation space the protocol allows to be obtained.

H\_Π: D\_Π → Y\_Π, the forward mapping: given an internal system X, the observation H\_Π(X) that would be obtained under the protocol.

Prot\_Π: the observation protocol frozen in advance, prescribing the objects, window, manner and record format of observation.

Actual observation: y ∈ Y\_Π.

### 1.3 Source of the forward mapping

H\_Π must be given by already established forms: the field system, the operating substrate or a sealed formal system states what observation the internal system X would produce under the protocol Prot\_Π. The source of H\_Π must be written out item by item. Positing out of nothing a unified total mapping "from probability to observation" does not constitute a legal forward mapping.

### 1.4 Admission to inversion

AdmInv(Π) = DomainFrozen ∧ ObsSetFrozen ∧ ForwardMapEstablished ∧ NoInternalQualificationLeak ∧ NoTargetPreselection

DomainFrozen: D\_Π is frozen before observation; the candidate domain is not re-cut after seeing the results.

ObsSetFrozen: Y\_Π and Prot\_Π are frozen before observation; no favorable observation is added after seeing the results.

ForwardMapEstablished: H\_Π has an established source according to 1.3.

NoInternalQualificationLeak: the observations in Y\_Π do not directly contain the internal qualifications to be inverted, including Probability, N\_P, DomainRelativeRateBearing, RateBearWitness and Law qualification. These quantities may not enter y as ordinary external observations.

NoTargetPreselection: D\_Π is not obtained by keeping in advance only candidates with Probability = 1, nor by screening with an internal qualification equivalent to Probability.

AdmInv is the working-domain admission of inversion, not a new ontological condition of probability, and is not counted as an H. When AdmInv(Π) = 0, inversion is not entered.

### 1.5 Candidate qualification labels

For each X ∈ D\_Π:

Class\_P(X) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}

Class\_P(X) = 1: a legal upstream conclusion has already signed Probability(X) = 1. One currently available sufficient source is N\_P(X) = 1 ⇒ Probability(X) = 1 (The Necessity Form of Probability), carrying no H.

Class\_P(X) = 0: a legal upstream conclusion has already signed Probability(X) = 0. When obtained at present from N\_P(X) = 0 by the contrapositive of the necessary direction of The General Form of Probability, it carries H2 ∧ H3^red; this condition is kept candidate by candidate with the label.

Class\_P(X) = ⊥: there is currently no legal upstream conclusion signing the Probability truth of this candidate.

Class\_P is the state of legal upstream signing and is not restricted to being obtained only from N\_P; if other established forms later sign qualification, they enter according to their sources and the conditions they carry.

The inversion read-out itself is symmetric between positive and negative; the sources of candidate qualification are asymmetric under the current state of upstream proof. This asymmetry comes from the present state of upstream proof, not from an ontological asymmetry of positive and negative in probability.

Class\_P describes the theoretical qualification of candidate internal systems, used to characterize the preimage; it is not an observation, does not enter y, and does not conflict with NoInternalQualificationLeak.

## Section Two　Statement of the Inversion Problem

### 2.1 Preimage

E\_Π(y) := H\_Π^{-1}(y) = { X ∈ D\_Π \| H\_Π(X) = y }

### 2.2 Observational equivalence

X\_a ∼\_Π X\_b ⇔ H\_Π(X\_a) = H\_Π(X\_b)

∼\_Π is an equivalence relation on D\_Π (reflexive, symmetric, transitive). When E\_Π(y) is non-empty, it is the equivalence class corresponding to the actual observation.

### 2.3 Problem

To obtain from y the bounds of the preimage E\_Π(y): what at most can be known from y about the internal system, and what at most can be known about probability qualification.

The preimage is not "the most probable explanation". Bayesian posteriors are not discussed here, no credibility is assigned to members of the preimage, and statistical goodness of fit does not stand in for structural identifiability.

### 2.4 Coverage of the preimage

In actual solving, the preimage is given by an enumerated candidate set Ê\_Π(y) ⊆ E\_Π(y). Define the preimage coverage certificate InvCover\_Π(y) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}:

1: it has been proven that Ê\_Π(y) = E\_Π(y), that is, the legal candidates have been checked in full;

0: it has been proven that Ê\_Π(y) ⊊ E\_Π(y), that is, at least one legal candidate is known not to be contained in the current enumeration;

⊥: neither can coverage be proven complete, nor has a definite witness of omission been obtained.

When InvCover\_Π(y) ≠ 1, the coverage of the preimage is not closed.

### 2.5 Specialization to probability

Let φ\_P(X) = Probability(X). The inversion problem about probability qualification is to ask whether φ\_P has one and the same value on E\_Π(y).

Probability is the object-level qualification truth of a candidate and does not become three-valued through inversion. In actual solving, that truth is not accessed directly here; instead Class\_P(X) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥} records whether the current upstream theory has already legally signed the Probability truth of the candidate. Class\_P(X) = ⊥ denotes that the qualification truth is currently not closed by the chain of theory, not that the Probability ontology has a third truth value.

## Section Three　Identifiability Analysis

3.1 Theorem One　Theorem of the factorization of identifiable properties

For an internal property φ: D\_Π → Z, define:

Ident\_Π(φ): there exists a function g\_φ: Im(H\_Π) → Z such that for all X ∈ D\_Π, φ(X) = g\_φ(H\_Π(X)). Here Im(H\_Π) = H\_Π(D\_Π) is the image of the forward mapping.

Then:

Ident\_Π(φ) ⇔ \[ ∀X\_a, X\_b ∈ D\_Π: X\_a ∼\_Π X\_b ⇒ φ(X\_a) = φ(X\_b) \] ⇔ ∃g\_φ: Im(H\_Π) → Z, φ = g\_φ ∘ H\_Π

The local form on a single observation y: define IdentAt\_Π(φ; y): there exists z ∈ Z such that for all X ∈ E\_Π(y), φ(X) = z. Then

IdentAt\_Π(φ; y) ⇔ φ is constant on E\_Π(y)

and write NonIdent\_φ^Π(y) := ¬IdentAt\_Π(φ; y); for φ = φ\_P, written NonIdent\_P^Π(y).

Proof

From left to middle: if φ = g\_φ ∘ H\_Π and H\_Π(X\_a) = H\_Π(X\_b), then φ(X\_a) = g\_φ(H\_Π(X\_a)) = g\_φ(H\_Π(X\_b)) = φ(X\_b).

From middle to right: if φ is constant on every equivalence class, for every y′ in Im(H\_Π) take any member X of E\_Π(y′) and define g\_φ(y′) := φ(X). By constancy, this definition does not depend on the member taken; then for any X, φ(X) = g\_φ(H\_Π(X)).

The right is the definition of the left. The local form is likewise, argued only on E\_Π(y). □

A large amount of data does not automatically mean identifiability. The real condition of identifiability is: the target property does not distinguish any two legal internal systems that the observation protocol cannot distinguish.

### 3.2 Identifiability of probability

By Theorem One, probability qualification is identifiable at y if and only if:

∀X\_a, X\_b ∈ E\_Π(y): Probability(X\_a) = Probability(X\_b)

"Looking like probability" is not identifiability. Identifiability depends only on whether probability qualification has one and the same value in the preimage.

3.3 Three counterexample templates of indiscernibility

The following three cases are protocol-relative structural counterexample templates; they declare no new physical realization or operating substrate, nor assert that some specific running body of probability already exists. The form of a template is: if the corresponding protocol Π\_out, Π\_freq, Π\_rate has obtained ForwardMapEstablished and hence AdmInv(Π) = 1, and there exists a probability candidate X\_1 obtaining the corresponding observation y, then as long as an external-selector candidate X\_0 can be constructed in D\_Π that copies the same observation while DomainRelativeRateBearing(X\_0) = 0, the two enter the same preimage, and that class of observation cannot universally identify probability qualification. These forward mappings are not newly built here. The three conclusion formulas below all hold under this template condition: besides AdmInv, it is required that the corresponding X\_1 and X\_0 really exist in D\_Π.

The negative label of X\_0 is obtained by the contrapositive of the necessary direction of The General Form of Probability, so the judgment "X\_0 is not a probability" in each template carries H2 ∧ H3^red (see 5.2).

Construction of the external-selector candidate: given X\_1, let X\_0 keep the dimensions, Law-bearing and common binding of X\_1, but have the relative generation among the dimensions decided instead by a mechanism M outside the organization, 𝒫 only executing the selected outcome. Section Eight of The General Form of Probability has already shown that in such cases the first two qualifications can be wholly true while DomainRelativeRateBearing fails. Thus N\_P(X\_0) = 0, Class\_P(X\_0) = 0.

Template One　Outcome sequence

Given a probability candidate X\_1, the protocol Π\_out records only the outcome sequence of the continuations, H\_{Π\_out}(X\_1) = y. Construct X\_0, arranging the selections of M so that X\_0 produces the same outcome sequence: H\_{Π\_out}(X\_0) = y.

So: AdmInv(Π\_out) = 1 ⇒ ( OutcomeSequence ⇏ Probability ).

Template Two　Stable empirical frequency

Given a probability candidate X\_1, the protocol Π\_freq records empirical frequency, whose limit is H\_{Π\_freq}(X\_1) = ρ*. Construct X\_0 so that the selections of M produce the same frequency under the same limit: H\_{Π\_freq}(X\_0) = ρ*.

So: AdmInv(Π\_freq) = 1 ⇒ ( StableEmpiricalFrequency ⇏ Probability ).

Even if the limit of the frequency is wholly exact, as long as an external selector can copy it, it still cannot identify by whom the rate is borne. Infinite data cannot make up for the structural non-injectivity of the forward mapping: the more data, the more exact the frequency estimate, but the two candidates produce one and the same frequency.

Template Three　Numerical rate read-out

Given a probability candidate X\_1, the protocol Π\_rate can read out exactly the rate values of the dimensions (ρ(v\_1), …, ρ(v\_n)). Construct X\_0 so that M selects by the same rate values: the rate values read out from the two are wholly the same, H\_{Π\_rate}(X\_1) = H\_{Π\_rate}(X\_0).

So: AdmInv(Π\_rate) = 1 ⇒ ( NumericRateRead ⇏ Probability ).

A rate value is not rate bearing. What is read out is the value of the relative-generation relation; what probability qualification asks is by whom this relation is borne.

### 3.4 Two inferences that may not be made

One may not write ObservedOutcomeAbsence ⇒ DimensionAbsence. That some dimension was not realized within the observation window is not enough to prove that the dimension does not exist: ρ(v\_i) = 0 ⇏ v\_i ∉ V\_P, and not being realized within the observation window is weaker still than zero rate.

One may not write FrequencyShift ⇒ ¬Probability. Probability qualification requires neither a fixed rate value nor a stable empirical frequency; cross-time change of frequency belongs to The General Form of Probability-Dynamics, The Mathematical Form of Probability-Dynamics and The Stability Form of Probability.

### 3.5 Intervention and separation

Intervention can enter the observation protocol, but only as a conditional opening. One may not write Intervention ⇒ Identification.

Let the protocol Π contain a family of legal interventions u, each intervention having a forward mapping H\_{Π,u}. Define separation:

Sep\_Π(X\_a, X\_b) = 1 if and only if ∃u ∈ Π: H\_{Π,u}(X\_a) ≠ H\_{Π,u}(X\_b)

By Theorem One, for probability qualification to be universally identifiable under Π, at least it is required that:

Probability(X\_1) ≠ Probability(X\_0) ⇒ ∃u: H\_{Π,u}(X\_1) ≠ H\_{Π,u}(X\_0)

Intervention itself is not evidence. What has discerning power is "legal intervention plus an established differential forward response": the responses of two candidates of different qualification under intervention u differ, and this difference is given by an established form. From "the system changed after the intervention" alone, probability qualification cannot be inferred.

## Section Four　Upper and Lower Bounds

4.1 Theorem Two　Theorem of the upper bound of observational equivalence

Under AdmInv(Π) = 1, given y = H\_Π(X), the finest legal resolution of the internal system by an inversion using only y is E\_Π(y). No legal inversion output depending only on y can further distinguish the members of the same preimage.

Proof

An inversion output depending only on y is a function K(y) of y. Let X\_a, X\_b ∈ E\_Π(y), and let K claim to distinguish the two, that is, output K\_a for X\_a and K\_b for X\_b, with K\_a ≠ K\_b. But H\_Π(X\_a) = H\_Π(X\_b) = y, so the two give K the same input, and K(y) can take only one value and cannot equal both K\_a and K\_b. Contradiction. □

Write KnowMax\_Π(y) := E\_Π(y). KnowMax is only the formal name of the upper bound of inversion, not a new ontological predicate.

4.2 Theorem Three　Theorem of the indiscernibility of probability

If there exist X\_1, X\_0 ∈ D\_Π such that:

Class\_P(X\_1) = 1, Class\_P(X\_0) = 0, H\_Π(X\_1) = H\_Π(X\_0) = y

then:

NonIdent\_P^Π(y) = 1

No inversion using only the observations of this protocol may sign Probability = 1 or Probability = 0 at this y.

Proof

By the meaning of the labels, Probability(X\_1) = 1 and Probability(X\_0) = 0, so φ\_P is not constant on E\_Π(y), and by the local form of Theorem One, NonIdent\_P^Π(y) = 1. Further, by Theorem Two, the output K(y) depending only on y is the same for X\_1 and X\_0. If K(y) signs Probability = 1, it is wrong for X\_0; if it signs Probability = 0, it is wrong for X\_1. Both are in the legal preimage, and the protocol can exclude neither. □

This is proven indiscernibility, not insufficiency of the current enumeration. As long as the added material still belongs to the same observation protocol that adds no separating power, or the extended protocol still keeps this pair of candidates with different labels observationally equivalent, adding such data cannot remove this indiscernibility; if new observation introduces a new legal separating quantity, it is already another inversion under a stronger protocol.

Condition propagation: Theorem Three itself neither adds nor inherits H2, H3^red; it takes as a premise that Class\_P(X\_1) = 1 and Class\_P(X\_0) = 0 already hold. If in some specific application Class\_P(X\_0) = 0 is obtained from N\_P(X\_0) = 0 by the contrapositive of the necessary direction of The General Form of Probability, then that negative label, and the application conclusions depending on it, carry H2 ∧ H3^red. The condition propagates along the source of the label and does not enter Theorem Three itself.

4.3 Lower bound: what necessarily cannot be known

By Theorem Three: as long as candidates with a positive label and a negative label are both present in the preimage, probability qualification necessarily cannot be known at that y. By the three counterexample templates of 3.3: under protocols that record only outcome sequences, empirical frequencies or numerical rates, as long as the template conditions are satisfied, there exists such a y, so these three classes of protocol cannot universally identify probability qualification.

### 4.4 Internal inversion states

InvState\_P^Π(y) ∈ { P^+, P^-, Mix, InvOpen, ClassOpen }

P^+: InvCover\_Π(y) = 1, E\_Π(y) non-empty, and all X ∈ E\_Π(y) have Class\_P(X) = 1.

P^-: InvCover\_Π(y) = 1, E\_Π(y) non-empty, and all X ∈ E\_Π(y) have Class\_P(X) = 0.

Mix: the preimage is known to contain at least one candidate with Class\_P = 1 and one candidate with Class\_P = 0. By Theorem Three, proven indiscernible. Mix does not take closure of coverage as a premise: it holds as soon as two legal members with different labels have been found.

InvOpen: not Mix, and InvCover\_Π(y) ≠ 1, that is, the preimage has not been proven to be checked in full.

ClassOpen: not Mix, InvCover\_Π(y) = 1, but at least one candidate has Class\_P = ⊥, that is, the probability qualification of a candidate itself has not yet been closed upstream.

If InvCover\_Π(y) = 1 while E\_Π(y) = ∅, then the observation y is not in the image of the forward mapping, which shows that the forward mapping or the protocol is wrong; this is a problem at the layer of AdmInv and does not enter the five states above.

The five states are pairwise disjoint and cover all cases with AdmInv(Π) = 1 and E\_Π(y) non-empty.

### 4.5 External three-valued read-out

Read^Inv\_{P,Π}(y) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}

P^+ → 1

P^- → 0

Mix, InvOpen, ClassOpen → ⊥

⊥ must be accompanied by its reason:

Reason\_⊥ ∈ { NonIdentifiable, InverseCoverageOpen, CandidateClassificationOpen }

corresponding respectively to Mix, InvOpen, ClassOpen. The three classes of reason may not be written together.

Conditions of the read-outs:

Read = 1: the positive labels of all candidates in the preimage carry conditions according to their sources; positive labels currently obtained through N\_P = 1 carry no H, and the read-out accordingly carries no H.

Read = 0: of the negative labels of all candidates in the preimage, those obtained by the contrapositive of the necessary direction carry H2 ∧ H3^red; the read-out carries them accordingly.

⊥ with the reason NonIdentifiable: this judgment depends on Class\_P = 0 of the negative-label candidates in it; if that negative label is obtained by the contrapositive of the necessary direction, the judgment carries H2 ∧ H3^red.

In addition, all read-outs take AdmInv(Π) = 1 as their admission, in which ForwardMapEstablished requires H\_Π to have an established source; the conditions carried by the source of H\_Π are inherited with the source.

4.6 Theorem Four　Theorem of the coverage of negative read-outs

NoPositiveWitness ⇏ NegativeIdentification

That is, "no candidate with Class\_P = 1 has been found" is not "all candidates have Class\_P = 0".

Read^Inv = 0 if and only if InvCover\_Π(y) = 1, E\_Π(y) is non-empty, and all candidates obtain Class\_P = 0.

Proof

By the definitions of 4.4 and 4.5, Read^Inv = 0 if and only if the state is P^-, and P^- requires coverage closed, the preimage non-empty and all candidates with negative labels. If coverage is not closed, the preimage may contain unenumerated candidates with Class\_P = 1, and signing 0 might then be wrong; so only ⊥ can be signed, with the reason InverseCoverageOpen. □

Likewise, signing 1 also requires coverage to be closed: an unenumerated member may have a negative label, so that the actual state is Mix.

## Section Five　Residue Declaration

### 5.1 The unknowable residue

The following residues must be declared explicitly and may not be left tacit:

NonIdentifiable (Mix): the preimage contains both probability and non-probability candidates. Proven indiscernible. To remove it, one can only change to a protocol able to separate them (3.5), not add data of the same kind that does not raise the separating power.

InverseCoverageOpen (InvOpen): the preimage has not been proven to be checked in full. This is a residue of solving, removable once the enumeration is completed or coverage is proven.

CandidateClassificationOpen (ClassOpen): the preimage has been checked in full, but the qualification of some candidate has not yet been closed upstream. This is a residue of qualification, removable once The Determination Form of Probability and others close the candidate qualification.

The three differ in nature: the first is structural, the second is enumerative, the third concerns qualification. Mix is not ordinary insufficiency of material; InvOpen and ClassOpen are not merged.

### 5.2 Condition ledger

H\_new = ∅.

Theorems One to Four, as inversion theorems, do not inherit H2, H3^red on account of their own proofs. If a specific application uses a negative label obtained by the conditional necessary direction, the condition propagates along that label to the conclusions of that application: Read = 0, ⊥ with the reason NonIdentifiable, and the judgment "X\_0 is not a probability" in the three counterexample templates of 3.3. The condition does not propagate wholesale along the name "Inversion Form".

ForwardMapEstablished is an admission condition of the inversion protocol, not a universal ontological bridge, and is not counted as an H; the conditions carried by the source of H\_Π are inherited with the source.

### 5.3 Breach methods

Breach of the upper bound: there exist two candidates in the same preimage, while a legal method depending only on y can distinguish their internal structures. Breaches Theorem Two.

Breach of identifiability: a property is claimed discernible at y (including read-outs signing 1 or 0 in 4.5), while there exist legal candidates in the same preimage with different values of that property. Breaches that discernibility conclusion.

Breach of a local indiscernibility conclusion: some specific protocol is claimed unable to identify probability qualification, while the protocol actually has an established legal separating quantity able to distinguish all candidates of different qualification. Breaches the corresponding conclusion of 3.3 or 4.3.

Breach of coverage: 0 or 1 is signed while the coverage of the preimage is not closed. Breaches Theorem Four.

Ineligible protocol: a so-called external observation directly leaks internal answers such as N\_P, RateBearWitness or Probability. This is not a breach of the main theorem but AdmInv(Π) = 0; inversion was not entered.

Not constituting a breach: the read-out is ⊥; the H2, H3^red carried by upstream negative labels are not yet closed; an original Mix is separated under a stronger protocol (that is another inversion under another protocol).

## Section Six　Interfaces

6.1 Boundary with The Determination Form of Probability

The Inversion Form of Probability: takes as input an existing legal observation protocol, forward mapping and observational results, and answers "what at most these observations allow to be known".

The Determination Form of Probability: establishes or invokes the operational checks, certificates, coverage and 1, 0, ⊥ criteria of the three qualifications of probability, and answers "how to check whether an instance is a probability".

So here Read\_{N\_P}^M, RateBearWitness^M, Law witnesses or N\_P itself are not taken as ordinary external observations; otherwise inversion would be written as determination, violating at the same time NoInternalQualificationLeak. The candidate qualification label Class\_P is given upstream; here it is only read, not determined.

### 6.2 Upstream

The General Form of Probability: the probability qualification core N\_P and the necessary direction (conditional).

The Mathematical Form of Probability: prohibited read-backs and formal objects.

The Necessity Form of Probability: the sufficient direction.

The field system and the operating substrate: the source of the forward mapping H\_Π.

### 6.3 Hand-down

The Determination Form of Probability: takes over the determination of candidate qualification needed to close ClassOpen. The three classes of reason attached when the inversion read-out is ⊥ are handed to that text to distinguish which residues can be removed by qualification determination (ClassOpen) and which cannot (Mix).

The Limit Form of Probability: the boundary of indiscernibility can serve as a reference for qualification boundaries at the layer of observation; the boundary of qualification itself is not decided by inversion.

The General Form of Probability-Dynamics, The Mathematical Form of Probability-Dynamics, The Stability Form of Probability: cross-time change of frequency and rate; no inference is made here.

### 6.4 What this text does not do

It does not answer how to check whether N\_P holds; it adds no ontological component of probability or fourth qualification; it does not write empirical frequency, numerical rate, randomness, normalization, positive rate or full coverage as qualification conditions; it makes no statistical estimation and introduces no Bayesian prior or posterior; it does not let the most probable explanation stand in for the legal preimage; it does not write not having found a counterexample as identifiability; it does not prove the first six texts anew; it does not research the dynamics, stability, source and limit of probability.
