# Master Diagram

## Section One　Basis of Aggregation and the Object of the Group Form

1.1 Why these twenty chapters must compose one group form

All twenty chapters study one and the same object: Intention. Chapters 1–3 close the structural core of Intention qualification, N\_I := P\_I ∧ SD ∧ GC ∧ TI; Chapters 4–12, within the working domain in which Intention qualification holds, close three kinds of object in turn: action, Desire-Gate switching and dynamics; Chapters 13–20 each bear one of eight tasks with respect to these closed objects: inversion, determination, limits, construction of the operating substrate, reachability, source, stability and cross-realization invariants. Chapters 4–19 take their object definitions, working domains and read-back conditions mainly from the chapters before them (for upstream chapters that come later in the numbering, see 5.3); the entry and the first two layers of Chapter 20 presuppose none of the earlier chapters, and it takes from Chapter 1 only N\_I, as the target of representation in its Section Nine. The chapters on determination, limits, reachability, source and stability further invoke the necessary directions, sufficient directions and formal read-backs of Chapters 1–12.

These inter-chapter relations can no longer be seen clearly from the chapters' own cross-references alone: the read-back conditions on one and the same formal witness have to be carried across four or five chapters; one and the same condition bears load over different ranges in different chapters; and some chapters come later in the numbering yet stand upstream of chapters numbered before them. To know, at an upgrade, which chapters a change to one chapter affects, the membership boundary, the directions of relations and condition propagation must be registered in one place. The twenty chapters therefore compose one group form, and this Master Diagram registers them as a whole.

1.2 The kind of group form and the standing of this Master Diagram

Basis of aggregation: the object axis; the object of study is Intention (its qualification, together with three kinds of derived object: action, Desire-Gate switching and dynamics). The Intention Group-Form is a bearing group form: each of the twenty chapters belongs to the Intention Group-Form alone, and upgrade propagation, sealing roll-up and the execution of attachment all run through it.

The Intention Group-Form is an organizational group form: it contains no group-level proposition to be proven that a single member cannot bear, and it sets up no group-charter form-text.

This Master Diagram is a group diagram and belongs to the integrative forms (Canon Chapter 1 v2.2, Part Five, Chapter Two; for the group-form specification, see Part Six, Chapter Four of the same text). It does no more than organize twenty chapters that already stand: it adds no ontological object, qualification condition, proposition of proof, formal reading, running mechanism or H, and it rewrites no definition, theorem or Status declaration of any member chapter. Wherever this Master Diagram and the text of a member chapter diverge, the text of the member chapter prevails in every case. Every load-bearing conclusion in this Master Diagram is marked, item by item, with its source chapter; the versions of the member chapters are given in the member table of Section Two, and the item-by-item sources in the attached Chapter-by-Chapter Source Check Table, which serves as this Master Diagram's table of terminological correspondence and theorem sources. The member texts cite the upstream chapters outside the group (5.2) without version numbers, and this Master Diagram has no means of supplying them (to be checked).

### 1.3 Notation

Chapter numbers refer to Chapters 1–20 of the Intention Group-Form; Chapters 10 and 12 are currently at v1.1, the other eighteen at v1.0. There are six relation properties, never mixed anywhere in this text:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;P  proof inheritance: a proof of some theorem in this chapter directly uses an upstream definition or theorem; if the upstream is withdrawn, that proof no longer holds  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;F  formal interface invocation: invokes an upstream formal reading, verdict or read-back rule; the main theorems of this chapter do not take it as a premise of proof  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I  instance upstream: instances, models or the operating substrate are taken from the upstream  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;M  methodological isomorphism: a chapter of the same form-kind in another group form serves as a methodological precedent  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;C  citation for contrast: cited only for comparison or to explain the division of labor  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;D  inheritance of discipline or protocol: carries over terminology, protocols, disciplines or corpus numbering

A dependency arrow is not an arrow of logical derivation, still less an arrow of temporal generation. The order of chapter numbers is not the order of proof dependence (5.3).

### 3.2 Status of each direction

Necessary direction: the main theorem of Chapter 1 "uses only the sufficient direction above and carries no H" (Status = Established); the main theorems of Chapters 4, 7 and 10 have Status = Established, H\_new = ∅. All four chapters prove only the necessary direction, and none of them obtains a theorem by definition. The minimality of Chapter 1, CurrentDerivedMinimal(N\_I), carries, in the deletion test of P\_I, the conditions of the necessary direction of The General Form of Probability: the v1.0 text of Chapter 1 records them as H2 ∧ H3^red, while The General Form of Probability v1.1 currently has H2 ∧ H3^red ∧ SC\_Hold (see 6.3 (3)); the text of Chapter 1 notes the conditions carried at the point of minimality, and the main theorem does not carry them.

Sufficient direction: the main theorems of Chapters 3, 6, 9 and 12 have Status = Established and inherit no H; each chapter carries out its closure review independently on the semantic domain it has frozen, without presupposing the fifth-qualification and cancelling-condition checks of the corresponding General Form, which serve only as consistency back-tests. The two-way formulas have Status = Established; each is the composition of the two directions after they have been proven independently in two chapters (Chapters 6, 9 and 12 state explicitly: not identity by definition).

Formal systems: the formal systems of Chapters 5, 8 and 11 are Established and presuppose no open H; the text of Chapter 2 does not state a Status, and its formal system and read-back are registered according to Theorems 1–8 (to be checked). Among the positive read-backs, those of A2 in Chapter 5, of S2 in Chapter 8, and of L4, the carrier site and the probability site in Chapter 11, together with Theorem 6 of Chapters 5, 8 and 11, are Conditional, the condition being the H\_LawRead applicable to the witnesses used, and Chapter 11 adds the conditions of the upstream positive-reading path; in Chapter 11 the positive read-back of the action projection propagates H\_LawRead along with the complete-action positive witness, those time windows of confirmed absence of complete action that contain the A2 duty-conformance sub-layer additionally carry H\_PAct-Rep, and the invocation of switching inherits the conditions of Theorem 6 of The Mathematical Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching; all other positive read-backs are Established. Theorems 1 and 5 of Chapter 2 propagate H\_LawRead along with the actual Law witnesses (as written out in the proof of Theorem 5); Theorem 6 of Chapter 2 belongs to the auxiliary layer and is not among them. Negative read-backs are registered separately by failure layer or refutation path (Section Six).

Formal-layer corollaries: Chapters 3, 6, 9 and 12 each have a formal-layer corollary of the form "formal reading 1 ⇒ the object holds", with Status = Conditional, the condition being the H\_LawRead applicable to the Law-bearing witnesses used, and Chapter 12 adds the conditions of the upstream positive-reading path; if the relevant Law qualification is already a given at the object layer, the condition does not arise (stated explicitly in Chapters 6, 9 and 12).

### 3.3 Working domain and locality

Action, switching and dynamics all lie within the working domain in which Intention qualification holds: the working domain of action and of switching is Intent\_{F,t} = 1; for the working domain of dynamics, Main Theorem One of Chapter 10 writes "Intention qualification persists over the whole of γ", while Chapter 12 writes "at the start of γ, Intent\_{F,t\_0} = 1", taking whole-segment persistence of qualification as antecedent L4. Chapter 12 says that the two compose the two-way formula "within the working domain in which Intention qualification persists over the whole of γ"; the two chapters write the working domain differently (to be checked: whether cross-annotation is needed).

Locality: the conclusions on switching hold for each fixed comparison sequence σ and imply nothing about any other σ; the conclusions on dynamics hold for each declared trajectory segment γ and imply nothing about other segments; a formal reading of qualification holds only for one declared anchor.

3.4 The four kinds of object do not absorb one another

Action does not imply switching, nor switching action (Ch. 4, 7); switching invokes only the first three layers of action, A1, A2 and A3, and does not require A4 (Ch. 7, 9); dynamics is not a time series of action readings, nor is it conditioned on action or switching (Ch. 10, 12, 14); upon exit from qualification, action, switching and dynamics are not judged 0 but recorded as OutDom (Ch. 14; since the current upstream conditions are not closed, the determiner cannot obtain OutDom and falls back to ⊥; see 7.2). Each of the four kinds of object is judged on its own: they are not combined into one total verdict, and they do not vote on or fill in for one another (Ch. 14); Chapter 17 seeks reachability separately for the four kinds of target. The dependence structure of determination in Chapter 14 divides downward from qualification into three branches; it is not a chain.
