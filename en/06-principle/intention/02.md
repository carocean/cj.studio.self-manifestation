# The Mathematical Form of Intention

## Section One　Purpose

To establish a formal system for the four necessary qualifications of Intention, such that all four qualifications of a candidate probability organization 𝒫\_I in the Intention-Domain can be evaluated on one and the same formal anchor, yielding a definite reading: holds (1), fails (0), or insufficient material (⊥).

The four necessary qualifications are:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_I = P\_I ∧ SourceDrive ∧ GateCorrespondence ∧ TuneInterface,   P\_I = InIntentDomain(𝒫\_I) ∧ Probability(𝒫\_I; C\_I, 𝔯\_I)

They determine respectively: whether 𝒫\_I lies in the Intention-Domain and is a formal probability organization; whether it stands in source-drive driving and itself bears source-drive continuation; whether its probability dimensions correspond one-to-one to Desire-Gates; and whether there is a tuning interface behind every Desire-Gate.

The formal objective is:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S\_I ↦ M\_I,   S\_I := N\_I

## Section Two　Core Thesis

The four items of N\_I can be represented as four three-valued readings on one and the same formal anchor; when the anchor is qualified, the four items are conjoined to give the formal-solution qualification IntentCoreCert^M; when the formal-solution qualification takes 1, it is read back as N\_I = 1 (with the condition H\_LawRead propagated along with the Law witness used; see Theorem 5):

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IntentCoreCert^M = 1 ⇒ N\_I = 1

M\_I must express exactly:

(1) Location and Probability. 𝒫\_I lies in the Intention-Domain and is a formal probability organization. Whether the probability organization holds follows the formal reading already available in the probability system; no separate determination of probability is built.

(2) Source-drive driving. What is determined is whether 𝒫\_I itself bears the continuation of the source drive, not whether there is a source-drive pulse at this moment, nor whether each probability dimension has its own pulse at this moment.

(3) One-to-one correspondence between probability dimensions and Desire-Gates. Every probability dimension has exactly one Desire-Gate and every Desire-Gate has exactly one probability dimension, no more and no fewer; correspondence is counted by duty, not by physical node.

(4) Tuning interface. Behind every Desire-Gate there is an interface leading to tuning; having an interface is not the same as tuning being under way, and effect output is not a tuning interface.

(5) Same-anchor. The four items must fall on the same probability organization, the same Continuation-Position, the same set of directions and the same set of Desire-Gates; items that each hold but belong to different objects cannot be pieced together into N\_I.

(6) Distinguishing failure from insufficiency. “Fails” and “not yet known” must be kept apart: not having found a witness does not mean that no witness exists. “The declaration was filled in wrongly” and “the structure does not exist” must likewise be kept apart: the former can only call for re-declaration, and cannot be grounds for announcing that the necessary structure of Intention fails.

(7) Auxiliary layer. Direction being borne by the system itself, directional distinction persisting until the successor responsibilities separate, and the specialization of rate-bearing in the probability working domain are given separate readings and form the auxiliary layer; they do not enter the formal-solution qualification. When two realizations are compared, the auxiliary layer must also separate two matters: whether the two realizations perform the same task, and whether each realization separately passes the directional attribution check. The former shows only that the two can be compared; the latter cannot be inferred from it.

What this system represents is Intention qualification, not Intention-Action: an actual source-drive pulse, Desire-Gate switching, tuning or manifestation lies outside this system. What can be distinguished and counted in this system are probability dimensions, Desire-Gates, source-drive realizations, tuning interfaces, anchors and certificates.

### 3.2 Definition sequence

Definition 1 (Declared parameters). χ\_I := (F, t, χ\_P, 𝒢\_I^raw, Σ\_I^src, 𝒯\_I^tune, Γ\_I). F is the Self-Manifestation-Field and t the moment of evaluation; χ\_P := (F, t, 𝒫\_I, C\_I, 𝔯\_I, V\_I, ℒ\_I) is the probability solving instance of The Mathematical Form of Probability, where V\_I is the set of formal direction dimensions and ℒ\_I is the Law-bearing registration of the dimensions; 𝒢\_I^raw is the realization registration family of the Desire-Gates; Σ\_I^src is the registration family of source-drive duty realizations; 𝒯\_I^tune is the registration family of tuning interfaces; Γ\_I is the protocol of freezing, identity and coverage. χ\_I constitutes the declared parameters of Intention qualification.

Desire-Gates and the source drive are both counted by duty, not by physical realization. One Desire-Gate duty may be borne jointly by several physical nodes, and one physical carrier may bear different Desire-Gate duties in different states; if Desire-Gates were counted by physical node, the one-to-one correspondence between probability dimensions and Desire-Gates would be swayed by accidents of physical wiring. Therefore the duty identity of each realization registration is first frozen, and registrations are then grouped into classes by equality of identity.

Definition 2 (Boundary-Gate duty identity). Each element of 𝒢\_I^raw is a duty-bearing realization registration: a physical realization together with the state in which it bears a Boundary-Gate duty in the current declaration; when the same physical carrier bears different Boundary-Gate duties in different states, these are registered as different elements. Γ\_I freezes the function GateRoleID: 𝒢\_I^raw → 𝒟\_G. g\_a ∼\_G g\_b if and only if GateRoleID(g\_a) = GateRoleID(g\_b). Properties of relations: reflexive, holds; symmetric, holds; transitive, holds; all follow directly from equality of identity, and ∼\_G is an equivalence relation. The quotient set is 𝒢̄\_I := 𝒢\_I^raw / ∼\_G; its elements are called Boundary-Gate duty classes (downstream chapters call them Desire-Gate duty classes, and call 𝒢̄\_I the Desire-Gate duty quotient class).

Definition 3 (Source-drive duty identity). Each element of Σ\_I^src is a duty-bearing source-drive realization registration. Γ\_I freezes the function SrcRoleID: Σ\_I^src → 𝒟\_S. s\_a ∼\_src s\_b if and only if SrcRoleID(s\_a) = SrcRoleID(s\_b). Properties of relations: reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity all hold, and ∼\_src is an equivalence relation. The family of realizations bearing the duty of the source-drive side is written \[S\_I\]\_src; it may be borne jointly by one or more physical realizations.

Definition 4 (Correspondence and pass-through between probability dimensions and Desire-Gates). Corr\_I^M, Pass\_I^M ⊆ V\_I × 𝒢̄\_I. Corr\_I^M(v\_i, \[G\]) means that the formal dimension v\_i and the Boundary-Gate duty class \[G\] correspond to each other in the declaration; Pass\_I^M(v\_i, \[G\]) means that v\_i legally leads to \[G\]. Properties of relations: both are relations between two different sets, so reflexivity, symmetry, antisymmetry and transitivity do not apply; full coverage on both sides, functionality and injectivity of Corr\_I^M are not part of the definition, and are evaluated by GC1 to GC4 of Definition 22.

Definition 5 (Formal anchor). K\_I^M := (F, t, 𝒫\_I, C\_I, V\_I, 𝒢̄\_I, Γ\_I).

Definition 6 (Causal equivalence). State\_a ∼\_C State\_b if and only if the two have the same set of action outcomes for every subsequent legal causal action. Properties of relations: reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity all hold, and ∼\_C is an equivalence relation.

A formal reading takes three values, not two. A two-valued system can say only “holds” or “fails,” so cases in which registration is incomplete or evidence has not yet arrived would be forced to read as “fails,” and a candidate organization that merely lacks material would be misjudged as a structural failure. The third value ⊥ is reserved for such cases. Correspondingly, the evaluation of quantifiers must also make a distinction: to say “every item holds” or “no item holds,” one must first confirm that the domain under consideration has been fully registered; to say “some item holds” or “some item fails,” one definite witness suffices.

Definition 7 (Three values and operators). 𝕋₃ := {1, 0, ⊥}, where ⊥ denotes insufficient material, coverage not closed, or declaration invalidity. ∧₃: 1 when both items are 1, 0 when either item is 0, otherwise ⊥. ∨₃: 1 when either item is 1, 0 when both items are 0, otherwise ⊥. ¬₃: exchanges 1 and 0 and leaves ⊥ unchanged. The three form strong Kleene logic and satisfy De Morgan's laws.

Definition 8 (Coverage guard). Closed(D) means: under the coverage protocol of Γ\_I, the registration of domain D is complete.

Definition 9 (Three-valued quantifiers). ∀₃ (over domain D): if some item is 0, it takes 0; if Closed(D) and every item is 1, it takes 1; otherwise it takes ⊥. ∃₃: if some item is 1, it takes 1; if Closed(D) and every item is 0, it takes 0; otherwise it takes ⊥. Every atomic reading specifies the positive certificate for taking 1, and the closure of coverage and the definite refutation required for taking 0; all other cases take ⊥.

A probability reading of 0 has two sources. One is structural zero: the declared organization really lacks the necessary structure of Probability. The other is declaration zero: the declared instance itself is unqualified, for example because the partition of dimensions, the Continuation-Position or the rate domain was filled in wrongly. The latter shows that this declaration cannot be used, not that the structure does not exist. If declaration zero were passed directly to Intention, one wrongly filled-in declaration would be read as “the probability organization of Intention does not exist.” Therefore, before entering this system, declaration zero is first re-read as ⊥.

Definition 10 (Probability necessary-core reading and its normalization). N\_PRead\_I^M := Read\_{N\_P}^M(χ\_P). According to The Mathematical Form of Probability, Read\_{N\_P}^M = 0 divides into structural zero and declaration zero. The normalized reading:

Definition 11 (Location reading). IntentLoc^M(𝒫\_I) ∈ 𝕋₃. Takes 1: there is a legal field-domain identity or boundary certificate showing that 𝒫\_I lies on the Intention-Domain side, before the Desire-Gates. What source-drive driving determines is bearing at the level of the organization: whether 𝒫\_I stands within source-drive driving and itself bears the continuation of the source drive. Three cases must not be confused here. First, the absence of a source-drive pulse at this moment does not cancel the qualification of bearing. Second, a probability dimension whose current rate is 0 is still a formal direction, and is not thereby required to present a source-drive bearing certificate of its own. Third, the continuation of the source drive must undergo direction-branching through 𝒫\_I, and the range of that branching is exactly the formal directions of 𝒫\_I; if some path outside the organization selects the direction in its place, the source drive is not borne by this organization. The four readings below determine respectively: whether the source-drive side lies within the organization, whether it is same-anchor with 𝒫\_I, whether the continuation enters 𝒫\_I, and whether the direction-branching domain is exactly V\_I.

The correspondence between probability dimensions and Desire-Gates is evaluated pair by pair: whether a given probability dimension and a given Desire-Gate duty class correspond, and whether that dimension legally leads to that Desire-Gate. Correspondence and pass-through are read separately, because a correspondence in the registration without an actual path is only a pairing in name.

Behind a Desire-Gate there are many state changes, and not all of them are tuning interfaces. Effect output also lies behind the Desire-Gate, but it is a result of manifestation, not the Principle-Field's command of tuning. The post-gate tuning reading therefore requires that the candidate interface be a legal successor of that Desire-Gate, that its duty belong to tuning command, and that it be same-anchor with the other three items.

Definition 14 (Post-gate tuning reading). PostGateTuneRoleRead^M(\[G\_i\], q), q ∈ 𝒯\_I^tune: takes 1 if q is a legally registered successor duty realization, there is a legal pass-through or successor relation between q and \[G\_i\], the duty identity of q belongs to tuning command, that is, to the integrative relation of Form Chapter 4 and not to effect output, and q is same-anchor with K\_I^M; takes 0 if the registration of q and the coverage of successors of \[G\_i\] are closed and it is determined that at least one of the four conditions above fails; otherwise ⊥.

It is not enough for each of the four readings to take 1. If the probability reading is taken from one organization, the source-drive reading from another, and the correspondence between probability dimensions and Desire-Gates uses a third set of directions, the four 1s pieced together do not describe one and the same organization. Anchor qualification checks whether the four items fall on the same object. Anchor invalidity shows that the splicing in this declaration is wrong, not that the structure of Intention does not exist.

Definition 15 (Anchor qualification). AnchorQual\_I^M(K\_I^M) ∈ 𝕋₃. Beyond the four items of N\_I, the directions of Intention also satisfy two universal conditions. First, direction is borne by the system itself: changing the internal organization of the system changes the directional relations; directional difference is not merely a label attached to outcomes; nor is it the case that a mechanism outside the boundary completely bears the whole generation while the system merely executes passively. Second, the distinction between two directions must persist until their respective successor responsibilities separate; if partway the two runs can no longer be distinguished yet the successors are still attributed separately to the original directions, the distinction is merely nominal. Definitions 16 and 17 give readings for these two conditions and make up the auxiliary layer (Definitions 26–28); they do not enter IntentCoreCert^M. The system X in both definitions is, in the core declaration, the organization in which 𝒫\_I lies; R\_X is the legal organization within it that bears the directional task; the continuation question is C\_I and the set of directions is V\_I. In task comparison, the same evaluation is made for each realization in the comparison registration together with its C and V.

Definition 17 (Directional-distinction persistence reading). For v\_i ≠ v\_j, Δ\_ij(τ) is the directional-distinction trajectory from the point at which the distinction is established to the first point of differing successor responsibility. PersistUntilDutyRead^M(v\_i, v\_j): takes 1 if there is a legal certificate showing that over the whole interval of Δ\_ij the two runs at no moment belong to the same ∼\_C class; takes 0 if the trajectory coverage is closed and it is determined that there is a moment τ\_c after which the two belong to the same ∼\_C class while the successors are still attributed to the original directions; otherwise ⊥, including the cases in which the point at which the distinction is established or the first point of successor responsibility cannot be determined.

When two realizations are compared, it must first be established that they perform the same task, or else the comparison is meaningless; but sameness of task decides only comparability, not duty. A legal re-realization may well spoil the directional bearing, so whether each end has system directional bearing and persistence of directional distinction must be reviewed separately. The standard of comparison must also be frozen before the two realizations are seen; if “what counts as the same task” is changed after the realizations have been examined, the comparison loses its grounds. Such a review is meaningful only for a multi-direction task: one and the same continuation question must have at least two distinguishable successor classes. With only one direction, persistence of directional distinction has nothing to apply to, a universal judgment over pairs of directions holds vacuously, and the result of the review loses its content; the multi-direction task is therefore the entry to the auxiliary layer and precedes the review of the two duties. The auxiliary layer also needs its own anchor: the task instance used for system directional bearing, persistence of directional distinction and rate-bearing specialization must be the same set of objects as 𝒫\_I, C\_I, V\_I and 𝒢̄\_I in the core declaration, or evidence stitching will likewise occur. The Desire-Gate side is already counted by duty class; on the direction side, the formal dimensions must likewise be aligned one by one with the directional duty classes in the task instance, and the two cannot be assumed identical by default. On the other hand, differences of internal realization do not by themselves constitute differences of task: material, number of nodes, topology, centralized or distributed organization, continuous or discrete operation, time scale, modular division, algorithm, mediating chain, and biological or artificial carrier may all differ.

SourceOrgBear^M is bearing at the level of the organization: it asks only whether the direction-branching domain equals V\_I, not whether every dimension has its own bearing certificate.

One-to-one correspondence cannot be checked from the direction side alone. That every probability dimension finds one Desire-Gate, and that different dimensions do not share a Desire-Gate, guarantees only injectivity: on the Desire-Gate side there may still be surplus Desire-Gates with no corresponding direction. A surplus Desire-Gate means that there is an exit not taken over by any probability dimension, and the directional organization and the Desire-Gate organization are no longer isomorphic. So the Desire-Gate side must be checked as well: every Desire-Gate has exactly one direction. True one-to-one correspondence means that neither the direction side nor the Desire-Gate side may have more or fewer; in addition, every corresponding pair must be a legal pass-through. Proposition 1. If GC1 to GC4 all take 1, then R\_+ := {(v\_i, \[G\]) : CorrRead\_I^M(v\_i, \[G\]) = 1} is a bijection between V\_I and 𝒢̄\_I. Proposition 2. That GC1 to GC3 alone take 1 is not sufficient to give a bijection. Proof: take V\_I = {v₁, v₂}, 𝒢̄\_I = {G₁, G₂, G₃}, with the correspondences v₁ — G₁ and v₂ — G₂, the correspondence domain closed and all other pairs taking 0; GC1 to GC3 all take 1, while GC4 takes 0 at G₃.

Proposition 3. The anchor guard cannot be implemented by ∧₃. Proof: if IntentCoreCert^M := \[AnchorQual = 1\] ∧₃ CoreRead, then when AnchorQual = 0 the guard is 0 and the conjunction is 0; if instead the guard takes ⊥ in this case, then when CoreRead = 0, ⊥ ∧₃ 0 = 0. ## Section Five　Running and Solving

This system represents qualification and does not evolve in time; “running” means evaluating the declared parameters χ\_I. Order of evaluation:

(1) Freeze Γ\_I, compute GateRoleID and SrcRoleID, and obtain 𝒢̄\_I and \[S\_I\]\_src;  
(2) take Read\_{N\_P}^M and its zero type from The Mathematical Form of Probability, and normalize by Definition 10;  
(3) evaluate the atomic readings (Definitions 11–17);  
(4) evaluate the quantifiers by Definition 9 and conjunction, disjunction and negation by Definition 7, obtain the four item readings by Definitions 20–24, and obtain AnchorQual\_I^M;  
(5) obtain IntentCoreCert^M by the piecewise form of Definition 25;  
(6) for the auxiliary declaration χ\_UI and the comparison registration Ξ\_task^M, evaluate DirRoleAlign^M and the auxiliary co-anchor CoAnchor\_UI^M by Definition 18, the atomic readings of task comparison by Definition 19, and the auxiliary-layer readings by Definitions 26–28; these do not take part in step (5).

Solvability: provided that Γ\_I is frozen and the registration families and quantification domains involved are all finitely enumerable, every step is completed in finitely many steps. Hence, for declared parameters χ\_I satisfying this condition, IntentCoreCert^M has a definite reading, taking values in 𝕋₃, and when it takes ⊥ a reason is attached (AnchorOpen, DeclarationInvalid, coverage not closed, or insufficient material). Declared parameters that do not satisfy this condition do not thereby take 0; their reading remains ⊥. The same holds for the solvability of the auxiliary-layer readings.

Standing of evaluation: IntentCoreCert^M is a reading of one declared anchor. One and the same system may have several legal declared anchors; the reading of one anchor does not represent the others.

### 7.2 Read-back of ⊥

Theorem 8. IntentCoreCert^M = ⊥ is read back neither as N\_I = 0 nor as Intent = 0. Proof: ⊥ comes from insufficient material, coverage not closed, or declaration invalidity, none of which provides a structural refutation.

This is the same as declaration zero not being read back as structural zero in The Mathematical Form of Probability.

### 7.3 Read-back of 0

IntentCoreCert^M = 0 arises only from CoreRead\_I^M = 0, that is, at least one item obtains a formal zero on a qualified anchor; declaration zero has already been turned into ⊥ by Definitions 10 and 25. Reading a formal zero back as a structural zero at the object layer requires two conditions: the negative determination domain of the failing item is closed, and the negative representation condition required by that failure path holds. On the Probability side, according to the actual upstream failure path, conditions such as H2 ∧ H3^red together with H^-\_M or H\_ρRep^- are carried; for the atoms newly added in this system, the completeness of their negative representation is not proved in this system, and their formal zeros are not read back as structural zeros. When structural zero holds, the conclusion obtained by taking the contrapositive of Intent ⇒ N\_I in The General Form of Intention belongs to The Determination Form of Intention.

Typical cases that can yield a formal zero: the location registration is closed and 𝒫\_I clearly lies outside the Intention-Domain; the Probability reading is structural zero; two directions merge into the same Boundary-Gate duty class; one formal dimension corresponds to two Boundary-Gate duty classes; there is a formal Boundary-Gate duty class with no corresponding direction; the coverage of successors of some Boundary-Gate duty class is closed and contains no tuning interface; the coverage of the source-drive side is closed and 𝒫\_I has no legal organization-level bearing of source-drive continuation. Typical cases that take ⊥: the Boundary-Gate registration, the source-drive role coverage or the search for tuning successors is incomplete; the field-domain location material is insufficient; the Probability reading is ⊥ or declaration zero; it cannot be determined whether two physical gate realizations belong to the same duty class; it cannot be determined whether two witnesses belong to the same 𝒫\_I and C\_I; the declared anchor is not closed or is invalid.

### 7.4 Structural-side correspondence

A direction whose current rate is 0 still belongs to V\_I; the current absence of a source-drive pulse does not cancel SourceDrive; the current absence of manifestation does not cancel N\_I; the current occurrence of the source drive, internal memory positions and random noise do not correspond to SourceDrive; one physical gate node does not correspond to one Boundary-Gate duty class; two directions sharing a physical path does not thereby violate the correspondence between probability dimensions and Desire-Gates; effect output and general state changes behind the Desire-Gate do not correspond to a tuning interface.

### 7.5 Purely formal auxiliary quantities

The following quantities have no counterpart on the structural side and are purely formal auxiliary quantities: the coverage guard Closed(·); the identity functions GateRoleID and SrcRoleID and the identity domains 𝒟\_G and 𝒟\_S; the reason markers attached to ⊥; the certificates of the object-layer bridges Cert\_PL^M, Cert\_SD^M, Cert\_GP^M and Cert\_TI^M. The evaluation of the four items of N\_I in Sections Three and Four rests on the structural meaning given in The General Form of Intention and does not presuppose these four bridges.

### 7.7 Breach method

Exhibit a contradiction within the formal system; or exhibit a conflict at read-back between a formal result and an already proved structural proposition, for example: IntentCoreCert^M = 1 while at least one of the four items at the object layer fails; four items on different anchors pieced together into a positive reading; GateCorrespondence^M taking 1 when there is a formal Boundary-Gate duty class with no corresponding direction; TuneInterface^M taking 1 when there is only effect output; SourceDrive^M taking 1 when there is only a current source-drive pulse; SourceDrive^M taking 0 when SourceDrive holds but there is currently no source-drive pulse or some dimension has a current rate of 0; a reading taking 0 while coverage is not closed; an upstream declaration zero propagated as P\_I^M = 0; the invalidity of a single declared anchor read back as N\_I = 0; TaskKeepRead^M = 1 used to infer SysBear or DirPersist for one end; DirClaimAudit^M = 1 read back as SysDirClaim = 1; differences of internal realization by themselves making TaskKeepRead^M take 0; DirClaimAudit^M taking 1 when \|V\| \< 2; CoAnchor\_UI^M taking 1 when auxiliary-layer readings are spliced across task instances; DirRoleAlign^M taking 1 when the formal dimensions and the directional duty classes do not form a bijection; or exhibit that the correspondence table omits a load-bearing symbol; or exhibit that the actual properties of some relation in the definition sequence differ from those declared.
