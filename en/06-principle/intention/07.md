# The General Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching

This chapter attempts a general proposition:

That Intention holds and that Intention Desire-Gate switching actually occurs on one of its switching comparison sequences necessarily means this: in the prior state of this sequence there is a real gate passage, through the Desire-Gate duty class \[G\_a\]; in the later state, the source-drive continuation of Intention itself really occurs, the probability organization bears, in that same continuation, the relative generation among the directions, and the direction thereby formed enters the Desire-Gate duty class \[G\_b\]; \[G\_a\] exits from bearing gate passage in the later state of this sequence; and this exit and the acquisition of \[G\_b\] belong together to the gate handoff borne by that one continuation in the later state, and are not borne separately by another, independent mechanism.

The question this raises is:

When Intention holds, when can one say that the Desire-Gate of Intention has switched? Does passing one gate count as switching? Does passing two gates one after the other count? If the gate state is altered from outside to another gate, does that count as switching by Intention? And what is it when the old gate happens to exit and the new gate happens to be obtained by Intention?

The degree to which this chapter answers the question must be stated in advance. This chapter proves only the necessary direction: if Intention Desire-Gate switching occurs, there is necessarily a certain structure. It does not prove that this structure suffices for Intention Desire-Gate switching to occur; the sufficient direction belongs to The Necessity Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching. This chapter does not prove Intention qualification or Intention-Action anew; how switching starts, proceeds, competes, fails, recovers and is sustained is left only as an interface and handed over to the three Intention-Dynamics chapters.

In this chapter, through sustained questioning, we seek where the proof ought to go and derive the General Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching.

Our objective is:

to distinguish Intention Desire-Gate switching from general gate-position switching, from current gate passage, from joint use and from externally forced gate swaps; to obtain the minimal necessary structure of Intention Desire-Gate switching, with no item in it absorbed by another; to show why the two ends must belong to one and the same comparison sequence, and why this binding is nevertheless not a further duty; and to give the minimality argument, the two inducing mechanisms, the boundaries and the breach paths.

The result first.

Formula of the Necessary Structure of Intention Desire-Gate Switching

Within the working domain Intent\_{F,t} = 1:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) ⇒ N\_IGS(σ)

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IGS(σ) := ∃w\_a, w\_b, \[G\_a\], \[G\_b\] \[ S1 ∧ S2 ∧ S3 ∧ S4 ∧ B\_same^IGS \]

S2　Intention's own current passage through the later gate (IntentDrivenCurrentGatePass): on the later-state witness w\_b, A1(w\_b) ∧ A2(w\_b) ∧ A3(w\_b, \[G\_b\]); that is, the source-drive continuation of Intention itself really occurs and enters 𝒫\_I, the probability organization bears, in the same continuation, the relative generation among the directions, and the formal direction thereby formed enters \[G\_b\] along the formal correspondence; on the sequence σ, Pass\_σ^+(\[G\_b\]) = 1.

S3　Prior-gate exit (PriorGateExit): Pass\_σ^+(\[G\_a\]) = 0; that is, \[G\_a\] no longer bears gate passage in the later state of σ. This is only a local exit on this sequence, not a deactivation of the whole system.

S4　Handoff bearing (HandoffBear): HandoffBear(w\_b; σ, \[G\_a\]) = 1; that is, the bearing state of \[G\_a\] in the later state of σ and the acquisition of \[G\_b\] belong to one and the same real gate-bearing event on w\_b; the later-state bearing state of \[G\_a\] is not borne separately by another, independent mechanism.

Difference of gate-duty identity is not an independent duty but a corollary: S2 ∧ S3 ⇒ \[G\_a\] ≠ \[G\_b\].

N\_IGS(σ) is the necessary core on a fixed comparison sequence σ: the σ that occurs in S1 to S4 and in B\_same^IGS is everywhere the same σ as in the antecedent and is not quantified anew. To say that at least one comparison sequence has the necessary core, write separately N\_IGS := ∃σ N\_IGS(σ); the main theorem and the interfaces of all chapters use N\_IGS(σ).

Placed at the head of the chapter, the above formula only announces the result and is not a premise of the subsequent derivation.

In one sentence

Desire-Gate switching of Intention is neither passing one gate nor seeing the gate change from one to another: it is that, on one and the same sequence, the prior state passes through one gate, the later state enters another gate through Intention's own source drive by way of probability, and the exit of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate belong together to the gate handoff borne by this one continuation.

Abstract

This chapter answers: when Intention holds and Intention Desire-Gate switching occurs, what structure must there be at minimum?

First, two things must be kept apart. General gate-position switching says only that the gate has changed from one to another; it can be brought about by external forcing, ordinary gating or other mechanisms. Intention Desire-Gate switching is the class of such switching that is made by Intention itself. This chapter studies the latter.

The questioning starts from current gate passage. Current passage through some Desire-Gate, taken alone, may be a first entry, with no “where it switched from”; so a real gate passage is required in the prior state. Given a prior gate, the later gate may still have been altered directly by an external selector or by ordinary gating, with Intention's own source drive and probability not bearing the continuation into the later gate; this is not switching by Intention, so the later gate is required to be obtained by Intention's own source drive, probability and direction on the same witness. If the later gate is obtained by Intention itself while the old gate still bears gate passage in the later state, this is continuation through the same gate or joint use with a newly added gate, and the gate has not been changed; so the old gate is required to exit in the later state of this sequence. That the two gates differ in duty identity follows from this and need not be set up separately. If the old gate exits and the new gate is also obtained by Intention itself, but the exit of the old gate happens to be borne separately by another, external mechanism, the two merely coincide; so the later-state bearing state of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate are required to belong to one and the same real gate-bearing event of the later state. The two ends must belong to one and the same comparison sequence and cannot be spliced together from different organizations or different sequences; this binding is a well-formedness discipline, not a fifth duty.

Each of the four items has a world lacking only it; none is absorbed by the others, and none can be deleted. Post-gate tuning command, manifestation, awareness, tuning results, behavior and effects do not enter the necessary core. Switching can be induced by two mechanisms, direct source-drive switching and switching induced by explicit reentry; both flow into one and the same chain of Intention's own source drive, probability and direction. Within the frozen semantic domain of this chapter there is no fifth independent duty and no independent cancelling condition. This chapter proves only the necessary direction and carries no new H.

Introduction

We say, “I was looking, and then I turned to listening.” Two different things are involved here: one is that a Desire-Gate is passed at this moment; the other is that the gate has changed from “looking” to “listening.” The first has already been discussed in The General Form of Intention-Action as the third layer of Intention-Action: the direction enters its corresponding Desire-Gate. Only the second is the question of this chapter.

The main book says: “Experiences can operate together and can also switch. What enables this is Intent.” Experiences can be used jointly at the same time or can switch one after another, and both are commanded by Intent. So switching must be kept apart from joint use; and the switching meant in this chapter must be enabled by Intention itself, not by something else changing the gate in its place. The main book also says that the process of switching Desire-Gates itself has no self-manifestation, and that only after switching to some Desire-Gate, when manifest circuits participate in the organization behind that gate, does manifestation take place. So switching comes before manifestation, and also before awareness.

The questions that arise from this are: Is passing one gate switching? Is passing two gates one after the other switching? If the gate state is altered from outside to another gate, is that switching by Intention? And what is it when the old gate happens to exit and the new gate happens to be obtained by Intention?

This chapter does not discuss whether Intention qualification and Intention-Action themselves hold; it does not set up the dynamical process by which switching starts, competes, fails, recovers and is sustained; nor does it discuss how switching is to be determined from external observation. These questions belong to the other chapters of this group form.

Preliminary Distinctions

## Section One　Working Domain and Object

The working domain of this chapter is Intent\_{F,t} = 1: what is studied is the change of gate-bearing between prior and later real gate passages within Intention that already holds. σ is the comparison sequence of one Desire-Gate switching, that is, the relation between prior and later gate passages that is declared for comparison; it is not an abstract Host-Body, slot or entity. The witnesses of the two ends are written w\_a and w\_b respectively. Pass\_σ^-(\[G\]) and Pass\_σ^+(\[G\]) read, respectively, whether gate passage in the prior state and in the later state of σ is borne by the Desire-Gate duty class \[G\].

This chapter does not require complete Intention-Action at its entry. Complete Intention-Action requires post-gate tuning command (A4), whereas Desire-Gate switching is a change of gate position at the layer of gate passage; The General Form of Intention-Action has already handed current gate passage over to the three Desire-Gate switching chapters; stopping at the gate is not complete Intention-Action, but the fact that “the gate has actually been passed” still holds.

## Section Two　Gate-Position Switching and Intention Desire-Gate Switching

The General Form of Intention-Action has already shown that the gate state can be changed by external forcing, ordinary gating or other mechanisms without going through the duty chain of Intention, so IntentAct ⇏ GateSwitch and GateSwitch ⇏ IntentAct. GateSwitch there is the fact of general gate-position switching: on σ the gate has changed from one duty class to another, regardless of who made the change.

What this chapter studies is not this general fact but the class within it that is made by Intention itself, written IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ). The relation between the two is:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IntentGateSwitch ⇒ GateSwitch,   GateSwitch ⇏ IntentGateSwitch

The world of an externally forced gate swap is GateSwitch = 1, IntentGateSwitch = 0. The determinations of The General Form of Intention-Action are fully retained in this chapter; the main theorem of this chapter speaks only of IntentGateSwitch.

## Section Three　Current Gate Passage and Desire-Gate Switching

Current gate passage (CurrentGatePass) says only that some Desire-Gate is really passed at this moment, not that the gate position has changed. The previous action was v\_a ⇝ G\_a, and this action is still v\_a ⇝ G\_a: the gate was passed both times, yet there was no switching. So:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;CurrentGatePass ≠ IntentGateSwitch

## Section Four　Desire-Gates Are Counted by Duty

Desire-Gates are counted by duty identity, not by physical node. One Desire-Gate duty can be borne jointly by several physical nodes, and one physical carrier can bear different Desire-Gate duties in different states. So to judge whether the two gates differ is to compare Desire-Gate duty classes \[G\], not physical nodes.

## Section Five　Joint Use and Switching

Experiences can operate together and can also switch. Two different Desire-Gates passed at the same time or one after the other may be the joint use of two action chains, or the addition of a new gate, or a switching on one and the same sequence. Merely seeing two different gates is not enough to say that switching has occurred.

## Section Six　External Induction and External Forcing

External conditions can trigger, disturb or change the field state and thereby induce a change of direction in Intention's own continuation; this is legal. If an external selector or ordinary gating directly alters the gate state to another gate, with Intention's own source drive and probability not bearing this continuation into the later gate, this is external forcing: it can be general gate-position switching, but it is not Intention Desire-Gate switching. External induction is not external bearing.

## Section Seven　Switching, Manifestation and Consciousness

The process of switching Desire-Gates itself has no self-manifestation; only after switching to some Desire-Gate, when manifest circuits participate in the organization behind that gate, does manifestation take place; Intention-Action occurs before awareness. So switching may already have occurred while there is as yet no manifestation and no awareness.

Line of Argument

We proceed by sustained questioning, step by step; the argument therefore forms a line and should be read from top to bottom.

Step One　Is Passing One Gate Already Switching?

We begin with the weakest case. At this moment there is a real gate passage, through the Desire-Gate \[G\_b\].

W-GS1　\[G\_b\] is currently passed, and there has been no gate passage at all on this sequence before; \[G\_b\] is being passed for the first time.

In W-GS1 there is gate passage, but no “where it switched from.” Switching says that the gate changes from one to another; with only one gate, there is nothing to change. So current gate passage alone does not license saying that switching has occurred. What is lacking is a real gate passage in the prior state of this sequence. This yields the first item:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S1 := PriorGatePass: on the prior-state witness w\_a there is a real, legal gate passage through the formal Desire-Gate duty class \[G\_a\]; Pass\_σ^-(\[G\_a\]) = 1.

What S1 requires is a real gate passage, not a historical label, an observer's annotation or a static reachability relation. It does not require complete Intention-Action in the prior state, does not require A4 in the prior state, and does not require the prior gate to be unique in the whole system.

Nor does S1 require a retrospective proof that \[G\_a\] was originally obtained by Intention's own source drive and probability. The task of S1 is only to provide a real prior-state reference for this switching; “who makes this switching” is borne by the later state (Steps Two and Four). If tracing the historical source of the old gate were made compulsory, it would exclude a legal case: \[G\_a\] is already a real prior state within the same gate system of Intention, and a new continuation of Intention itself then really changes direction from this prior state to \[G\_b\]. The main book does not require that the historical source of the old gate be the same as the current source of this switching.

Step Two　Given a Prior Gate and a Different Later Gate, Is That Already Switching?

Keep S1: the prior state passes through \[G\_a\]. The later state also has a real gate passage, through \[G\_b\].

W-GS2　In one and the same Intention qualification organization, the prior state passes through \[G\_a\]; then a complete external selector or ordinary forced gating directly alters the gate state to \[G\_b\], the old gate exits accordingly, and the organization merely executes this result; Intention's own source drive does not continue in the later state, nor does the probability organization bear, in the later state, the relative generation among the directions.

In W-GS2 the gate does change from \[G\_a\] to \[G\_b\]; this is an instance of general gate-position switching. But the main book says of joint use and switching: “What enables this is Intent.” Switching by Intention must be enabled by Intention. The later gate in W-GS2 was selected for Intention from outside; Intention did not itself go into it. This agrees with the determination of The General Form of Intention-Action: when an external selector directly decides which Desire-Gate is reached, skipping Intention's own source-drive continuation and probability bearing, that is not Intention acting. So W-GS2 is at most GateSwitch = 1, while IntentGateSwitch = 0.

Note further that requiring only that “the later state has a gate passage” is not enough either: the layer of gate passage itself says only that a direction has entered a gate, not whether this continuation was formed by Intention's own source drive by way of probability. What is lacking is that the later gate be obtained by Intention's own source-drive continuation, probability bearing and direction on the same witness. This yields the second item:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S2 := IntentDrivenCurrentGatePass: on the later-state witness w\_b, A1(w\_b) ∧ A2(w\_b) ∧ A3(w\_b, \[G\_b\]); Pass\_σ^+(\[G\_b\]) = 1.

That is: the source-drive duty of Intention itself really occurs in the later-state continuation and enters 𝒫\_I; in the same continuation, 𝒫\_I, as a probability organization, really bears the relative generation among the formal directions; and the formal direction thereby formed enters \[G\_b\] along the formal correspondence. That w\_b belongs to the later state of σ is guaranteed by co-binding (Step Five), so this gate passage is precisely the gate passage of the later state of σ.

S2 is stronger than ordinary current gate passage: a passage through the later gate alone is not enough; the later gate must be really obtained by Intention itself through the chain of source drive, probability and direction. An external stimulus can induce S2, as long as the actual bearing in the later state is still accomplished by A1, A2, A3. S2 does not require A4, does not require tuning success, and does not require manifestation or awareness.

The two ends are asymmetric here: S1 does not trace the source of the prior gate, while S2 requires the later gate to be obtained by Intention itself. The reason was given in Step One: the prior gate is only a reference, while the later gate bears “who makes this switching.”

Step Three　Given a Prior Gate and a Later Gate Obtained by Intention Itself, Is That Already Switching?

Keep S1, S2: the prior state passes through \[G\_a\], and in the later state Intention's own A1, A2, A3 go into \[G\_b\].

W-GS3　The prior state passes through \[G\_a\], and in the later state Intention's own continuation goes into \[G\_a\] again; that is, \[G\_b\] and \[G\_a\] are the same duty class.

W-GS3 is same-gate continuation, or repeated passage through the same Desire-Gate.

W-GS3-B　The prior state passes through \[G\_a\]; in the later state Intention itself newly obtains gate passage through \[G\_b\], while \[G\_a\] still bears gate passage in the later state of this sequence.

W-GS3-B is joint use or addition: a new gate has been added, and the old gate has not given way.

What the two worlds have in common is that the old gate still bears gate passage in the later state of this sequence. The gate has not been changed, so there is no switching to speak of. What is lacking is the exit of the old gate in the later state. This yields the third item:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S3 := PriorGateExit: Pass\_σ^+(\[G\_a\]) = 0.

S3 requires only that \[G\_a\] exit from the bearing of the later state of this sequence; it does not require \[G\_a\] to be globally deactivated in the whole system, in other joint-use chains or in other duties.

Corollary: the two gates differ in duty identity.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S2 ∧ S3 ⇒ \[G\_a\] ≠ \[G\_b\]

For S2 gives Pass\_σ^+(\[G\_b\]) = 1 and S3 gives Pass\_σ^+(\[G\_a\]) = 0; if \[G\_a\] and \[G\_b\] were the same duty class, the same duty would both bear and not bear on the same later state, which is a contradiction. So difference of gate-duty identity is not an extra duty but a result implied by the structure of gate change itself.

What is compared are Desire-Gate duty classes, not physical nodes. So: if the same Desire-Gate duty is realized by different physical nodes before and after, it is still the same duty class before and after, it still bears gate passage in the later state, S3 does not hold, and this is not switching; if the same physical carrier bears \[G\_a\] in the prior state and \[G\_b\] in the later state, the duty classes differ, and this can be switching.

Step Four　If the Old Gate Exits and the New Gate Is Obtained by Intention Itself, Is That Already Switching?

Keep S1, S2, S3: the prior state passes through \[G\_a\], \[G\_a\] exits in the later state, and in the later state Intention's own A1, A2, A3 go into \[G\_b\].

W-GS4　While Intention itself moves toward \[G\_b\], the exit of \[G\_a\] on this sequence happens to be borne separately by another, external mechanism, and does not belong to the same event as this continuation of Intention. The two things merely coincide in time.

In W-GS4 the old gate has indeed exited, and the new gate has indeed been obtained by Intention itself, but the exit of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate are not one and the same thing: the giving way of the old gate is borne by another mechanism and does not belong to the event borne by this continuation of Intention. This is not Intention switching from \[G\_a\] to \[G\_b\]; it is only Intention going into a new gate just when the old gate happened to be vacated.

So W-GS4 is not Intention Desire-Gate switching. What is lacking is that the later-state bearing state of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate belong to one and the same real gate-bearing event of the later state. This yields the fourth item:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S4 := HandoffBear: HandoffBear(w\_b; σ, \[G\_a\]) = 1; that is, the bearing state of \[G\_a\] in the later state of σ and the acquisition of \[G\_b\] belong to one and the same real gate-bearing event on w\_b; the later-state bearing state of \[G\_a\] is not borne separately by another, independent mechanism.

S4 reads only a relation of attribution; it does not repeat the content of the other items:

S4 does not say whether the prior state passed through \[G\_a\]; that is S1;  
S4 does not say whether the old gate has already exited; that is S3;  
S4 does not say whether this event on w\_b is a continuation of Intention itself, or whether it enters \[G\_b\] through source drive and probability; that is S2;  
S4 says only whether, in the later state of σ, the bearing state of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate are two sides of one and the same gate-bearing event on w\_b.

So S4 does not use “replacement” or “switching” as defining words, nor is it a renaming of “switching has already occurred”; it is not circular.

This attribution is an attribution of real bearing, not a counterfactual causal dependence. It does not assert that “without this event on w\_b, \[G\_a\] would not have exited”; it asserts only that in the real later state, the bearing state of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate belong to one and the same gate-bearing event, rather than being borne separately by mutually unrelated mechanisms. What the upstream has established is a class of attribution disciplines of same chain, same witness and no complete external bearing; S4 uses only this class of disciplines and adds no causal semantics.

Only the four items together state the full meaning of Intention Desire-Gate switching: S3 and S4 together make the exit of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate belong to one and the same gate-bearing event on w\_b; S2 says that this event is precisely the continuation in which Intention's own source drive enters \[G\_b\] by way of probability. Thus the exit of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate are both borne by one and the same continuation of Intention itself.

Step Five　Can the Two Ends Be Spliced Together from Different Sequences?

S1 to S4 have all been obtained. Question: can the two ends be taken from unrelated places?

W-Splice　w\_a belongs to action sequence I and w\_b to action sequence II, and I and II are independent of each other; or even, viewed globally, the old gate is seen to exit somewhere and the new gate to be entered somewhere. Or w\_a belongs to qualification organization X and w\_b to qualification organization Y.

In W-Splice the items may each be true separately, but these facts are not facts of one and the same switching. So the two ends must be bound to one and the same comparison sequence. Write this binding as B\_same^IGS; its minimum content is:

B1 Same-anchor: w\_a and w\_b are anchored to one and the same Intention qualification anchor K\_I.  
B2 Ordered: under a protocol frozen in advance, w\_a really precedes w\_b; two simultaneous, unordered events may not be put into an order arbitrarily.  
B3 Sequence attribution: by event lineage, control lineage, action records or other identity data frozen in advance, the two ends are judged to be the prior and later states of one and the same gate-change sequence σ; the same sequence may not be defined backward from “the gate finally seen to be different.”  
B4 Not spliced across qualification organizations: \[G\_a\] of organization X and \[G\_b\] of organization Y may not be spliced together into one switching.

The protocol on which the judgment rests is written Γ\_IGS and is frozen before comparison: what counts as the same qualification anchor, GateRoleID, what counts as a prior event and a later event, what counts as the same gate-change sequence, and how independent joint-use branches are distinguished from the prior and later states of one sequence. None of these may be altered after the result has been seen.

B\_same^IGS answers whether these facts are facts of one and the same declared event. It does not answer “what further duty Intention Desire-Gate switching requires.” So it is a well-formed binding discipline of witnesses, of the same kind as the common witness in Intention-Action and the co-anchor in Intention qualification, and not a fifth duty. Nor does it create a persistent entity such as a “switching slot”; it only prescribes whether two real action units can be put into the same σ for comparison.

Step Six　Can the Four Items Be Deleted?

Each of the four items has a world lacking only it, in which the other three all hold:

Delete S1: W-GS1. In the later state Intention itself goes into \[G\_b\], so S2 holds; there is no bearing by \[G\_a\] on σ at all, so S3 and S4 hold only vacuously, with no exit that needs to be borne separately; but there is no gate passage in the prior state, and no “where it switched from.” A world lacking S1 can only take this vacuous form, which shows precisely that what S1 bears is the independent content “there is a prior state to switch from,” not given by the other three items.

Delete S2: W-GS2. The prior state passes through \[G\_a\], \[G\_a\] exits in the later state, and this exit and the acquisition of \[G\_b\] belong together to the event borne by that one external execution on w\_b, so S1, S3, S4 all hold; but the later gate was altered by external forcing, and there is no real bearing by Intention's own A1, A2, A3. This is general gate-position switching, not Intention Desire-Gate switching.

Delete S3: W-GS3. The prior state passes through \[G\_a\], in the later state Intention's own continuation goes into \[G\_a\] again, and the later-state bearing state of \[G\_a\] is borne precisely by this continuation, so S1, S2, S4 all hold; but the old gate has not exited: this is same-gate continuation. W-GS3-B likewise lacks S3: the old gate still bears in the later state, which is joint use or addition; there the old gate is usually still borne by its own chain, so the world often lacks S4 as well; it therefore serves only as a supplementary example, and the counterexample lacking only S3 is W-GS3.

Delete S4: W-GS4. S1, S2, S3 all hold, but the exit of the old gate is borne separately by another, external mechanism and merely coincides with this continuation of Intention.

Delete B\_same^IGS: the facts of the items can be spliced together from different qualification anchors and different action sequences, and the identity of the event is lost (W-Splice). B\_same^IGS cannot be deleted, but it is a well-formedness discipline, not a fifth duty.

Each item has a counterexample lacking only it, so no item is absorbed by the others, and none of the four can be deleted. The difference in duty between the two gates has been obtained as a corollary and is not counted separately.

Step Seven　Do Post-Gate Tuning Command, Manifestation and Consciousness Enter the Necessary Core?

Question: does Intention Desire-Gate switching require that the action of the later state has already entered post-gate tuning command?

W-GS5　The later gate \[G\_b\] has already been really passed by Intention itself, and S1 to S4 and B\_same^IGS all hold; but this action then stops at the gate and does not enter post-gate tuning command: A4(w\_b) = 0.

In W-GS5 the gate has been changed. Step Eight　Joint Use, Local Switching and Physical Realization

Same-gate continuation: \[G\_a\] in the prior state and still \[G\_a\] in the later state; S3 = 0; not switching.

Joint use or addition: the existing w\_a passes through \[G\_a\], and a further w\_b passes through \[G\_b\]. If both are on the same σ and \[G\_a\] still bears in the later state, S3 = 0; if \[G\_b\] belongs to another, independent sequence, B\_same^IGS = 0. Even if \[G\_a\] ≠ \[G\_b\], this is not switching.

Local switching: in some legal σ, the prior state has Pass\_σ^-(\[G\_a\]) = 1, and the later state has Pass\_σ^+(\[G\_a\]) = 0 and Pass\_σ^+(\[G\_b\]) = 1; the later gate is obtained by Intention itself, and the exit of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate belong together to the gate handoff borne by this one continuation; meanwhile, elsewhere in the system there is still another legal action chain through \[G\_a\]. This is still Intention Desire-Gate switching on σ:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;local switching ≠ global deactivation of the old gate

Global gate change: if \[G\_a\] exits on all relevant branches and only \[G\_b\] remains, this is a special strong case of Intention Desire-Gate switching, not a general necessary condition.

Physical re-realization: the physical node changes from g\_1 to g\_2 while the GateRoleID stays the same; the duty of the old gate still bears in the later state, S3 = 0, and this is not switching. If the same physical carrier bears \[G\_a\] in the prior state and \[G\_b\] in the later state, the duty classes differ; when the other items hold, this can be switching.

Step Nine　Is There a Fifth Duty, or an Independent Cancelling Condition?

Keep S1 to S4 and B\_same^IGS holding. Question: is there in the frozen semantic domain a fifth necessary duty that cannot be assigned to these four items and the binding?

u21 and u30 both belong to Q3. Together they give two layers of requirement: first, joint use and switching must be kept apart; second, this change of gate must be enabled by Intention itself, and cannot be attributed to Intention merely because Intent = 1 and the gate state has changed. Q3 makes no further requirement of manifestation, consciousness, post-gate tuning command, a unique gate, comparison of probability values or global disappearance of the old gate.

u48, u49, u50 all belong to I9. Their logical direction is layering: the process of switching differs from manifestation; once the gate is reached, manifestation can occur only when manifest circuits behind the gate participate; action occurs before awareness. What this conjunction does is separate switching from manifestation and consciousness, not add conditions to switching.

The cross-combination of u30 with u48 to u50: “can operate together and can also switch” together with “switching itself does not manifest” shows only that switching is a change of gate that can be produced by Intention and is layered apart from manifestation; it adds no new duty of obtaining.

The combination with the upstream “correspondence between probability dimensions and Desire-Gates”: Desire-Gate duty classes correspond one-to-one to formal directions, which guarantees that the gate passages in S1 and S2 are formal gate events; it makes no further requirement of comparing probability values, a current maximum rate, a currently unique direction or a unique gate passage.

The combination with A4: A3 and A4 together are the last two layers of complete Intention-Action, but the truth value of Intention Desire-Gate switching does not need A4; adding A4 would wrongly exclude the world in which the gate is changed and action stops at the gate (W-GS5).

So, within the frozen semantic domain U\_IGS^src and the present upstream boundary:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;FifthIndependentIGSDuty\_I = ∅

We ask again: when S1 to S4 and B\_same^IGS all hold, is there still an independent factor that can by itself make switching fail to hold? Reviewing item by item: no A4, tuning not succeeding, experience not changing, no manifestation, no awareness, no behavior or effect: none of these cancels; external stimuli taking part in the induction do not cancel, as long as the later gate is still obtained by Intention's own A1, A2, A3 and the exit of the old gate still belongs together with the acquisition of the new gate to the gate handoff borne by this one continuation; other joint-use chains in the system, and the old gate still being active on other chains, do not cancel the local switching on this σ; a time gap between the two ends does not cancel as long as the sequence binding is still legal, since the main book does not require seamless switching; a brief overlap between the two ends does not by itself cancel, as long as S3 holds in the final later state, and the concrete process of competition is handed over to the Intention-Dynamics chapters; unreadable probability values are a problem of representation and do not cancel; the rate value of the old gate not having dropped to zero does not cancel, since the fact of gate passage and the probability rate value are not the same object; the new gate not being the “maximum-rate” gate does not cancel, since there is no selector in the system that reads off the maximum. So, within the frozen semantic domain:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IndependentCancel\_IGS = ∅

These two checks are only closure checks of the general form, showing that the necessary structure has no omission within the frozen semantic domain. Step Ten　General Formula and Main Theorem

The necessary core of Intention Desire-Gate switching:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IGS(σ) := ∃w\_a, w\_b, \[G\_a\], \[G\_b\] \[ S1 ∧ S2 ∧ S3 ∧ S4 ∧ B\_same^IGS \]

σ is a fixed comparison sequence, and S1 to S4 and B\_same^IGS are all read on this same σ. Here S1 = PriorGatePass, S2 = IntentDrivenCurrentGatePass, S3 = PriorGateExit, S4 = HandoffBear; B\_same^IGS is the co-binding discipline and is not counted as a fifth duty. Difference of gate-duty identity is the corollary S2 ∧ S3 ⇒ \[G\_a\] ≠ \[G\_b\] and is not counted as a duty.

Main theorem (Theorem of the Necessary Structure of Intention Desire-Gate Switching). Within the frozen semantic domain of this chapter, suppose Intent\_{F,t} = 1. If Intention Desire-Gate switching occurs on the comparison sequence σ, then:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) ⇒ N\_IGS(σ)

Proof. Suppose IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1.

Switching says that the gate changes from one to another; W-GS1 shows that without a prior-state gate passage there is no “where it switched from” (Step One). So on the prior-state witness w\_a there is a real gate passage through \[G\_a\]; that is, S1 holds.

By “What enables this is Intent” in Q3, Intention Desire-Gate switching is enabled by Intention; W-GS2 shows that a later gate altered by external forcing is only general gate-position switching, not switching by Intention (Step Two). So on the later-state witness w\_b, Intention's own source-drive continuation, probability bearing and the entry of direction into \[G\_b\] hold on the same witness; that is, S2 holds.

By “can operate together and can also switch” in Q3, switching is kept apart from joint use; W-GS3 and W-GS3-B show that if the old gate still bears gate passage in the later state, this is continuation through the same gate or joint use with a newly added gate, and the gate has not been changed (Step Three). So \[G\_a\] exits in the later state of σ; that is, S3 holds.

Again by “What enables this is Intent” in Q3, switching as one event is enabled by Intention, and the giving way of the old gate also lies within this event; W-GS4 shows that if the exit of the old gate is borne separately by another, external mechanism, the acquisition of the new gate merely coincides with it (Step Four). So the bearing state of \[G\_a\] in the later state of σ and the acquisition of \[G\_b\] belong to one and the same real gate-bearing event on w\_b; that is, S4 holds.

By W-Splice, the two ends must be same-anchor and ordered, belong to the same sequence, and not be spliced across qualification organizations (Step Five). So B\_same^IGS holds.

Taken together, on the same comparison sequence σ fixed by the antecedent, there exist w\_a, w\_b, \[G\_a\], \[G\_b\] such that S1 ∧ S2 ∧ S3 ∧ S4 ∧ B\_same^IGS holds; that is, N\_IGS(σ).

For the deletion test, see Step Six; for the exclusion of post-gate tuning command, manifestation and consciousness, see Step Seven; for the checks for a fifth duty and for cancelling conditions, see Step Nine. The proof is complete.

This theorem gives the necessary direction and does not prove its converse N\_IGS(σ) ⇒ IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ). Nor does this chapter obtain the theorem by defining IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) := N\_IGS(σ). This theorem does not speak of general GateSwitch: an externally forced gate swap can have GateSwitch = 1 while N\_IGS(σ) does not hold, and this does not conflict with this theorem.

Two Mechanisms Inducing Switching

Intention Desire-Gate switching can be induced by two mechanisms. They are not two definitions of switching, nor do they add duties; the difference between them lies before A1, that is, on the side of what induces the switching, and once they enter the necessary core they share one and the same S2, S3 and S4.

Direct source-drive switching. The current field state, already formed sedimentary relations or legal environmental disturbances change the relative generation that the probability organization of Intention really bears. Then a new source-drive continuation of Intention itself really occurs (A1); the same probabilized Law Ecology bears the new relative generation (A2); the real direction enters the new corresponding Desire-Gate \[G\_b\] (A3); and the exit of the old gate in the later state of this sequence and the acquisition of the new gate belong together to the gate handoff borne by this one internal continuation (S3, S4). Environmental disturbance can induce, but cannot act as an external selector that directly specifies \[G\_b\]; as long as the direction and the gate are still borne by Intention's own chain, it is switching by Intention.

Switching induced by explicit reentry. Musing, thinking, introspection or recollection, or experience that has already manifested, returns through legal circuits and changes the current dynamical state of Intention. Then it is still required that the new source-drive continuation occur in Intention itself (A1); that the relative generation be re-formed through the same probabilized Law Ecology (A2); that the new formally corresponding Desire-Gate \[G\_b\] be entered (A3); and that the exit of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate belong together to the gate handoff borne by this one internal continuation (S3, S4). Explicit reentry is only an antecedent that induces or modulates; it is not an observer reading off probability values and then selecting \[G\_b\] by hand; it may not bypass A1 and A2 to specify the later gate directly.

Whether musing or thinking circuits participate does not decide whether switching holds, only how this switching is induced. How reentry starts a switching and how switching proceeds belong to the three Intention-Dynamics chapters.

Breach Argument

K-GS1: There is a legal case of Intention Desire-Gate switching, but no real gate passage in the prior state.

K-GS2: There is a legal case of Intention Desire-Gate switching, but the later gate is not obtained by Intention's own A1, A2, A3 on the same witness. Minimal counterexample form: the later gate is directly specified by a complete external selector, and this case should still be judged Intention Desire-Gate switching, not merely general gate-position switching.

K-GS4: There is a legal case of Intention Desire-Gate switching, but the exit of the old gate in the later state is borne separately by another, independent mechanism and does not belong to the same gate-bearing event as the acquisition of the new gate.

K-GS5: There is a legal case of Intention Desire-Gate switching, yet under no Γ\_IGS that is frozen in advance and free of the conclusion to be proven can the two ends be bound as one and the same switching sequence. This would show that the present binding structure is too strong or incomplete.

K-GS6: Two independent legal action chains pass through \[G\_a\] and \[G\_b\] respectively, and the system still judges this to be Intention Desire-Gate switching because the gates differ. This would show that S3 or B\_same^IGS fails to exclude joint use.

K-GS7: Any one of A4, tuning success, experience change, manifestation or awareness is taken as a necessary condition of Intention Desire-Gate switching.

K-GS8: A change of physical node alone is judged to be switching, or switching is refused when the same physical carrier changes its GateRoleID.

K-GS9: \[G\_a\] is required to be completely deactivated in the whole system, so that a world in which the gate has been changed on this σ while \[G\_a\] is still legally used elsewhere is judged a failure.

K-GS10: Switching is acknowledged only when rate values, the maximum rate, rate-value crossing or a threshold are read off. This violates the upstream discipline that Intention does not read off probability.

K-GS11: An independent necessary duty X directly authorized by the corpus appears in the frozen semantic domain: when S1 to S4 and B\_same^IGS all hold, the failure of X makes switching fail to hold, and X cannot be assigned to the existing four items, the binding, consequence, dynamics or realization.

The following do not constitute a breach of this chapter: an externally forced gate swap being GateSwitch rather than IntentGateSwitch; action stopping at the gate after the gate is changed; no manifestation; no awareness; no tuning result or behavior; the old gate still being used elsewhere; a time gap between the two ends; a brief overlap during switching; unreadable probability values; a change of physical node with the duty unchanged not being judged switching; a world that satisfies N\_IGS(σ) on the same fixed comparison sequence σ yet has no IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ), which belongs to the sufficient direction and is dealt with by The Necessity Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching.

Closing

Starting from current gate passage, this chapter has pressed its questions all the way through: why passing one gate is not yet switching; why a gate altered from outside is not switching by Intention; why it is not switching when the later gate is obtained by Intention while the old gate still bears in the later state; why it is still not switching when the old gate happens to exit while the new gate is obtained by Intention; and why the two ends cannot be spliced together. Each step dealt only with the gap left by the step before; the four items of structure acquired load-bearing standing one by one along the chain of questions, each has a counterexample lacking only it, and all fall on one and the same comparison sequence; the difference in duty between the two gates is implied jointly by the later gate passage and the exit of the old gate.

The conclusions of this chapter compress into two sentences:

Desire-Gate switching of Intention is this: on one and the same sequence, the prior state passes through one gate, the later state enters another gate through Intention's own source drive by way of probability, and the exit of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate belong together to the gate handoff borne by this one continuation.

Intention Desire-Gate switching is not current gate passage, not seeing that the gate has changed, not joint use, and not an externally forced gate swap; it does not require that tuning has already been commanded behind the gate, does not require manifestation or consciousness, and does not require the old gate to disappear from the whole system.
