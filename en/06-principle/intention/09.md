# The Necessity Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching

## Section One　Purpose

To prove: when Intention already holds, on a fixed comparison sequence σ, provided that the following four items and the co-binding of the two ends all hold, an Intention Desire-Gate switching has occurred on σ, and no fifth independent switching condition need be satisfied.

The four items are:

First, real prior-gate passage: in the prior state of σ there is a real, legal gate passage through the Desire-Gate duty class \[G\_a\].

Second, Intention's own current passage through the later gate: in the later state of σ, the source-drive continuation of Intention itself really occurs and enters 𝒫\_I, the probability organization bears the relative generation among the directions within that same continuation, and the direction thus formed enters the Desire-Gate duty class \[G\_b\] along the formal correspondence.

Third, prior-gate exit: \[G\_a\] no longer bears gate passage in the later state of σ.

Fourth, handoff bearing: the bearing state of \[G\_a\] in the later state of σ and the acquisition of \[G\_b\] both belong to one and the same real gate-bearing event of the later state; the later-state bearing state of \[G\_a\] is not borne alone by another, independent mechanism.

Co-binding: the two ends, prior and later, are same-anchor and ordered, belong to one and the same σ, and do not cross qualification organizations.

In formulas:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IGS(σ) ⇒ IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ)

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IGS(σ) := ∃w\_a, w\_b, \[G\_a\], \[G\_b\] \[ S1 ∧ S2 ∧ S3 ∧ S4 ∧ B\_same^IGS \]

The working domain is Intent\_{F,t} = 1.

“No fifth independent switching condition need be satisfied” means specifically: whether, after the switching, the action enters the post-gate tuning command; whether it manifests; whether there is awareness; whether the tuning succeeds; whether experience is ultimately changed; whether there is behavior or effect; whether the old gate is still active elsewhere in the system; whether there is a time gap between before and after; whether there is a brief overlap during the transition; whether the new gate corresponds to the direction with the maximum current rate; whether the rate value of the old direction drops to zero; whether the switching was induced by an external stimulus or by explicit reentry; whether there are other candidates on the same σ; whether there are other legal action chains in the system: none of these affects the conclusion.

## Section Four　Rules of Classification

### 4.1 Classification

Each atom is assigned to one of the following classes according to its duty with respect to the question “does this Intention Desire-Gate switching on σ hold?”

Positive switching semantics U\_IGS^+: by virtue of what an Intention Desire-Gate switching on σ holds. By the question answered, this chapter reads two load-bearing points from the corpus:

U\_Switch　real switching: whether a switching of the gate from one duty class to another has really occurred on σ, rather than a first gate passage, a same-gate continuation, a joint use, an addition, or two gates pieced together from different sequences.  
U\_IntentBear　commanded by Intention: whether what enables this switching is Intention itself, rather than a complete external selector having decided the later gate on Intention's behalf, or Intention having merely acquired the new gate while the old gate happened to exit through another mechanism.

The two load-bearing points do not correspond one-to-one to S1 to S4; they are read independently from the corpus, and their number is decided by the corpus, not padded out to match the number of items in the four-item structure.

Cancelling side: atoms that, when the positive switching semantics are all realized, can on their own make IntentGateSwitch = 0.

Remaining semantics U\_IGS^O: the boundaries of switching, the consequences that follow switching, and the phase relation between switching and awareness. Each has theoretical significance, but none bears the universal obtaining duty of deciding whether this switching holds.

### 4.2 Criteria of classification

(a) Criterion of question. Ask whether the atom answers “by virtue of what does this switching hold,” or rather “does the switching have self-manifestation,” “what happens after the switching,” or “which comes first, switching or awareness.”

(b) Criterion of phase. Ask whether the atom speaks of something within the switching itself or of a consequence after the switching.

(c) Criterion of deletion. Suppose that what the atom speaks of does not appear in this switching, with the other atoms unchanged. If no sentence in the corpus therefore denies that this switching holds, the atom does not belong to the positive switching semantics. This criterion is used only to re-check atoms already assigned to U\_IGS^O under (a).

(d) Review of the cancelling side. If an atom looks formally like a negation or a restriction, it requires a dedicated review: supposing the positive switching semantics are all realized, can it on its own make IntentGateSwitch = 0? If, in order to take effect, it must make some positive semantics fail, it is not an independent cancelling condition.

### 4.3 Review of irreducible conjunctions

After the single atoms have been classified, the irreducible conjunctions authorized by the frozen corpus must still be checked. Two atoms that individually do not decide the truth value of switching may together produce a new force, so “the conjunction is not a switching condition either” cannot be inferred from “none of the parts is a switching condition.” If someone claims that some conjunction bears a new obtaining duty or cancelling duty, grounds explicitly authorizing that combination to bear the duty must be found in the frozen corpus; such authorization can occur within one sentence, or be formed by causal, conditional, referential, summarizing or anaphoric relations across sentences.

For a conjunction to become a fifth duty, it must also satisfy the following: when the positive switching semantics are all realized and the four items and the co-binding hold together, its failure makes IntentGateSwitch = 0, and this cancellation does not appear as the exit of any of the four items or of the co-binding.

## Section Five　Item-by-Item Classification of the Atoms

### 5.1 Positive switching semantics

u30　Experiences can operate together and can also switch. U\_Switch. Q3 sets “operating together” and “switching” side by side as two different ways in which experiences are invoked: joint use is several experiences being invoked at once; switching is turning from one to another. So a switching must be a turn from one gate to another, and it cannot be joint use: if the old gate is still bearing and the new gate has merely been added, that is joint use or addition, not switching. The word “switching” itself also excludes the case of a single gate and the case of the same gate before and after: with nowhere to switch from, or with the gate the same before and after, there is no turning from one to another. It answers whether this is a switching.

u21　What enables experiences to operate together or to switch is Intent. U\_IntentBear. Q3 says “What enables this is Intent”: switching is enabled by Intent. Applied to this switching, it requires that this change of gate be enabled by Intention itself, with both the yielding of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate belonging to what Intention does this time; if something external changes the gate on Intention's behalf, or Intention merely enters the new gate while the old gate exits through some other mechanism, neither is a case of “What enables this is Intent.” It answers who enables this switching.

### 5.2 Remaining semantics

u48　The process of switching Desire-Gates itself has no self-manifestation. U\_IGS^O, boundary. It says that the switching process carries no self-manifestation, not that switching requires something.

u49　Only after switching to a certain Desire-Gate, when manifest circuits participate in the organization behind that gate, does manifestation take place. U\_IGS^O, consequence. What it prescribes is the condition of manifestation: after switching to the gate, manifest circuits behind the gate must also participate. The phrase “switching to a certain Desire-Gate” in the sentence states both that the object of switching is a Desire-Gate and that, once the gate is reached, the switching is already complete; this point serves to clarify the object and the completion point of U\_Switch, and bears no separate obtaining duty.

u50　Intention-Action occurs before awareness. U\_IGS^O, phase boundary.

### 5.3 Review of the cancelling side

The atoms that look formally like negations or restrictions are u48, u49, u50.

u48, “has no self-manifestation”: what it denies is the self-manifestation of the switching process, not the switching. Suppose the positive switching semantics are all realized without any self-manifestation: no sentence in the corpus therefore makes IntentGateSwitch = 0. On the contrary, it shows that switching may lack self-manifestation to begin with.

u49, “only … does manifestation take place”: what it prescribes is the condition of manifestation, not the condition of switching. It says that manifestation comes later than switching and depends on the manifest circuits behind the gate; it does not say that switching depends on manifestation. Suppose the positive switching semantics are all realized and no manifest circuit behind the gate participates: manifestation does not occur, yet the switching has occurred all the same.

u50, “occurs before awareness”: I9 derives this sentence with “so” from “the switching process has no self-manifestation” and “manifestation only after reaching the gate”; switching is a change of action at the level of the Desire-Gate, and likewise lies before awareness. It excludes taking awareness as a condition of switching. Suppose the positive switching semantics are all realized and there is not yet awareness: the corpus states explicitly that the action has already occurred, and the switching has already occurred too.

None of the three can on its own make IntentGateSwitch = 0 when the positive switching semantics are all realized.

## Section Six　Closure of Switching Semantics

6.1 Theorem One　Theorem of the Semantic Closure of Intention Desire-Gate Switching

On the frozen switching semantic domain U\_IGS^src: the positive semantics of an Intention Desire-Gate switching on σ are exactly two: real switching, given by u30, and commanded by Intention, given by u21; the remaining atoms do not decide the truth value of each switching; the cancelling side is empty; and the frozen corpus contains no irreducible conjunction bearing a fifth duty or a cancelling duty. That is:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;U\_IGS^+ = { u21, u30 },   U\_IGS^O = { u48, u49, u50 }  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;FifthIndependentIGSDuty\_I = ∅,   IndependentCancel\_IGS = ∅

Proof

First, the positive semantics are exhausted. U\_IGS^src is a finite set, and all five atoms have been classified by the criteria of Section Four. Only u30 and u21 are assigned to the positive class; the other three are assigned by criteria (a) and (b) to boundary, consequence or phase relation, and re-checked by criterion (c): when a switching has no self-manifestation, when no manifestation takes place after the switching, or when there is not yet awareness of it, no sentence in the corpus therefore denies that the switching has occurred.

Second, no single atom bears a fifth duty. u30 bears “a switching, not a joint use, not a first gate passage, not a same-gate continuation”; u21 bears “enabled by Intention itself.” u48 is a boundary of the switching process, u49 is the condition of manifestation after switching, and u50 is the phase relation between action and awareness; none of them is a universal obtaining condition of each switching.

Third, the cancelling side is empty. u48, u49, u50 have been reviewed item by item under criterion (d). The following candidates are not independent cancelling conditions either:

Stopping at the gate after switching, without entering the post-gate tuning command: by u49, “switching to a certain Desire-Gate” already holds before anything happens behind the gate.  
No manifestation: by u48 and u49, switching can precede manifestation, and manifestation is a consequence of the participation of manifest circuits behind the gate.  
No awareness: by u50, action occurs before awareness.  
Tuning not succeeding, experience not ultimately changing, no behavior or effect: these all lie behind the gate and belong to what comes after the switching.  
The old gate still being active elsewhere in the system, in other joint-use chains or in other duties: u30 distinguishes joint use from switching with respect to one and the same gate change under comparison; joint use elsewhere does not turn this switching into joint use.  
A time gap between before and after: the corpus does not require switching to be seamless.  
A brief overlap during the transition: what u30 distinguishes is whether the result of switching is a turn from one to another or two being invoked at once; with brief coexistence during the transition, the result is still a switching, provided the old gate has yielded in the later state.  
The new gate not being the gate corresponding to the direction with the maximum current rate, the rate value of the old direction not having dropped to zero: the corpus does not decide switching by rate values; Intention does not read probabilities, and rate values are not conditions of switching.  
An external stimulus taking part in the induction: u21 requires that what enables this be Intention; the external world can induce it, while what bears this switching can still be Intention itself.  
Musing, thinking, introspection, recollection or already manifested experience returning through legal circuits and inducing this switching: this is the source of the induction, not the bearer; as long as the subsequent source drive, probability and gate passage are still borne by Intention itself, u21 is realized all the same.  
Other candidates on the same σ, other legal action chains in the system: the corpus does not require that there be only one candidate or one chain at a time.

Fourth, there is no irreducible conjunction bearing a fifth duty or a cancelling duty. What is reviewed here is not “several atoms appearing together in one sentence,” but whether the frozen corpus gives a joint authorization across two or more atoms, that is, takes the joint holding of these atoms itself as grounds for obtaining or cancelling this switching.

First, authorization within one sentence is reviewed.

Q3 writes u30, “can operate together and can also switch,” and u21, “What enables this is Intent,” in one sentence. Taken together they say: both the joint use and the switching of experiences are enabled by Intent. Applied to one switching, this gives two things, “this time it is a switching” and “this time it is enabled by Intention,” which are exactly U\_Switch and U\_IntentBear; the sentence adds no third thing beyond these two, such as that switching must be accompanied by joint use, must have some result, or must be completed at some moment.

I9 writes u48, u49, u50 in one sentence, all of them belonging to U\_IGS^O. The conclusion of the sentence is “Intention-Action occurs before awareness”; it precisely excludes the reading that adds manifestation or awareness as a condition, and does not authorize the combination of these boundaries and consequences to bear an obtaining duty or a cancelling duty for switching.

Next, the conjunctions between positive atoms and remaining atoms are reviewed. u21 ∧ u48, u21 ∧ u49, u21 ∧ u50: taking “enabled by Intent” together with “switching has no self-manifestation,” “manifestation only after switching” and “action before awareness” says only that a switching enabled by Intention can be without self-manifestation and can precede manifestation and awareness; it produces no third obtaining duty. u30 ∧ u48, u30 ∧ u49, u30 ∧ u50: taking “a switching, not a joint use” together with these boundaries says only that switching is a change of gate on a layer separate from manifestation; it produces no new switching condition. u48 ∧ u49, u49 ∧ u50, u48 ∧ u49 ∧ u50: the frozen corpus does not authorize these combinations themselves to decide the truth value of switching.

Then authorization across sentences is reviewed. The relation between Q3 and I9 is this: Q3 says that switching is enabled by Intent; I9 says that the process of switching has no self-manifestation, that manifestation comes only after the gate is reached, and that action precedes awareness. The “switching to a certain Desire-Gate” of I9 takes up the “switch” of Q3, stating that the object of switching is a Desire-Gate and that switching is complete once the gate is reached; it serves only to clarify the object and the completion point of U\_Switch. No cross-sentence relation additionally authorizes, as a fifth duty or an independent cancelling condition, the joint holding of two or more atoms that by themselves do not decide the truth value of switching.

The cases that really can block an Intention Desire-Gate switching all take effect by making some positive semantics fail: with only one gate, with the same gate before and after, with the old gate still bearing while the new gate merely joins, or with before and after taken from different sequences, u30 is not realized; with the later gate selected on Intention's behalf by something external, or with the exit of the old gate borne alone by another mechanism, u21 is not realized. These are absences of positive semantics, not independent cancelling conditions.

By the four points above, Theorem One is proven. □

This conclusion is obtained independently by this chapter on the frozen switching semantic domain, and does not cite the results of the checks of the same name in The General Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching.

## Section Seven　Semantic Realization

7.1 Theorem Two　Theorem of the Semantic Realization of Intention Desire-Gate Switching

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IGS(σ) ⇒ u30 ∧ u21

Specifically:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S1 ∧ S2 ∧ S3 ∧ B\_same^IGS ⇒ u30  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S2 ∧ S4 ∧ B\_same^IGS ⇒ u21

Both are read on the same σ and the same set of witnesses (w\_a, w\_b, \[G\_a\], \[G\_b\]). Both are joint realizations across items: the full meaning of a real switching requires the prior gate, the later gate, the exit of the old gate and the same sequence to be in place together; the full meaning of being commanded by Intention requires that the later gate be acquired by Intention itself, that the yielding of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate both belong to the real gate handoff corresponding to that one continuation on w\_b, and that before and after belong to the same sequence. This must not be misrepresented as “each item on its own realizes one complete semantic atom.”

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;S1    S2    S3    S4    B\_same  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;u30       ✓     ✓     ✓           ✓  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;u21             ✓           ✓     ✓

Proof

First, S1 ∧ S2 ∧ S3 ∧ B\_same^IGS ⇒ u30.

S1 gives: in the prior state of σ there is a real, legal gate passage through \[G\_a\], Pass\_σ^-(\[G\_a\]) = 1. So there is a place “switched from”; this is not a first gate passage.

S2 gives: in the later state of σ there is a real gate passage through \[G\_b\], Pass\_σ^+(\[G\_b\]) = 1. So there is a place “switched to.”

S3 gives: \[G\_a\] no longer bears gate passage in the later state of σ, Pass\_σ^+(\[G\_a\]) = 0. So the old gate has yielded, and the new gate was not added while the old gate was still in place; this excludes joint use and addition.

By S2 ∧ S3, \[G\_a\] ≠ \[G\_b\]: if the two were the same duty class, the same duty would both bear and not bear in the same later state, a contradiction. So before and after are two different gates, not a same-gate continuation; since what is compared is the duty class, the case in which the physical node changes while the duty stays the same is also excluded.

B\_same^IGS gives: the two ends, prior and later, are same-anchor and ordered, belong to one and the same σ, and do not cross qualification organizations. So the prior state and the later state are the before and after of one and the same gate change, not pieced together from two independent action chains or two organizations.

Together: on the same σ, the gate has turned from \[G\_a\] to another duty class \[G\_b\], the old gate yielding and the new gate bearing, before and after belonging to one and the same gate change. This is the switching that u30 speaks of, not joint use; from it the general gate-position switching also follows:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IGS(σ) ⇒ GateSwitch\_F(σ)

This step does not use S4. General gate-position switching does not ask who made it; S4 bears its load in the next step.

Second, S2 ∧ S4 ∧ B\_same^IGS ⇒ u21.

S2 gives: on the later-state witness w\_b, A1(w\_b) ∧ A2(w\_b) ∧ A3(w\_b, \[G\_b\]). That is, the one continuation on w\_b is the real occurrence of the source-drive duty of Intention itself, entering 𝒫\_I; the probability organization bears the relative generation among the directions within the same continuation, and the direction thus formed enters \[G\_b\] along the formal correspondence. The new gate was not selected on Intention's behalf by a complete external selector; Intention itself walked into it through the chain of source drive, probability and direction.

But S2 alone is not enough. Intention can walk into \[G\_b\] on its own while \[G\_a\] happens to exit through another mechanism; then the new gate is acquired by Intention, but the yielding of the old gate does not lie within the gate handoff of this continuation of Intention. The two things merely coincide, and one cannot say of this switching that “What enables this is Intent.”

S4 gives: the bearing state of \[G\_a\] in the later state of σ and the acquisition of \[G\_b\] both belong to one and the same real gate-bearing event on w\_b, and the later-state bearing state of \[G\_a\] is not borne alone by another, independent mechanism. The event on w\_b in S4 is exactly the continuation on w\_b in S2: both are designated by the same later-state witness w\_b; S2 reads the internal chain of this continuation, and S4 reads what this event bore. So the yielding of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate both belong to the real gate handoff on that one continuation of Intention itself identified by S2.

B\_same^IGS gives: this later-state handoff and the prior state of S1 belong to the same σ and the same qualification organization, and are not pieced together from elsewhere.

Together: in this change of gate from \[G\_a\] to \[G\_b\], the yielding of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate both belong to the real gate handoff corresponding to this continuation of Intention itself on w\_b. This is what u21's “What enables this is Intent” means when applied to this switching.

S2 and S4 are each indispensable here. S2 can show only that the new gate is acquired by Intention itself, not that the whole switching is borne by Intention; S4 can show only that the state of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate belong to one event, not that this event is a continuation of Intention itself. Only when joined on the same w\_b do the two make “What enables this is Intent” concrete as the real bearing of this switching.

Third, same sequence and same witnesses. The antecedent of N\_IGS(σ) is ∃w\_a, w\_b, \[G\_a\], \[G\_b\] \[ S1 ∧ S2 ∧ S3 ∧ S4 ∧ B\_same^IGS \], with σ fixed by the main proposition, and not (∃ S1) ∧ (∃ S2) ∧ (∃ S3) ∧ (∃ S4). So both realizations above occur in the same gate change on the same σ: the gate really switched from one to another, and this switching was enabled by Intention itself. What is proven is not that “several things once occurred separately,” but one Intention Desire-Gate switching.

By the three points above, N\_IGS(σ) ⇒ u30 ∧ u21, that is, N\_IGS(σ) completely realizes U\_IGS^+. □

7.2 The vacuous case of S4 bears no load in the main theorem

To show that S1 is not absorbed by the other items, The General Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching admits a world in which there is no bearing of \[G\_a\] on σ at all, and S3 and S4 hold only vacuously. The Mathematical Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching correspondingly sets up a vacuous attribution branch for S4, open only when the old-gate state domain is confirmed to be empty.

The antecedent of this chapter is N\_IGS(σ) = 1, in which S1 holds: the prior state of σ does contain a real gate passage through \[G\_a\]. So under the antecedent of this chapter the old-gate side does have a state requiring attribution, and S4 cannot hold vacuously; it must be the real attribution, on w\_b, of the later-state bearing state of the old gate together with the acquisition of the new gate. It is exactly this real attribution that the second realization above uses. No step in the proof of the main theorem depends on a vacuous S4.

7.3 The difference of gate duties is not a fifth condition

“The two gates, before and after, have different duties” is not set up separately as a fifth antecedent. It already follows from S2 ∧ S3 and bears load in the first realization; setting it up again as a separate item would double-count what S2 and S3 already realize. Likewise, “the switching is already complete” is not set up as an antecedent: switching is complete once the gate is reached (u49), and the closure of the switching already lies in the gate passage of S2, the yielding of the old gate in S3 and the handoff attribution in S4.

## Section Eight　Theorem of the Necessity of Intention Desire-Gate Switching

8.1 Theorem Three　Theorem of the Necessity of Intention Desire-Gate Switching

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IGS(σ) ⇒ IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ)

Proof

Suppose Intent\_{F,t} = 1, let σ be a fixed comparison sequence, and suppose N\_IGS(σ) = 1. By Theorem Two, all the positive switching semantics in the frozen switching semantic domain, u30 and u21, are completely realized on the same σ and the same set of witnesses: on σ the gate really switched from \[G\_a\] to \[G\_b\], and the yielding of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate both belong to the real gate handoff corresponding to this continuation of Intention itself on w\_b. By Theorem One, on the frozen switching semantic domain the positive switching semantics are exactly these two; there is no fifth duty, no independent cancelling condition, and no irreducible conjunction bearing a fifth duty or a cancelling duty. Therefore all the positive semantics of an Intention Desire-Gate switching on σ have been obtained, and no independent factor cancels it. By the corpus-domain declaration of Section Three, IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1. □

What is obtained here is not “a structure similar to Intention Desire-Gate switching.” The four items and the co-binding completely realize, on the same σ, all the positive switching semantics obtained by independent analysis of the frozen corpus; the proof does not depend on defining IntentGateSwitch as N\_IGS(σ).

Nor does the proof take the route “GateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1 and Intent = 1, so IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1.” The working domain Intent = 1 says only that Intention qualification already holds, not that this change of gate was made by Intention; in a world of externally forced gate alteration, Intent = 1 and GateSwitch = 1 can hold together while IntentGateSwitch = 0. Being commanded by Intention must be obtained through the real bearing of S2 and S4 on w\_b.

### 8.2 Anti-sufficiency worlds

Let

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;W\_IGS^- := { w ∣ N\_IGS(σ, w) = 1 ∧ IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ, w) = 0 }

On the frozen switching semantic domain, in the working domain Intent = 1 and for a fixed σ,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;W\_IGS^- = ∅

Proof: suppose w ∈ W\_IGS^-. Then IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ, w) = 0, so by the corpus-domain declaration either some positive switching semantics is not realized, or some cancelling condition takes effect. The former contradicts Theorem Two: N\_IGS(σ, w) = 1 already realizes u30 and u21 on the same σ. The latter contradicts Theorem One: the set of cancelling conditions is empty, and there is no irreducible conjunction bearing a cancelling duty. So W\_IGS^- is empty. □

W\_IGS^- = ∅ is a conclusion within the frozen switching semantic domain and the working domain, not an absolute assertion about every possible theory. It shows where the necessity comes from: not from Intention Desire-Gate switching having been defined as the four-item structure, but from all the positive switching semantics having been completely realized by the four items and the co-binding on the same σ, with no independent cancelling item.

### 8.3 Consistency back-test

The General Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching checked, on its frozen semantic domain, that there is no fifth universal duty and no independent cancelling condition. This agrees with the conclusion of Theorem One of this chapter. The necessary direction and the sufficient direction have obtained compatible results on the same semantic domain. This back-test takes no part in the proof of Theorem Three.

### 8.4 Two-way closure

The General Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching: IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) ⇒ N\_IGS(σ), Status = Established.

Theorem Three: N\_IGS(σ) ⇒ IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ), Status = Established.

Combining the two gives:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) ⇔ N\_IGS(σ)

Two-way formula Status = Established. This is not an identity by definition, but the composition of two directions, each proven independently in its own chapter. It holds for each fixed comparison sequence σ; a conclusion about one σ does not imply conclusions about other comparison sequences.

Intention Desire-Gate switching thus has a complete structural characterization: on a comparison sequence, the prior state really passes through one gate; in the later state the source drive of Intention itself enters another gate by way of probability; the old gate yields in the later state; the yielding of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate both belong to the real gate handoff on this one continuation of Intention itself; and before and after are the two ends of one and the same gate change.

## Section Nine　Boundary Cases

Each case below is taken with respect to a fixed σ.

### 9.1 First gate passage

In the later state Intention itself enters \[G\_b\], and the prior state of σ has no gate passage at all. S1 does not hold, so N\_IGS(σ) = 0; the case does not fall under the antecedent of Theorem Three and does not constitute an anti-sufficiency world.

### 9.2 Same-gate continuation

The prior state passes through \[G\_a\], and in the later state the continuation of Intention itself enters \[G\_a\] again. S3 does not hold, so N\_IGS(σ) = 0; this does not constitute an anti-sufficiency world.

### 9.3 Joint use and addition

In the later state Intention itself newly acquires \[G\_b\], while \[G\_a\] still bears gate passage in the later state of σ; or \[G\_b\] belongs to another sequence unrelated to the prior state. S3 or B\_same^IGS does not hold, so N\_IGS(σ) = 0; this does not constitute an anti-sufficiency world.

### 9.4 Externally forced gate alteration

The prior state passes through \[G\_a\]; a complete external selector or ordinary forced gating changes the gate state directly to \[G\_b\], and the old gate exits accordingly. Here GateSwitch\_F(σ) can hold, and S1, S3, S4 can all hold, but S2 does not hold, so N\_IGS(σ) = 0 and IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 0. This is a general gate-position switching; it does not fall under the antecedent of Theorem Three and does not constitute an anti-sufficiency world.

### 9.5 Coincidental exit

In the later state Intention itself acquires \[G\_b\], and \[G\_a\] does exit in the later state of σ, but the state of the old gate in the later state is borne by another, independent event. S1, S2, S3 hold but S4 does not, so N\_IGS(σ) = 0; this does not constitute an anti-sufficiency world.

9.6 Splicing across sequences and across organizations

The prior state is taken from action sequence I and the later state from action sequence II, independent of it; or before and after belong to two different qualification organizations. B\_same^IGS does not hold, so N\_IGS(σ) = 0; this does not constitute an anti-sufficiency world.

### 9.7 Physical re-realization

The physical nodes before and after differ, while the GateRoleID is the same. The duty of the old gate is still borne in the later state, so S3 does not hold and N\_IGS(σ) = 0; this is not a switching of duty. When one and the same physical carrier bears \[G\_a\] in the prior state and \[G\_b\] in the later state, and the other items hold, N\_IGS(σ) = 1 and IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1.

### 9.8 Stopping at the gate

N\_IGS(σ) = 1, and the action of the later state then stops at the gate without entering the post-gate tuning command. IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1: switching is complete once the gate is reached, and the incompleteness of the action does not cancel the switching.

9.9 No manifestation, no awareness, no result

N\_IGS(σ) = 1; no manifest circuit behind the gate participates, the system is not yet aware, the tuning does not succeed, experience does not change, and there is no behavior or effect. IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1.

### 9.10 The old gate still active elsewhere

N\_IGS(σ) = 1, and elsewhere in the system another legal action chain still passes through \[G\_a\]; it does not belong to the later state of σ. IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1: this is a local switching on σ, and global deactivation of the old gate is not required.

### 9.11 Time gap and brief overlap

N\_IGS(σ) = 1; there is a gap between before and after, or the old gate and the new gate briefly coexist during the transition, but in the later-state evaluation the old gate has already yielded. IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1.

9.12 External induction, direct source drive and explicit reentry

An external stimulus changes the field state and induces a new source-drive continuation of Intention itself to change direction; or, with no external disturbance, the current field state and the established sedimentary relations directly cause the source-drive continuation to change direction; or musing, thinking or already manifested experience returns through legal circuits and changes the current dynamical state of Intention, after which the new source drive still changes direction through the same probabilized Law Ecology. As long as A1, A2, A3 of S2 hold on w\_b, and the handoff of S4 belongs to the real gate handoff corresponding to this continuation on w\_b, N\_IGS(σ) = 1 and IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1. If a so-called reentry bypasses source drive and probability and assigns the later gate directly, S2 does not hold, and the case does not fall under the antecedent.

### 9.13 Multiple candidates and rate values

There are several candidates on the same σ, one of which satisfies N\_IGS(σ); the new gate is not the gate corresponding to the direction with the maximum current rate; the rate value of the old direction has not dropped to zero. IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1. A unique candidate is not required, and switching is not judged by rate values.

### 9.14 Incidental consequences

N\_IGS(σ) = 1, and after the switching, manifestation, behavior and effect also occur. IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1; the appearance of consequences does not change the holding of the switching.

## Section Eleven　Breach

The case directly breaching the main theorem is:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IGS(σ) = 1 ∧ IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 0

By the level at which they occur, breaches divide into the following classes. If a breach of any class appears, the matter must be re-reviewed:

Breach One　Omission in the semantic domain: outside the frozen switching semantic domain there exist sentences that already belong to the current theory and directly decide the truth value of an Intention Desire-Gate switching, and that cannot be assigned to U\_IGS^+ or U\_IGS^O.

Breach Two　Counterexample of a fifth duty: there exists a single atom or an irreducible conjunction with grounds in the frozen corpus that, while the four items and the co-binding hold together, is still a universal additional obtaining condition of IntentGateSwitch.

Breach Three　Failure of the realization mapping: N\_IGS(σ) = 1, but GateSwitch\_F(σ) = 0, or the real bearing of this switching is not attributable to Intention itself, showing that the mapping of Section Seven is mistaken.

Breach Four　Anti-sufficiency world: there exists a legal w with N\_IGS(σ, w) = 1 but IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ, w) = 0.

Breach Five　Counterexample of independent cancellation: there exists an X such that, with the four items and the co-binding all still holding, X still independently makes IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 0, and X is authorized by the frozen corpus.

The following erroneous formulations also breach this chapter: defining IntentGateSwitch as N\_IGS(σ); taking the “no fifth duty, no independent cancelling condition” of The General Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching as a premise of the main proof; using Read\_{N\_IGS(σ)}^M = 1 directly as the antecedent of the main proof without reading back through The Mathematical Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching to the object-layer N\_IGS(σ); adding H\_PAct-Rep to the main sufficient direction of this chapter; inferring IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1 directly from Intent = 1 and GateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1, bypassing S2 and S4; assigning u21 wholly to S2 and dropping S4's bearing of “the yielding of the old gate and the acquisition of the new gate belong to this one gate handoff”; assigning u30 wholly to S3 and dropping the bearing by S1, S2 and the co-binding of “from where to where, and one and the same gate change”; letting the vacuous case of S4 bear load in the main proof while ignoring that S1 already gives a real prior gate; setting up “the two gates, before and after, have different duties” or “the switching is already complete” as a fifth antecedent; adding the post-gate tuning command, tuning success, manifestation, awareness, experience change, behavior or effect to the sufficient antecedent; adding maximum rate, rate-value crossing, thresholds or the dropping of the old direction's rate value to zero to the sufficient antecedent; requiring global deactivation of the old gate; requiring the switching to have no gap or no transitional overlap; judging external induction or explicit reentry in itself to be external bearing even when S2 and S4 are both borne by Intention itself; requiring a unique candidate on a σ; treating the number of switching candidates as a count of Intention; inferring conclusions about other comparison sequences from the conclusion on one σ.

The following do not constitute a breach:

Read\_{N\_IGS(σ)}^M = ⊥: this only indicates insufficient formal material.

Externally forced gate alteration is GateSwitch, not IntentGateSwitch: its S2 does not hold, and it does not fall under the antecedent.

Stopping at the gate after switching, with no manifestation, no awareness, no tuning result or behavior: these do not belong to the obtaining conditions of switching.

The old gate still being used elsewhere, a gap between before and after, a brief overlap during the transition, the new gate not being of maximum rate, external factors or reentry taking part in the induction, multiple candidates on the same σ: none of these cancels the switching.

Worlds that satisfy part of the four items and the co-binding while N\_IGS(σ) does not hold: they do not fall under the antecedent of the main theorem.
