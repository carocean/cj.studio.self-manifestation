# The Inversion Form of Intention

## Section One　Purpose

Under a legal observation protocol Π frozen in advance, and given an actual observation y, to determine: at most, what can be known from y about whether Intention has dynamics on a segment of a declared trajectory γ; at most, which parts of the legal dynamics segment L1 to L6 and of the three sites, namely source drive, bearing Law and probability organization, can be known; when indiscernibility has already been proven, when the preimage has merely not been exhaustively searched, when only the dynamics classification of a candidate itself has not been closed, and when two-way closure cannot legally be invoked to sign a value for the whole segment γ of a candidate.

Observation does not reduce the internal system to a single answer; it only narrows the possible internal systems down to a preimage. Whether Intention-Dynamics can be identified from it depends not on how much the result looks like Intention moving, but on whether this preimage can still accommodate, at the same time, legal candidates with Intention-Dynamics and legal candidates without it.

This chapter does not redefine IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I), N\_IDyn^γ, LegalIntentDynSegment\_F^γ(K\_I; Γ\_IDyn), SourceOccurrenceDyn\_I^γ, CarrierDyn\_I^γ or IntentProbDyn\_I^γ; it adds no fourth site of dynamics, does not rebuild the formal system of The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics, and does not answer how to check whether a real instance has Intention-Dynamics.

### 1.1 Frozen upstream conclusions

The General Form of Intention-Dynamics:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IDyn^γ := LegalIntentDynSegment\_F^γ(K\_I; Γ\_IDyn) ∧ DynActual\_I^γ  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DynActual\_I^γ := SourceOccurrenceDyn\_I^γ ∨ CarrierDyn\_I^γ ∨ IntentProbDyn\_I^γ

The six items of the legal segment are: L1, γ is a segment of a real running trajectory of the Sedimentation-Field; L2, every state within the segment is produced by the legal running of the field, with no out-of-segment write-in; L3, the qualification anchor is not replaced; L4, Intention qualification persists at every field state of γ; L5, the evidence for the three sites is taken from the same γ and the same run; L6, the protocols of identification, comparison and attribution are frozen before the results are seen. Necessary direction IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I) ⇒ N\_IDyn^γ, Status = Established.

The Necessity Form of Intention-Dynamics: sufficient direction N\_IDyn^γ ⇒ IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I), Status = Established, carrying no H. Combining the two chapters, within the common working domain:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I) ⇔ N\_IDyn^γ

The base working domain is:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;W\_IDyn := Intent\_{F,t\_0} = 1

that is, Intention holds at the starting point of γ. The persistence of qualification over the whole segment is the structural component L4 of LegalIntentDynSegment; it is not merged into the base working domain, nor is it made a separate working-domain assumption. Hence:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IDyn^γ = 1 ⇒ IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I) = 1, carrying no H;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;N\_IDyn^γ = 0 ⇒ IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I) = 0; this step is the contrapositive of the necessary direction and adds no condition; if N\_IDyn^γ = 0 itself is obtained from the negative read-back of The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics, it carries the conditions of the chosen refutation path.

The contrapositive step is further restricted by the L4 discipline; see below.

The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics: the three-valued formal system and positive read-back Read\_{N\_IDyn^γ}^M = 1 ⇒ N\_IDyn^γ = 1; negative read-back follows the chosen refutation path (7.3 of that chapter). That chapter also closes the following disciplines, all of which this chapter keeps in force:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;EvidenceSplice ⇏ N\_IDyn^γ = 0  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;CarrierBindMismatch ⇏ CarrierDyn\_I^γ = 0  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;ProbBindMismatch ⇏ IntentProbDyn\_I^γ = 0  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;L5 takes only 1 or ⊥, never 0

A 0 for L4 means that qualification exits or re-forms within γ: it can be read back as this declared segment not being a legal dynamics segment of one and the same Intention, that is, N\_IDyn^γ = 0; but this declared segment thereby falls out of the working domain (The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics, 7.3; the interface of The Necessity Form of Intention-Dynamics) and is not read back as IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I) = 0; it should be re-segmented at the boundary of the exit from qualification. So when L4 = 0, the whole segment γ no longer uses the contrapositive chain N\_IDyn^γ = 0 ⇒ IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I) = 0. This is the special-case discipline of L4, not a merging of L4 into the base working domain.

Hence, when this chapter is to invert the whole segment γ as one segment of dynamics of one and the same Intention, it requires, besides the base working domain, that L4 = 1 has already been signed; this requirement is a whole-segment applicability condition of the inversion task, not a new condition of IntentDyn.

### 1.2 Three epistemic levels

This chapter distinguishes three levels concerning Intention-Dynamics:

Truth-value level: whether Intention has dynamics on γ.  
Site level: which of the three sites are moving.  
Detail level: how exactly a given site moves internally; for example, whether the source drive starts, persists, stops or undergoes re-onset after stopping, which bearing Law rises, falls or migrates, whether it is the relations or the structure of the probability organization that changes, and whether there is intermediate restoration.

This chapter proves that among the three levels there is no general top-down implication: identifiability of the truth value does not imply identifiability of position, and identifiability of position does not imply identifiability of detail; a bottom-up implication requires that the information identified at the lower level suffice to determine the upper-level target.

## Section Two　Observation Protocol, Forward Mapping and Admission to Inversion

### 2.1 Observation protocol

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Π = (D\_Π, Y\_Π, H\_Π, Prot\_Π)

D\_Π: the domain of legal candidate internal systems. Each member X is a fully specified candidate internal world, including at least: the Sedimentation-Field F, the declared trajectory γ, the qualification anchor K\_I, the comparison and attribution protocol Γ\_IDyn, the history of Intention qualification on γ, the identities of runs and branches, the history of internal updates and write-ins, the real history of the source-drive duty \[S\_I\]\_src, the history of the bearing Laws and their degrees, the formal probability organization 𝒫\_I and the history of its Probability-Dynamics, together with the related action, switching, tuning, post-gate output, manifestation and effect. A candidate is a complete internal world, not a scattered fragment of explanation.

Y\_Π: the observation space that the protocol allows to be obtained. It may include external behavior, output outside the field, already manifested results, effect sequences, legally readable field-side projections, time series, responses after legal intervention, and other observation interfaces already established upstream. No kind of observation is stipulated in advance here as having inherently greater resolving power.

H\_Π: D\_Π → Y\_Π, the forward mapping: given a candidate internal world X, the observation H\_Π(X) that would be obtained under the protocol.

Prot\_Π: the observation protocol frozen in advance, specifying the object of observation, the declared window, the time window, sampling, resolution, permitted interventions, observation channels, record format and the version of the candidate domain.

Actual observation: y ∈ Y\_Π.

### 2.2 Source of the forward mapping

H\_Π must be given by forms already established: the field system, the sealed formal systems, the legal external interfaces already established, and the operating-substrate interface to be given later by The Construction Form of the Intention Operating Substrate, which together state what observation a candidate internal world X would produce under Prot\_Π. The sources of H\_Π must be written out item by item.

An assumption such as “if there is Intention-Dynamics, some behavior will appear” may not serve as the forward mapping and then be used to invert Intention-Dynamics. A unified overall mapping from Intention-Dynamics to observation, assumed without grounds, does not constitute a legal forward mapping.

### 2.3 Admission to inversion

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;AdmIInv(Π) := DomainFrozen ∧ ObsSetFrozen ∧ ForwardMapEstablished ∧ NoInternalAnswerLeak ∧ NoTargetPreselection ∧ SameDeclaredWindow

DomainFrozen: D\_Π is frozen before observation; after the results are seen, unfavorable candidates are not deleted and the candidate domain is not re-cut.

ObsSetFrozen: Y\_Π and Prot\_Π are frozen before observation; after the results are seen, no observation favorable only to one interpretation is added.

ForwardMapEstablished: H\_Π has an established source in accordance with 2.2.

NoInternalAnswerLeak: y does not directly contain the answers to be inverted, nor does it directly contain the internal verdicts and certificates that sign these answers. These include: IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I), N\_IDyn^γ; Read\_{N\_IDyn^γ}^M, Read\_{LegalIDynSeg}^M, Read\_{SourceOccurrenceDyn}^{M,γ}, Read\_{CarrierDyn}^{M,γ}, Read\_{IntentProbDyn}^{M,γ}; the formal verdicts of L1 to L6; SameIDynBind^M, SameCarrierDynBind^M, SameIntentProbDynBind^M; and upstream formal certificates that directly sign the truth values of the dynamics at the three sites. Separating these answers out of legally readable field-side projections through established forward relations is identification, not leakage; leakage is putting the answers themselves, or the verdicts that sign them, into y. Once these enter the observation side, inversion has been replaced by determination.

NoTargetPreselection: D\_Π is not obtained by keeping in advance only candidates with Intention-Dynamics, nor by screening with an internal verdict equivalent to IntentDyn, nor by presupposing that “here it must be the source drive that moves” or “here it must be the probability organization that moves” and keeping only candidates with the corresponding position.

SameDeclaredWindow: all candidates in D\_Π take the same declared window: the same declared object, the same starting and ending evaluation points, and the same declared Γ\_IDyn. Whether Γ\_IDyn is frozen in advance within a given candidate is that candidate's L6, not a matter of admission. Positive candidates may not take the whole segment while negative candidates take a part, nor may one candidate take as its object what lies before the point of exit from qualification while another takes what lies after it. Candidates with different declared windows do not belong to the same inversion problem.

When y contains material from another run, another branch or another anchor, three cases are distinguished:

The protocol did not allow such material to enter in the first place, yet it was mixed in after the results were seen: ObsSetFrozen fails, the protocol is invalid, and inversion is not entered.

The protocol legally declared in advance observations of other runs or other objects: this material is still part of y, and H\_Π states how it is produced by the candidate worlds; it does not bear the internal attribution of the current γ and may have no separating power for the current target, but it does not make the protocol invalid.

The material is treated as evidence of the current γ itself: this is evidence splicing or a target-binding error; it gives no candidate in the preimage a 0; if it enters the classification of a candidate, the corresponding label of that candidate is ⊥ (EvidenceSplice, CarrierBindMismatch or ProbBindMismatch).

None of the three cases amounts to IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I) = 0 or to N\_IDyn^γ = 0.

AdmIInv is the admission to inversion, not a new ontological condition of Intention-Dynamics, and is not counted in H. When AdmIInv(Π) = 0, inversion is not entered.

## Section Three　Candidate Labels and Whole-Segment Applicability

Candidate labels describe the theoretical classification of a candidate internal world and are used to characterize the preimage; they are not observations, do not enter y, and do not conflict with NoInternalAnswerLeak. What a label records is whether current upstream theory has already legally signed the corresponding truth value of that candidate; ⊥ means that the truth value has not currently been closed by the theoretical chain, not that Intention-Dynamics ontologically has a third truth value.

### 3.1 Whole-segment applicability label

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dom(X) ∈ {In, Out, ⊥}

Dom is not a truth value of Intention-Dynamics, nor a new definition of the working domain; what it records is whether, for a candidate X, two-way closure can legally be invoked to sign a value for the whole segment γ taken as one segment of dynamics of one and the same Intention.

In: X lies within the base working domain W\_IDyn, and a legal upstream conclusion has already signed L4(X) = 1, that is, qualification persists at every field state of γ. When this is given at the object layer, it carries no H; when it is obtained from the positive reading of L4 in Theorem 1 of The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics, it carries H\_LawRead along the Law-bearing witness used.

Out: a legal upstream conclusion has already signed one of the following two failures, with its reason attached:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Reason\_Out ∈ { StartDomainFail, QualExit }

StartDomainFail: Intention does not hold at the starting point of γ, X does not lie within the base working domain W\_IDyn, and the Intention-Dynamics question of this group form does not arise at all on this segment. It is obtained from the negative verdict on qualification of The Determination Form of Intention (through the contrapositive of the necessary direction of The General Form of Intention) and carries its conditions.

QualExit: Intention holds at the starting point, but L4(X) = 0: qualification exits or re-forms within γ. It is obtained through the L4 path of The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics, must inherit the negative-representation conditions of 7.3 of The Mathematical Form of Intention, and is closed by the negative-verdict path of The Determination Form of Intention.

Both enter Out, but from different sources, and neither may substitute for the other: the former is the failure of the base working domain; the latter is the failure of the L4 component of the legal segment while the base working domain holds.

⊥: no legal upstream conclusion currently signs In or Out as above. A reason for openness is attached:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Reason\_DomOpen ∈ { StartDomainOpen, QualExitOpen }

StartDomainOpen: neither Intent\_{F,t\_0} = 1 nor its negation has yet been legally signed, and whether the candidate enters the base working domain W\_IDyn is not closed. However rich the subsequent qualification material may be, before the starting point is closed this is not a question of whether an exit from qualification occurs.

QualExitOpen: Intent\_{F,t\_0} = 1 has been signed, but whether L4 persists over the whole of γ has not yet been legally closed, or the conditions required by the negative path of L4 have not yet been closed.

### 3.2 Dynamics truth-value label

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Class\_IDyn(X) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥, OutDom}

Class\_IDyn(X) = 1: a legal upstream conclusion has already signed IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I; X) = 1. The current sufficient source is N\_IDyn^γ(X) = 1 ⇒ IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I; X) = 1 (The Necessity Form of Intention-Dynamics), carrying no H. When N\_IDyn^γ(X) = 1 is given at the object layer, the label carries no H; when it is obtained from a formal reading through Theorem 6 of The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics, it carries the conditions of the positive reading path used. N\_IDyn^γ = 1 includes L4 = 1, and L4 = 1 includes Intention holding at the starting point, so Class\_IDyn(X) = 1 implies Dom(X) = In.

Class\_IDyn(X) = 0: Dom(X) = In, and a legal upstream conclusion has already signed N\_IDyn^γ(X) = 0 through a path other than L4, from which IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I; X) = 0 is obtained through the contrapositive of the necessary direction. The conditions carried follow the chosen path: paths L1, L2, L3 and L6 carry no H; on the path on which all three sites are 0, the source-drive site carries no H, the carrier site carries the upstream negative conditions for the carrier, and the probability site carries H2 ∧ H3^red together with whichever of H\_PAct-Rep, H\_ρRep^-, H^-\_M the actual failure path uses. The contrapositive step adds no condition; the conditions used for Dom(X) = In are carried in addition.

Class\_IDyn(X) = OutDom: Dom(X) = Out, with the reason for Out attached.

Class\_IDyn(X) = ⊥: all other cases. ⊥ has a sub-reason attached: when Dom(X) = ⊥, StartDomainOpen or QualExitOpen according to the reason for openness of Dom; when Dom(X) = In while the dynamics truth value is not closed, DynClassOpen, and where this comes from evidence splicing or evidence mismatch, EvidenceSplice, CarrierBindMismatch or ProbBindMismatch is attached in addition.

When N\_IDyn^γ(X) = 0 has already been signed through L2 or a similar path while Dom(X) = ⊥, Class\_IDyn(X) = ⊥, with StartDomainOpen or QualExitOpen attached according to the reason for openness of Dom, and not 0: when Intention at the starting point is undetermined, whether the candidate lies within the base working domain is undetermined; when L4 is actually 0, the whole segment no longer uses the contrapositive chain; so while Dom is undetermined, the contrapositive may not be taken.

Class\_IDyn is a state of legal upstream signing and is not restricted to being obtained from N\_IDyn^γ; if other established forms later sign the truth value of Intention-Dynamics, they enter according to their sources and the conditions they carry.

The conditions carried by positive and negative labels are asymmetric: a positive label obtained at the object layer through the sufficient direction carries no H, while a negative label may carry conditions according to the chosen path. This asymmetry comes from the current state of the upstream proofs, not from an ontological asymmetry between positive and negative in Intention-Dynamics.

3.3 OutDom is not merged into 0 and is not a third truth value

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OutDom ≠ IntentDyn = 0  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;OutDom ≠ a third truth value of IntentDyn

IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I) remains a two-valued property at the object layer. OutDom is an applicability state of the inversion task: current theory cannot legally invoke two-way closure for this whole segment γ to sign a value for it. Under StartDomainFail, this segment does not lie within the base working domain; under QualExit, by the L4 discipline, the whole segment no longer uses the contrapositive chain, and the correct treatment is to re-segment at the qualification boundary and declare the new γ\_1 and γ\_2 separately. Treating an OutDom candidate as an ordinary negative candidate violates the L4 discipline already closed by The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics and The Necessity Form of Intention-Dynamics.

Re-segmentation produces new inversion problems: the new declared windows and their protocols must be declared and frozen anew in accordance with AdmIInv; segment boundaries can be given only by a legal determination of the qualification boundary, and may not be cut according to which part of y favors some interpretation.

### 3.4 Component labels

For φ ∈ {N, L1, …, L6, Src, Car, Prob}:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Class\_φ(X) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}; the three sites may also take OutDom

Class\_N: 1 through N\_IDyn^γ(X) = 1; 0 through any closed negative path, including the L4 path. For a candidate whose qualification exits within γ, N\_IDyn^γ = 0 is the correct structural verdict: it says only that this declared segment is not a legal dynamics segment, and does not extend to IntentDyn. So an OutDom candidate can have Class\_N = 0 while Class\_IDyn = OutDom.

Class\_{Li}: 1 through the positive reading of each item in Theorem 1 of The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics, with L4 carrying H\_LawRead along the witness and the others carrying no H; 0 through the corresponding negative path, with L1, L2, L3 and L6 carrying no H and L4 carrying the conditions of the L4 path. Class\_L5 takes only 1 or ⊥: the inability of current material to prove the same trajectory is not L5 = 0 at the object layer; evidence splicing yields only ⊥ (EvidenceSplice).

For the three sites, Class\_Src, Class\_Car and Class\_Prob: this chapter inverts the three sites as positions of the whole-segment dynamics of one and the same Intention, so it signs 1 or 0 only when Dom(X) = In; when Dom(X) = Out it records OutDom; when Dom(X) = ⊥ the label is ⊥, with StartDomainOpen or QualExitOpen attached according to the reason for openness of Dom. Both the 1 and the 0 of Class\_Src carry no H. The 1 of Class\_Car carries H\_LawRead along the witness, and its 0 carries the upstream negative conditions for the carrier; CarrierBindMismatch yields only ⊥. The 1 of Class\_Prob carries the conditions of the positive reading path used and adds no H; its 0 carries H2 ∧ H3^red and the conditions of the actual failure path; ProbBindMismatch yields only ⊥.

## Section Four　Statement of the Inversion Problem

4.1 Preimage and observational equivalence

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;E\_Π(y) := H\_Π^{-1}(y) = { X ∈ D\_Π \| H\_Π(X) = y }  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;X\_a ∼\_Π X\_b ⇔ H\_Π(X\_a) = H\_Π(X\_b)

∼\_Π is an equivalence relation on D\_Π. When E\_Π(y) is nonempty, it is the equivalence class corresponding to the actual observation.

### 4.2 Family of inversion properties

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Φ\_IDyn = { φ\_I, φ\_N, φ\_L1, …, φ\_L6, φ\_Src, φ\_Car, φ\_Prob }

φ\_I(X) := IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I; X), a two-valued property at the object layer.  
φ\_N(X) := N\_IDyn^γ(X).  
φ\_Li(X) := Li(X), i = 1, …, 6.  
φ\_Src(X) := SourceOccurrenceDyn\_I^γ(X), φ\_Car(X) := CarrierDyn\_I^γ(X), φ\_Prob(X) := IntentProbDyn\_I^γ(X).

Position: for candidates with Dom(X) = In,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Site(X) := { s ∈ {Src, Car, Prob} \| φ\_s(X) = 1 }

φ\_Site(X) := Site(X). DynActual\_I^γ(X) = 1 if and only if Site(X) ≠ ∅; when the legal segment holds, N\_IDyn^γ(X) = 1 if and only if Site(X) ≠ ∅.

These are all object-layer truth values of the candidates; they do not become three-valued because of inversion, nor do they acquire a third value because whole-segment applicability fails. In actual solving these truth values are not accessed directly; instead, the labels of Section Three record whether current theory has already legally signed them. For a candidate with Dom(X) = Out, current theory does not sign its φ\_I or the whole-segment values of the three sites, and the label records OutDom.

Hence, for φ\_I and the three sites, when the preimage contains an OutDom candidate, it cannot be confirmed that these properties are constant on E\_Π(y), even if the values of all other members have been signed: the values of an OutDom candidate have no legal source. So IdentAt\_Π(φ\_I; y) cannot be obtained on such a preimage.

4.3 Objects that do not enter the inversion core

The start, continuation, stop and restart of action, strengthening and weakening of the directional type and the carrier type, Desire-Gate switching, post-gate counter-holding and Principle-Action, manifestation, tuning results and behavioral effects: none of these is a component of N\_IDyn^γ. They are action projections, typed readings, adjacent gate-layer events, outputs or consequences; they may enter inversion as supplementary reports, but they do not enter the inversion core, nor may they be elevated by inversion into conditions of Intention-Dynamics.

### 4.4 The problem

From y, find the bounds of the preimage E\_Π(y): the most that can be known from y about a candidate internal world, and the most that can be known about each property in Φ\_IDyn.

The preimage is not the most probable explanation, not the best-fitting explanation, not the psychologically most reasonable explanation, and not the simplest explanation. No Bayesian prior or posterior is introduced here, no credibility is assigned to members of the preimage, and statistical goodness of fit does not replace structural identifiability.

### 4.5 Coverage of the preimage

In actual solving, the preimage is given as an enumerated candidate set Ê\_Π(y) ⊆ E\_Π(y). The preimage coverage certificate:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;InvCover\_Π(y) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}

1: it has been proven that Ê\_Π(y) = E\_Π(y); all legal candidates have been found.  
0: it has been proven that Ê\_Π(y) ⊊ E\_Π(y); at least one legal candidate is known not to be included in the current enumeration.  
⊥: completeness of coverage cannot be proven, nor has an explicit witness of omission been obtained.

When InvCover\_Π(y) ≠ 1, coverage of the preimage is not closed.

## Section Five　Identifiability and the Upper Bound

5.1 Theorem One　Theorem of the Factorization of Identifiable Properties

For an internal property φ: D\_Π → Z, define Ident\_Π(φ): there exists a function g\_φ: Im(H\_Π) → Z such that, for all X ∈ D\_Π, φ(X) = g\_φ(H\_Π(X)), where Im(H\_Π) = H\_Π(D\_Π). Then:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Ident\_Π(φ) ⇔ \[ ∀X\_a, X\_b ∈ D\_Π: X\_a ∼\_Π X\_b ⇒ φ(X\_a) = φ(X\_b) \] ⇔ ∃g\_φ: Im(H\_Π) → Z, φ = g\_φ ∘ H\_Π

Local form: define IdentAt\_Π(φ; y): there exists z ∈ Z such that, for all X ∈ E\_Π(y), φ(X) = z. Then

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IdentAt\_Π(φ; y) ⇔ φ is constant on E\_Π(y)

Write NonIdent\_φ^Π(y) := ¬IdentAt\_Π(φ; y); for φ\_I, write NonIdent\_I^Π(y).

Proof: From left to middle: if φ = g\_φ ∘ H\_Π and H\_Π(X\_a) = H\_Π(X\_b), then φ(X\_a) = g\_φ(H\_Π(X\_a)) = g\_φ(H\_Π(X\_b)) = φ(X\_b). From middle to right: if φ is constant on every equivalence class, then for each y′ in Im(H\_Π), take any member X of E\_Π(y′) and set g\_φ(y′) := φ(X); by constancy, this definition does not depend on the member taken, so for any X, φ(X) = g\_φ(H\_Π(X)). The right is the definition of the left. The local form is argued in the same way, only on E\_Π(y). □

A large quantity of data does not automatically mean identifiability. The real condition of identifiability is that the target property does not distinguish any two legal candidates that the observation protocol cannot distinguish.

5.2 Theorem Two　Theorem of the Upper Bound of Observational Equivalence

Under AdmIInv(Π) = 1, given y = H\_Π(X), the finest legal resolution of the candidate internal world attainable by inversion using only y is E\_Π(y); no legal inversion output that depends only on y can further distinguish members of the same preimage.

Proof: An inversion output that depends only on y is a function K(y) of y. Suppose X\_a, X\_b ∈ E\_Π(y), and K claims to distinguish the two, that is, it outputs K\_a for X\_a and K\_b for X\_b, with K\_a ≠ K\_b. But H\_Π(X\_a) = H\_Π(X\_b) = y; the two give K the same input, and K(y) can take only one value. Contradiction. □

Write KnowMax\_Π(y) := E\_Π(y). KnowMax is only a formal name for the upper bound of inversion, not a new ontological predicate.

5.3 Proposition One　Refinement of the Protocol

Suppose Π′ and Π have the same candidate domain and there exists a function f such that H\_Π = f ∘ H\_Π′; Π′ is then said to refine Π. Then for any y′ ∈ Im(H\_Π′), E\_Π′(y′) ⊆ E\_Π(f(y′)); and for any φ, Ident\_Π(φ) ⇒ Ident\_Π′(φ), while the converse does not hold.

Proof: H\_Π′(X) = y′ implies H\_Π(X) = f(y′), which gives the inclusion. If φ = g ∘ H\_Π, then φ = (g ∘ f) ∘ H\_Π′. For the failure of the converse, it suffices to have one pair of candidates that are separated under Π′ and equivalent under Π but differ in their value of φ. □

Hence the fact that a stronger protocol can later separate candidates does not imply that the property was identifiable under the original protocol in the first place; nor does unidentifiability under the original protocol imply unidentifiability under all protocols.

5.4 Proposition Two　Same-Type Repetition Does Not Increase Separation

Suppose an observation component h is added to Π, giving H\_Π^h(X) := (H\_Π(X), h(X)). If X\_a ∼\_Π X\_b and h(X\_a) = h(X\_b), then X\_a ∼\_{Π^h} X\_b.

Proof: Immediate from the definition. □

Hence: added material increases separating power only if it takes different values on some pair of candidates that were previously equivalent. Repeatedly collecting data of exactly the same type, which yield the same output for this pair of candidates, cannot remove the observational equivalence between them. A real strengthening of the protocol must add new channels, new timing, new interventions, higher resolution or new legal forward separating quantities.

## Section Six　Dynamics Truth Value, Dynamics Position and Dynamics Detail

6.1 Theorem Three　Theorem of the Identifiability of the Truth Value of Intention-Dynamics

Suppose AdmIInv(Π) = 1.

(1) If E\_Π(y) is nonempty and all its members have Class\_IDyn = 1, then IdentAt\_Π(φ\_I; y), with value 1; if all its members have Class\_IDyn = 0, then IdentAt\_Π(φ\_I; y), with value 0.

(2) If there exist X\_1, X\_0 ∈ E\_Π(y) such that Class\_IDyn(X\_1) = 1 and Class\_IDyn(X\_0) = 0, then NonIdent\_I^Π(y) = 1; no inversion using only the observations of this protocol may sign IntentDyn = 1 or IntentDyn = 0 at this y.

Proof: (1) By the meaning of the labels, φ\_I takes the same value on all members, and IdentAt follows from the local form of Theorem One. (2) By the meaning of the labels, φ\_I(X\_1) = 1 and φ\_I(X\_0) = 0, so φ\_I is not constant on E\_Π(y), and by Theorem One, NonIdent\_I^Π(y) = 1. Further, by Theorem Two, an output that depends only on y is the same for X\_1 and X\_0: signing 1 is wrong for X\_0, and signing 0 is wrong for X\_1; both lie in the legal preimage, and the protocol cannot exclude either. □

(1) concerns the preimage itself. Actual solving can see only the enumerated set Ê\_Π(y), so when (1) enters external read-out, closure of coverage is also required; see Section Eight. (2) holds as soon as two legal members with different labels have been found, and does not presuppose closure of coverage.

(2) is indiscernibility already proven, not a shortfall of the current enumeration. As long as the added material still belongs to the same observation protocol that does not increase separating power, or the extended protocol still leaves this pair of candidates observationally equivalent, adding such data cannot remove this indiscernibility; if new observations introduce a new legal separating quantity, that is already another inversion under a stronger protocol.

Condition propagation: Theorem Three itself neither adds nor inherits any H; it presupposes that the labels already hold. In a specific application, the conclusion carries the conditions of the labels used: in (1), a value of 1 carries the conditions of the positive labels and a value of 0 those of the negative labels; the conclusion of (2) carries the conditions of the positive label of X\_1 and the negative label of X\_0.

6.2 Proposition Three　Identifiability of Composition Implies Identifiability of the Truth Value

If all members of E\_Π(y) have Dom = In, and φ\_L1, …, φ\_L6, φ\_Src, φ\_Car, φ\_Prob are all identifiable at y, then φ\_N and φ\_I are both identifiable at y.

Proof: φ\_N is a function of these nine components: N = (L1 ∧ … ∧ L6) ∧ (Src ∨ Car ∨ Prob). Each component is constant on E\_Π(y), so φ\_N is constant as well. On candidates with Dom = In, by the two-way formula, φ\_I = φ\_N, so φ\_I is constant. □

On a preimage all of whose members have Dom = In, the inversions of φ\_N and φ\_I have the same value, and the label conditions are also the same, because the contrapositive step adds no condition. When the preimage has members with Dom = Out, the two come apart: such a member can have Class\_N = 0 while Class\_IDyn = OutDom.

6.3 Theorem Four　The Dynamics Position Cannot Be Inferred from the Dynamics Truth Value

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IdentAt\_Π(φ\_I; y) ⇏ IdentAt\_Π(φ\_Src; y) ∧ IdentAt\_Π(φ\_Car; y) ∧ IdentAt\_Π(φ\_Prob; y)

This is a protocol-relative structural template; it declares no new running realization and does not assert that any specific running already exists. The template condition is: AdmIInv(Π) = 1, and D\_Π does contain the candidates described below.

Two-candidate template: let X\_S, X\_C ∈ D\_Π, both with Dom = In and with the labels of L1 to L6 all 1; Class\_Src(X\_S) = 1; Class\_Src(X\_C) = 0, Class\_Car(X\_C) = 1. By The Necessity Form of Intention-Dynamics, Class\_IDyn(X\_S) = Class\_IDyn(X\_C) = 1. If H\_Π(X\_S) = H\_Π(X\_C) = y, and all members of E\_Π(y) have Class\_IDyn = 1, for example E\_Π(y) = {X\_S, X\_C}, then IdentAt\_Π(φ\_I; y) holds, with value 1; yet φ\_Src(X\_S) = 1 and φ\_Src(X\_C) = 0, so by Theorem One, NonIdent\_Src^Π(y) = 1.

The negative label of the source-drive site carries no H, so the conclusion of the two-candidate template carries no H when the positive labels are taken from the object layer.

Three-candidate template: further let X\_P ∈ D\_Π, with Dom = In, the labels of L1 to L6 all 1, Class\_Src(X\_P) = 0, Class\_Car(X\_P) = 0 and Class\_Prob(X\_P) = 1; and let Class\_Car(X\_S) = Class\_Prob(X\_S) = 0 and Class\_Prob(X\_C) = 0. If all three belong to E\_Π(y), and all members of E\_Π(y) have Class\_IDyn = 1, then φ\_I is identifiable at y while none of the three sites is identifiable. Here the negative labels of the carrier site and the probability site each carry their conditions, and the conclusion of the three-candidate template carries them accordingly.

Hence inferring the identifiability of the three sites from IdentAt\_Π(φ\_I; y) cannot serve as a general rule of inversion: for every protocol whose D\_Π contains such candidates, this inference fails at the corresponding y. □

Knowing that Intention is moving is not the same as knowing where it is moving.

6.4 Inversion of position: single site, multiple sites and site mixture

Identifiability of position, that is, IdentAt\_Π(φ\_Site; y), is equivalent to the identifiability of all three sites. When position is identifiable, it falls into the following cases according to the value of Site:

Single site: Site is constantly a set containing only one site; only one site is moving.  
Multiple sites: Site is constantly a set containing two or three sites; within one and the same candidate, two or three sites are in fact moving at the same time.  
No site: Site is constantly the empty set; in this case, if the legal segment is identifiable as 1, then by Proposition Three, φ\_I is identifiable as 0.

When position is not identifiable and two members already found have different values of Site, this is site mixture: several observationally equivalent candidates each interpret this y as a different dynamics position.

Multiple sites and site mixture may not be conflated. The former means that several sites are in fact moving within one and the same candidate, which is determinate knowledge about the internal world; the latter means that different candidates each give a different position, which is an indiscernibility of the protocol. Under site mixture, one site may not be forcibly chosen on the basis of the most probable, most reasonable or simplest explanation.

6.5 Proposition Four　Identifiability of Position Does Not Imply Identifiability of Detail

For any detail property φ\_Det, for example whether the source drive on γ starts, persists, stops or undergoes re-onset after stopping; which L\_ij rises, falls or migrates; whether the Probability-Dynamics is a change of the relative-generation relation or a change of the formal dimensions, the common binding or the rate-bearing organization; whether there is intermediate restoration; the action projection and typed strengthening and weakening: by Theorem One, IdentAt\_Π(φ\_Det; y) if and only if φ\_Det is constant on E\_Π(y). That Site is constant on E\_Π(y) does not make φ\_Det constant; as long as D\_Π contains two observationally equivalent candidates with the same position and different details, position is identifiable while detail is not. □

For example, suppose two candidates both have only the source-drive site moving; in one, the source-drive continuation starts within γ, while in the other it was already continuing before the starting point of γ; if the protocol cannot see the history of the source drive before the starting point of γ, the two can be observationally equivalent.

Among the three levels there is no general top-down implication. A bottom-up implication requires that the information identified at the lower level suffice to determine the upper-level target: when the identified detail belongs to an already closed candidate object and suffices to determine the truth value of each of the three sites, position is identifiable; when both the legal segment and the position are identifiable and the members of the preimage all have Dom = In, the truth value is identifiable (Proposition Three). The identifiability of an arbitrary detail does not automatically imply the identifiability of position. Top-down:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;knowing that it moves ⇏ knowing where it moves ⇏ knowing how it moves

## Section Seven　External Results, Intervention and Protocol Strengthening

7.1 Theorem Five　Intention-Dynamics Cannot in General Be Inferred Back from External Results

If there exist X\_1, X\_0 ∈ D\_Π such that Class\_IDyn(X\_1) = 1, Class\_IDyn(X\_0) = 0 and H\_Π(X\_1) = H\_Π(X\_0) = y, then NonIdent\_I^Π(y) = 1.

This is Theorem Three (2) applied to external results. The following two templates are protocol-relative structural templates, with the same template condition as in 6.3: AdmIInv(Π) = 1, and D\_Π does contain the corresponding candidates.

Template One　Out-of-segment write-in: given X\_1, the protocol Π\_out records only behavior outside the field, manifestation, effects and tuning results. Construct X\_0: qualification persists over the whole segment, with Dom = In; the external output is the same as that of X\_1, but the changes in the state of Intention itself on γ are written in directly by a process outside the segment rather than produced by the legal running of the field. Then L2(X\_0) = 0; through the L2 path, N\_IDyn^γ(X\_0) = 0, and through the contrapositive, Class\_IDyn(X\_0) = 0, carrying no H. If Π\_out cannot see the internal write-in path, H\_{Π\_out}(X\_0) = H\_{Π\_out}(X\_1). The conclusion of this template carries no H when the positive label is taken from the object layer.

Template Two　External-mechanism compensation: given X\_1, construct X\_0: the legal segment holds, qualification persists over the whole segment, and none of the three sites of Intention itself moves; the same external behavior, manifestation or effect is produced by an external device, by another probability organization not belonging to K\_I, by a Law structure that bears no formal direction of 𝒫\_I, by an environmental mechanism, or by a non-Intention field process. Then Class\_Src(X\_0) = 0, carrying no H; Class\_Car(X\_0) = 0, carrying the upstream negative conditions for the carrier; Class\_Prob(X\_0) = 0, carrying H2 ∧ H3^red and the conditions of the actual failure path; and Class\_IDyn(X\_0) = 0, carrying these conditions. If the protocol records only these external results, the two are observationally equivalent. In this template, the judgment that “X\_0 has no Intention-Dynamics” carries the above conditions.

Hence: under a protocol that records only external results, as long as the template conditions are met, there exists a y such that:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;AdmIInv(Π\_out) = 1 ⊢ ObservedOutcome ⇏ IntentDyn

Behavior, manifestation, effects, tuning results, external actions and coarse-grained outputs cannot by themselves serve as a general basis for inferring Intention-Dynamics back, unless established forward relations separate candidates with different truth values. □

This conclusion is protocol-relative. One may not write “behavior can never be used to infer Intention-Dynamics back,” nor “Intention-Dynamics is unobservable”; one may write only: under protocol Π, if the preimage contains legal candidates with different truth values, the protocol cannot identify Intention-Dynamics on this observation.

### 7.2 Inferences not to be made

One may not write NoObservedOutput ⇒ IntentDyn = 0. Complete action, Desire-Gate switching, manifestation, tuning success, behavior and effect are not conditions for Intention-Dynamics to obtain (The Necessity Form of Intention-Dynamics); failing to observe output is weaker than there being no output.

One may not write OutputChange ⇒ some site is moving, nor establish a correspondence between stimulus and dynamics from the intuitions of empirical psychology: that a certain stimulus necessarily triggers the source drive, that a certain behavior necessarily represents Probability-Dynamics, that a certain change of attention necessarily represents carrier dynamics, or that a certain manifestation necessarily means Intention-Dynamics. Each of these correspondences must be given by established forward relations.

One may not write Read^Inv = ⊥ ⇒ IntentDyn = 0, nor NonIdent\_I^Π(y) = 1 ⇒ IntentDyn = 0: unidentifiability is an epistemic boundary, not a negation of the object.

### 7.3 Intervention and separation

Intervention may enter the observation protocol, but only conditionally. One may not write Intervention ⇒ Identification.

Suppose the protocol Π contains a family of legal interventions u declared in advance in Prot\_Π, each intervention having a forward mapping H\_{Π,u}. Define:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sep\_Π(X\_a, X\_b) = 1 ⇔ ∃u ∈ Π: H\_{Π,u}(X\_a) ≠ H\_{Π,u}(X\_b)

By Theorem One, for φ to be identifiable in general under Π, it is required at least that Sep\_Π(X\_a, X\_b) = 1 for any two candidates with φ(X\_a) ≠ φ(X\_b).

What has discriminating power is legal intervention together with an established differential forward response: the intervention u is legal; H\_{Π,u} has an established source; candidates with different values do respond differently to u under the protocol; and the actual response falls into one of the classes. The mere fact that “the system changed after the intervention” implies neither that Intention has dynamics nor which site is moving.

Intervention can enter only through the legal interfaces of the field. If an intervention writes directly into the state of the source drive, the bearing Laws or the probability organization of Intention, it constitutes an out-of-segment write-in in the intervened candidate, L2 fails, and the segment is no longer a legal dynamics segment; such an intervention changes the object being inverted and is not a separation of the original object.

### 7.4 Protocol strengthening

By Propositions One and Two: when a Mix under the original protocol is separated under a stronger protocol, that is another inversion under another protocol and does not imply identifiability under the original protocol in the first place; repetition of data of the same type is not protocol strengthening.

## Section Eight　Inversion States and External Read-Out

### 8.1 Internal inversion state

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;InvState\_I^Π(y) ∈ { I^+, I^-, Mix, DomOpen, InvOpen, ClassOpen }

The states are determined in the following order:

Mix: the preimage is known to contain at least one candidate with Class\_IDyn = 1 and one candidate with Class\_IDyn = 0. By Theorem Three (2), indiscernibility is proven. Mix does not presuppose closure of coverage: it holds as soon as two legal members with different labels have been found.

DomOpen: not Mix, and either the preimage is known to contain a candidate with Class\_IDyn = OutDom, or InvCover\_Π(y) = 1 while the preimage contains a candidate with Dom = ⊥. The former holds as soon as an OutDom member has been found and does not presuppose closure of coverage.

InvOpen: neither of the above two, and InvCover\_Π(y) ≠ 1, that is, it has not been proven that the preimage has been exhaustively searched.

ClassOpen: none of the above three, InvCover\_Π(y) = 1, but at least one candidate has Class\_IDyn = ⊥; in this case these candidates all have Dom = In, and it is the dynamics truth value itself that is not closed.

I^+: InvCover\_Π(y) = 1, E\_Π(y) is nonempty, and every X ∈ E\_Π(y) has Class\_IDyn = 1.

I^-: InvCover\_Π(y) = 1, E\_Π(y) is nonempty, and every X ∈ E\_Π(y) has Class\_IDyn = 0.

If InvCover\_Π(y) = 1 while E\_Π(y) = ∅, the observation y is not in the image of the forward mapping, which shows that the forward mapping or the protocol is in error; this is a problem at the level of AdmIInv and does not enter the six states above.

The six states are pairwise disjoint and cover all cases in which AdmIInv(Π) = 1 and y is in the image of the forward mapping: when the state is not Mix, DomOpen or InvOpen, coverage is closed, the preimage contains no OutDom and no Dom = ⊥, the labels all lie in {1, 0, ⊥}, and 1 and 0 do not occur together, so the state falls into one of ClassOpen, I^+ and I^-.

When Mix and OutDom members occur together, the state is recorded as Mix, with the OutDom witness given as a supplementary report: the pair of candidates with Dom = In and different labels are observationally equivalent under Π, and this indiscernibility does not disappear when L4 is closed or the trajectory is re-segmented.

### 8.2 External three-valued read-out

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Read^Inv\_{IDyn,Π}(y) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I^+ → 1  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I^- → 0  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mix, DomOpen, InvOpen, ClassOpen → ⊥

⊥ must have a reason attached:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Reason\_⊥ ∈ { NonIdentifiable, WorkDomainOpen, InverseCoverageOpen, CandidateClassificationOpen }

These correspond respectively to Mix, DomOpen, InvOpen and ClassOpen. CandidateClassificationOpen additionally has the sub-reason DynClassOpen, EvidenceSplice, CarrierBindMismatch or ProbBindMismatch attached. WorkDomainOpen additionally has a sub-reason attached:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Reason\_WorkDomainOpen ∈ { StartDomainOpen, StartDomainFail, QualExitOpen, QualExit }

The first two concern the base working domain; the last two concern L4. The four classes of reason may not be conflated, and their respective sub-reasons may not substitute for one another.

Within DomOpen, if coverage is closed and all members are OutDom, then the fact that two-way closure cannot legally be invoked for the whole segment has itself been identified; the IntentDyn read-out for the whole segment is still ⊥, with reason WorkDomainOpen, not 0. If these OutDom candidates are all obtained through QualExit, the L4 component can be read out as 0 (Section Nine); if any of them is a StartDomainFail candidate, L4 = 0 cannot be inferred from OutDom alone, and L4 is still declared according to its own component inversion result. This declared segment is then treated according to the corresponding reason: in the case of starting-point failure, the base working domain was not entered; in the case of mid-course exit from qualification, the trajectory is re-segmented at the qualification boundary.

Conditions of the read-outs:

Read = 1: the positive labels of all candidates in the preimage carry conditions according to their sources; positive labels obtained from object-layer N\_IDyn^γ = 1 through The Necessity Form of Intention-Dynamics carry no H, and the read-out accordingly carries no H.

Read = 0: the negative labels of all candidates in the preimage carry conditions according to the chosen paths; the read-out carries their conjunction. Only when a path passes through the probability site does it carry H2 ∧ H3^red and the failure-path conditions for probability; only when a path passes through the carrier site does it carry the negative conditions for the carrier; paths L1, L2, L3, L6 and the source-drive site carry no H; in addition, the conditions used for Dom = In of each candidate are carried.

⊥ with reason NonIdentifiable: this judgment depends on Class\_IDyn = 1 of the positively labeled candidate and Class\_IDyn = 0 of the negatively labeled candidate, and carries the conditions of these two labels.

⊥ with reason WorkDomainOpen, given by an OutDom member already found: this judgment depends on how that member's Out label was obtained, and carries the conditions of the path used.

All read-outs take AdmIInv(Π) = 1 as their admission; conditions carried by the sources of H\_Π are inherited along with those sources.

8.3 Theorem Six　Theorem of the Coverage of Negative Read-Outs

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;NoPositiveWitness ⇏ NegativeIdentification

That is, “no candidate with Class\_IDyn = 1 has been found” is not the same as “all candidates have Class\_IDyn = 0.”

Read^Inv\_{IDyn,Π}(y) = 0 if and only if InvCover\_Π(y) = 1, E\_Π(y) is nonempty, and all candidates have obtained Class\_IDyn = 0. Hence the preimage contains no OutDom and no ⊥, and the conditions of the negative labels used have all been registered.

Proof: By the definitions in 8.1 and 8.2, Read = 0 if and only if the state is I^-, and I^- requires closure of coverage, a nonempty preimage, and negative labels for all candidates. If coverage is not closed, the preimage may contain an unenumerated candidate with Class\_IDyn = 1 or an OutDom candidate; signing 0 might then be wrong, so only ⊥ can be signed, with reason InverseCoverageOpen unless the state has already fallen into Mix or DomOpen; if an OutDom member has been found, the state is DomOpen, and 0 is not signed either. □

Likewise, signing 1 also requires closure of coverage: an unenumerated member may carry a negative label, so that the actual state is Mix; or it may be OutDom, so that the actual state is DomOpen.

8.4 Theorem Seven　Theorem of the Splitting of the Working Domain

If the same preimage is known to contain a candidate X\_out with Class\_IDyn = OutDom, then the current observation y cannot yield a determinate IntentDyn inversion read-out for the whole segment γ: no inversion using only the observations of this protocol may sign IntentDyn = 1 or IntentDyn = 0 for the whole segment γ. In particular, if the preimage contains both a candidate X\_in for which a value can legally be signed for the whole segment (Dom(X\_in) = In) and X\_out, starting-point qualification or L4 must first be separated, and the qualification boundary determined or the trajectory re-segmented.

Proof: By Theorem Two, an output that depends only on y is the same for X\_out and for the other members of the preimage, so if the whole-segment read-out signs a determinate value, it is at the same time signing that value for X\_out. Signing 0 records X\_out as IntentDyn = 0: under QualExit this violates the discipline that a 0 for L4 is not read back as IntentDyn = 0; under StartDomainFail the question does not arise on this segment. Signing 1 has no legal source for X\_out: its L4 does not hold, N\_IDyn^γ cannot be 1, and the sufficient direction does not apply; current theory signs no value for its whole-segment IntentDyn. Neither signing has a legal basis. □

This theorem is not inferred from φ\_I failing to be constant on the preimage: OutDom is not a third truth value of IntentDyn but an applicability state of the inversion task. What the theorem says is that the whole-segment read-out has no legal source, not that different truth values have been proven. The label of X\_in may be 1, 0 or ⊥: the split depends only on X\_in being legally signable for the whole segment and X\_out not being so, not on whether the dynamics truth value of X\_in has been closed. So a working-domain split is not an ordinary Mix: OutDom ≠ 0. It is removed not by adding data of the same type, but by strengthening the protocol's separation of the qualification history, by closing starting-point qualification or L4, or by re-segmenting at the qualification boundary (3.3).

Condition propagation: Theorem Seven itself carries no H; in application it carries the conditions of the path used for the Out label of X\_out, and the conditions of the positive reading used for Dom = In of X\_in.

### 9.2 Key points for each component

φ\_N: an OutDom candidate can have Class\_N = 0. So when all members are OutDom, coverage is closed and the L4 paths are all closed, φ\_N can be read out as 0 while φ\_I is read out as ⊥ (WorkDomainOpen). When all members of the preimage have Dom = In, the read-outs of φ\_N and φ\_I are the same.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;InvProfile\_IDyn^Π(y) := ( InvState\_I, InvState\_N, InvState\_L1, …, InvState\_L6, InvState\_Src, InvState\_Car, InvState\_Prob, InvState\_Site )

## Section Ten　Residue Declaration

The following residues must be declared explicitly and may not be left undeclared:

NonIdentifiable (Mix): the preimage contains both legal candidates with Intention-Dynamics and legal candidates without it. Indiscernibility has been proven, and it is structural. It can be removed only by switching to a protocol that can separate them (7.3 and 7.4), not by adding data of the same kind that do not raise separating power, nor by making the same determination once more on the same candidate.

InverseCoverageOpen (InvOpen): it has not been proven that the preimage has been exhaustively searched. This is a residue of enumeration and can be removed once the enumeration is completed or coverage is proven.

CandidateClassificationOpen (ClassOpen): the preimage has been exhaustively searched, but there are candidates with Dom = In whose dynamics truth value has not yet been closed upstream, including non-closure caused by evidence splicing and evidence mismatch. This is a residue in candidate classification; it can be removed once The Determination Form of Intention closes the dynamics classification of the candidates, or once same-trajectory, same-object material is obtained anew for the candidates.

WorkDomainOpen (DomOpen): the preimage contains OutDom candidates (Intention does not hold at the starting point, or qualification exits or re-forms within γ), or, with coverage closed, there are still candidates whose whole-segment applicability has not been determined (starting-point Intention not yet closed, or the whole-segment persistence of L4 not yet closed). This is a residue of whole-segment applicability: two-way closure cannot legally be invoked for the current declared segment as a whole. To remove it, observation of the qualification history must be strengthened, L4 or starting-point qualification must be closed by The Determination Form of Intention, or the trajectory must be re-segmented at the qualification boundary.

The four differ in nature: the first is structural, the second is a residue of enumeration, the third is a residue in candidate classification, and the fourth is a residue of whole-segment applicability.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Mix ≠ InvOpen ≠ ClassOpen ≠ DomOpen

They may not be lumped together as “insufficient information.” DomOpen is not merged into Mix, because OutDom is not 0; ClassOpen is not merged into InvOpen, because the preimage has been exhaustively searched.

When the read-out of a component is ⊥, it is likewise declared with whichever of these four reasons applies; φ\_N and L1 to L6 have no DomOpen and do not declare WorkDomainOpen.

## Section Eleven　Condition Ledger

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;H\_new = ∅

Theorems One, Two and Six and Propositions One to Four: pure inversion structure, Status = Established, inheriting no H.

Theorems Three, Four, Five and Seven: as inversion theorems, they inherit no H by virtue of their own proofs; they presuppose that the candidate labels already hold. In specific applications, conditions propagate along the labels used:

Positive labels: obtained from object-layer N\_IDyn^γ = 1 through The Necessity Form of Intention-Dynamics, they carry no H; obtained from a formal reading through Theorem 6 of The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics, they carry the conditions of the positive reading path used, that is, the H\_LawRead applicable to the Law-bearing witnesses actually used and the conditions of the upstream positive-reading path.

Conclusions of the templates: the two-candidate template of Theorem Four and Template One of Theorem Five carry no H when the positive labels are taken from the object layer; the three-candidate template of Theorem Four and Template Two of Theorem Five carry the conditions of the negative labels of the carrier site and the probability site.

Conditions do not propagate wholesale under the name “Inversion Form.”

AdmIInv, and ForwardMapEstablished within it, are admission conditions of the inversion protocol, not ontological bridges, and are not counted in H; conditions carried by the sources of H\_Π are inherited along with those sources.

Status: the general structure of inversion is Established; a specific inversion read-out is Conditional, its condition being the conjunction of the conditions carried by the labels used and by the sources of H\_Π; when none of the labels used carries a condition, it is Established.

## Section Thirteen　Interfaces

13.1 Boundary with The Determination Form of Intention

The Inversion Form of Intention takes as input an existing legal observation protocol, forward mapping and observation result, and answers “what, at most, do these observations allow us to know?”

The Determination Form of Intention establishes or invokes legal internal interfaces, certificates, coverage and upstream verdicts, and answers “how do we check whether a real instance has Intention-Dynamics on γ, and how do we sign 1, 0 or ⊥?”

So Read\_{N\_IDyn^γ}^M, the verdicts of L1 to L6, binding readings or N\_IDyn^γ itself are not treated here as ordinary external observations; otherwise inversion would have been written as determination. Candidate labels are given upstream; here they are only read, not determined.

The reasons attached when the inversion read-out is ⊥ are passed to that chapter, which distinguishes them: ClassOpen can be closed by determination of the candidates' dynamics; DomOpen can be partly closed by determination of starting-point qualification and L4 and by fixing the qualification boundary; Mix does not disappear when the same determination is run once more on the same candidate, unless a new interface with real separating power is added, which is already a new protocol.

### 13.2 Upstream

The General Form of Intention-Dynamics: N\_IDyn^γ, the legal segment L1 to L6, the three sites and their attribution binding, the necessary direction.

The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics: the three-valued formal system, positive read-back, the paths and conditions of negative read-back, the disciplines of evidence splicing and mismatch, the special-case discipline of L4.

The Necessity Form of Intention-Dynamics: the sufficient direction and the two-way formula; the base working domain Intent\_{F,t\_0} = 1.

The General Form of Intention, The Necessity Form of Intention, The Mathematical Form of Intention: Intention qualification and its negative-representation conditions, used for the Dom label.

The field system and the sealed formal systems: the sources of the forward mapping H\_Π.

The Inversion Form of Probability: its general structure of inversion is of the same type as this chapter's; this chapter does not take that chapter's inversion conclusions about probability qualification as premises.

### 13.3 Hand-down

The Determination Form of Intention: takes over the parts of ClassOpen and DomOpen that can be closed by determination, including the determination of starting-point qualification and L4 and the fixing of the qualification boundary.

The Limit Form of Intention: the unidentifiability in this chapter is an identifiability boundary relative to the observation protocol; at what limits qualification and dynamics truly fail or exit is studied in that chapter. Unidentifiability in inversion is not ontological nonexistence.

The Construction Form of the Intention Operating Substrate: it must give the actual interfaces on which H\_Π can legally rely: which internal states are observable, which external outputs are produced by which internal structures, which interventions are legal, and which interventions can separate candidates.

The Reachability Form of Intention, The Stability Form of Intention: the reachability of dynamics targets and the persistence of dynamics under disturbance; no inference about these is made here.

### 13.4 What this chapter does not do

This chapter does not answer how to check whether N\_IDyn^γ holds; it adds no component of Intention-Dynamics and no fourth site of dynamics; it does not write action projection, strengthening and weakening, Desire-Gate switching, manifestation, tuning results or behavior as conditions of dynamics; it makes no statistical estimates and introduces no Bayesian prior or posterior; it does not replace the legal preimage with the most probable explanation; it does not write the failure to find a counterexample as identifiability; it does not write the failure to find a positive candidate as negative identification; it does not write exit from qualification as absence of dynamics; it does not re-prove the first twelve chapters; and it does not develop the determination, limit, operating substrate, reachability, source or stability of Intention.
