# The Reachability Form of Intention

## Section One　Purpose, Entry Conditions and the Single Proposition to Be Proven

### 1.1 Purpose

The Construction Form of the Intention Operating Substrate has already given a family of runnable Sedimentation-Field systems: in a field that already has a legal Intention structure, source drive, action, Desire-Gate switching, tuning and cross-time dynamics actually run, and the readings of qualification, action, switching and dynamics are all taken from the real history on one and the same unified running certificate. The construction has been given.

What remains is the question of reachability: starting from an initial state declared in advance, and using only the permitted inputs frozen in advance together with the system's own legal dynamics, does there exist a finite legal real history in which some class of Intention target already defined upstream actually holds? If there is none, on what grounds can one say that there is none? And if for the time being one can neither find a path nor prove that none exists, how should this be recorded?

What follows answers only this question: it does not redefine Intention, Intention-Action, Intention Desire-Gate switching or Intention-Dynamics, does not re-prove the conditions under which qualification holds, does not write reachability into the constitution of Intention qualification, and adds no dynamical engine of any kind. The meaning, working domain and read-back conditions of the four classes of target are all taken over from the sealed upstream.

### 1.2 Entry conditions

(1) The construction has been given: The Construction Form of the Intention Operating Substrate v1.0 and its reference model v14.5 (Appendix C lists the SHA-256 of the ten source files).

(2) The initial state is clearly delimited: it is declared in Section Two.

(3) The permitted input set is clearly delimited: it is declared in Section Three and frozen before any path is constructed.

(4) The target belongs to one of four classes whose upstream definitions, entries and working domains are already sealed: Intention qualification (The General Form of Intention, The Mathematical Form of Intention, The Necessity Form of Intention), current action (The General Form of Intention-Action, The Mathematical Form of Intention-Action, The Necessity Form of Intention-Action), Desire-Gate switching (The General Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching, The Mathematical Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching, The Necessity Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching) and Intention-Dynamics (The General Form of Intention-Dynamics, The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics, The Necessity Form of Intention-Dynamics); verdicts and entry routing follow The Determination Form of Intention.

1.3 Division of labor with neighboring form-texts

The Construction Form of the Intention Operating Substrate answers “can an existing Intention actually run in the field”; this chapter answers “can it be legally reached from a given initial state.” The Source Form of Intention addresses the source conditions for the first formation of the Intention structure; The Stability Form of Intention addresses persistence, failure and recovery once it holds; The Construction Form of the Intention Generation Substrate (Chapter 21) constructs, in a base Sedimentation-Field, the complete history in which Intention qualification holds for the first time. The initial states used here are all built on an already placed Intention-dedicated structure, so qualification reachability here is only “qualification holding anew on an already placed structure,” not the first generation of Intention.

Reachability is not Intention qualification, current action or Intention-Dynamics itself; it is not stability, persistence or recovery; it is not controllability; and it is not a guarantee that every legal history arrives.

### 1.4 The single proposition to be proven

Given a frozen task specification, a target is reachable if and only if there exist a finite real history that starts from the initial state, uses only permitted inputs at each step and stays within the legal successors, and a witness on this history, such that the upstream-defined target actually holds on that witness. A valid formal hit certificate suffices to sign reachability; a negative certificate composed of a closed successor domain, a necessary condition covering every way the target can be hit, and an invariant suffices to sign unreachability; when neither has been obtained, the case can only be recorded as undecided.

## Section Two　Task Specification, Initial States and Targets

### 2.1 Frozen task specification

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Ξ\_R = (Spec, X\_0, U^adm, 𝒯, G, Π)

Spec is the reference running specification Spec\_IB^ref of The Construction Form of the Intention Operating Substrate; X\_0 is the initial state; U^adm is the permitted input set; 𝒯 ∈ {I, IAct, IGS, IDyn} is the target type; G is the concrete target under that type; Π is the verification protocol, comprising the target monitor, the history binding rule, the comparison-sequence rule and the declared-segment rule. Ξ\_R does not change over the whole history; it is a frozen task specification, not a dynamic state.

### 2.2 States and random realizations

Within the permitted sets of this chapter, the only random branch is the dimension selection of organizational events (a field pulse with no given path has a random continuation of its own, but that lies outside the permitted sets of this chapter): the probability organization selects a formal dimension according to the current relative-generation relation Rel, and the probability of selecting dimension d is exactly Rel(d). Field-layer pulses are executed along given paths and contain no random branch.

The state X declared here is the totality of state quantities of the field, the probability layer and the intention side, excluding the random source itself; the random source decides only which legal successor is actually taken from one and the same state. Hence one and the same X\_0 can have several realizations: the reference model seeds the random source with a stream number s, and different stream numbers give different realizations on the same initial state. The formation process of the initial state applies pulses only along given paths and draws on no random source, so the initial state is the same under every stream number. Appendix A checks this quantity by quantity for stream numbers 0 to 5: the initial states for stream numbers 0 to 5 are completely identical in the sedimentation amount, repetition record, threshold, availability and masking of every edge; in the sedimentation of every node; in the running and sedimentation counts; in the field log, path dynamics and degree records; in the binding, generation gates and carrier links of the probability layer; and in the gate-bearing, sessions and event counts of the intention side. Only the random source differs.

Apart from the history records, the state X omits only two kinds of quantity: the random source, and the running-identity identifier (taken from object addresses and different at each construction). Neither affects the successors under permitted inputs. The running identity enters only the identity check of records and no dynamical update. The random sources carried by the field layer and the probability layer are not drawn under permitted inputs: the field-layer pulses among the permitted inputs all carry given paths, and the field layer draws a random continuation only when there is no given path (fldb\_work.py, lines 61 to 69), while the probability layer's own random events are not in the permitted sets; the x0 command of Appendix A checks that the states of these two random sources are unchanged before and after a sequence of permitted inputs. The random source of the intention side is used only for dimension selection. The duty registration, the correspondence, the tuning-candidate registration, the carrier-candidate registration and the protocols are likewise completely identical under different stream numbers. Hence, under permitted inputs, the successor is determined only by X, the input and the sample drawn for dimension selection.

Event records and certificate items belong to history, not to state; they are produced by permitted inputs, and readers and read-back all take their material from history, so the identity of a history is guaranteed by the certificate, not by the state.

A random realization is a sequence of uniform sample values used for dimension selection; the reference model's seeding by stream number is only one way of giving a random realization. Under the uniform-sampling idealization of the dimension-selection kernel, any sample sequence within the support is a random realization. The state of the random source is entered into the full-state digest of the unified running certificate, so rewriting the random source partway through a history is a write-in that bypasses legal operations and breaks the certificate chain; a random realization can be chosen only at the initial state.

### 2.3 The four classes of target

Every class of target is judged by the actual holding of the upstream object, written ActualTarget\_G(w), where w is a witness on some history.

Qualification target G\_I (state target): Intent\_{F,t} = 1 at some evaluation point t on the history. The witness is a point on the certificate.

Action target G\_IAct (event target): some real event w on the history with Intent = 1 at its start and IntentAct(w) = 1. An event whose qualification at its start is not 1 is routed at the entry of the action verdict according to The Determination Form of Intention (OutDom or ⊥); it is neither a hit nor a failure of the action target.

Switching target G\_IGS^{→\[G\_b\]} (sequence target): the comparison-sequence rule σ is frozen in advance as “each organizational event on the certificate compared with the immediately preceding organizational event”; the target is some pair of adjacent organizational events (w\_a, w\_b) on the history with Intent = 1 at the start of both, IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) = 1, and the gate of the later state belonging to duty class \[G\_b\].

Dynamics target G\_IDyn (segment target): some declared segment γ on the history with qualification 1 at its starting point and IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I) = 1, that is, all six items of the legal dynamics segment hold and at least one of the three sites has dynamics.

The time window for event, sequence and segment targets is the certificate history after the initial-state registration point: every event used by a witness must be an event of this history, prior-state events included. None of the initial states of this chapter has any event before the registration point (the gate-bearing state is empty), so no prior-state event is left out by this window.

Each of the four classes of target inherits the entry and working domain of its own upstream; they do not fill in for one another, nor are they combined into one total verdict of Intention reachability.

The formal reading of qualification is 1 (IntentCoreCert^M = 1), both formal dimensions are in V, Rel = (A: 0.51829, X: 0.48171), there is as yet no event, and the gate-bearing state is empty.

Source-drive-eroded initial state X\_0^er: on X\_0, formation pulses of the construction layer refresh only the other edges and the two dimensions, not the source-drive segment, until the source-drive segment loses its Law-formation (as in the exit segment of V6 of The Construction Form of the Intention Operating Substrate). The formal readings are then SourceDrive^M = 0, Probability = 1, IntentCoreCert^M = 0. Zero-rate initial state X\_0^z: on X\_0, the generation gate closes the generation of dimension A to 0. All three initial states are declared before the unified running certificate is established. The certificate takes a zero-step IdleStep as its starting registration; the full-state digests before and after this registration are the same, it changes no state, and it is not counted in the path. For each instance declared in this chapter, the target is not hit at the initial state: on X\_0 and X\_0^z there is as yet no event; on X\_0^er the formal reading of qualification is 0. This is only a declaration about these instances, not a general requirement on targets: for a general qualification target, if the formal reading of qualification at the initial state is already 1, there is a formal hit at step zero (H = 0; see 4.2 and 3.5); state targets and event targets have different temporal regimes, and an event target requires at least one event.

The qualification of the initial state and the reachability of the target are two different matters:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;¬R\_Ξ(X\_0, G) ⇏ ¬Intent(X\_0),   IntentCoreCert^M(X\_0) ≠ 1 ⇏ ¬Reach\_Ξ^M(X\_0, G\_I)

The former appears on X\_0^z: the formal reading of qualification at the initial state is 1 (object-layer Intent(X\_0^z) = 1 according to the read-back conditions of G\_I in 11.2), while switching into \[G\_A\] is unreachable (Section Eight); so the object-layer witness of the former is Conditional along with the read-back conditions of G\_I, while its formal-layer witness (IntentCoreCert^M(X\_0^z) = 1 while Reach\_Ξ^M = 0) is unconditional. The latter appears on X\_0^er: the formal reading of qualification at the initial state is 0, while the qualification target is reachable at the formal layer (Section Six). The second is a separation at the formal layer; its object-layer form (Intent(X\_0) = 0 ⇏ ¬R\_Ξ(X\_0, G\_I)) would further require a negative verdict on qualification at the object layer, and the negative read-back of the atoms newly added in this group form is not closed, so here it is declared only at the formal layer.

## Section Three　Permitted Inputs, System Dynamics and Legal Successors

### 3.1 Permitted inputs

U^adm is a subset of the legal operation set of the unified running certificate, frozen once by Ξ\_R before any path is constructed. Enlarging it requires a separate declaration and a fresh reachability argument. The permitted sets actually used in this chapter:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;U\_ev = {OrgEvent}  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;U\_neg = {OrgEvent, SourceOnlyEvent, IdleStep}  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;U\_neg⁺ = U\_neg ∪ {GenGate(A, 1.0)}

OrgEvent is one complete organizational event: starting from the source-drive node, passing through dimension selection by the probability organization, and running through the carrier, gate-passage segment and post-gate segment of that dimension. Applying OrgEvent is an input; which dimension is selected, and whether the event constitutes action or switching, are facts that follow from the system running according to Spec, not outcomes that can be imposed from outside.

### 3.2 System dynamics

The following occur automatically under Spec; they are not external operations and are not counted in U^adm: decay and sedimentation in the field; the determination of Law-formation; the computation of the formal dimensions, the relative-generation relation and the common binding of the probability organization; dimension selection in organizational events; the updating of gate-bearing records; and the recording by the unified running certificate.

### 3.3 Legal successors

Define the set-valued map F\_R(X, u): under the frozen Spec and input u, the set of one-step successors that the system dynamics produces with positive probability. OrgEvent has at most two successors, corresponding respectively to selecting A and selecting X, with probabilities equal to the current Rel(A) and Rel(X); a dimension whose Rel is 0 is not among the successors. Every other permitted input has exactly one successor.

The reference model has a numerical terminus: after the same dimension has borne several dozen consecutive organizational events, the exponential weight at the entry of the bearing Law overflows and the operation fails partway. This is the numerical representational capacity of the reference program reaching its limit, not the termination of Sedimentation-Field dynamics:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;numerical termination of the reference program ≠ ontological termination of Sedimentation-Field dynamics

Two successor relations are therefore distinguished. F\_R is the successor relation in the sense of Spec. F\_R^ref is the successor relation given by the executable semantics S\_ref of the reference program: when the input completes on X, F\_R^ref(X, u) is the set of completed states that the program produces with positive probability; when the input fails partway on X, the model convention C\_num sets F\_R^ref(X, u) = ∅. C\_num is only a convention about the reference program, not an assertion about the field. The reference working domain W\_ref is the set of all states reached under S\_ref by completed legal operations.

Before the numerical terminus, F\_R^ref agrees with the successors of the reference specification; every history in this chapter consists of completed operations. The theorems of Section Five hold for any successor relation of the form given in this section; when they are applied to the reference instance, the successor relation is taken to be F\_R^ref. Every conclusion that uses C\_num (the exact value of the hitting probability, the lock-in probabilities and the failure of guaranteed arrival in Section Seven, and the exhaustive tail-segment certificate of 8.3) holds only for S\_ref and is not extended to the Sedimentation-Field itself; the conclusions insensitive to C\_num (the explicit paths of Section Six, the invariant-type negative certificate of 8.1, and the lower bound on the hitting probability in Section Seven) hold equally in any semantic extension that agrees with S\_ref before the numerical terminus and keeps the dimension-selection rule, the routes, the write-in scope and the generation-gate multiplier unchanged; this is stated separately in each place.

### 3.4 Forbidden operations

A path may not: write any state quantity of the field, the probability layer or the intention side directly, bypassing legal operations; specify the dimension selection; change gates externally; modify the duty registration, the correspondence, the tuning-candidate registration or the carrier-candidate registration; modify the protocols, the comparison-sequence rule, the declared-segment rule or the target; rewrite the random source partway through; or enlarge the permitted set partway through.

To compute exact kernel probabilities, Section Seven takes out every successor in F\_R^ref(X, u) one by one; this is an analysis of the successor set, not an operation on a path, and it enters no positive path.

### 3.5 Historical legality

Historical legality consists of two parts, both of which must hold for the history to be usable in a proof of reachability. The first is operational legality: whether the inputs and successors are legal is decided by the frozen Spec, U^adm and the successor relation. The second is record legality: whether the running record satisfies the conditions the proof requires is decided by the unified running certificate. That is, a history γ = (X\_0, u\_0, X\_1, …, u\_{H−1}, X\_H) is legal if and only if u\_k ∈ U^adm and X\_{k+1} ∈ F\_R(X\_k, u\_k) for all k \< H, and the unified running certificate reads, on the segment from the initial-state registration point to X\_H,

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Legal\_R(γ) := L1 ∧ L2 ∧ L3 ∧ L5 ∧ L6 = 1

L1 to L3, L5 and L6 are the items of the legal segment of The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics: the trajectory lies in the same run and the same branch with counts that never go backward; state write-ins are closed under legal operations; the duty identity is unchanged; the load-bearing evidence is on the same track; the protocols are frozen. They are proof conditions on the running record and are not the same as the legality of every physical operation; whether the operations themselves are legal is still decided by the first part. L4 (whole-segment qualification persistence) is not a condition of historical legality: the history for a qualification target necessarily spans a change of qualification; L4 appears only as a component of the Intention-Dynamics target itself.

A history of length 0, (X\_0), contains no operation and no write-in that would need closure by the certificate; by convention, Legal\_R((X\_0)) = 1. The unified running certificate reads ⊥ on a zero-width declaration; that is the read-out rule for declared segments and does not alter this convention. Zero-step histories are used only for a hit of a qualification target at the initial state.

## Section Four　Reachability Propositions, Hit Certificates and Verdicts

### 4.1 Object-layer reachability

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;R\_Ξ(X\_0, G) ⟺ ∃γ ∈ ℋ\_Ξ(X\_0) ∃w ⪯ γ : ActualTarget\_G(w)

ℋ\_Ξ(X\_0) is the set of finite legal histories starting from X\_0, and w ⪯ γ means that the witness belongs to this history.

### 4.2 Formal hit certificate

CertHit\_G(w) = 1 if and only if: w belongs to a history with Legal\_R = 1; for action, switching and dynamics targets, the entry qualification of w (at the start of the action event, at the start of both events of the switch, at the starting point of the dynamics declared segment) has formal reading 1; the formal reading of the target takes 1 on w, and all the formal solvability conditions this reading requires hold. A qualification target has no separate entry: its formal reading is itself the qualification reading, and the witness may be the initial-state registration point, that is, a zero-step hit (H = 0) is allowed; the qualification instance of this chapter takes an initial state whose formal reading of qualification is 0, so there is no zero-step hit; in the instance the hit is at step 1 (6.3). The formal readings of the four classes of target are:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;G\_I: IntentCoreCert^M = 1 (The Mathematical Form of Intention)  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;G\_IAct: Read\_{N\_IAct}^M(w) = 1, with IA-M1 to IA-M12 all holding (The Mathematical Form of Intention-Action)  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;G\_IGS: Read\_{N\_IGS(σ)}^M(w\_a, w\_b) = 1, with IGS-M1 to IGS-M10 all holding (The Mathematical Form of Intention Desire-Gate Switching)  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;G\_IDyn: Read\_{N\_IDyn^γ}^M = 1, with IDyn-M1 to IDyn-M14 all holding and all six items of the legal segment equal to 1 (The Mathematical Form of Intention-Dynamics)

Between a formal hit and holding at the object layer there are two directions, which must be registered separately and cannot be replaced by the single phrase “the monitor is correct”:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;No false positives: CertHit\_G(w) = 1 ⇒ ActualTarget\_G(w)  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;No false negatives: ActualTarget\_G(w) ⇒ MonitorHit\_G(w)

No false positives is given by the upstream positive read-back and the sufficient direction of the Necessity Forms, with conditions inherited according to their original upstream attribution (the condition table of Section Eleven). No false negatives requires that the monitor read all legal events and all state write-ins on the history, and that a structural-layer target have a corresponding positive witness at the formal layer; the latter is exactly the reverse representation required by the upstream negative read-back. Only when no false negatives and complete coverage of legal successors hold together can object-layer unreachability be inferred from “the monitor did not hit.”

Formal-layer reachability is defined as:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Reach\_Ξ^M(X\_0, G) = 1 ⟺ ∃γ ∈ ℋ\_Ξ(X\_0) ∃w ⪯ γ : CertHit\_G(w) = 1

### 4.3 Three quantifiers

Instance reachability: R\_Ξ(X\_0, G).

Reachability within the initial-state domain: for a declared initial-state domain D, ∀X\_0 ∈ D, R\_Ξ(X\_0, G) holds.

Guaranteed arrival: every maximal legal history starting from X\_0 hits G. Under S\_ref and the permitted set U\_ev, Section Seven considers only histories truncated at the first hit; these histories all hit within 40 steps or, after lock-in, end at the numerical terminus along the unique successor chain, so all of them are finite. Continuations after the first hit are not considered. A permitted set containing IdleStep or SourceOnlyEvent can have infinitely long histories.

Guaranteed arrival implies instance reachability, but not conversely; reachability within the initial-state domain implies the reachability of each instance in it, but not conversely. Reachability is not controllability: no input in the permitted set can specify the dimension selection, and the same input sequence leads to different successors under different random realizations.

### 4.4 Reachability verdict

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Det\_R^𝒯(Ξ\_R) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥, OutDom}

OutDom: X\_0 is not within the working domain declared by Ξ\_R (for example, it lies beyond the numerical terminus), or 𝒯 does not belong to the four classes of target. It is routed out separately, before any determination on the evidence.

⊥ (DeclarationInvalid): Ξ\_R or its registration was not frozen before running, or the permitted set, the comparison-sequence rule, the declared-segment rule or the target was changed partway through. This follows the upstream discipline that “declaration invalidity only falls back to ⊥.”

1: a legal history and a CertHit\_G = 1 on it have been obtained, and the conditions required for the positive read-back of that target in 11.2 are already closed. Under the discipline of The Determination Form of Intention that “when the verdict is 1, the object certainly holds,” Det\_R = ⊥ (LawReadOpen) while these conditions are not closed, and the formal-layer reachability Reach\_Ξ^M = 1 is registered separately.

0: a negative certificate has been obtained (Theorem Two of Section Five).

⊥: neither has been obtained. No hit within finitely many steps, or no hit over finitely many realizations, can only be recorded as ⊥.

Each of the four classes of target has its own Det\_R^𝒯; they are not combined into a total verdict.

## Section Five　Reachability Theorems

Theorem One (Soundness of Positive Paths)　Let the task specification Ξ\_R be frozen, and suppose: (1) X\_0 is in the declared working domain; (2) there is a finite history γ with u\_k ∈ U^adm and X\_{k+1} ∈ F\_R(X\_k, u\_k) for all k \< H; (3) Legal\_R(γ) = 1; (4) there is w ⪯ γ with CertHit\_G(w) = 1. Then

(I) formal-layer reachability holds: Reach\_Ξ^M(X\_0, G) = 1;

(II) when the positive read-back conditions corresponding to the target type hold (the condition table of Section Eleven), object-layer reachability holds: R\_Ξ(X\_0, G) = 1.

Proof. (I) By (2) and (3), γ is a finite legal history starting from X\_0; by (4), there is a formal hit certificate on γ; by the definition in Section Four, formal-layer reachability holds. (II) Through upstream positive read-back, the formal reading in CertHit\_G(w) = 1 yields that N\_I, N\_IAct, N\_IGS(σ) or N\_IDyn^γ holds on w, and the sufficient direction of the respective Necessity Form then yields that Intent, IntentAct, IntentGateSwitch\_F(σ) or IntentDyn\_F^γ(K\_I) holds on w; the formal reading of the entry qualification is likewise read back through The Mathematical Form of Intention and The Necessity Form of Intention. Hence ActualTarget\_G(w) holds on γ, and R\_Ξ(X\_0, G) = 1. This conclusion is conditionalized by the read-back conditions used; it does not become unconditional because (I) is unconditional. □

Theorem Two (Negative Sufficiency on a Closed Domain)　Let the task specification Ξ\_R be frozen and let D be a set of states, and suppose:

(1) X\_0 ∈ D, and X\_0 is in the declared working domain; D is a set of states under the successor relation used;

(2) closure: for all X ∈ D and u ∈ U^adm, F\_R(X, u) ⊆ D;

(3) necessary condition: according to the target type, a necessary condition is given that covers every legal way of hitting the target, the zero-step way included.  
　　State target: a state predicate Nec\_G^st is given such that, whenever ActualTarget\_G holds at some evaluation point, the state at that point satisfies Nec\_G^st;  
　　event, sequence and segment targets: a one-step predicate Nec\_G^step is given such that, whenever ActualTarget\_G(w) holds, the history used by w within the time window of 2.3 necessarily contains a step satisfying Nec\_G^step;  
　　the negative read-back of the failure of the necessary condition is a direct representation or already Established, and the coverage conditions this negative read-back requires hold on the record of that history;

(4) invariant: for a state target, no state in D satisfies Nec\_G^st (X\_0 included); for event, sequence and segment targets, no step from any state in D under any permitted input satisfies Nec\_G^step.

Then R\_Ξ(X\_0, G) = 0.

Proof. First, every finite legal history starting from X\_0 lies in D: the only history of length 0 is X\_0 ∈ D; if all legal histories of length k lie in D, then by (2) the next state of any legal extension of them is still in D.

State target: every evaluation point on every legal history, the initial-state registration point of the zero-step history included, has its state in D; by (4), no point satisfies Nec\_G^st; by (3), no point makes ActualTarget\_G hold.

Event, sequence and segment targets: a witness of this kind requires at least one event, and a zero-step history contains no event, so there is no zero-step hit. On a legal history of length at least 1, every step starts from a state in D under a permitted input and so, by (4), does not satisfy Nec\_G^step; by 2.3, the events used by a witness all lie in this history after the initial-state registration point, so by (3) no witness in it makes ActualTarget\_G hold.

In both cases there is no hitting history, and R\_Ξ(X\_0, G) = 0. □

Corollary One (Tail-Segment Form)　Let G be a sequence target composed of pairs of adjacent events (such as the switching target of 2.3), and let h be a legal history prefix starting from X\_0 with final state X\_L. Suppose that the state set D contains X\_L and is closed under U^adm (Theorem Two (2)); that the one-step predicate Nec\_G^step is tied to the later-state event, i.e., whenever ActualTarget\_G(w\_a, w\_b) holds, the step that produces the later-state event w\_b satisfies Nec\_G^step; that no step from any state in D under any permitted input satisfies Nec\_G^step (Theorem Two (4)); and that, for every legal extension of h, the pair formed by the last event of h and the first event after X\_L (the pair straddling X\_L) does not constitute a hit. Then no legal extension of h has a hit after X\_L.

Proof. By closure, all states after X\_L are in D. A witness completed after X\_L is a pair of adjacent events. If both events lie after X\_L, the step that produces the later-state event starts from a state in D and does not satisfy Nec\_G^step, whereas a hit requires that this step satisfy it, so the pair does not constitute a hit; otherwise it is the pair straddling X\_L, which by hypothesis does not constitute a hit. □

Corollary One speaks only of what comes after X\_L; it does not say that the whole history containing X\_L never hits, nor does it change the reachability verdict at the initial state.

The conclusion of Theorem Two is object-layer unreachability and does not pass through a monitor. If a monitor is used instead to give a negative verdict, no false negatives (4.2) and complete coverage of successors must be proven separately; if either is lacking, a monitor's failure to hit can only be recorded as ⊥.

Theorem Three (Monotonicity of the Permitted Set and Non-Extrapolation of the Restricted Domain)　Let Ξ\_R and Ξ\_R′ differ only in their permitted sets, with U^adm ⊆ U^adm′. Then R\_Ξ(X\_0, G) = 1 ⇒ R\_Ξ′(X\_0, G) = 1; but R\_Ξ(X\_0, G) = 0 does not imply R\_Ξ′(X\_0, G) = 0 (the instance witness for the latter statement is unconditional at the formal layer and, at the object layer, follows the read-back conditions of 11.2; see the proof).

Proof. Forward direction: every input at every step of a legal history in Ξ\_R lies in U^adm′, the successor map is unchanged, and the items of historical legality depend only on the history itself, so the history is also a legal history in Ξ\_R′, with the same witness. The converse fails: Section Eight gives an instance on X\_0^z with Reach^M = 0 and Det\_R = 0 (U\_neg) but Reach^M = 1 (U\_neg⁺); the closure condition (2) of Theorem Two does not hold for the newly added GenGate. This counterexample holds unconditionally at the formal layer (both sides are formal readings); at the object layer, the side R\_Ξ = 0 is Established by Theorem Two, and the side R\_Ξ′ = 1 follows the read-back conditions of 11.2. □

Theorem Three also applies to working domains: unreachability proven on a restricted working domain implies at most unreachability within that restricted domain; unless there is a separate proof of equivalent coverage, it does not imply unreachability within a larger working domain.

Theorem Four (Conservative Extension by a Read-Only Monitor)　Suppose a monitor only reads the records of the unified running certificate and the event records, invokes no legal operation and writes no state quantity. Then, under the same initial state, the same permitted input sequence and the same random realization, the two histories with and without the monitor have identical full-state digests at every step.

Proof. The unified running certificate is passive and does not update the state of the field or the probability layer (3.3 of The Construction Form of the Intention Operating Substrate); the read-out layer changes no subsequent state of the running layer or the probability layer (3.1 of the same chapter, U8); readers do not enter the causal update of the field (Theorem One (6) of that chapter, V46, V47). The monitor is only a combination of these readers and records and invokes no legal operation, so it changes neither the state nor the random source at any step; the two histories have the same inputs and the same random realization, and are therefore identical step by step. The differential run of Appendix A checks that after 40 steps the full-state digests of the two sides are the same and that the dimension selections are the same at every step. □

Conservativeness guarantees only that the monitor does not write back into the original system, not that its recognition is correct: a monitor that misses an event, or that splices together records from different histories, is just as conservative, yet it will produce false negatives or false positives (Section Ten).

Theorem Five (Finite-Rank Reachability)　This comes in two versions.

(I) The state version, used only for state targets, that is, targets for which ActualTarget depends only on the current state (qualification targets). Let the declared state domain D be contained in the working domain, and let there be a function ρ: D → ℕ such that: (1) when ρ(X) = 0, ActualTarget\_G holds at X; (2) for all X ∈ D with ρ(X) \> 0, there exist u ∈ U^adm and X′ ∈ F\_R(X, u) with X′ ∈ D and ρ(X′) \< ρ(X), and this step does not break Legal\_R. Then R\_Ξ(X, G) = 1 for all X ∈ D, and there is a hitting history of length at most ρ(X).

(II) The prefix version, used for historical event targets (action, switching and dynamics targets). The witnesses of such targets use the event records and earlier events, and the same state can be reached by different histories, so a rank defined on states alone does not suffice to guarantee a hit. The rank is therefore defined on legal history prefixes: let 𝒟 be a family of finite legal history prefixes starting from X\_0, and for a prefix h in 𝒟 (whose final state is written last(h)) let there be a function ρ: 𝒟 → ℕ such that: (1) when ρ(h) = 0, there exist u ∈ U^adm and X′ ∈ F\_R(last(h), u) such that the extended prefix h·(u, X′) is legal and carries a witness on which ActualTarget\_G holds; (2) when ρ(h) \> 0, there exist u and X′ ∈ F\_R(last(h), u) such that h·(u, X′) ∈ 𝒟, h·(u, X′) is legal and ρ(h·(u, X′)) \< ρ(h). Then for every h ∈ 𝒟 there is a legal extension of h that hits the target, using at most ρ(h) + 1 additional steps; in particular, if the empty prefix (X\_0) ∈ 𝒟, then R\_Ξ(X\_0, G) = 1. All the historical material used by the witness lies within the given prefix and its extension; no other history is tacitly allowed to supply antecedents.

The prefix version can also be written on monitor-extended states (X, m), where m is the read-only monitor's record of the history that has occurred (for example, the gate-bearing and entry qualification of the previous event). By Theorem Four, adding m does not change the successors of the original system; as long as m records all the historical material the target witness requires, a rank on (X, m) is equivalent to a rank on prefixes.

Proof. (I) Starting from X, repeatedly take a successor according to (2); each one remains in D and ρ strictly decreases over the natural numbers, so a state with ρ = 0 is reached in at most ρ(X) steps, and by (1) it is already a hit. (II) Likewise, repeatedly extend the prefix: a prefix with ρ = 0 is reached in at most ρ(h) steps, and one further step by (1) gives a hit; every step uses a permitted input, stays within the legal successors and remains legal, so the resulting prefix is a legal history and the witness belongs to it. □

If ActualTarget\_G in (1) is replaced by CertHit\_G = 1, the conclusion of Theorem Five becomes Reach\_Ξ^M = 1 accordingly, and the object layer follows the read-back conditions of Theorem One (II).

The quantifier of Theorem Five is “every state (or every prefix) has a descending successor”; what it yields is only the existence of a path, not that every legal history arrives, nor any probabilistic conclusion. ρ is only a proof tool, not a new ontology of Intention, and it enters no read-back. This chapter constructs no ρ for the reference instance.

Lemma One (Positive Probability and Existence)　Suppose: (1) the input policy π is fixed in advance and is a map from legal history prefixes to permitted inputs (when the permitted set contains only one input, π is unique); (2) every prefix h has finitely many successors under π(h); (3) every successor has positive probability, and at every prefix that has successors, the successor probabilities sum to 1; (4) a terminal prefix is a prefix h at which π(h) has no successor on last(h), and a maximal history is a history that ends at a terminal prefix or is truncated at its first hit; (5) the lengths of maximal histories have a uniform upper bound; (6) a “hit” means a formal hit certificate CertHit\_G = 1, computed by the monitor according to the definition of Section Four, and the monitor recognizes this formal hit correctly (it misses no event and does not splice across histories). Then the probability P\_π(hit) of hitting G starting from X\_0 is the sum, over the hitting histories, of the products of the successor probabilities at their steps, and: P\_π(hit) \> 0 if and only if there is a hitting legal history under policy π; P\_π(hit) = 1 if and only if every maximal history under policy π hits.

Proof. By (2) and (5), all maximal histories under policy π form a finite tree; by (3), the probability of each maximal history is the product of the successor probabilities at its steps and is positive, and the probabilities of all maximal histories sum to 1 (by induction on the depth of the tree: the probabilities of the children of each internal node sum to 1). Hitting histories are truncated at their first hit and form part of the leaves of this tree. Hence P\_π(hit) \> 0 if and only if there is a hitting leaf; P\_π(hit) = 1 if and only if the non-hitting leaves have total probability 0, and since every leaf has positive probability, if and only if there is no non-hitting leaf. □

The conclusion of Lemma One is at the formal layer: probability one is not to be interpreted as necessary arrival in a general Sedimentation-Field, and positive probability becomes object-layer reachability only through the read-back conditions of Section Eleven. Lemma One is a general proposition. Its application to the reference kernel of Section Seven requires its hypotheses to be checked one by one, which is done in 7.2; the P(hit) of Section Seven is P\_π(hit) under the policy π ≡ OrgEvent; its probabilities presuppose the idealization of uniform sampling and are a computation on the dimension-selection kernel, not a statistical estimate about natural systems.

Lemma Two (Persistence of Certificate Legality)　Suppose that a history γ of length at least 1 starts from the initial-state registration point; that the random realization is chosen before the registration point; that every subsequent step is an input in U^adm, executed and completed through the legal operation interface of the unified running certificate, with no other write-in in between; and that no input in U^adm writes the duty registration, the correspondence, the tuning-candidate registration, the carrier-candidate registration or the protocol objects. Then Legal\_R(γ) = 1.

Proof. Check item by item against the read-out rules for L1 to L6 in 3.3 of The Construction Form of the Intention Operating Substrate. L1: the endpoint lies after the starting point; the actual state at the starting point is exactly the state after the record at the registration point; every record is taken from the same object, and the run and branch identities are unchanged; the running count and the sedimentation count increase only through pulses and decay and never decrease. L2: every step is executed through the legal operation interface, which records a full-state digest once before and once after the operation; there is no other write-in between two steps (the random realization was chosen before the registration point and is not rewritten afterward), so adjacent records join end to end and the state at the end of the segment joins the last record; the operation of every record lies in the legal operation set, the two authoritative logs are appended to only by these operations, and the audit passes. L3: the duty-identity snapshot consists of the duty registration, the correspondence and the candidate registrations, none of which is written by any input in U^adm, so it is the same at every point. L5: the load-bearing evidence within the segment is exactly the events produced by these operations themselves, in the same run, the same branch, the same anchor and the same protocol, carrying evidence keys and registered by the same segment certificate. L6: the protocol snapshot consists of the protocol objects and their histories, none of which is written by any input in U^adm, so the frozen state is unchanged. □

A history of length 0 is legal by the convention of 3.5. Lemma Two speaks only of the record legality of a history; operational legality (the first part of 3.5) is guaranteed separately by U^adm and the successor relation.

Formal-layer guaranteed arrival (4.3) is stated for all input policies; when the permitted set contains only one input, it coincides with “every maximal history under policy π hits” in Lemma One.

## Section Ten　Separation Propositions and Counterexamples

The following counterexamples are all actually obtained in the reference specification (Appendix A); each one breaks down a common misjudgment.

Counterexample One (cross-history splicing): the prior-state event is taken from an organizational event entering \[G\_X\] in one run, and the later-state event from an organizational event entering \[G\_A\] in another run. The sequence number of the prior-state event is smaller than that of the later-state event, but their running identities differ, so IGS-M8 and IGS-M10 do not hold, the switching reading is ⊥, and the monitor does not hit. The two pieces of material found are each real, yet they do not constitute a hit on one and the same history.

Counterexample Two (spurious target): the prior state is an organizational event entering \[G\_X\], and the later state is an external gate-change event that changes the gate-bearing to gA (external gate change is in none of the permitted sets of this chapter and is used here only to construct the counterexample). A monitor that takes gate occupancy as its target hits; but the reading of Intention Desire-Gate switching is ⊥ (IGS-M4), and the switching verdict is not 1. The switching reading here first checks IGS-M1 to IGS-M10 and is ⊥ if the conditions are incomplete; V20 of The Construction Form of the Intention Operating Substrate takes the readings of S1 to S4 directly for the external gate change, with S2 equal to 0, and records the switching as 0. In neither place is it 1. Gate occupancy is not Intention Desire-Gate switching: CertHit must have no false positives with respect to the target itself.

Counterexample Three (omitted legal successors): 8.2. Signing unreachability on U\_neg⁺ with the closed set D of U\_neg leaves out the successors of GenGate.

Counterexample Four (monitor false negative): the realization for stream number 6 selects X, X, X, X, A, X. A monitor that compares only the gate-bearing after the 1st event with that after the 6th event reports “no change”; an event-by-event monitor reads two switches, one of them into \[G\_A\]. The end-state-comparison monitor is conservative, yet it misses the switches within the segment. This is also an instance of the upstream principle that “the end states of a unit do not stand in for cross-time facts.”

Counterexample Five (declaring unreachability after no hit within finitely many steps): stream number 0 has no hit within 83 steps; to declare on that basis that the target is unreachable from X\_0 would be wrong: P(hit) = 0.497, stream number 1 hits at step 4, and in the exact tree hitting histories run up to 40 steps. Without a complete negative certificate, no hit within finitely many steps can only be recorded as ⊥. Stream number 0 later passes through a lock-in state, and in S\_ref histories passing through it no longer hit afterward (8.3); but this is a conclusion about histories after lock-in, not about X\_0.

Counterexample Six (zero-rate misjudgment): on X\_0^z, Rel(A) = 0; switching into \[G\_A\] is unreachable on U\_neg and reachable on U\_neg⁺. Zero rate is a property of a state, not of the reachable set; “a zero-rate direction is never reachable” holds only on permitted sets that contain nothing restoring its generation, and only with a closed-domain proof.

Counterexample Seven (working-domain confusion): the first event in 6.3 has action formal reading 1, while the formal reading of qualification at its start is 0 and the qualification verdict is ⊥. Counting it as a hit of the action target confuses the target reading with the entry of the target.

Counterexample Eight (structurally compatible but without a path): on X\_0^z, GateCorrespondence = 1, the correspondence and pass-through A ↔ \[G\_A\] both hold, and a switch into \[G\_A\] is structurally compatible; yet it is unreachable on U\_neg. Structural compatibility does not mean that a path of actual arrival exists.

Counterexample Nine (preinstalled structure passing as endogenous generation): the qualification target of 6.3 is reachable from an initial state whose formal reading of qualification is 0, but this is renewed Law-formation on an already placed structure. To write it up as “Intention is generated by the Sedimentation-Field on its own” oversteps the construction starting point of this chapter.

This yields the following separations:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;reachability ≠ qualification, action or dynamics itself; reachability ≠ stability; reachability ≠ controllability; reachability ≠ guaranteed arrival;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;no hit in one run ≠ unreachable; zero rate ≠ never reachable; an initial state whose formal reading of qualification is 0 ≠ necessarily unreachable;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;structural compatibility ≠ reachability; formal hit ≠ holding at the object layer (read-back conditions required); a conservative monitor ≠ a correct monitor.

## Section Twelve　Breach

According to the norms of the Reachability Form, breaches are of two kinds.

First, proving that no permitted path can arrive: for the initial states declared in Section Two and the permitted sets frozen in Section Three, proving that some target signed as reachable at the formal layer (Reach\_Ξ^M = 1) has no witness on any legal history in any finite number of steps.

        except OverflowError:

            b = A.point()

class FixedDraw(random.Random):
    """One realization of the uniform draw inside the kernel's support (every draw returns v)."""
    def __init__(self, v): super().__init__(0); self.v = v
    def random(self): return self.v

DRAW = {'A': 0.0, 'X': 1 - 1e-12}       # choices are ordered (A, X): r=0 selects A, r near 1 selects X

def step_choice(I, d):

def lock_chain_ok(I, alone, prev=None):
    """Once only one dimension remains in V the successor under OrgEvent is unique; follow it to the numeric
    horizon and confirm that the missing dimension never re-enters V and no switch into A occurs."""
    J = copy.deepcopy(I); J.rng = FixedDraw(DRAW[alone]); n = 0
    while True:
        r = rel(J)
        if not (len(r) == 1 and abs(r.get(alone, 0.0) - 1.0) <= 1e-15):    # single successor with probability 1
            return dict(steps_to_horizon=n, reentered=True, chain_node_not_single=True)
        try: ev = J.org_event()
        except OverflowError: return dict(steps_to_horizon=n, reentered=False)
        n += 1
        if len(J.P.V()) > 1: return dict(steps_to_horizon=n, reentered=True)
        if prev is not None and hit_switch_into(J, prev, ev): return dict(steps_to_horizon=n, reentered=True, hit=True)
        prev = ev

def tree():
    I0 = ir.build(0)
    P_hit = 0.0; P_lockX = 0.0; P_lockA = 0.0; leaves = []; chains = []
    nodes = dict(internal=0, bad=0, max_norm_error=0.0, min_branch_probability=1.0, intent_not_1_at_internal=0)
    lock_words = []; xa = dict(pairs=0, not_hit=0)
    def go(I, prev, prob, word):
        nonlocal P_hit, P_lockX, P_lockA
        V = I.P.V()
        if len(V) != 1:
            r = rel(I); nodes['internal'] += 1
            if not (len(V) == 2 and all(r.get(d, 0.0) > 0 for d in ('A', 'X'))): nodes['bad'] += 1
            nodes['max_norm_error'] = max(nodes['max_norm_error'], abs(sum(r.values()) - 1.0))
            nodes['min_branch_probability'] = min(nodes['min_branch_probability'], min(r.values()))
            nodes['intent_not_1_at_internal'] += int(I.qualification()['Intent'] is not True)
        if len(V) == 1:
            ch = lock_chain_ok(I, V[0], prev); chains.append(ch)
            rl = ''.join(f'{k}{len(list(g))}' for k, g in itertools.groupby(word))   # run-length form, e.g. A11X29
            lock_words.append(dict(word=rl, alone=V[0], last_event_gate=(prev or {}).get('gate_after')))
            if V[0] == 'X': P_lockX += prob
            else: P_lockA += prob
            leaves.append((word, 'lock-' + V[0], prob)); return
        for d in ('A', 'X'):
            nxt, p = step_choice(I, d)
            if nxt is None: continue
            J, ev = nxt
            is_hit = prev is not None and hit_switch_into(J, prev, ev)
            if prev is not None and prev.get('dim') == 'X' and d == 'A': xa['pairs'] += 1; xa['not_hit'] += int(not is_hit)
            if is_hit:
                P_hit += prob * p; leaves.append((word + d, 'hit', prob * p)); continue
            go(J, ev, prob * p, word + d)
    go(I0, None, 1.0, '')
    return dict(P_hit=P_hit, P_lock_X=P_lockX, P_lock_A=P_lockA, total=P_hit + P_lockX + P_lockA, leaves=len(leaves), node_checks=nodes,
                hit_leaves=sum(1 for _, t, _ in leaves if t == 'hit'), lock_leaves=sum(1 for _, t, _ in leaves if t != 'hit'),
                longest_word=max(len(w) for w, _, _ in leaves), hit_lengths=dict(Counter(len(w) for w, t, _ in leaves if t == 'hit')),
                lock_chains=len(chains), any_reentry=any(c['reentered'] for c in chains), lock_leaves_detail=lock_words,
                x_then_a_pairs=xa['pairs'], x_then_a_pairs_not_hit=xa['not_hit'],
                lock_chain_nodes_single_successor_prob_1=sum(c['steps_to_horizon'] for c in chains) if not any(c.get('chain_node_not_single') for c in chains) else None,
                horizon_range=(min(c['steps_to_horizon'] for c in chains), max(c['steps_to_horizon'] for c in chains)))
