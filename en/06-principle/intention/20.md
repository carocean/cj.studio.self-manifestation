# The Universal Invariant Form of Intention

## Section One　Purpose, Form-Kind and Single Task

### 1.1 Form-kind

This chapter is an Invariant Form, an auxiliary form.

The current version of the Canon Chapter does not yet include a chapter on the “Invariant Form,” so this chapter adopts the following provisional specification: once an object, qualification, duty or relation has obtained a legal definition, an Invariant Form studies what must persist and what may change under a family of legal substitutions of realization or changes of representation frozen in advance, at which level of abstraction the persistence occurs, and on what grounds different realizations are judged to bear the same duty. Its typical question is “the realization can be exchanged; what cannot be?” It does not answer anew “what must an object minimally have in order to hold?”; that question belongs to the General Form.

### 1.2 Single task

The single task of this chapter is to provide, for the four-item necessary core of The General Form of Intention, a cross-realization structural support distinct from direct semantic judgment by the main book.

This support has three layers. First, in a theory-neutral layer that does not presuppose the ontology of On Self-Manifestation, it determines the duties that system orientation cannot escape. Next, it proves that once these duties enter the Sedimentation-Field, they obtain the structure of Law and Probability under the existing conditions on the Law side. Finally, it establishes the four representation bridges by which, once they enter the Self-Manifestation-Field, they lead to the four-item structure of The General Form of Intention, and states explicitly which parts have already closed independently and which still await the closing of conditions.

This chapter does not prove The General Form of Intention anew, does not change any of its conclusions, and does not prove that having these duties suffices to be Intention; the latter belongs to The Necessity Form of Intention.

### 1.3 Preview of results

Layer One, the theory-neutral layer:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MultiDirectionalTask(C, V) ∧ SysDirClaim(X; C, V) passes the check ⇒ SysBear(X) ∧ DirPersist(X)

That is, every realization that declares “these different directions belong to the system itself,” and whose declaration passes the check, must have two duties: system directional bearing (SysBear) and persistence of directional distinction (DirPersist). Across different realizations related by task persistence, both duties must hold anew.

Layer Two, the Sedimentation-Field representation layer: in the Sedimentation-Field, system directional bearing specializes to rate-bearing, the formal directions are borne by Law, and Probability is obtained. This layer inherits the bridge conditions of The Law Group-Form.

Layer Three, the Self-Manifestation-Field representation layer: in the Self-Manifestation-Field, on the basis of upstream field structures independent of The General Form of Intention, through four representation bridges each with its own registered conditions, and under co-anchor binding, these duties and Probability settle into

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;P\_I ∧ SourceDrive ∧ GateCorrespondence ∧ TuneInterface

that is, the four-item structure of The General Form of Intention.

In one sentence: what is universal is duty; under H2 ∧ H\_3^red, and when the four Self-Manifestation-Field representation bridges close, the four items constitute the complete realization of these duties in the Self-Manifestation-Field.

This preview is not a premise of the subsequent derivation.

## Section Two　Neutral Task Entry and System-Attribution Declaration

### 2.1 Intention is uncountable

Intention is one and uncountable: Intention is Intention itself. This chapter says only “Intention.” Duties, structures, nodes, directions and realizations can all be distinguished and counted, but counting them is not counting Intention.

### 2.2 Task entry

A candidate system X enters this chapter by first declaring a task instance:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;χ\_UI = (X, B\_X, C, V, Γ\_task)

Here X is the declared system, B\_X is the system boundary frozen in advance, C is a continuation question, V = {v₁, …, v\_n} is the set of successor classes of C, and Γ\_task is the task comparison protocol frozen in advance.

The entry stipulates only three things:

(1) Object layer: what this chapter studies is system-level directional qualification, not a single isolated event. This item only distinguishes qualification from occurrence; it does not stipulate how the qualification is realized, nor does any re-walk support follow from it.

(2) Multidirectional problem:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MultiDirectionalTask(C, V): \|V\| ≥ 2

That is, one and the same continuation question has at least two distinguishable successor classes. This stipulates only that there are different destinations; it does not stipulate that they are borne by Law, that they compose a Probability, or who bears their relative generation, nor whether there are Desire-Gates.

(3) System-attribution declaration:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SysDirClaim(X; C, V)

That is, the declaration that the directional differences on C belong to the orientation role of the system X, not to the role of the researcher, of a description protocol, or of a mechanism outside the boundary.

At the entry, SysDirClaim is only a declaration still to be checked, not an established structural fact, nor is it defined by the duties this chapter is to compare. Whether it holds is decided by the causal attribution check of Sections Four and Five.

### 2.3 Levels of comparison

What this chapter compares is duties, not physical structures, topologies or behavioral outcomes. A duty states what something does in the system and in what relation it stands to the other duties; it does not state what does it or where. One physical structure can bear several duties, and several physical structures can jointly bear one duty:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Duty ≠ Module

### 2.4 Two questions kept apart

“What Intention is” is answered by The General Form of Intention; this chapter answers only “which duties cannot be escaped under change of realization, and why, in On Self-Manifestation, they settle into those four items.”

### 2.5 Working domain

Ω\_Sed is the Sedimentation-Field, that is, the working domain of The Law Group-Form. Ω\_SM is the Self-Manifestation-Field described by On Self-Manifestation: in the Intent-Field there are the Intention-Domain and the Desire-Gates, and behind the Desire-Gates there is the tuning side. This description of the working domain does not stipulate where the probability organization lies, nor how directions correspond to Desire-Gates; these are handled by the representation bridges of Section Nine. Ω\_SM is contained in Ω\_Sed.

## Section Three　Task-Persistence Comparison Protocol and the Family of Re-Realizations

### 3.1 The task-persistence relation

The task-persistence relation X ∼\_task X′ between two realizations X and X′ requires only that the following be the same:

T1　Task identity: the same continuation question C, or a task isomorphism ψ\_C: C ≅ C′ frozen in advance.  
T2　Direction-category signature: between V and V′ there is a role correspondence ψ\_V: V ↔ V′ frozen in advance. It is only a correspondence of task categories and does not stipulate how directions are produced internally.  
T3　System-boundary rule: the rule demarcating inside from outside persists; the boundary may not be moved after the fact in order to count a selector as “the system itself.”  
T4　Legal interface signature: the task meaning of the normal input, output and environment-interaction interfaces persists.  
T5　Comparison protocol: Γ\_task is fixed before comparison; “what counts as the same task” is not changed after both realizations have been inspected.

### 3.2 What is allowed to change

T6　Freedom of internal realization: arbitrary changes are allowed in material, number of nodes, topology, centralization or distribution, continuity or discreteness, time scale, modular division, algorithm, mediating chains, and biological or artificial carrier.

3.3 No content to be proven in the protocol

The definition of the task-persistence relation contains neither SysBear nor DirPersist. Whether one realization is task-persistent with another depends only on T1 to T5, not on whether the two duties hold in it.

### 3.4 Mode of comparison

This chapter does not claim that “a realization with Intention still has Intention after any legal re-realization.” A legal re-realization may well spoil the directional qualification. What this chapter compares are two realizations that have each passed the attribution check:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;X ∼\_task X′,  SysDirClaim(X) and SysDirClaim(X′) each pass the check

and then it asks which duties are indispensable at both ends.

## Section Four　Obtaining System Directional Bearing, and Counterexamples

### 4.1 What is checked

SysDirClaim(X; C, V) declares that the directional differences belong to X. The question the check asks is whether this declaration has grounds on the object side. If no causal responsibility of X can be found on the object side for the directional differences, and for why the current continuation falls into one class, the declaration fails.

### 4.2 Four failure worlds

W1, label: the directional “differences” are only the observer's labels, names or modes of read-out; when the labels change, the system's directional relations do not change at all. The differences the declaration speaks of are not on the object side.

W2, inert relay: changing the legal state of X's internal organization changes none of the reachability relations, relative generation, ordering, availability or actual landing direction of the directions in V; the directions are wholly determined elsewhere, and X merely passes them on.

W3, complete external bearing: outside the boundary there is a mechanism M\_out that, with X only passively executing, completely bears the whole generative responsibility among the directions in V. The Probability Group-Form has already given a ruling of the same type on external selectors: the execution of the outcome can be done by X, but the bearing of the rate does not lie in X.

W4, physical co-location: inside X there is indeed some structure that affects the directions, but it does not belong to the legal duty organization of this directional task; it is only a side branch physically located within the boundary. The Probability Group-Form already has the discipline: physical co-location is not attribution of duty.

In all four worlds, the declaration SysDirClaim comes to nothing on the object side.

### 4.3 System directional bearing

Hence, for the declaration to hold, X must have system directional bearing:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SysBear\_X(C, V) = 1

if and only if there exists a legal causal organization R\_X inside the boundary of X such that:

SB1　System-side efficacy: under frozen legal external-interface conditions, changing the legal state of R\_X can change at least one of the following for the directions in V: reachability relations, relative-generation relations, direction ordering, direction availability, or the direction actually landed on.  
SB2　Non-label: changing only the observer's labels, names or modes of read-out, without changing X's causal organization, does not change the system's directional relations.  
SB3　No complete external bearing: there is no mechanism outside the boundary that, with X only passively executing, completely bears the whole generative responsibility among the directions.  
SB4　Duty attribution, not physical co-location: R\_X belongs to the legal duty organization of this directional task, and is not merely physically located within X.

Theorem One (Theorem of System-Directional Bearing). If SysDirClaim(X; C, V) passes the check, then SysBear\_X(C, V) = 1.

Proof. SB1 to SB4 are respectively what W2, W1, W3 and W4 lack; if any one of the four fails, the declaration falls into the corresponding failure world and comes to nothing on the object side. The proof is complete.

4.4 System directional bearing does not yield endogenous launch

Consider world W5: an external input E\_t reaches X, and inside X there is an already formed, complex directional organization R\_X, with E\_t → R\_X → v\_i. Different states of R\_X do change the direction; the directional differences are not labels; the external input does not directly specify any particular v\_i; so part of the responsibility for generating the direction does belong to X, and SysBear = 1. Yet in the absence of current external input, X never launches this continuation on its own: the start of the event comes entirely from outside, and X is responsible only for deciding “where to” after the event has been triggered. Therefore:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SysBear ⇏ EndoDrive

What system directional bearing really gives is only system-direction generative responsibility: the explanation of “why this direction and not another” cannot be moved entirely out of X. A system can be started by external events and yet internally bear the generation of direction. The stronger launch duty on the source-drive side is not obtained in the theory-neutral layer.

## Section Five　Obtaining Persistence of Directional Distinction, Its Independence, and Counterexamples

### 5.1 Failure world

W6, pre-handover confluence: at an upstream stage, X does itself form two different directions v\_a ≠ v\_b, and SysBear = 1; but before producing any different successors the two directions merge completely, v\_a → h, v\_b → h, and from h on there is no physical or causal difference whatever. Even if different outputs appear later, they can only be produced anew by new factors after h, and cannot be attributed to the original v\_a and v\_b. The declaration that “the successor differences belong to these two directions of X” then comes to nothing.

5.2 Persistence of directional distinction

Hence, for the declaration to hold, it is further required that:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DirPersist\_X(C, V) = 1

That is: if v\_i ≠ v\_j are two different directional roles borne by X itself, then from the formation of the directional difference until the two directions first obtain different successor responsibilities, this difference cannot vanish completely from X's causal organization. Formally: if two legal runs have, before some moment t\_c, entirely entered the same causal equivalence class, State\_i(t\_c) ∼\_C State\_j(t\_c) (∼\_C meaning that the two are indistinguishable for all subsequent legal causal action), and from t\_c on there is no direction-specific legal causal difference, while the successor differences are still declared to be borne by the original v\_i and v\_j, then attribution fails.

In one sentence: a directional difference must persist up to the point where it actually discharges different successor responsibilities.

This does not require discrete gates, single nodes, a single jump, the same topology or explicit module boundaries; continuous fields, distributed systems and state encodings can all satisfy it.

Theorem Two (Theorem of the Persistence of Directional Distinction). If SysDirClaim(X; C, V) passes the check, then DirPersist\_X(C, V) = 1.

Proof. If DirPersist fails, the declaration falls into W6. The proof is complete.

### 5.3 The two are independent

SysBear does not yield DirPersist: in W6, SysBear = 1 while DirPersist = 0.

DirPersist does not yield SysBear: consider W7, in which a selector outside the boundary carries two different labels all the way through to two executors, M\_out → v\_a → out\_a, M\_out → v\_b → out\_b; the directional distinction persists throughout, DirPersist = 1, while the whole directional responsibility lies outside the system, SysBear = 0.

Therefore the two cannot be merged.

5.4 Discrete handover is not a neutral invariant

Consider W8, a continuous dynamical system: the directions v\_a and v\_b pass through no discrete gate, and there is no “first handover node” that could be naturally cut out; in the continuous state flow the directional difference gradually widens, x\_a(t) ≠ x\_b(t), and finally each produces different successors. Directional identity persists, DirPersist = 1, yet there is no discrete handover v\_i → G\_i. So discrete handover is not a theory-neutral invariant; what truly cannot be escaped is the persistence of directional distinction.

## Section Six　Theorem of the Invariance of System-Directional Duty

Theorem Three (Theorem of the Invariance of System-Directional Duty). Let X ∼\_task X′, and let SysDirClaim(X) and SysDirClaim(X′) each pass the check. Then:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SysBear(X) = SysBear(X′) = 1,  DirPersist(X) = DirPersist(X′) = 1

That is, different legal realizations of the same system-orientation role may change their carrier, but cannot lose system directional bearing or persistence of directional distinction.

Proof. By X ∼\_task X′, the two ends belong to the same task comparison class frozen in advance; this relation serves only to establish that the two can be compared, and does not itself guarantee the persistence of any duty to be proven. By hypothesis, the SysDirClaim of X and that of X′ each independently pass the checks of Sections Four and Five. So by Theorems One and Two we have SysBear(X) = DirPersist(X) = 1 and SysBear(X′) = DirPersist(X′) = 1 respectively. Hence, in all task-equivalent realizations that still independently obtain qualification for the same system-orientation role, both duties are indispensable, while the physical realizations bearing them may differ. The proof is complete.

The task-persistence relation is responsible for “comparability,” and the attribution check for “each end having the duties of its own”; it is not that the task-persistence relation yields the persistence of duties.

The invariant here is an invariant across realizations that obtain role qualification, not an invariant with automatic fidelity along a transformation trajectory: in every realization that still obtains the same system-orientation role, however different the physical realization, both duties must hold anew. This is a different task from the Stability Form's study of whether an established qualification can persist and return under permitted change.

Duties that do not enter the theory-neutral layer:

EndoDrive does not enter: W5 shows that system directional bearing does not yield endogenous launch.

IntMod does not enter: a system can hand directions directly to behavioral control; from the mere fact that it has no internal tuning, one cannot conclude in the theory-neutral layer that it bears no system-orientation role.

Discrete handover does not enter: W8 shows that in a continuous realization directional identity can persist without discrete handover.

Re-walk support does not enter: in the neutral layer, a concrete directional content that has never been repeated cannot be excluded merely because it has not occurred again; re-walk support is restored only at the Sedimentation-Field layer.

## Section Seven　Variable Realization Items and Non-Invariants

None of the following is a theory-neutral invariant; they change with realization: physical material; number of nodes; topology; modular division; centralized or distributed bearing; continuous or discrete encoding; time scale; algorithm; length of mediating chains; biological or artificial carrier; whether there are discrete gates; whether a gate is a single node; whether probability exists in numerical form and whether it is readable; the specific tuning algorithm; the particular ring topology of the Power-Ring.

Endogenous launch, internal modulation and discrete handover are not invariants in the theory-neutral layer either; only in the Self-Manifestation-Field, through the conditional representation bridges of Section Nine, do they obtain a stronger standing.

## Section Eight　Sedimentation-Field Representation: Law and Probability

Let X ∈ Ω\_Sed. On entering the Sedimentation-Field, the Sedimentation-Field discipline of On Self-Manifestation is restored.

8.1 A formal directional role must be borne by Law

In the Sedimentation-Field, for a direction to become a current formal directional role, it cannot be merely a bare candidate, a new path or a one-off occurrence; The Probability Group-Form stipulates that probability dimensions are already sedimented paths, not possible paths. A new path becomes a formal direction dimension only by passing through “actual occurrence → sedimentation → obtaining Law-bearing → entering the probability organization.”

Two kinds of “new” must be distinguished. A new concrete target or content can appear for the first time and be carried by an existing directional organization already borne by Law, for example wanting for the first time to go to a city one has never been to; a new independent directional duty dimension must first obtain Law-bearing. New content can appear immediately; a new formal direction dimension cannot appear immediately without qualification.

### 8.2 The route of obtaining Law

This chapter fixes the following route and does not mix it with other routes.

In the Sedimentation-Field, a formal direction dimension is an already sedimented path, so the structure R that bears it directly has the semantics of a “current sedimentary carrying organization”; Established is read directly from this, without passing through H1, the bridge “relation-carrying realization → sedimentary state.” DynamicRole is given by the formal directional role itself: a formal directional role that does not really enter the generation of the successor relation it declares is not current formal directional bearing; so R really enters this successor generation. Here we do not rely on the broad disjunction of SB1, because some of the changes SB1 lists, such as the gating of availability, are only upstream conditions and do not amount to the carrying structure really entering the later generation. ΩSupport must be obtained from StableReplay. A formal direction dimension can be stably re-walked within its claimed scope of application: the corpus registered in The Probability Group-Form says that each direction is a “familiar Law-continuation route” sedimented through reward and punishment along the path, and a familiar route is precisely a path that can be borne again when similar conditions recur; getting from StableReplay to ΩSupport requires H2, registered in The Law Group-Form, together with H\_3^red. Under these conditions, invoking the sufficient direction of The Necessity Form of Law, each formal direction is borne by Law, that is, MultiLawDimension.

8.3 Specializing system directional bearing to rate-bearing

SysBear is a superordinate duty; it requires only that the organization inside X can change at least one of the reachability relations, relative generation, ordering, availability or actual landing of the directions. Once the probability question is entered, it is not yet enough.

Consider world W9: several Law-borne directions jointly answer the same continuation question; X's directional organization D can decide which directions are reachable, so SysBear holds; but among the reachable directions, relative generation is borne by another mechanism M, which lies either outside the boundary or inside the boundary without belonging to D.

In the Sedimentation-Field, several Law-borne formal directions already jointly answer the same continuation question, and what answers “why does this continuation fall into this class and not that one?” is precisely the relative generation among the directions: according to The Probability Group-Form, the relative-generation relation among the dimensions at one Continuation-Position is the relation of how continuation is generated among the dimensions, and a numerical rate is only one representation of it. In W9, D only sets where one can go; what distinguishes among the places one can go is M. So the declaration that “the directional differences belong to the orientation role of the system in which D lies” comes to nothing at its core, namely “which class it falls into”: the system supplies only a menu, and the orientation lies in M. The ruling of The Probability Group-Form on this is the same: the execution of outcomes, local activity, and side branches within the same physical system cannot replace the probability organization's own bearing of relative generation.

Hence, in the Sedimentation-Field, for SysDirClaim to pass the check, the rate-bearing specialization of SysBear is further required:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SysRateBear(D; C): among the formal directions of C, the relative-generation relation is borne by the directional organization D itself

SysRateBear is contained in SysBear but is not identical to SysBear; it is the duty specialization of SysBear once the probability question is entered, and is obtained independently in the Sedimentation-Field by W9.

### 8.4 Obtaining Probability

C is frozen by the task entry, and the formal directions jointly answer it, that is, CommonContinuationBind. Thus

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;MultiLawDimension ∧ CommonContinuationBind ∧ SysRateBear ⇒ N\_P

where SysRateBear is exactly the content of DomainRelativeRateBearing. Invoking the sufficient direction of The Necessity Form of Probability, N\_P ⇒ Probability, we obtain Probability.

At this layer DirPersist guarantees that the distinction among the probability dimensions persists up to where they obtain different successor responsibilities, so that the dimensions do not merge midway.

Theorem Four (Theorem of the Sedimentation-Field Representation). Let X ∈ Ω\_Sed, and let SysDirClaim(X) pass the check. Under H2 ∧ H\_3^red, the formal directions of X are borne by Law, its directional organization bears the relative generation of rate, and Probability is obtained.

This chapter adds none of these conditions; they are existing registrations of The Law Group-Form. The sufficient direction of Probability itself carries no H.

## Section Nine　Self-Manifestation-Field Representation: The Four-Item Necessary Core

Let X ∈ Ω\_SM. Below, using upstream field structures independent of The General Form of Intention, we establish the four representation bridges one by one. Each bridge uses only independent antecedents weaker than its target structure; wherever the independent antecedents are insufficient for closure, this is registered explicitly as an H. The corpus of The General Form of Intention is used only at the end of this section for a consistency back-test, not as antecedents of proof.

The independent upstream structures used:

SMGateBoundary　The relation classification of Form Chapter 4: the Boundary-Gate relation is Gate, Pass and their tuning-control relation. It says only that Gate and Pass belong to this class of control boundaries; it does not say that directions correspond one-to-one to gates.  
SMTuningStructure　The relation classification of Form Chapter 4: the integrative relation is the Principle-Field commanding-tuning relation and the TunePriority weighting relation; TunePriority is defined as “the assignment, by the Intention-Field through the Desire-Gates, of tuning-resource allocation weight and parameter-update contribution to circuits,” and the pathway it cites from Outer Chapter 9 is “Intention-Domain Power-Ring → Desire-Gate → commanding tuning.” It shows that a legal structure of “commanding tuning through the Desire-Gates” exists in the Self-Manifestation-Field; it does not say that behind every Desire-Gate there is a tuning interface.  
SMSourceStructure　The structures of Micro-Core Theory and The Intent-Field inherited by The Yin-Manifestation Group-Form: structures such as A/B, the Power-Ring and the Driving-Factor give “current endogenous issuance” and “B-side action,” and the Intent-Field gives B-side action and backward command; the Power-Ring is only one realization among them. It shows that an endogenous-source side exists in the Self-Manifestation-Field; it does not say that all Desire-Gate continuations of the Intention directional organization are issued by it.

The statement registered in The Probability Group-Form, “Probability driven by source drive is born in the Intent-Field,” is a location statement, and “Desire-Gates correspond to the direction dimensions of probability” is a relational statement of the source text; neither is an independent structural theorem, and this section uses them not as antecedents of proof but only for back-testing.

### 9.1 P\_I

By Theorem Four, X's directional organization obtains Probability. To obtain P\_I, it must further be proven that this organization lies in the Intention-Domain and is not a probability organization of some other field. The independent upstream structures do not give this location. We register:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;H\_PL (Intention-Domain probability-location bridge): in the Self-Manifestation-Field, the directional organization that bears the current directional duty of Intention and obtains formal Probability belongs to the Intention-Domain side, and is not a probability organization of another field.

Hence:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Probability ∧ IntentDirectionalRole ∧ H\_PL ⇒ InIntentDomain, and hence P\_I

Here IntentDirectionalRole means that what this organization bears is the orientation role declared by SysDirClaim. P\_I does not depend logically on SourceDrive, so it does not carry H\_SD; it carries H\_PL and the conditions of Theorem Four.

### 9.2 SourceDrive

By Theorem Four, X's directional organization bears the relative generation of rate (SysRateBear). By SMSourceStructure, the side of current endogenous issuance already has an independent structural basis; by SMTuningStructure, its legal pathway in the current realization of the Self-Manifestation-Field can run through the Intention-Domain Power-Ring and the Desire-Gates to commanding tuning.

But W5 of the theory-neutral layer shows that system directional bearing does not yield endogenous launch. To exclude continuations that are “started externally and only oriented internally” from also reaching the Desire-Gates through the Intention directional organization, a universal statement is needed: all Desire-Gate continuations of the Intention directional organization are issued by the endogenous-source side. The independent upstream structures do not give this universal statement. We register:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;H\_SD (source-drive complete-issuance bridge): in the Self-Manifestation-Field, every Desire-Gate continuation belonging to the Intention directional organization is issued from the endogenous-source side of the Intention-Domain.

Hence:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SysRateBear ∧ SMSourceStructure ∧ H\_SD ⇒ SourceDrive

That is, this organization is itself driven by the source drive and bears the continuation of the source drive in every direction. The source drive is written as the driving to which the Intention organization itself is subject, not as an object outside the organization attached after the fact.

### 9.3 GateCorrespondence

By DirPersist, the directional distinction persists up to where it obtains different successor responsibilities; by SMGateBoundary, Gate and Pass belong to the Boundary-Gate relation in the Self-Manifestation-Field.

But SMGateBoundary says only that Gate and Pass belong to the Boundary-Gate relation; it does not say that the place where a direction first obtains successor responsibility is a Boundary-Gate. Moreover, DirPersist allows one direction to be borne jointly by several physical components, allows several directions to share a pathway and be distinguished by state, and also allows one direction to enter, at the same time, two cooperative gate roles that never produce a new independent choice. So two further premises are needed: the boundary at which a direction first obtains successor responsibility is a Boundary-Gate, and Boundary-Gates are partitioned by directional duty. The independent upstream structures do not give these two points. We register:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;H\_GP (Boundary-Gate partition bridge): in the Self-Manifestation-Field, the first formal control boundary at which a direction obtains successor responsibility is a Boundary-Gate, and Boundary-Gates are partitioned by directional duty; each Boundary-Gate duty class corresponds to one and only one directional duty class, and a Boundary-Gate role neither adds a direction nor merges existing directions.

Hence:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DirPersist ∧ SMGateBoundary ∧ H\_GP ⇒ GateCorrespondence

The correspondence holds at the level of duty classes: ∀ directional duty class \[v\_i\]\_D, ∃! Boundary-Gate duty class \[G\_i\]\_D; ∀ \[G\_i\]\_D, ∃! \[v\_i\]\_D. No single physical node is required.

### 9.4 TuneInterface

By GateCorrespondence, every directional duty class has its Boundary-Gate duty class. By SMTuningStructure, a legal structure of commanding tuning through the Desire-Gates exists in the Self-Manifestation-Field.

But the entry stipulates that V consists of successor classes, which guarantees only that each direction is not a label without successors, not that its successors must be a tuning interface; in theory Gate\_i → Effect\_i may occur, bypassing the tuning side and bearing the successor directly. To exclude it, a universal statement is needed: the legal successor duties of the current Desire-Gates of Intention all enter the tuning side. The independent upstream structures do not give this universal statement. We register:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;H\_TI (Desire-Gate–tuning completeness bridge): in the Self-Manifestation-Field, the legal successor duty of every formal Desire-Gate is borne by the tuning side; no formal Desire-Gate bypasses the tuning side to bear the complete successor duty of Intention directly.

Hence:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;GateCorrespondence ∧ SMTuningStructure ∧ H\_TI ⇒ TuneInterface

This bridge does not assert that Intention, in every possible case, has internal tuning; it shows only that in the Self-Manifestation-Field the legal successors behind the Desire-Gates fall on the tuning side.

### 9.5 Co-anchor binding

The four bridges above prove the four structures separately. If the four items were allowed to be taken from different things, evidence stitching would arise: organization A satisfies P\_I, organization B satisfies SourceDrive, direction family C satisfies GateCorrespondence, Desire-Gate chain D satisfies TuneInterface; each of the four items is true, yet together they do not constitute one and the same necessary core. The Probability Group-Form already has a discipline for problems of the same type: the dimensions of one and the same Probability must be bound together to the same Continuation-Position, generation domain and organization, and several duties must hold on the same witness.

So when the four items are conjoined, co-anchor binding must be satisfied:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;CoAnchor\_I(K\_I): K\_I = (χ\_UI, 𝒫\_I, C\_I, \[V\]\_D, \[𝒢\]\_D)

That is, the Probability, SysRateBear, DirPersist, directional duty classes, Boundary-Gate duty classes and tuning interfaces invoked by the four bridges are all anchored to the same task instance χ\_UI, the same formal directional organization 𝒫\_I and the same Continuation-Position C\_I, together with the same corresponding directional duty family \[V\]\_D and the same Boundary-Gate duty family \[𝒢\]\_D; the four items may not be spliced across organizations, Continuation-Positions, direction families or Desire-Gate chains.

Co-anchor binding is a well-formed binding discipline, not a fifth duty, and it adds no H. What it anchors are organizations, Continuation-Positions and duty families, all of which can be distinguished and counted; it does not count Intention itself.

### 9.6 Theorem

Theorem Five (Theorem of the Universal Representation of Intention). Let X ∈ Ω\_SM, and let SysDirClaim(X) pass the check. Then:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Probability ∧ IntentDirectionalRole ∧ H\_PL ⇒ P\_I  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;SysRateBear ∧ SMSourceStructure ∧ H\_SD ⇒ SourceDrive  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;DirPersist ∧ SMGateBoundary ∧ H\_GP ⇒ GateCorrespondence  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;GateCorrespondence ∧ SMTuningStructure ∧ H\_TI ⇒ TuneInterface

Taken together, under the conditions of Theorem Four, H2 ∧ H\_3^red, and under H\_PL ∧ H\_SD ∧ H\_GP ∧ H\_TI:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;∃K\_I \[ CoAnchor\_I(K\_I) ∧ P\_I(K\_I) ∧ SourceDrive(K\_I) ∧ GateCorrespondence(K\_I) ∧ TuneInterface(K\_I) \]

That is, the four items hold jointly on one and the same set of anchors. The four bridges each carry their own conditions and are not chained to one another; only TuneInterface has GateCorrespondence as an antecedent, and it therefore also depends on H\_GP.

9.7 Consistency back-test against The General Form of Intention

The corpus registered in The General Form of Intention is consistent with the four bridges: I7 (probability lies in the Intention-Domain, not behind the Desire-Gate; probability dimensions lead to the Desire-Gates in one-to-one correspondence) supports H\_PL and H\_GP; I6 and I8 (between the source drive and the Desire-Gate there is a path, there is a Law; Intention is a probability organization driven by source drive) support H\_SD; Q5 and I2 (the Intention-Domain connects experience, passing through the Tuning-Field, and commands tuning; once past the Desire-Gate, it commands tuning to break the holding of Law in the Experience-Field) support H\_TI; Q4 and Q6 are consistent with the structure of the Intention-Domain and of the core of the source drive. The statements registered in The Probability Group-Form, “Probability driven by source drive is born in the Intent-Field” and “Desire-Gates correspond to the direction dimensions of probability,” are also consistent with them.

Within the system of The General Form of Intention, these corpus items support the four bridges, but they cannot close them independently on this chapter's behalf; so the four bridges are registered in this chapter as H.

Corollary One. The four items of The General Form of Intention are not four arbitrarily chosen components. What is truly universal across realizations is system directional bearing and persistence of directional distinction; on entering the Sedimentation-Field, the former specializes to rate-bearing, and the directions are borne by Law and compose a Probability; on entering the Self-Manifestation-Field, constrained by the relation classification of Boundary-Gate and integrative relations and by the structure of the endogenous-source side, they settle into the positions of the four-item structure. This chapter breaks this settling down into four bridges, one by one: for each bridge, which part already has an independent cross-realization basis and which part still depends on premises specific to the Self-Manifestation-Field have been registered separately.

Corollary Two. The Intention-Domain, the Desire-Gates and the Tuning-Field are realization names of the Self-Manifestation-Field; other realizations may lack structures of the same names, but cannot lack system directional bearing and persistence of directional distinction.

## Section Ten　Layered Breach

Breaches are registered in three layers. The rupture of a bridge in a representation layer does not invalidate the theory-neutral layer.

### 10.1 Breaching the theory-neutral layer

K-Inv1: there exist X ∼\_task X′ such that the SysDirClaim at both ends passes the check, while SysBear or DirPersist fails at one end.  
K-Inv2: there exists a legal realization whose declaration passes the check yet which fails to satisfy one of SB1 to SB4.  
K-Inv3: there exists a legal realization whose declaration passes the check yet in which the directional difference has vanished completely before discharging successor responsibility.  
K-Inv4: SysBear and DirPersist can be derived from each other, so that the two can be merged.  
K-Inv5: the definition of the task-persistence relation in fact depends on the two duties, so that Theorem Three is circular.  
K-Inv6: some conclusion depends on an undeclared realization condition, such as material, number of nodes, neural realization, discrete time, numerical probability, a unique carrier, or a single gate with a single node.

10.2 Breaching only the Sedimentation-Field representation layer

K-Inv7: H2 or H\_3^red fails, so that Law-bearing of the formal directions cannot be obtained; or the ruling of W9 fails, that is, in the Sedimentation-Field SysBear need not specialize to rate-bearing. This invalidates only Theorem Four and the items of Theorem Five that depend on it; it does not breach Theorem Three.

10.3 Breaching only the Self-Manifestation-Field representation layer

K-Inv8: one of SMGateBoundary, SMTuningStructure and SMSourceStructure is shown not to accord with the upstream original text, or to be insufficient to bear the bridge for which it is used; one of H\_PL, H\_SD, H\_GP and H\_TI is shown not to hold; or there exists X ∈ Ω\_SM in which the corresponding duties and Probability all hold, while one of P\_I, SourceDrive, GateCorrespondence and TuneInterface fails. This breaches only the corresponding item of Theorem Five; it does not breach Theorem Three.

K-Inv9: cross-anchor evidence stitching. The four items can each be obtained in different directional organizations, different Continuation-Positions, different direction families or different Desire-Gate–tuning chains, yet there is no common set of anchors on which the four hold jointly; if N\_I is nevertheless judged to hold, Theorem Five is breached.

The following do not constitute a breach: two realizations using different materials; one being centralized and the other distributed; one direction being borne jointly by several nodes; several directions sharing one physical pathway while their duty states remain distinguishable; Desire-Gate duty having no unique node; the rate values of probability being unreadable; the current rate of some dimension being zero; there being no actual source-drive pulse at present; current Intention not having manifested or not having turned into behavior; an external stimulus having induced Intention; the existence, in the theory-neutral layer, of system-orientation realizations without endogenous launch or internal modulation; a legal re-realization spoiling the directional qualification so that the declaration no longer holds.

### 11.2 Strength

What this chapter proves are semantic-level duty invariants and representations; it provides no executable cross-realization decision algorithm. Layer One does not assert that every system has Law, Probability, an Intent-Field, Desire-Gates or a Tuning-Field; Layers Two and Three hold only within the working domains of the Sedimentation-Field and the Self-Manifestation-Field.

This chapter may not use the looser concepts of the theory-neutral layer to weaken The General Form of Intention in reverse: the neutral layer does not require historical recurrence, but in On Self-Manifestation a new-path candidate is not a formal direction dimension, and Intent ⇒ P\_I remains unchanged.

12.2 Institutional interface of the Canon Chapter

The current Canon Chapter stipulates fifteen auxiliary forms divided into five groups, and lists “whether the five groups exhaust all future dependent tasks” as Canon-4-H1, subject to continuous back-testing. It also lists “invariants” as a priority object of observation for candidate auxiliary forms, and stipulates that a new auxiliary form must pass all six points of the candidate test at once: it has an independent task to be proven, cannot be naturally absorbed by the existing form-kinds, has an independent derivation procedure, has independent completion conditions, has an independent breach method, and can be reused across several objects.

The task of this chapter is to compare which duties cannot be escaped across different task-persistent realizations; its derivation procedure is “entry freezing → comparison protocol → attribution check → holding separately at both ends → layered representation”; its breach method and completion conditions are given in Sections Ten and Eleven. It differs from the General Form (necessary constitution), the Mathematical Form (exact representation), the Necessity Form (sufficient direction), the Stability Form (persistence and return of an established qualification under permitted change) and the Construction Form (constructing what satisfies). As for reuse across objects, in The Probability Group-Form a change of realization is not a change of the formal probability organization, and in The Principle Group-Form one and the same duty can be borne through long mediating chains, through a change of carrier, or jointly in distributed fashion; both exhibit tasks of the same type, and these serve only as back-tests.

The invariant task and the six-point candidate test of this chapter have closed; this chapter therefore constitutes an empirical counterexample to Canon-4-H1, the “exhaustiveness of the five groups,” and triggers a subsequent upgrade of the Canon Chapter: the auxiliary forms rise from five groups to six, with a new sixth group, the “invariant group,” and from fifteen kinds to sixteen, with a new sixteenth auxiliary form, the “Invariant Form”; the principal forms remain five. Until the Canon Chapter completes its upgrade, the current text of the Canon Chapter still stands, as a matter of version fact, at fifteen auxiliary forms in five groups.
