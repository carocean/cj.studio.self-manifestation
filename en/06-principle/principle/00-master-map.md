# Master Diagram

## Section One　Purpose, Working Domain, and Declaration of Non-Addition

### 1.1　Nature of this text

This is the group-level master diagram drawn after the thirteen form-texts of the Principle Group-Form of the Principle Chapter were sealed. As a group form it is not a fourteenth form-text, takes no number in the Principle Group-Form sequence, and bears no proof obligation of any form-kind.

It adds no ontological condition to Principle, adds no ontological predicate, adds no H, and modifies no sealed definition, theorem, or status declaration in the thirteen. Wherever this text and the body of the thirteen diverge, the thirteen govern.

### 1.2　The single task

Without adding any ontological condition to Principle, to set out: the closure structure among the thirteen; their dependencies; the generative path of the objects; the four audit directions applied to Principle; the qualification gates at each critical transition; the downstream interfaces; and the boundary of group closure within the declared working domain.

Seven questions must be answered. What theoretical object have the thirteen jointly closed "Principle" into? Which dependencies among them are hard and logical, and which are merely semantic inheritance or runtime dependence? What exactly is the relation among Law, Principle, Act, and a new Law? Which relations are structural qualification, which are historical source, and which are no more than generation reachability? Why can Boundary, Stability, Grading, and Source not substitute for one another? What has the Principle Group-Form settled, and where do the later group-forms take over? Under what conditions may one declare the Principle Group-Form closed within the present working domain?

### 1.3　Upstream texts

The thirteen form-texts of the Principle Group-Form, all sealed at v1.0: The General Form of Principle; The Mathematical Form of Principle; The Necessity Form of Principle; The General Form of Principle-Action; The Mathematical Form of Principle-Action; The Necessity Form of Principle-Action; The Construction Form of the Principle Running Base; The Reachability Form of Principle Generation; The Determination Form of Principle Generation; The Boundary Form of Principle; The Stability Form of Principle; The Grading Form of Principle; The Source Form of Principle.

### 1.4　Working domain

The closure described here is confined to the single-Principle work domain: one target Law A, one candidate Principle P, and one relation domain J declared in advance are fixed, and this one Principle relation is examined. Competition, conjunction, cancellation, cascading, mutual qualification as Principle among several Principles, and contention over the same dynamical degree of freedom lie outside the closure of this text; see Section Ten.

## Section Two　The Four Zones of the Thirteen

The thirteen are not thirteen steps in a line. They form four functional zones.

2.1　Zone One　Closure of Principle qualification (texts 1, 2, 3)

The question: under what further structure does an already standing Law become a Principle relative to another Law?

The core requirement: P must first be a Law in full; and CounterHoldRole, by its upstream definition, must be borne by the same established duty d\\\* through which P acquired its Law qualification, and borne against the holding-tendency that another Law A is currently exercising. Hence it is not "P has a Law, and then some other duty acts against A" — that is duty substitution, which nowhere stands in the thirteen.

Note well: the same-duty requirement is written inside CounterHoldRole; it is not a second condition set beside it.

The General Form of Principle gives the necessary direction; The Mathematical Form gives the formal representation and read-back; The Necessity Form gives the sufficient direction.

2.2　Zone Two　Closure of Principle-action (texts 4, 5, 6)

The question: when is an already standing Principle actually acting?

The core object is CurAnti, with at least three layers: the same d\\\* actually occurs in the current actual Continuation; its current legal realization actually enters the holding-tendency that A is currently bearing; and on the dynamical degree of freedom A actually holds, the established counter-hold is discharged.

The General Form of Principle-Action gives the necessary direction; The Mathematical Form of Principle-Action gives the formal representation; The Necessity Form of Principle-Action gives the sufficient direction.

2.3　Zone Three　Closure of running and generation (texts 7, 8, 9)

The Construction Form of the Principle Running Base proves that Law, Principle, CounterHoldRole, Act, and CurAnti, as defined by the first six, can jointly obtain executable witnesses within one legal Sedimentation-Field running base. The Reachability Form of Principle Generation asks: from a declared initial state, using only legal field dynamics, does there exist a finite path along which the formal solution set of the candidate Law changes from empty to non-empty? The Determination Form of Principle Generation asks: once a new Law stands, can one determine whether it was in fact produced by the generative history of Principle-action?

2.4　Zone Four　The four audits of Principle (texts 10, 11, 12, 13)

The Boundary Form asks when Principle truly crosses between 0 and 1. The Stability Form asks how a standing Principle persists, lapses, is replaced, or returns along legal successor trajectories. The Grading Form asks how the counter-holding degree of two standing Principles compare. The Source Form asks how one first standing of Principle came about.

These four are not a miscellany of meta-properties. They are four distinct audits of one and the same object, each with its own entry; see Section Six.

## Section Three　Master Diagram of Text-Level Dependency

### 3.1　Three kinds of dependency

Dependency arrows come in three kinds, never mixed in this text:

　　H　hard logical dependency: the later text's proofs cite the earlier text's definitions or theorems directly; withdraw the earlier and the later's proof does not stand;  
　　S　semantic or protocol inheritance: the later text keeps the earlier's terms, protocols, or disciplines, but its principal theorem does not take them as antecedents;  
　　R　runtime-realization dependency: the later text's legality judgements must land on the running base or history-legality rules constructed by the earlier.

The three are not interchangeable. Note especially: a dependency arrow is not an arrow of logical entailment, still less an arrow of temporal generation. "Text 11 depends on text 10" in no way means that Boundary generates Stability.

### 3.3　How to read it

Within Zone One: 1 → 2 → 3 are hard dependencies. The formal representation of 2 answers to the object-level requirements of 1, and the sufficient direction of 3 presupposes the structures of 1 and 2.

Zone Two: the structures of 4 through 6 depend hard on 1 through 3 — what Principle-action acts out is precisely the qualification defined in Zone One. Text 5 on text 4, and text 6 on texts 4 and 5, are likewise hard.

It inherits the objects, qualifications, and action conditions already frozen by the first six, and constructs the running base on which those conditions can jointly obtain executable witnesses within one legal Sedimentation-Field. It does not presuppose a running base constructed by the first six — the first six construct no running base; text 7 is itself the bearer of that construction. Hence the dependency here is H, not R.

Text 8 depends on text 7 for runtime realization. Text 9 stands to text 8 in semantic inheritance: it keeps the same running and history protocols, but its criterion and text 8's reachability theorem are independent of each other. Text 8 adjudicates whether one can get there; text 9 adjudicates, once one has got there, whether that is in fact how it came about. Text 9 also depends on text 7 for runtime realization.

Text 12 is a side branch; it depends hard on 1 through 3 and stands in no entailment relation to 10 or 11. Text 13 is the crossing node: hard on 1 through 3 (its structural chain cites the sufficient direction of The Necessity Form); hard on 10 (its entry condition cites the boundary at which Principle first goes from 0 to 1); runtime-dependent on 7 (its permitted dynamics and in-field role formation rest on the running base); and semantically inheriting from 12 — the disciplines of the grading protocol are kept, but grading is in no sense an antecedent of the LawBorn principal theorem.

Texts 10, 11, 12, 13 must not be drawn as 10 → 11 → 12 → 13.

T3　Current action.

　　Principle ∧ CurAnti ⇒ Act.

This is the closure of Zone Two. The converse does not hold:

　　Principle ⇏ Act.

A Principle may stand while currently at rest: the bearing Law is not triggered; the target has no inflow on this beat; the degree of freedom the target holds is not in bearing; this instance of Read is not satisfied. Each is a legal case in which Principle still stands and Act does not.

Neither stage may be compressed into "Act generates a new Law." And

　　Act ⇏ NewLaw.

Reachability is the existence of one path. It is not that every act generates a new Law, and still less that every legal path is in fact taken.

The third relation deserves particular note: Genesis is the entry condition of text 13, not its conclusion. That a Principle has first come to stand says only that the study of its source may begin; it says nothing about what that source is.

### 5.4　The seven conditions of LawBorn

G1 prior Law qualification; G2 the same law-conferring duty; G3 the duty's identity carried through; G4 at the genesis end, Law still borne by the same duty; G5 CounterHoldRole goes from 0 to 1; G6 the role-formation history h\_{d\\\*} of that same duty belongs to some certified minimal sufficient source set; G7 not a direct qualification write, but formed by permitted Sedimentation-Field or running-base dynamics.

The division most easily blurred is that between G5 and G6. G5 adjudicates only that the relational qualification is newly arisen; it does not adjudicate which side brought the change about. Source attribution is carried entirely by G6. Hence a pure A-side change may perfectly well satisfy G5 and still fail to be LawBorn, because G6 fails.

5.5　Three disciplines of the source protocol

The candidate family of history-units must be frozen in advance and finite, \|ℋ^Π\| \< ∞. Finiteness guarantees that a non-empty sufficient set must contain a minimal sufficient subset, and so prevents "infinite descent with no minimal source" from being misread as no source at all.

The empty set is background control, not a source candidate: the domains of Replayable and Suff include the empty set, but the empty set must not enter 𝒮\_src^Π. If Suff\_Π(∅) = 1, the declared candidate domain has failed to capture the real bearing, and then

　　Source^Π = ⊥,

not 0.

That a replay is not legally executable does not mean the history is not a source: Replayable = 0 says only that the present protocol cannot answer that counterfactual.

The three disciplines share one root. Unreplayability says the protocol's power to delete is limited; background sufficiency says its power to partition is limited; a certificate not found says exhaustion is not yet complete. None of the three is evidence that the source is zero; all three fall to ⊥.

5.6　Internal classifications that do not enter this diagram

PureLawBorn, CoLawBorn, ASource, and DirectWriteSource, internal to The Source Form, are incidental classifications of source attribution. They constitute no grading, are not group-level structure, and do not enter the principal diagrams of this master diagram.

## Section Six　The Four Audits of Principle

### 6.1　Four different entries

All four audit Principle, but their entries differ and must not be drawn as four symmetric arrows. The difference of entry is not merely formal: it directly determines the dependency spoken of in 6.3 — the entry to Source is itself a Boundary event.

　　Boundary (text 10): the object is the 0 ↔ 1 crossing of Principle qualification itself;  
　　Stability (text 11): what enters is an already standing Principle, with the same identity, relation domain, and stability protocol fixed; legal successor trajectories are then examined;  
　　Grading (text 12): the entry condition is expressly Principle = 1, after which the counter-holding degree is compared as a partial order on the two dimensions D and R;  
　　Source (text 13): what enters is not a Principle = 1 in general, but the first-genesis event Genesis\_τ — that one crossing 0 → 1.

6.2　What each does that no other can do in its place

Boundary adjudicates entry into and exit from qualification at the object layer. One of its key results: the exit of one realization, one certificate, or one duty does not automatically amount to the exit of Principle. Only when that exit extinguishes the last complete qualification certificate, or together with other exits causes CounterHoldRole to fail, does it rise to boundary responsibility.

Stability adjudicates persistence, recovery, replacement, and return under permitted change and a trajectory protocol. It divides the identity being preserved into four layers — the same certificate, the same duty, the same Principle, some Principle still present in the candidate family — and what is preserved differs by layer.

Grading adjudicates the counter-holding degree relation between two standing Principles, as a multi-dimensional partial order, erecting no total score and no total order.

Source adjudicates the source attribution of one first genesis.

6.3　The four audits cannot substitute for one another

Boundary, Stability, Grading, and Source each carry a different question, and the result of an audit along any one axis cannot substitute for the adjudication that another axis is there to carry.

"Cannot substitute" here does not mean that no dependency exists among the four. In particular, the entry condition of Source is Genesis\_τ itself, the boundary event at which Principle first crosses from 0 to 1. Source therefore takes one Boundary event as its entry premise. But knowing what the source is does not determine when that Principle will later exit, whether it will return, or what its subsequent boundary trajectory will be.

At least the following must not be traded for one another:

　　Boundary cannot stand in for Stability — not having crossed at present is not stability under permitted perturbation; and one boundary crossing does not by itself settle recovery and persistence under a stability protocol.  
　　Stability cannot stand in for Grading — a Principle may be strong and brittle, or weak and stable; counter-holding degree and the power to persist are different questions.  
　　Grading cannot stand in for Source — two Principles may carry identical counter-holding degree profiles and differ in source attribution.  
　　Boundary cannot stand in for Source — two Principles may have identical 0 → 1 boundary trajectories, one generated as LawBorn and the other from some other source.  
　　Source cannot stand in for later Boundary adjudication — the same source type may correspond to wholly different later exits, returns, and boundary trajectories.

Entry-condition or semantic dependencies may therefore exist among the four audits, but none may substitute for another's adjudicative office.

## Section Eight　Group-Level Principles of Non-Conflation

The following six are the group-level disciplines of the Principle Group-Form, binding on anyone who cites the results of the thirteen.

GR-1　Qualification is separate from action. Principle ⇏ Act. A qualification may lie at rest.

GR-2　Reachability is separate from actual generation. Reachable ⇏ ActuallyGenerated. That a legal path exists is not that the path was taken.

GR-3　Qualification is separate from source. Principle ⇏ LawBornSource. That a Principle stands is not that it was born of Law.

GR-4　Realization is separate from object identity. CertificateLoss ⇏ PrincipleLoss. The exit of one certificate, one carrier solution, one Bind family, one Anti degree of freedom, or even one duty does not automatically amount to the exit of qualification.

GR-5　Degree is separate from qualification. Counter-holding degree must not be used to define Principle qualification in reverse. The weakest legal Principle is still a Principle; a low grade is not a deficient qualification.

GR-6　The four audits cannot substitute for one another. Boundary, Stability, Grading, and Source each have an independent task; entry-condition or semantic dependencies may exist among them — Source, for instance, takes a Genesis boundary event as its entry condition — but the result of an audit along any one axis must not substitute for the adjudication another axis is to carry.

These six, together with the dependency disciplines of Section Three, constitute the basic constraint of this master diagram: one arrow carries one meaning only, and the four kinds of relation — structural qualification, current action, generation reachability, historical attribution — each have their own bridge. No terminal fact may cross over the bridge that is supposed to bear it.

## Section Nine　Downstream Interfaces

This section delivers interfaces only. It does not settle in advance what the downstream group-forms are to develop.

### 9.1　To the Law-Change Group-Form

The Principle Group-Form has settled three things, and the strength of each must be delivered with it; the conclusion alone must not be handed over. First, Principle can actually enter field dynamics — given by text 7 within the construction and model it declares. Second, from a declared initial state there exists a finite legal path along which the candidate Law's formal solution set turns from empty to non-empty, and that path may carry a counter-holding history possessing source qualification — given by text 8, whose reading from the formal solution up to the Law layer requires conditional read-back. Third, for a new Law that already stands, one can determine whether it was generated by Principle-action — given by text 9, whose adjudication domain is the narrowing declared there.

The Law-Change Group-Form therefore need not reopen the question whether Principle can generate a new Law; but in citing these three it must carry their antecedents and adjudication domains along.

What it should take up is the question of identity: how Law identity is preserved across change; what counts as a change of the same Law; what counts as a Law's branching, merging, or replacement; what counts as the old Law still standing while another Law is added.

The deliverable is a certified generative history of a new Law; the point of takeover is Law identity across change.

### 9.2　To the Probability Group-Form

One safe declaration only: the 1, 0, and ⊥ of the Principle Group-Form are not the same kind of thing as a probability value. Note especially

　　⊥ ≠ some intermediate probability.

⊥ marks insufficiency of adjudicating conditions. It is not a quantity lying between 0 and 1.

Deliverable: legal states, legal trajectories, legal source candidates, and frozen qualification criteria. This text does not prescribe the ontology of the Probability Group-Form.

### 9.3　To the Intentionality Group-Form

Deliverable: the directed relation; CounterHoldRole; the current capacity to discharge, Act; and the capacity to alter successor dynamical paths.

One must not write Principle = intentionality, nor Act = intention. Claims of the form "intentionality just is the directed generation of future states" are not proven results in the Principle Group-Form. Whether and how the structures above further constitute intentionality is for the later Intentionality Group-Form to adjudicate on its own.

## Section Ten　Group Closure and the Admission of a New Form-Text

### 10.1　What closure means

"Closure" does not mean that no question about Principle remains. The closure spoken of here is confined to this: the Principle Group-Form is closed within the declared single-Principle work domain.

### 10.2　Criteria of group closure

　　C1　With each text's frozen working domain, inherited conditions, and original Status unchanged, the necessary task and the sufficient task of Principle qualification are both complete: the sufficient direction keeps Status: Established, while the necessary direction and the two-way closure keep the Status: Conditional declared upstream;  
　　C2　Under the same proviso, Principle and Act are separated, and both the necessary and the sufficient direction of Act are independently borne; the group-level closure alters the Status of texts 4 through 6 in no way, and in particular must not thereby raise a conditional result at the formal layer to an unconditional one;  
　　C3　The formal structures have a unified legal running witness: its domain of standing is confined to the construction, model, seed set, and parameter neighbourhood declared in text 7, and what is signed is existence and constructive capacity;  
　　C4　Both reachability and actual attribution of new-Law generation are independently borne: reachability by text 8 over the initial state, permitted inputs, and path it declares; attribution by text 9 within its narrowed adjudication domain; and the two must not substitute for each other;  
　　C5　The four audits Boundary, Stability, Grading, and Source are each independently established; entry-condition or semantic dependencies may hold among them, but none may stand in for another;  
　　C6　The relations that must not be conflated across layers have been explicitly declared;  
　　C7　The remaining questions have explicit downstream interfaces and no longer constitute a gap in the single-Principle theory.

With all seven in hand, group closure may be declared. It must not on that basis be written as "the Principle Group-Form has exhausted every question about Principle."

And one overriding discipline:

　　Group closure does not imply a Status upgrade.

That a task is borne within the group, and whether its result is Established or Conditional, are two different matters. Anyone citing the results of the thirteen must carry along the Status and antecedents as originally declared; this master diagram raises the strength of no result.

The source of the Conditional must be recorded along with it. It is not caution in wording, but load-bearing antecedents still in transmission from upstream — including the formal bridges listed in The Mathematical Form of Principle, and antecedents transmitted from the Law Group-Form and not yet closed on the Law side. Those antecedents are closed under their original attribution, are not borne by the Principle Group-Form, and are not closed automatically by the group closure of the Principle Group-Form. Hence "the Principle Group-Form is closed" and "all its results hold unconditionally" are two different statements.

10.3　The boundary of the many-body question

Competition, conjunction, cancellation, cascading, mutual qualification as Principle among several Principles, and contention over the same dynamical degree of freedom belong to a future many-body extension. They do not at present block the sealing of the thirteen within the single-Principle work domain; and should a many-body extension later be opened, that is no ground for saying the present single-Principle theory is not closed — unless it is later proved that many-body relations alter the ontological qualification of a single Principle in reverse. Should such a proof appear, the matter returns to the corresponding form-text among the thirteen for review; it is not adjudicated by this master diagram.

### 10.4　Admission of a new form-text

After the sealing of the thirteen, any candidate addition to the Principle Group-Form must satisfy all four of the following:

　　first, the question cannot be reduced to the qualification, action, running, generation, boundary, stability, grading, or source of the existing thirteen;  
　　second, it does not belong to law-change, probability, intentionality, engineering realization, or the many-body extension;  
　　third, a failing case can be constructed — all thirteen stand, and the irreducible question still cannot be answered;  
　　fourth, in the absence of that form-text a gap would remain in the theoretical closure of Principle itself.

Lacking any one of the four, no form-text may be added.

One procedural requirement besides: in judging whether a question "can be reduced," one must compare it against the single task of each of the thirteen, one by one, and not go by similarity of name. A different name with the same task is still reducible; the same name with a different task may well be a genuinely new form.

## Section Eleven　Conclusion of the Master Diagram

The Law Group-Form established how a structure of Continuation acquires holding-tendency within a Sedimentation-Field. On that basis the Principle Group-Form establishes a further layer: an already standing Law, through the same duty by which it qualified as a Law, can acquire counter-holding qualification against the holding-tendency another Law is currently exercising; this qualification may lie at rest, or may enter current actual action; its action can land in legal Sedimentation-Field dynamics; and from a declared initial state there exists a finite legal path along which the candidate Law's formal solution set turns from empty to non-empty. Reading from that formal solution up to the Law layer requires a separate conditional read-back.

The Principle Group-Form at the same time puts each of the following in its own place: current qualification, the crossing of qualification, current action, generation reachability, attribution of actual generation, stability, degree, and source. The result of that separation is this: no terminal fact may cross over the qualification bridge or the historical bridge that is supposed to bear it.

Compressed further into one sentence: Principle is not a second kind of basic object beside Law, but a counter-holding structure — a structure of hold-breaking — arising inside the system of Law; it carries the Sedimentation-Field from "able to form and maintain structures of Continuation" to "able, with structures already formed, to intervene internally upon a holding-tendency already in force, and thereby alter what is generated next."

Of the saying "Law gives birth to Principle, Principle gives birth to new Law," the strict version is this. Law is the structural basis of Principle. For some Principles, the genesis can be certified thus: the very duty by which P qualified as a Law acquired, within a legal Sedimentation-Field history, a counter-holding role it did not have before. A Principle already standing may, when the action conditions are met, enter actual counter-holding. And within legal dynamical history there exist reachable paths to the generation of a new Law. Whether any given new Law was in fact generated by Principle-action still requires an independent generative-history adjudication.

Of "Principle breaks Law," in the strict sense the Principle Group-Form now carries, only this may be said: Principle acts against the holding-tendency of another Law — it breaks the hold; it does not annihilate the Law. One must not write Principle ⇒ ¬Law(A), nor that Act necessarily makes A exit its Law qualification. Counter-holding is not winning against A, is not the cancellation of A's Law qualification, is not an alteration of the final outcome, and still less is it the necessary generation of a new Law.

Two final declarations. First, the Principle Group-Form is closed within the declared single-Principle work domain. Second, many-Principle coupling, law-change, probability, intentionality, inversion from finite observation, and concrete engineering realization are none of them handled here: this master diagram does not reach beyond its bounds to take them up.
