# The Boundary Form of Principle

## Section One　Basic Settings

### 1.1　The single irreducible task

With one candidate Principle P, the target Law A, the Law application domains I\_P and I\_A of both, the relation domain J declared in advance, and the Law identities of P and A all fixed, one question only is answered:

　　when does Principle\_F,t(P; A, J) truly cross from standing to not standing, or from not standing back to standing?

And this in particular must be answered: when is the lapse of one concrete counter-holding realization, one carrier solution, one Bind family, one Anti degree of freedom, one existing duty, or one instance of acting merely the exit of some certificate, and when does it suffice to make Principle itself exit?

Not studied: how a Principle is generated; the persistence and termination of acting; stability width, duration of retention, recovery, hysteresis, re-entry trajectories, and probability; the grading of Principle.

### 1.2　Form-kind and dependence

The form-kind is a Limit Form, an auxiliary form of boundary discrimination. After a principal form has given a structure, it discriminates the boundary at which that structure stands.

It depends on the following sealed form-texts: The General Form of Principle v1.0, The Mathematical Form of Principle v1.0, The Necessity Form of Principle v1.0; and it inherits the boundary disciplines already matured in The Boundary Form of Law — the comparison regulation is frozen before the comparison, a boundary must be actually attained, supremum and infimum are kept apart from the point attained, and the endpoints are asymmetric. It modifies no upstream definition, adds no ontological predicate, and adds no H.

Should a principal form be broken through or withdrawn, the conclusions of this form-text lapse or become pending.

### 1.3　Limit direction and limit condition

Limit direction: before each boundary comparison, Γ declares in advance one directed comparison path together with its rules of legal change. An exit instance undergoes, along that path, the crossing of Principle from 1 to 0; an entry instance undergoes the crossing from 0 to 1. The direction of comparison is not time, and does not require every underlying supporting component to be monotone in the same direction item by item — the boundary of a Principle may precisely present the case in which one duty exits while another enters, and the joint covering relation crosses in the end. Where decreasing or increasing support is adopted as a particular comparison order, Γ must define that order in advance.

Limit condition: walking along the declared direction to some place, such that, with A still a Law, the legal ways of maintaining Principle qualification pass from present to absent (exit), or from absent to present (entry).

What is to be discriminated is the position of that place, its structural form, and the degenerate result of crossing it. A Limit Form answers only where something lapses or where it is attained; it does not bear retention, replacement, or return.

1.4　Objects of comparison and the comparison regulation Γ

Before a boundary comparison begins, the following are frozen once:

　　P; A; I\_P; I\_A; J; the Law identities of P and A; the identity content within Rel(P) and Rel(A) used to recognize the same object of comparison; which field state quantities are permitted to vary; the direction of comparison; the resolution of comparison; the rule of carrier correspondence.

Where something stands again after P is changed, A is changed, J is narrowed, or the Rel content used to recognize identity is rewritten, that is not called the original Principle recovering or continuing; it is a different object of comparison.

J must not, after a failure has appeared, be narrowed to

　　J⁻ = { j ∈ J \| RelPass(j) = 1 }

and the original Principle then be declared still to stand.

1.5　Two different boundaries must first be kept apart

First, the boundary of Principle qualification. Only where both ends of the comparison still satisfy the Principle working domain is

　　Cross^Q\_Γ(X⁻, X⁺) ⇔ Dom\_Principle(X⁻) ∧ Dom\_Principle(X⁺) ∧ Principle(X⁻) ≠ Principle(X⁺)

defined, where the working domain is defined once and closed as the conjunction of the upstream common working-domain conditions:

　　Dom\_Principle(X) ⇔ Law\_X(A, I\_A) = 1 ∧ P ≠ A ∧ I\_P ≠ ∅ ∧ J ≠ ∅.

Nothing is added beyond these four, and no opening is left to be filled later. With adjacency

　　Adjacent\_Γ(X⁻, X⁺)

added, it is called a located boundary of qualification:

　　Boundary^Q\_Γ ⇔ Adjacent\_Γ ∧ Cross^Q\_Γ.

By direction of crossing it divides again into two, and the two must not be written as one:

　　Boundary⁻\_Γ ⇔ Adjacent\_Γ ∧ Dom\_Principle(X⁻) ∧ Dom\_Principle(X⁺) ∧ Principle(X⁻) = 1 ∧ Principle(X⁺) = 0 (exit);  
　　Boundary⁺\_Γ ⇔ Adjacent\_Γ ∧ Dom\_Principle(X⁻) ∧ Dom\_Principle(X⁺) ∧ Principle(X⁻) = 0 ∧ Principle(X⁺) = 1 (entry).

These two formulas agree exactly with those used in Section Seven; the two places must not differ.

Second, exit from the domain of definition on the target side. Where Law(A) goes from 1 to 0, this must not be written directly as an ordinary Principle = 0; it is recorded as OUT\_A. This is the target relational object's exit from Principle's working domain. The Necessity Form of Principle has already listed "A subsequently losing its Law" as the temporal boundary of the Principle relation, and not as an independent Disqualification while every current qualification is retained.

This form-text therefore uses four marks:

　　1: Principle stands within the working domain;  
　　0: Principle determinately does not stand within the working domain;  
　　OUT\_A: A has exited the target Law working domain;  
　　⊥: formal input, evidence, or evaluation is insufficient.

These four belong to three layers: 1 and 0 are the ontological truth values of Principle within the working domain; OUT\_A is a label of exit from the domain of definition; ⊥ is a label of undecidedness. Neither OUT\_A nor ⊥ is a third ontological truth value of Principle.

### 1.6　Core closure at the structural layer

While A still belongs to the working domain, the following are strictly inherited:

　　necessary direction: Principle(P; A, J) ⇒ Law(P, I\_P) ∧ CounterHoldRole(P; A, J), Status = Conditional;  
　　sufficient direction: Law(P, I\_P) ∧ CounterHoldRole(P; A, J) ⇒ Principle(P; A, J), Status = Established;  
　　two-way closure: Conditional.

The necessary direction and the two-way closure must not be upgraded merely because the sufficient direction is Established.

## Section Two　The Argument from Unavoidability

2.1　Why an exit can be borne only by the two top-level structures

Within the frozen semantic domain M1 through M12, with A still a Law, the identities of P, A, and J unchanged, and the antecedents of the upstream necessary direction holding, an exit of Principle's internal qualification can be borne only by one of the following:

　　B\_P⁻: Law(P, I\_P) goes from 1 to 0;  
　　B\_CH⁻: CounterHoldRole(P; A, J) goes from 1 to 0.

The entry direction corresponds to two items, and both are permitted to change at once:

　　B\_P⁺: Law(P, I\_P) goes from 0 to 1;  
　　B\_CH⁺: CounterHoldRole(P; A, J) goes from 0 to 1.

The obligations of the two directions must be kept distinct, and the condition of certification must not be conflated with the exhaustion of responsibility.

　　Certification: for a candidate exit pair whose left end has been certified Principle = 1, it suffices for the negative end that at least one of Law(P) and CounterHoldRole determinately fail at the right end, and the pair is then a Boundary⁻. For a candidate entry pair whose left end has been certified Principle = 0, both items must hold at the right end together before the positive end is certified by the sufficient direction.  
　　Responsibility: for an exit that has already occurred, its top-level responsibility must fall to at least one of B\_P⁻ and B\_CH⁻; that is an exhaustion, not a definition.

The asymmetry follows: certifying an exit requires only one item to fail at the right end, while certifying an entry requires both items complete at the right end.

There is no third independent Disqualification X such that

　　Law(P) = 1 ∧ CounterHoldRole = 1 ∧ X = 1 ∧ Principle = 0.

The reason is inherited directly from three results already proved in The Necessity Form of Principle: first, within the frozen canonical domain, the current qualification for Principle is exhausted as Q\_a through Q\_d; second, the independent Disqualifications are empty; third, the principal sufficiency theorem gives Law(P) ∧ CounterHoldRole ⇒ Principle. Taken together: where the two top-level structures are both retained, Principle cannot be 0, so any legal change that makes Principle exit must fall upon one of those two.

That exhaustion is an exhaustion within the currently frozen canonical domain. Should the canonical source or the upstream theory later add a legal independent Disqualification, this form-text must be re-examined in step; it must not be written as an absolute exhaustion across every future theory.

2.2　Why a single-point lapse unavoidably does not suffice

One side of Principle's counter-holding relation is borne by CounterHoldRole (the other being Law(P); see 2.1), and CounterHoldRole is characterized at the formal layer by "there exists one duty covering the whole of J." An existential quantifier means this: after any one concrete witness exits — a carrier solution, a Bind family, an Anti degree of freedom, a complete certificate, or even a duty — the quantifier is still satisfied so long as another legal witness takes its place. A single-point lapse therefore does not suffice to constitute a boundary; that is not caution, but a consequence of the quantifier structure itself.

2.3　Why a lapse of acting unavoidably does not belong to the boundary of qualification

The fourth through sixth upstream texts have strictly distinguished "qualification as a Principle" from "currently acting." Principle = 1 with Act = 0 is perfectly legal. Hence there being no input at present, the target not being in bearing on this beat, the duty not having actually discharged on this beat, and the current acting stopping — none of these can be a condition of Principle qualification exiting.

2.4　Why the formal layer unavoidably cannot be raised directly to the ontological layer

Adjudication at the formal layer rests upon the representation conditions of The Mathematical Form of Principle and the Law-side antecedents it invokes. That the formal certificate set is empty says that no usable certificate exists within that formal representation domain; to read it directly as an ontological Principle = 0 is to take the representation conditions as identically true by default. When a conclusion of the formal layer is raised to the ontological layer, therefore, M1 through M8 and the Law-side representation antecedents invoked must be inherited along with it.

## Section Three　Core Definitions

The four items defined in this section are all pure formal auxiliary objects. They are not ontological objects and bear no defining office for Principle; they serve only to characterize, within the formally adjudicable domain, the survival or extinction of counter-holding qualification.

### 3.1　The formal certificate set

Define the pure formal auxiliary object

　　𝒲^M\_CH(X): the set of formal counter-holding relation certificates on state X.

The name points to CounterHoldRole^M alone and not to Principle: what it characterizes is formal counter-holding qualification, not the ontological qualification of Principle. Principle's ontological qualification is still borne by the two items Law(P) and CounterHoldRole, and reading a formal object back to the ontological must pass through M1 through M8 of The Mathematical Form of Principle and its inherited antecedents. A certificate C consists of the following items; this enumeration is a closed definition and leaves nothing to be filled in later:

　　the fixed duty d;  
　　for each j ∈ J: a fixed legal carrier solution R\_j ∈ 𝒮(F, P, I\_P); the Bind family ℱ\_{B,j} corresponding to that R\_j; the joint evaluation specification of that j; the Type-sensitive Sat and RelPass certificates within that j;  
　　the type of what the duty restricts;  
　　the RoleNonDegenerate certificate;  
　　A's held degrees of freedom and its holding-direction;  
　　the Anti certificate;  
　　the covering result over the whole of J.

Every item is taken from the certificate components already present in The Mathematical Form of Principle; this form-text neither adds nor removes any. Should upstream be upgraded and the components change, this form-text is re-examined accordingly.

By the finally sealed Mathematical Form of Principle, the quantifier hierarchy is: what is fixed across the whole of J is one and the same duty d; different j may use their own different legal R\_j; different j may use the ℱ\_{B,j} corresponding to their own R\_j; but within one and the same j, R\_j or the Bind family must not be changed between evaluations in order to splice qualification together.

It must not be written as "one and the same Bind family must cover the whole of J."

### 3.2　The duty covering sets

Define the pure formal auxiliary set

　　𝒟\_X(j) = { d ∈ Duties(Rel(P)) \| there exist a legal R\_j and its corresponding ℱ\_{B,j} such that RelPass\_d(P; A, j) = 1 }.

Here RelPass\_d(P; A, j) = 1 means that the complete RelPass determinately holds — that there exist a legal carrier solution and its corresponding Bind family satisfying every Type-sensitive Sat requirement within that j — and not a bare Boolean label.

Note that 𝒟\_X(j) admits only duties whose RelPass is determinately 1: those with RelPass 0 and those with ⊥ are alike excluded. From 𝒟\_X(j) = ∅ one therefore cannot read directly that "every duty determinately fails on that context"; the two are synonymous only where Dec\_CH(X) = 1.

3.3　The antecedent of overall adjudicability

Define the pure formal auxiliary predicate

　　Dec\_CH(X) = 1,

meaning that on state X, for every j ∈ J and every d ∈ Duties(Rel(P)), RelPass\_d(j) has fallen to 0 or to 1, with none at ⊥. Every 𝒟\_X(j) is then a determinate set, and its being empty is equivalent to every duty determinately failing on that context; the overall CounterHoldRole^M likewise takes only 0 or 1.

This requirement is universal item by item, and is not a matter of "taking as many as will serve": one undecided RelPass\_d(j) may be precisely the one that would have made the intersection non-empty, and so Dec\_CH(X) = 0.

Dec\_CH is not an ontological predicate but an accounting of adjudicability under the three-valued regime. Whoever infers "CounterHoldRole^M = 0" from "the certificate set is empty" must first have Dec\_CH(X) = 1; otherwise all that follows is "CounterHoldRole^M ∈ {0, ⊥}."

3.4　The accounting correspondence under a comparison regulation

For a comparison regulation Γ, define the pure accounting correspondence τ\_Γ, used to track whether the several components of a left-end certificate still have legal correspondents at the right end. τ\_Γ is auxiliary accounting only; it adds no ontology and presupposes no structural retention whatever.

It appears only in the statement of Theorem 4.5, where it serves to tell apart "the old certificate exits" from "the certificate set goes extinct." None of the principal results of this form-text takes τ\_Γ as an antecedent, and deleting it affects the standing of no theorem.

## Section Four　Structural Characterization

4.1　The formal certificate set and formal counter-holding qualification

Theorem 4.1　Formal certificate characterization theorem

　　𝒲^M\_CH(X) ≠ ∅ ⇔ CounterHoldRole^M\_X(P; A, J) = 1.

Proof. By The Mathematical Form of Principle, CounterHoldRole^M = 1 if and only if there exists a fixed duty d such that for every j ∈ J there exist a legal R\_j and its corresponding ℱ\_{B,j} making RelPass\_d(j) = 1. Registering the components of that existential witness item by item as in 3.1 gives a complete certificate, so the left side is non-empty. Conversely, a complete certificate gives by definition just such a d together with the R\_j, ℱ\_{B,j}, and RelPass certificates of each j, so CounterHoldRole^M = 1. □

What this theorem signs is an equivalence of the positive-valued state; it does not sign the complementary equivalence of the negated state: one may not simply negate it to obtain 𝒲^M\_CH = ∅ ⇔ CounterHoldRole^M = 0. The reason is that The Mathematical Form of Principle permits RelPass and the overall CounterHoldRole^M to take ⊥, while an empty certificate set says only that no positive certificate has been obtained, which is compatible with CounterHoldRole^M = 0 and with CounterHoldRole^M = ⊥ alike. An antecedent of adjudicability must therefore be added; see 3.3 and Theorem 4.2′.

This theorem is Established at the formal layer. In reading it back to the ontological CounterHoldRole, M1 through M8 of The Mathematical Form of Principle and the Law-side representation antecedents it invokes must be retained; the formal layer must not be raised unconditionally to the ontological.

4.2　The intersection representation of the covering sets

Theorem 4.2　Intersection representation theorem

　　CounterHoldRole^M\_X(P; A, J) = 1 ⇔ ⋂\_{j ∈ J} 𝒟\_X(j) ≠ ∅.

Proof. This is a set-theoretic rewriting of the existing definition ∃d ∀j RelPass\_d(j) = 1: d belongs to every 𝒟\_X(j) if and only if on every j it has a legal R\_j and ℱ\_{B,j} making RelPass hold. □

As with Theorem 4.1, what this theorem signs is an equivalence of the positive-valued state: one may not negate it to obtain "the intersection is empty ⇔ CounterHoldRole^M = 0." By 3.2, 𝒟\_X(j) admits only duties whose RelPass is determinately 1, so an empty intersection is likewise compatible with CounterHoldRole^M = 0 and with ⊥.

Theorem 4.2′　Extinction equivalence within the adjudicable domain

If Dec\_CH(X) = 1, then

　　𝒲^M\_CH(X) = ∅ ⇔ ⋂\_{j ∈ J} 𝒟\_X(j) = ∅ ⇔ CounterHoldRole^M\_X = 0.

Proof. Where Dec\_CH = 1, every RelPass\_d(j) takes 0 or 1, so CounterHoldRole^M likewise takes only 0 or 1. By Theorems 4.1 and 4.2, non-emptiness is then equivalent to taking 1, and so emptiness is equivalent to taking 0. □

If Dec\_CH(X) ≠ 1, then from 𝒲^M\_CH(X) = ∅ one obtains only CounterHoldRole^M\_X ∈ {0, ⊥}, and must not write = 0.

The order of quantifiers must be stressed: ∀j ∃d\_j cannot take the place of ∃d ∀j. The former permits different contexts to use different duties; the latter requires one and the same duty to cover the whole domain.

4.3　The two and only two formal types of the extinction of relational qualification

Theorem 4.3　Dichotomy theorem

Where Dec\_CH(X) = 1, that is, where every 𝒟\_X(j) is adjudicable,

　　⋂\_{j ∈ J} 𝒟\_X(j) = ∅

if and only if one of the following holds:

　　local extinction: ∃ j ∈ J with 𝒟\_X(j) = ∅;  
　　global consistency break: ∀ j ∈ J, 𝒟\_X(j) ≠ ∅, while ⋂\_{j ∈ J} 𝒟\_X(j) = ∅.

The two are mutually exclusive and exhaustive.

Proof. Suppose the intersection is empty. If some 𝒟\_X(j) is empty, that is the first type. If every 𝒟\_X(j) is non-empty while the intersection is still empty, that is the second. The two are divided by whether an empty local set exists, and so are mutually exclusive; and together they exhaust every case in which the intersection is empty. Conversely, the first type plainly makes the intersection empty, and the second gives an empty intersection directly by its definition. □

The first type says: on at least one relational context, no duty has RelPass taking 1. The second says: on every context some duty has RelPass taking 1, but there is no one duty taking 1 on all contexts — that is, counter-holding is present everywhere locally while there is still no covering overall. Both are stated under Dec\_CH = 1, so "not taking 1" and "taking 0" are synonymous there and contain no undecidedness.

4.4　The minimal formal pattern of a global consistency break

Take J = { j₁, j₂ } with two duties d₁ and d₂, and freeze within this pattern

　　Duties(Rel(P)) = { d₁, d₂ }.

This freezing cannot be dispensed with. Were there a further duty d₃ in Rel(P) passing on both j₁ and j₂, the intersection would not be empty and the pattern would fail. Hence where the set of duties has more than two members, it must be declared in advance that each of the rest determinately fails on at least one j, so that they do not enter the intersection witness; otherwise the sets below are only local slices of candidate duties and not complete 𝒟(j).

Left end X⁻: on j₁, d₁ passes and d₂ does not; on j₂, d₁ passes and d₂ passes. Under the freezing above, 𝒟(j) is given completely by the truth values of these two duties:

　　𝒟⁻(j₁) = { d₁ }, 𝒟⁻(j₂) = { d₁, d₂ }, with intersection { d₁ },

so CounterHoldRole^M = 1.

Right end X⁺: on j₁, d₁ passes and d₂ does not; on j₂, d₁ does not pass and d₂ does. Then

　　𝒟⁺(j₁) = { d₁ }, 𝒟⁺(j₂) = { d₂ }, each local set non-empty, the intersection empty,

so CounterHoldRole^M = 0.

Every "passes" in the table must be read as the complete RelPass holding — that there exist a legal R\_j and its corresponding Bind family satisfying every Type-sensitive Sat — and not as a bare Boolean label.

Under the freezing above, the truth value of every cell at both ends is already given, so

　　Dec\_CH(X⁻) = Dec\_CH(X⁺) = 1.

By Theorem 4.2′ one therefore obtains directly

　　CounterHoldRole^M(X⁻) = 1, CounterHoldRole^M(X⁺) = 0,

and this pattern closes completely with Theorems 4.2′ and 4.3; the reader need supply no antecedent.

Declaration of strength: the above is a satisfiable pattern of the quantifier structure, not a constructed running instance. What it proves is that where ∀j ∃d\_j is still true, ∃d ∀j may still fail — which follows directly, as a boundary case, from the existing requirements of The Mathematical Form of Principle (a fixed duty must cover the whole of J; duty drift is forbidden). This form-text does not sign the instance-reachability claim that "those four cell values can be realized together within some concrete Sedimentation-Field"; to sign that, one must construct a legal instance satisfying joint evaluation, AHold, Anti, Bind, and Law(P) in full, which is the task of a construction form or a reachability form. By the Canon Chapter, that a later-stage instantiation has not been carried out does not of itself negate a formal conclusion.

4.5　A single certificate's exit does not suffice

Theorem 4.4　Single-certificate-exit insufficiency theorem

Let C ∈ 𝒲^M\_CH(X⁻) while C ∉ 𝒲^M\_CH(X⁺). If the right end still has some C′ ∈ 𝒲^M\_CH(X⁺), then CounterHoldRole^M(X⁺) = 1.

Proof. By Theorem 4.1, a non-empty certificate set at the right end gives CounterHoldRole^M(X⁺) = 1 at once. □

None of the following therefore suffices of itself to entail a boundary of Principle, and none may be upgraded automatically: some one R\_j exits; some one Bind family lapses; some one Anti degree of freedom disappears; some one complete old certificate exits. Only where such an exit brings about the extinction of the certificate set, or together with other exits causes CounterHoldRole to lapse, does it rise to boundary responsibility.

By the same token, the lapse of an old duty d₁ does not automatically amount to a boundary of Principle. A duty d is not a cross-temporal external identity of Principle(P; A, J); so long as the right end has another duty d₂, and d₂ by itself covers the whole of the fixed J completely, one and the same Principle qualification may continue to stand.

Do not miswrite "d must be fixed across the whole of J at the time of qualification" as "it must forever be the same d at different times."

4.6　The decomposition of certificate extinction

Theorem 4.5　Certificate-extinction decomposition theorem

　　𝒲^{M,+}\_CH = ∅ if and only if: every old certificate of the left end has exited at the right end, and the right end has no new complete certificate taking their place.

This theorem characterizes only the formal fact that the certificate set is empty; to read from it further that CounterHoldRole^M = 0 requires Dec\_CH(X⁺) = 1 besides (Theorem 4.2′).

Proof. If the right end's certificate set is empty, it contains neither a legal correspondent of an old certificate nor a new certificate, so both conditions hold. Conversely, if every old certificate has exited and no new certificate takes their place, then no complete certificate exists at the right end and the set is empty. □

That every old certificate has exited says only that the original witnesses have exited; only "every old certificate exited and no substitute certificate" yields the extinction of the certificate set. τ\_Γ here bears accounting alone, and bears no assumption of structural retention whatever.

4.7　The fixed-certificate boundary and the certificate-extinction boundary

Along one and the same directed Γ comparison path declared in advance, with the boundary value actually attained. This section compares only the two boundaries of the formal certificate set itself and does not touch the value of CounterHoldRole^M, so no Dec\_CH antecedent is needed; to read CounterHoldRole^M = 0 further at q^∂\_𝒲 requires Dec\_CH = 1 there besides. Let

　　q^∂\_fix(C): the attained boundary at which the fixed complete formal certificate C first loses its qualification;  
　　q^∂\_𝒲: the attained boundary at which the whole of 𝒲^M\_CH first becomes empty.

Theorem 4.6　Boundary order theorem

　　q^∂\_fix(C) ≤ q^∂\_𝒲.

If, after the fixed certificate has lapsed and before the certificate set finally goes extinct, there is a strictly succeeding comparison state at which a substitute certificate continues to bear, then

　　q^∂\_fix(C) \< q^∂\_𝒲.

Proof. The certificate set is necessarily non-empty while C is still qualified, so the boundary at which it becomes empty is no earlier than the boundary at which C loses qualification, which gives the inequality. If between the two there is a strictly succeeding comparison state at which a substitute certificate still bears, then at that state the certificate set is non-empty while C has lapsed, so the strict inequality holds. □

A strict inequality must not be signed merely because a momentary substitute exists at that same boundary point. Where supremum and infimum have not been proved actually attained, only numerical bounds may be reported, and one may not thereby read off the truth value of Principle at the boundary point, the responsibility for the failure, or which qualification broke first.

### 4.8　Asymmetry of the endpoints

Theorem 4.7　Endpoint asymmetry theorem

Certifying one concrete boundary of Principle qualification does not require first proving Principle ⇔ Law(P) ∧ CounterHoldRole over the whole Γ domain.

In the exit direction, with the left end 1 and the right end 0: the positive qualification of the left end can prove directly that Law(A) = 1, Law(P) = 1, and CounterHoldRole = 1, and then the Established sufficient direction of The Necessity Form of Principle gives Principle(X⁻) = 1; the negative qualification of the right end proves Law(A) = 1 together with Law(P) = 0 or CounterHoldRole = 0, and then the contrapositive of the necessary direction of The General Form of Principle gives Principle(X⁺) = 0. The negative end must retain the Conditional antecedents of The General Form of Principle.

In the entry direction, with the left end 0 and the right end 1, the obligations of the two ends are exchanged: the left end proves Law(A) = 1 together with Law(P) = 0 or CounterHoldRole = 0, and the contrapositive of the necessary direction gives Principle(X⁻) = 0, retaining the Conditional antecedents of The General Form of Principle; the right end proves Law(A) = 1, Law(P) = 1, and CounterHoldRole = 1, and the sufficient direction gives Principle(X⁺) = 1. The negative-end obligation of an entry therefore lies on the left and the positive-end obligation on the right.

Three statuses are accordingly listed separately: the structural positive end, by sufficiency, is Established; the structural negative end, by the contrapositive of necessity, is Conditional; and where the formal layer is read back by way of CounterHoldRole^M, it is Conditional. These statuses must not be flattened.

4.9　The three-valued discipline at the formal layer

The formal layer admits the three values 1, 0, and ⊥. Where some necessary formal input is unadjudicable and there is no determinate failure, ⊥ propagates. Where an existing definition can independently and determinately yield 0, that determinate failure is handled by the upstream three-valued rules even where other formal inputs are unadjudicable, and must not be upgraded unconditionally to ⊥.

The two most consequential: CounterHoldRole^M = ⊥ must never be read as CounterHoldRole = 0; and an insufficiency of formal input must never be read as "there is no Principle in reality."

## Section Five　Principal Conclusions

### 5.1　Properties within the boundary

Stated pointwise. For any legal comparison state X, so long as Law(A) = 1, Law(P) = 1, and CounterHoldRole = 1, the sufficient direction of The Necessity Form of Principle gives Principle(X) = 1, irrespective of whether that state is currently acting.

That some certificate exits at X while another complete certificate still exists at X says only that this comparison state has not yet crossed the boundary of qualification; it signs no conclusion about retention over a whole path. Duration of retention, the availability of substitutes, and return all belong to The Stability Form of Principle.

### 5.2　The position of the boundary

Theorem 5.1　Top-level exhaustion theorem for the boundary of Principle qualification

Within the fixed working domain and the frozen semantic domain M1 through M12, with A still a Law:

　　An exit (Boundary⁻) can be borne only by one of the following: B\_P⁻, the exit of P's Law qualification; or B\_CH⁻, the exit of CounterHoldRole. There is no third independent Disqualification.  
　　An entry (Boundary⁺) requires the candidate-entry antecedent first: Dom\_Principle at both ends, adjacency, and the left end already certified Principle = 0. Under that antecedent, Boundary⁺ holds if and only if Law(P) = 1 and CounterHoldRole = 1 hold together at the right end, that is, the one or two items lacking at the left end are made up together at the right. Lacking that antecedent, both items complete at the right end says only that Principle = 1 at the right end, which may be no more than 1 to 1 and constitutes no entry.

For the proof see 2.1. This theorem is an exhaustion within the currently frozen canonical domain, and not an absolute exhaustion across every future theory.

5.3　The degenerate result of crossing out

After crossing out, with A still a Law, Principle is 0 within the working domain. The two responsibilities are permitted to occur together, so there are three readings:

　　B\_P⁻ occurs and B\_CH⁻ does not: at the right end P no longer has Law qualification, and the original counter-holding relation has lost its Law-side bearer; whether counter-holding qualification still has formal meaning here is handled separately under the corresponding working domain.  
　　B\_P⁻ does not occur and B\_CH⁻ does: P is still a Law but no longer has counter-holding qualification against A — there is no legal witness whatever covering the whole of J with one and the same duty. P and A may still coexist in the same field here and may still act upon each other; only those actions no longer constitute a Principle.  
　　Both occur together: a unique responsibility must not be assigned by force. This accords with Section Six, which claims no uniqueness for the concrete component bearing the boundary.

The degeneration must not be read in reverse: that Principle is 0 at the right end does not alter the fact that it once stood at the left end.

The corresponding result in the entry direction is: after crossing in, with A still a Law, Principle is 1 within the working domain; and this likewise does not retroactively alter the fact that it did not stand at the left end.

### 5.4　Exit on the target side

Where Law(A) goes from 1 to 0, it is recorded as OUT\_A. It cannot retroactively alter a Principle that stood before A lost its Law.

Do not write "P successfully brought A down" as P never having been a Principle at all; it may be precisely that the actual consequence of a Principle finally destroyed its own target relational object.

While A is still within the working domain, an exit of Principle at the ontological layer admits in the end only two top-level responsibilities: first, P losing its Law; second, CounterHoldRole lapsing. ## Section Seven　The Simplest Expression

The definition of a boundary and the cause of a boundary are two things, and must not be written as one biconditional.

First, the definition of a boundary. The truth-value crossing of Section One governs:

Second, the compression of structural responsibility. It is a one-way implication only, and must not be read in reverse:

　　Boundary⁻\_Γ ⇒ B\_P⁻ ∨ B\_CH⁻, holding under the Conditional antecedents of the necessary direction of The General Form of Principle;  
　　Boundary⁺\_Γ ⇒ Law(P)(X⁺) = 1 ∧ CounterHoldRole(X⁺) = 1.

The converse does not hold. Take a left end with Law(P) = 1 and CounterHoldRole = 0: Principle at the left end is already 0; let Law(P) fall from 1 to 0 at the right end, so B\_P⁻ occurs and the two states are adjacent — but this is Principle from 0 to 0, and no Boundary⁻. The entry side is the same: where both items already hold together at the left end, the right end satisfying both fully gives Principle from 1 to 1, which is no Boundary⁺ either.

The converse on the entry side is available only under these conditions: the left end's Principle = 0 has been legally certified, Dom\_Principle holds at both ends, and the two are adjacent; then both items holding together at the right end gives, by the sufficient direction of The Necessity Form of Principle, Principle(X⁺) = 1, and Boundary⁺ holds.

## Section Eight　Interpretation

This section is interpretation. It is not part of the formal structure and bears no obligation of proof.

The boundary of a Principle does not lie where some one counter-hold disappears. One Law's counter-holding qualification against another is a qualification that many different witnesses may bear in turn: this time by this carrier solution, next time by another; this time by way of this Anti degree of freedom, next time by another; even this stretch by this duty and another stretch by another duty — so long as there is still one and the same duty that can cover the whole of the relation domain declared in advance. The disappearance of the qualification is the exhaustion of all such ways of bearing.

That was said of losing. The acquiring side is its dual: for a Principle to stand again, it is not enough to make up some one witness; the candidate Principle must itself become a Law again, and there must again be a duty that can cover the whole relation domain — both at once.

There are therefore two very different kinds of "no longer." One is that some concrete road is broken while other roads remain; then the Principle is still there. The other is that every stretch, looked at singly, is still passable, and yet no one road can any longer be found that goes from beginning to end; then the Principle is no longer there. The latter is the global consistency break, and it is the form this text most wants attended to, because locally everything looks normal. And one thing more must be added: to say "can no longer be found" presupposes that every stretch has been checked; if some stretch has not been checked, then it can neither be said that one can be found, nor that none can.

There is yet another "no longer" that does not belong here at all: the target Law itself has exited. Then it is not that the Principle has lost its qualification, but that the holding-tendency it was directed toward no longer exists. The Principle may even have been among the causes of that exit.

The last sentence may be compressed thus: the boundary of a Principle is not where some one counter-holding realization disappears, but — with the target still a Law — where the candidate Principle itself loses Law qualification, or where the last counter-holding qualification able to cover the whole relation domain with one and the same duty disappears altogether; and where the target Law itself exits, one enters a boundary of another kind, that of the target's domain of definition.

## Section Nine　Final Conclusion

Within the frozen comparison regulation Γ and the frozen semantic domain M1 through M12:
