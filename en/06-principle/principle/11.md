# The Stability Form of Principle

## Section One　Purpose, Form-Kind, and the Single Irreducible Task

### 1.1　Placement of the form-kind

This form-text is a Stability Form, an auxiliary form. After a principal form has given the structure and the Boundary Form has discriminated the boundary of instantaneous qualification, it answers how an already standing Principle relation is retained, lapses, and returns under permitted change and along legal successor trajectories.

### 1.2　The single irreducible task

With one and the same target Law A, one and the same candidate Principle identity P₀, one and the same relation domain J declared in advance, and one and the same stability protocol all fixed, one question only is answered: once Principle stands, under what identity and at what quantifier level is it retained through permitted state changes and legal successor trajectories?

That question divides into six:

　　whether one and the same counter-holding certificate can go on bearing;  
　　whether, after the original duty exits, a new certificate of the same duty can take its place;  
　　whether, after the original duty exits, the same P₀ can still retain Principle by some other duty;  
　　whether, after P₀ exits, some other candidate Principle can take over against the same A;  
　　whether qualifications at each layer, once lapsed, can return within the prescribed time limit;  
　　what strength the claims above attain at each of the three quantifier levels: a single trajectory, a declared trajectory family, and every legal future.

Not proved again: what a Principle is; Principle, CounterHoldRole, and Act. Not studied: how a Principle generates a new Law; intentionality; the grading of Principle by strength.

### 1.3　Dependence

It depends on the sealed The General Form of Principle v1.0, The Mathematical Form of Principle v1.0, The Necessity Form of Principle v1.0, and The Boundary Form of Principle v1.0; and it inherits the stability disciplines already matured in The Stability Form of Law — the fixed-object layer and the existence layer are kept apart, recovery is placed after the first actual lapse, a boundary must be actually attained, and the quantifiers of a single trajectory, a trajectory family, and all legal trajectories must not be traded for one another. It modifies no upstream definition, adds no ontological predicate, and adds no H.

Should a principal form be broken through or withdrawn, the conclusions of this form-text lapse or become pending.

## Section Two　The Stability Instance and Frozen Identity

### 2.1　The stability instance

One examination of stability takes as its object one frozen stability instance:

　　Σ\_P = ( F\_t, A, I\_A, P₀, I\_{P₀}, J, C₀, d₀, Π, Δ, τ, H ).

Here F\_t is the initial Sedimentation-Field; A the fixed target Law identity, with I\_A its Law application domain; P₀ the original candidate Principle identity, with I\_{P₀} its Law application domain; J the relation domain of P₀ relative to A declared in advance; C₀ the initial complete formal counter-holding certificate; d₀ the fixed duty used by C₀; Π the family of candidate Principle identities frozen in advance, with P₀ ∈ Π — its sense is given in 2.2; Δ the class of permitted state perturbations; τ the rule of cross-state identity correspondence for certificate components, see 2.3; and H = (T\_obs, T\_rec, h) the dynamic stability protocol.

### 2.2　The discipline of freezing

A, P₀, J, the content within Rel used to adjudicate the same identity and the same duty, and the candidate family Π may none of them be modified after the fact once stability has been lost. Anything that stands again after any of these is changed does not belong to the recovery of the original stability instance; it is another stability instance.

Π is not a bare set of names. For each member, its Law identity, its Law application domain declared in advance, and the Rel identity content used to recognize that same candidate are all frozen together with Π. Formally one may write

　　Π = { ( P\_α, I\_{P\_α}, IdRel(P\_α) ) }\_α,

while the body still calls its members P for short.

Hence, modifying the Law application domain or the Rel identity content of any candidate in Π counts as replacing that candidate; it may not be counted as a takeover within the original Π, nor as Principle never having broken off. This accords with the discipline of freezing P₀: if only names are frozen and not the identity of adjudication, "the same candidate taking over" has no cross-temporal object to refer to.

Π above all must be frozen in advance: to add a newly appearing candidate Principle into Π after the fact and then declare that "Principle never broke off" is to put the conclusion into the premise.

2.3　Cross-state identity of a certificate

C₀ contains components such as the carrier solution R\_j of each j, members of the Bind family, and Anti witnesses. Once the field state changes, these objects may migrate, be rewritten, or lose their correspondents, so "is it still the same C₀" requires a rule; otherwise the fixed-certificate layer could be read either as the literal object being unchanged to the letter, or as any certificate of the same shape being found at will.

To that end a rule of cross-state identity correspondence τ is frozen along with Σ\_P: it maps each component of C₀ to its corresponding object on a perturbed or successor state, doing identity accounting alone and presupposing no structural retention whatever. τ is of a kind with the τ\_Γ of The Boundary Form of Principle, a pure formal auxiliary object adding no ontology.

τ must be declared in advance together with Δ, and its manner of correspondence may not be rewritten after stability has been lost. Where τ gives no correspondent for some component, that component is taken as having no correspondent on that state.

## Section Three　Permitted Perturbations, the Target Working Domain, and the Adjudicable Domains

### 3.1　Permitted perturbations

A perturbation is a map

　　η: F\_t ↦ F\_t^η.

The class of permitted perturbations is written Δ^adm. What is permitted to vary is the field state and the actual bearing realizations legally derived from it: the carrier solution R\_j of each j; the actual members within a Bind family; Anti degrees of freedom; sedimentation, impedance, availability, and the competitive structure; the Law-bearing realization of P; and whether some duty can still cover the whole of J at present.

The following may not be altered under cover of a perturbation: the identity of A; the identity of P₀; J; I\_A and I\_{P₀}; the content within Rel(P₀) used to adjudicate the same duty; and the candidate family Π. To alter them is to leave the original stability instance.

A perturbation that is legal but unadjudicable may not be deleted from the perturbation class, nor adjudicated 0 directly; it enters ⊥ under the three-valued regime.

3.2　The target working domain is adjudicated first

The stability of a Principle differs from that of a Law in one respect that goes to the root: the target A may itself exit Law. For any trajectory γ, therefore, Law\_s(A, I\_A) is adjudicated first. Define

　　DomA\_γ(H) = 1 ⇔ for every s ∈ J\_obs, Law\_s(A, I\_A) = 1.

If there is an actual moment s\_o with Law\_{s\_o}(A, I\_A) = 0, that trajectory is marked OUT\_A. If there is no known 0 while something is unadjudicable, it is ⊥.

The three differ from one another:

　　OUT\_A ≠ 0, OUT\_A ≠ ⊥.

OUT\_A says that the target relational object has exited Principle's stability working domain; it is not an ordinary loss of stability by Principle.

The trajectory layer may use the OUT\_A label. The family layer and the guarantee layer erect no four-valued stability quantity, but set up target-domain guarantee as an independent axis of its own; see Section Ten.

### 3.3　The adjudicable domains

The adjudicable domain required by each stability task is declared separately: the ontological layer requires the adjudicability of Law and Principle; the formal layer requires the representation conditions of The Mathematical Form of Principle and the Law-side antecedents it invokes; cross-layer read-back requires M1 through M8 of that text besides. Wherever an adjudicable domain is insufficient, ⊥ is recorded and 0 must not be filled in.

## Section Four　Four Layers of Instantaneous Stability Qualification

The stability of a Principle must first state which identity is being retained. The four layers below run from lower to higher, each with its own instantaneous qualification defined.

### 4.1　The fixed-certificate layer

Write τ(C₀) for the correspondents of C₀'s components on X. Define a three-valued qualification:

　　Q\_C(X) = 1 where τ(C₀) still constitutes a complete legal certificate on X, that is, where its components all have legal correspondents and satisfy item by item the complete-certificate requirements frozen in 3.1 of The Boundary Form of Principle;  
　　Q\_C(X) = 0 where there is a decisive failure: some necessary component has no correspondent on X, or its correspondent determinately fails to satisfy the corresponding requirement;  
　　Q\_C(X) = ⊥ where there is no decisive failure while at least one necessary certificate component is undecided.

What is retained is one and the same complete formal counter-holding certificate; "the same" is adjudicated by τ.

### 4.2　The fixed-duty layer

Define a three-valued qualification:

　　Q\_d(X) = 1 where for every j ∈ J, RelPass\_{d₀}(j) = 1, that is, d₀ ∈ ⋂\_{j ∈ J} 𝒟\_X(j);  
　　Q\_d(X) = 0 where there is a j ∈ J with RelPass\_{d₀}(j) = 0;  
　　Q\_d(X) = ⊥ where there is no 0 while at least one RelPass\_{d₀}(j) is ⊥.

R\_j, the members of a Bind family, Anti degrees of freedom, and the like may be replaced one by one, but the duty must still be the same d₀.

Note that d₀ ∉ ⋂\_j 𝒟\_X(j) is not the same as Q\_d(X) = 0: by 3.2 of The Boundary Form of Principle, 𝒟 admits only those whose RelPass is determinately 1, so "not belonging" is compatible with 0 and with ⊥ alike.

### 4.3　The fixed-Principle-identity layer

The two layers below are ontological, their three values given by the ontological adjudicable domain: where that domain is insufficient, the value is ⊥.

　　Q\_P(X) = 1 ⇔ Principle\_X(P₀; A, J) = 1;  
　　Q\_P(X) = 0 ⇔ Principle\_X(P₀; A, J) = 0;  
　　Q\_P(X) = ⊥ where the adjudicable domain of Principle is insufficient on that state.

What is retained is the Principle qualification of one and the same candidate; the duty may change.

### 4.4　The candidate-family existence layer

　　Q\_Π(X) = 1 ⇔ there is a P ∈ Π with Principle\_X(P; A, J) = 1.

What is retained is only that "against one and the same A there is always some Principle." Its three-valued regime: where at least one is determinately 1, Q\_Π = 1; where every frozen candidate is determinately 0, Q\_Π = 0; where there is no 1 while some ⊥ remains, Q\_Π = ⊥.

"No P has been found as yet" must not be written as 0.

### 4.5　The formal counter-holding layer

Write also the formal-layer qualification

　　Q\_{CH^M}(X) := CounterHoldRole^M\_X(P₀; A, J),

whose three values are inherited directly from The Mathematical Form of Principle: 1, 0, or ⊥. It belongs to the formal layer and is not an ontological qualification.

The three-valued regime of the four layers and of this one is thereby closed: the lapse-time set of Section Six admits only those s with Q\_ℓ(s) determinately 0; ⊥ neither enters Fail nor counts as retention.

## Section Five　The Five-State Structure of Replacement

### 5.1　The order of adjudication

The five states must not be inferred upward from the certificate layer. The ontological Principle must be adjudicated first, and the formal layer then used to subdivide the manner of its bearing.

The five states are defined within the following subdomain: A is still within the working domain; Π is frozen; every ontological and formal judgement required is decidable, that is, Principle(P₀), the Principle of each candidate, Q\_C, and Q\_d all take 1 or 0; and the representation antecedents suffice to support the relevant formal diagnosis.

Outside that subdomain no state is assigned by force: where some qualification is undecided it is recorded ⊥, and where the target has exited it is recorded OUT\_A; neither occupies any of the five states.

### 5.2　The five states

　　S1　original-certificate state: Principle(P₀) = 1 and Q\_C = 1.  
　　S2　same-duty replacement state: Principle(P₀) = 1, Q\_C = 0, while Q\_d = 1.  
　　S3　same-Principle-different-duty state: Principle(P₀) = 1, while Q\_d = 0. A formal diagnosis may state further that some other duty bears it, but Principle must not be inferred unconditionally from a formal quantity.  
　　S4　different-Principle-takeover state: Principle(P₀) = 0, while there is a P₁ ∈ Π \\ { P₀ } with Principle(P₁) = 1.  
　　S5　Principle-vacancy state: for every P ∈ Π, Principle(P) = 0.

All five states are stated by the three-valued qualifications Q\_C and Q\_d defined in Section Four, and not by literal writings such as "C₀ ∈ 𝒲^M\_CH": after a perturbation the correspondent is given by τ and need not be the original object in the literal sense.

5.3　Mutual exclusivity and exhaustiveness

Within the common decidable subdomain above, S1 through S5 are mutually exclusive and exhaustive.

Proof. Divide first by the value of Principle(P₀). On the branch taking 1: divide first by the value of Q\_C, with Q\_C = 1 being S1; those with Q\_C = 0 divide again by the value of Q\_d, with Q\_d = 1 being S2 and Q\_d = 0 being S3. The instantaneous chain Q\_C ⇒ Q\_d (Section Six) is used here, so S1 necessarily has Q\_d = 1 and does not overlap S3. On the branch taking 0: divide by whether Π has another candidate standing, giving S4 and S5. Within the common decidable subdomain every qualification takes 1 or 0 with no ⊥, so each division is mutually exclusive and exhaustive. □

OUT\_A and ⊥ do not belong to the five states.

5.4　A residual certificate does not alter the verdict

Even where Q\_C = 1, that is, where the old certificate still has a complete legal correspondent under τ, so long as Law(P₀) has already exited and Principle(P₀) = 0, that state enters S4 or S5, and must not be assigned to S1 on account of the residual certificate. The existence of a formal certificate is no proof of ontological qualification.

## Section Six　The Dynamic Protocol, Retention, Recovery, and Stability

6.1　The instantaneous chain at the formal layer

Upstream has already given: a complete certificate entails that the duty it uses covers the whole of J, and the latter entails that formal counter-holding qualification stands. Stated in the three-valued qualifications of Section Four, the instantaneous layer has

　　Q\_C ⇒ Q\_d ⇒ Q\_{CH^M},　Status = Established.

Q\_C is used throughout here, and "C₀ ∈ 𝒲^M\_CH" is not used as a cross-state antecedent: after a perturbation the correspondent is given by τ.

The converse in general does not hold: the original certificate exits while another certificate of the same duty takes its place; d₀ exits while another duty takes its place.

6.2　The ontological layer and the cross-layer bridge

Write the ontological structural auxiliary formula

　　Q\_P^str(X) = Law\_X(P₀, I\_{P₀}) ∧ CounterHoldRole\_X(P₀; A, J).

It is merely the conjunction of two existing conditions, not a newly added ontological predicate. By the sufficient direction of The Necessity Form of Principle,

　　Q\_P^str = 1 ⇒ Principle(P₀; A, J) = 1,　Status = Established.

The formal layer must not write CounterHoldRole^M ⇒ Principle directly. Only where M1 through M8 of The Mathematical Form of Principle and all its inherited representation antecedents hold is there CounterHoldRole^M ⇒ CounterHoldRole, which then enters Principle in conjunction with Law(P₀). Every stability bridge from the formal layer to the ontological is therefore marked Conditional.

6.3　The protocol and the three quantities

Freeze H = (T\_obs, T\_rec, h). For any identity layer ℓ ∈ { C, d, CH^M, P, Π } take its qualification function Q\_ℓ(s). The lapse-time set is

　　Fail^ℓ\_γ(H) = { s ∈ J\_obs \| Q\_ℓ(s) = 0 }.

It admits only those determinately 0: a moment with Q\_ℓ(s) = ⊥ does not enter the lapse-time set, nor does it thereby count as standing.

Where it is non-empty, write b\_ℓ = inf Fail^ℓ\_γ(H). That an infimum is not an actual point of lapse must be kept in view throughout.

Retention:

　　Keep^ℓ\_γ = 1 ⇔ for every s ∈ J\_obs, Q\_ℓ(s) = 1.

Recovery: Recover^ℓ\_γ = 1 requires all seven of the following — the lapse-time set is non-empty; b\_ℓ \< t\_r; the time limit for recovery is counted from the boundary of the first lapse, and is not reset by any later lapse; there is an actual failure point s\_f with b\_ℓ ≤ s\_f \< t\_r; Dur(b\_ℓ, t\_r) ≤ T\_rec; Q\_ℓ stands continuously from t\_r to the end of the observation window; and after recovery it has at least the verification length h.

Stability:

　　Stable^ℓ\_γ ⇔ Keep^ℓ\_γ ∨ Recover^ℓ\_γ.

Where the three values fall must be set down explicitly and not left vague. If within the observation window there is a moment with Q\_ℓ(s) = ⊥, that moment neither enters the lapse-time set nor satisfies the moment-by-moment requirement of retention; neither Keep nor Recover may then be adjudicated 1, and the stability quantity is recorded ⊥ — unless a determinate lapse has already occurred on that trajectory and all seven items of recovery have been completed, with the undecided stretch affecting none of those seven, in which case Recover = 1 may still be adjudicated.

In short: Stable^ℓ\_γ = 1 must be supported by determinate values of 1; Stable^ℓ\_γ = 0 must be supported by a determinate lapse together with a determinate failure of the recovery conditions; everything else is ⊥. An insufficient adjudicable domain is likewise ⊥. Where A exits the working domain, that trajectory reports OUT\_A and not Stable = 0.

## Section Seven　The Dynamic Stability Monotonicity Theorem and Two Stability Chains

### 7.1　The monotonicity theorem

Theorem 7.1　Dynamic stability monotonicity theorem

Suppose that on one and the same trajectory, under one and the same H, and within a common dynamic adjudicable domain, Q₁(s) = 1 ⇒ Q₂(s) = 1 for every s. Then

　　Stable\_H(Q₁) ⇒ Stable\_H(Q₂).

Proof. Two cases.

First, Q₁ falls under Keep. Then Q₁(s) = 1 for every s, and the moment-by-moment implication gives Q₂(s) = 1, so Q₂ likewise falls under Keep and stability holds.

Second, Q₁ falls under Recover. The moment-by-moment implication gives Fail(Q₂) ⊆ Fail(Q₁). If Fail(Q₂) is empty, Q₂ falls under Keep and stability holds. If non-empty, the inclusion gives b₂ ≥ b₁. Take Q₁'s moment of recovery t\_r: from t\_r onward Q₁ is constantly 1, so Q₂ is constantly 1 from t\_r onward as well, and Q₂ therefore has no lapse after t\_r, its moment of recovery being no later than t\_r. Moreover Fail(Q₂) is non-empty and contained in Fail(Q₁), so an actual failure point exists; and b₂ ≥ b₁ gives Dur(b₂, t\_r) ≤ Dur(b₁, t\_r) ≤ T\_rec; while the verification length h is guaranteed by its being constantly 1 after t\_r. All seven items are in hand, so Q₂ falls under Recover. □

Status = Established. The antecedents must be explicit: one and the same trajectory, one and the same H, a common dynamic adjudicable domain. Lacking any of the three, the theorem does not apply.

A common dynamic adjudicable domain here means: at every moment within the observation window both Q₁(s) and Q₂(s) take 1 or 0, with none at ⊥. If either is undecided at some moment, the moment-by-moment implication cannot be verified and the inclusion of the Fail sets no longer holds, so the theorem does not apply.

### 7.2　Two stability chains

From Theorem 7.1 and the instantaneous chain of 6.1 comes the formal-layer stability chain

　　Stable\_C ⇒ Stable\_d ⇒ Stable\_{CH^M},　Status = Established.

From P₀ ∈ Π comes the instantaneous implication Q\_P ⇒ Q\_Π: where Q\_P = 1, P₀ is itself the existential witness for Q\_Π. Theorem 7.1 then gives the ontological-layer stability chain

　　Stable\_P(P₀) ⇒ Stable\_Π,　Status = Established.

### 7.3　Three converse counterexamples

All three converses in general fail, and a minimal counterexample is given for each.

First, certificate replacement. Suppose d₀ covers J throughout the observation window while at some moment C₀ exits and another complete certificate of the same duty, C₁, takes over, and C₀ does not return within the time limit for recovery. Then Stable\_d = 1 while Stable\_C = 0.

Second, duty replacement. Suppose CounterHoldRole^M is constantly 1 throughout the observation window while at some moment d₀ leaves the intersection and another duty d₁ covers J, and d₀ does not return within the time limit. Then Stable\_{CH^M} = 1 while Stable\_d = 0.

Third, Principle-identity replacement. Suppose P₀ exits at some moment while P₁ ∈ Π takes over at once and goes on standing, and P₀ does not return within the time limit. Then Stable\_Π = 1 while Stable\_P(P₀) = 0.

Of the third it must be recorded especially: this is not P₀ recovering, nor is it "one and the same Principle being retained"; what is retained is only that the existence of a Principle has not broken off.

## Section Eight　The Formal–Ontological Bridge and the Non-Conjunctivity of Asynchronous Recovery

### 8.1　The cross-layer bridge

Stable\_{CH^M} ⇒ Stable\_P must not be written.

The conditional version: if Law\_s(P₀, I\_{P₀}) = 1 throughout the observation interval, and M1 through M8 of The Mathematical Form of Principle and all its inherited representation antecedents hold moment by moment, then

　　Stable\_{CH^M} ⇒ Stable\_P(P₀),　Status = Conditional.

To use a two-way equivalence of the formal and the ontological, one must inherit in full the necessary direction of The General Form of Principle and the converse representation conditions of The Mathematical Form of Principle besides, and must not take them as unconditional by default.

8.2　The non-conjunctivity of asynchronous recovery

Theorem 8.1　Non-conjunctivity of asynchronous recovery

In general

　　Stable(Q₁) ∧ Stable(Q₂) ⇏ Stable(Q₁ ∧ Q₂).

Counterexample. Take T\_rec = 2. Suppose Law(P₀) lapses at t = 1 and recovers at t = 2; CounterHoldRole lapses at t = 9 and recovers at t = 10. Each satisfies the recovery conditions on its own, so each is stable. But the conjunction Q\_P^str = Law(P₀) ∧ CounterHoldRole first lapses at t = 1, while the two stand together only at t = 10, so Dur(1, 10) = 9 \> T\_rec = 2 and the recovery condition is not satisfied. Hence

　　Stable\_Law ∧ Stable\_CH ⇏ Stable\_{Q\_P^str}.

Status: a negative result established by counterexample, Established.

This theorem stops here and goes no step further. To read a loss of stability by Principle from a loss of stability by Q\_P^str requires the contrapositive of the necessary direction of The General Form of Principle together with its dynamic read-back antecedents, and that direction is Conditional; hence Stable\_Law ∧ Stable\_CH ⇏ Stable\_P is not signed here.

Corollary: one cannot certify the stability of Principle merely from Law(P₀) and CounterHoldRole each being stable. To read the stability of Principle further from the joint structure, one must still proceed by the upstream necessary and sufficient interfaces and their respective Statuses. The safest course is to adjudicate Principle directly, or to adjudicate its legal joint qualification directly while retaining the cross-layer antecedents.

## Section Nine　The Quantifiers Do Not Commute

Theorem 9.1　A Principle persisting and Principle never breaking off

　　there exists a P ∈ Π such that for every s ∈ J\_obs, Principle\_s(P) = 1  
　　⇒ for every s ∈ J\_obs there exists a P ∈ Π with Principle\_s(P) = 1.

The converse does not hold.

Proof. Forward: take the witness P and use that same P as the existential witness at each s. Converse counterexample: let Π = { P₁, P₂ }, with only P₁ standing in the first half of the observation window and only P₂ in the second. Then some candidate stands at every moment, while no candidate stands throughout the window. □

In one sentence: one and the same Principle persisting entails that Principle never breaks off; Principle never breaking off does not entail that one and the same Principle persists.

The certificate layer and the duty layer have their corresponding non-commutation of quantifiers:

　　there exists a C standing for every s ⇒ for every s there exists a C standing, and the converse does not hold;  
　　there exists a d standing for every s ⇒ for every s there exists a d standing, and the converse does not hold.

The counterexamples in both places are of the same shape: one certificate or one duty bears the first half of the window and another bears the second.

## Section Ten　Trajectory Stability, Trajectory-Family Stability, and Guaranteed Stability

The three quantifier levels must be kept apart and must not pass for one another.

### 10.1　A single trajectory

Stable^ℓ\_γ concerns one concrete legal trajectory alone, its value given by the three-valued regime of 6.3.

If the target exits within the observation window on that trajectory, OUT\_A is reported by 3.2 and no stability value is given besides; if the adjudicable domain is insufficient, ⊥ is reported. The conclusion of one trajectory must not be generalized to the family or to the guarantee layer; see 10.2 and 10.3.

### 10.2　A declared trajectory family

First freeze a non-empty family 𝒢^ℓ ⊆ TrajDec\_ℓ. As at the guarantee layer, the family layer too has two axes, target domain and stability, and their order may not be reversed.

Adjudicate the target domain first:

　　FamilyDom\_A = 1 ⇔ for every γ ∈ 𝒢^ℓ, DomA\_γ = 1.

If the family contains a trajectory whose target exits, FamilyDom\_A = 0; if there is no known exit while something is unadjudicable, it is ⊥. Such trajectories are recorded neither as Stable = 0 nor as ⊥, and still less may they be deleted from the family.

Only after FamilyDom\_A = 1 is

　　FamilyStable\_ℓ = 1 ⇔ for every γ ∈ 𝒢^ℓ, Stable^ℓ\_γ = 1

adjudicated.

Where the family contains a trajectory whose stability is 0, FamilyStable = 0. Where the family is empty, or where it cannot be confirmed to belong to the corresponding adjudicable trajectory set, it is ⊥.

10.3　The guarantee over every legal future

First define the legal trajectory set TrajLegal(F\_t^η, H). In defining it, neither unadjudicable trajectories nor OUT\_A trajectories may be deleted.

Adjudicate the target-domain guarantee first:

　　GuaranteedDom\_A = 1 ⇔ on every legal trajectory, A retains Law throughout the observation window.

Only after GuaranteedDom\_A = 1 is GuaranteedStable\_ℓ adjudicated. If there is a legal but unadjudicable in-domain trajectory, GuaranteedStable = ⊥.

One trajectory that can recover must not pass for FamilyStable, and FamilyStable must not pass for GuaranteedStable.

## Section Eleven　Stability Boundaries, Replacement Regions, and Hysteresis

### 11.1　Levels of recovery

Recover\_C, Recover\_d, Recover\_P, and Recover\_Π are each defined separately and must not pass for one another.

For instance, where P₀ lapses while P₁ takes over at once and P₀ later returns: one may then have Recover\_P(P₀) = 1, while the candidate family was never vacant, so that no recovery event at the Π layer arises at all. Where recovery occurs at a lower layer, the higher layer may never have lapsed.

11.2　A stability boundary and a qualification boundary must not be conflated

The qualification boundary of The Boundary Form of Principle answers: at which q the current qualification crosses between 1 and 0. The stability boundary of this form-text answers: starting from a perturbed state q and running under protocol H, when retention or recovery is no longer satisfied.

The two in general cannot be compared, and each direction has its counter-model.

First, qualification already lost while stability still holds. Suppose that at some perturbation parameter the current qualification is 0, while that lapse falls within the time limit for recovery and it then returns under the protocol and satisfies the verification length. The qualification boundary has been crossed there, while Stable = 1.

Second, qualification still standing while stability is already lost. Suppose that at some perturbation parameter the current qualification is 1, while its legal successors enter an unrecoverable lapse within the observation window. The qualification boundary has not been crossed there, while Stable = 0.

The two directions together show that there is no general order between the two kinds of boundary. Hence q^∂\_qualification ≤ q^∂\_stability must not be signed unconditionally, nor its converse — unless there is some antecedent of monotonicity of the stability value in q besides, and this form-text has no such theorem.

### 11.3　The order of stability boundaries

For one and the same totally ordered perturbation axis q declared in advance, with the corresponding set of failure parameters non-empty and an infimum existing:

　　formal layer: q^∂\_C ≤ q^∂\_d ≤ q^∂\_{CH^M}, Status = Established;  
　　ontological layer: q^∂\_P ≤ q^∂\_Π, Status = Established.

The middle stretch is in general not comparable: there is no unconditional order between q^∂\_{CH^M} and q^∂\_P, and each direction has its counter-case. First, Law(P₀) exits before formal counter-holding qualification: P₀'s Principle qualification then loses stability first while the formal layer is still stable, so q^∂\_P \< q^∂\_{CH^M}. Second, formal counter-holding qualification exits first while Law(P₀) is retained throughout and the representation antecedents hold throughout: the formal layer then loses stability first and the ontological follows, so q^∂\_{CH^M} ≤ q^∂\_P. Both directions are possible, so there is no general order.

If Law(P₀) is retained throughout the comparison domain and the formal and ontological representation antecedents hold throughout, the two may be connected conditionally.

Where the failure set of some layer is empty, positive infinity must not be written; what should be reported is

　　NoExitOnΓ,

that is, no exit at that layer has been attained within the declared range of Γ.

11.4　Stability replacement regions and strict inequality

What this section treats is stability boundaries, so a replacement region must be defined by stability quantities, and the strict inequality of a stability boundary must not be inferred directly from the fact of a replacement at instantaneous qualification.

Define the stability replacement regions:

　　Sub^St\_C = { q \| Stable\_C(q) = 0 and Stable\_d(q) = 1 };  
　　Sub^St\_d = { q \| Stable\_d(q) = 0 and Stable\_P(q) = 1 };  
　　Sub^St\_P = { q \| Stable\_P(q) = 0 and Stable\_Π(q) = 1 }.

They say that at that perturbation parameter the lower-layer identity is no longer stable under protocol H while the higher-layer identity still is.

It must be stressed: a region being non-empty does not suffice to entail a strict inequality of infima. It is entirely possible that at some q, Q\_C = 0 while Q\_d = 1, and yet the stability of both layers finally comes out 0, with the infima of their two stability failure sets the same.

To sign

　　q^∂\_{C,St} \< q^∂\_{d,St},

the following are required besides: the first stability failure points of both layers must be actually attained; and there must be, between the lower layer's stability failure point and the higher layer's, a strictly succeeding comparison state lying within Sub^St\_C — or else the monotonicity of stability failure along the q axis must be declared in advance. The other two are the same.

What can be obtained from the fact of a replacement at instantaneous qualification — C₀ has exited while d₀ still covers J, or d₀ has exited while P₀ is still a Principle, or P₀ has exited while P₁ stands — is only the strict order of qualification boundaries in the sense of The Boundary Form of Principle, and not the order of stability boundaries of this form-text. The two must not be used interchangeably.

### 11.5　Hysteresis

Hysteresis must be defined layer by layer of identity, and must not be spoken of generally as "the hysteresis of a Principle." There are severally Hys\_C, Hys\_d, Hys\_P, and Hys\_Π.

Before hysteresis is adjudicated the following must be frozen: the same q axis; the same identity object; the same A, J, and Π; the same resolution; the same protocol H; and the forward and reverse paths declared in advance. Only after both the forward exit boundary and the reverse entry boundary have been actually attained may hysteresis be adjudicated.

On a scalar axis one may write Hys\_ℓ = \| q*\_{ℓ,out} − q*\_{ℓ,in} \|. In the general case of a partial order or of paths, one may only report that the two boundaries do not coincide, and no width is defined.

The recovery time T\_rec and the width of hysteresis are not the same quantity, and writing them into each other is forbidden.

## Section Twelve　The Simplest Expression and Principal Conclusions

### 12.1　The simplest expression

With the stability instance Σ\_P frozen, A within the working domain, and the required adjudicable domains sufficient:

　　Stable^ℓ\_γ ⇔ Keep^ℓ\_γ ∨ Recover^ℓ\_γ,　ℓ ∈ { C, d, CH^M, P, Π };  
　　Stable\_C ⇒ Stable\_d ⇒ Stable\_{CH^M};  
　　Stable\_P ⇒ Stable\_Π;  
　　Stable\_{CH^M} ⇒ Stable\_P: only where Law(P₀) is retained throughout and the representation antecedents hold moment by moment, Conditional.

The converses of the three stability chains above all fail; for the counterexamples see 7.3. Two further things must not be written into the simplest expression: that the components each being stable entails their conjunction being stable (Theorem 8.1); and that Principle never breaking off entails one and the same Principle persisting (Theorem 9.1).

The simplest expression holds only under the declared antecedents; away from them, each quantity takes ⊥ under the three-valued regime of 6.3 and must not be taken as 0 by default.

### 12.2　Principal conclusions

The stability of a Principle is not some one counter-holding realization staying unchanged throughout. Before any claim of stability is adjudicated, it must first be stated which identity is required to be retained: one and the same certificate, one and the same duty, one and the same Principle, or only that against one and the same target Law there is always some Principle.

The bearing at a lower layer may be replaced while the qualification at a higher layer is retained; but once P₀ has been taken over by another P, what is retained is no longer one and the same Principle, but only that the existence of a Principle has not broken off.

Where the target Law itself exits, OUT\_A is reported; that is not a loss of stability.

## Section Sixteen　Interfaces, Declaration of Dependence, and Version Note

### 16.1　Interfaces

To The Grading Form of Principle: the task of grading different Principle relations and different duties by counter-holding strength, the strength of competition, and net action is delivered. This form-text adjudicates stability alone and gives no grade of strength.

To the Intentionality Group-Form: only the structural fact that "a Principle relation may stably retain its qualification, and that Principles against one and the same target Law may undergo a takeover of identity such that their existence does not break off" is delivered; intentionality must not be inferred directly from stability. This form-text neither defines nor studies the stability of acting, and so delivers no conclusion whatever about "stable action."

To the tasks of law-change and Principle generation: this form-text proves neither why a Principle generates a new Law nor how P's loss of stability and takeover come about.

To The Boundary Form of Principle: the division of labour is strictly kept — The Boundary Form of Principle adjudicates the boundary of instantaneous qualification, and this form-text adjudicates retention, recovery, replacement, guarantee, and hysteresis under permitted change and a trajectory protocol. Neither may swallow the other.
