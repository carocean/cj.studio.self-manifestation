# The Grading Form of Principle

## Section One　Form-Kind, the Single Task, and Entry Conditions

### 1.1　Form-kind

This form-text is a Grading Form of the multi-dimensional partial-order type. It erects no single overall grade and gives no weighted total score whatever; what it erects is a regime of grading by multi-dimensional partial order, with incomparability, incommensurability, and undecidedness strictly kept apart.

### 1.2　The single task

For an already standing Principle, and without altering its qualification or bringing in any other object, to erect a legal grading regime for its counter-holding degree, and to make explicit: the grading dimensions; the grade value range of each dimension; the direction of order of each dimension; the non-degeneracy of each dimension; the non-redundancy of each dimension; the partial-order properties of each dimension and of the joint comparison; the conditions of incomparability; the conditions of incommensurability; the conditions of undecidedness; the rule of projection; and the prohibition of any arbitrary total score or single overall grade.

### 1.3　Entry conditions

This form-text grades only objects with Principle\_F,t(P; A, J) = 1.

One with Principle = 0 is not called a "grade-zero Principle"; it has simply not entered the domain of objects graded here. For one with Principle = ⊥, the qualification to enter is undecided, and no grade is given.

What is graded here is neither the host P as a whole nor all of P's duties, but the counter-holding structure formed by the one fixed duty through which P acquired its Principle identity relative to A.

No grade relation whatever may be turned back into a condition of Principle's qualification. A weak counter-hold is still a legal Principle: a low grade is not a deficient qualification, still less no qualification at all.

### 1.4　Dependence

It depends on the sealed The General Form of Principle v1.0, The Mathematical Form of Principle v1.0, and The Necessity Form of Principle v1.0, and uses their existing terms: Law, Principle, CounterHoldRole, DeliveryLimit, ResultLimit, DeliveryAnti, ResultAnti, Type, Read, J, A's holding-direction preorder, Att, Rest. It modifies no upstream definition, adds no ontological predicate, and adds no H.

Should a principal form be broken through or withdrawn, the conclusions of this form-text lapse or become pending.

## Section Two　Express Exclusions

The following are not studied here and may not serve as grading dimensions: whether acting is occurring; the strength of acting; net effect; victory or defeat in competition; stability; whether a Principle generates a new Law; the probability and propensity of triggering; the depth and maturity of P as a Law; engineering resources, energy consumption, and efficiency.

The strength of competition must be excluded above all. Local victory and defeat in competition need not be transitive — the case in which first beats second, second beats third, and third beats first violates no upstream structure whatever — and by the Canon Chapter a non-transitive relation may not be called a grading. To put competition into a grading dimension is to pass a non-partial-order off as a partial order.

## Section Three　The Fixed Grading Dimensions

The official dimensions are fixed at two:

　　𝒟\_G = { D, R },

where D is the delivery counter-holding dimension and R the result counter-holding dimension. The two correspond respectively to the upstream DeliveryAnti and ResultAnti.

A dimension may not be deleted for the occasion because some object has only the delivery type or only the result type. Where a dimension does not apply on some legal evaluation unit, that dimension takes NA on that unit; the dimension is not deleted. Deleting a dimension would make the profiles of two objects differ in length, so that comparison could not proceed at all, and would let "does not apply" quietly become "has no counter-hold." The reference implementation of the appendix therefore raises an error outright where profile lengths differ, rather than truncating silently — to truncate silently would be to condone an input this section forbids.

## Section Four　The Comparison Regulation Γ\_G

One grading task must freeze a complete comparison regulation Γ\_G in advance, containing at least: the common target Law A; the scope of comparison, complete or projected; the common relation domain; the set of legal grade evaluation units; the two fixed dimensions D and R; the common normalization space on the A side; the rule of normalization witnesses; the rule of the Rest anchor; the legal result base domain; the rule for preserving the structure of A's holding-direction; the identity of projections; and the resolution together with the reading rules required.

The comparison domain must not be narrowed, the evaluation units changed, or the regulation replaced after the comparison results have been seen, so as to manufacture a ranking.

Where the original task is a complete comparison, a maximal common comparable subdomain taken after the fact can form a projected comparison only, and must not pass for a complete comparison. This accords with the rule of projection of Section Thirteen.

## Section Five　Legal Grade Evaluation Units

A grade evaluation unit is not written down as some fixed triple. The official definition is

　　u ∈ 𝒰\_{Γ\_G},

where 𝒰\_{Γ\_G} is the set of legal grade evaluation units induced by the original Type and Read semantics.

The requirements are these: a single-member type may use single-member evaluation units; a set type keeps the original set evaluation boundary; a measure type must not automatically raise a qualification measure proportion into a grade value; temporal and durational types keep the original window, time unfolding, and quantifier unit; and a Grading Form must not re-dissect the upstream Type and Read.

In one sentence: Type and Read determine what a legal grade evaluation unit is; they do not determine how the units are to be summed into a total score. Summation itself is forbidden here; see 11.2 of Section Eleven.

## Section Six　The Normalization Domain

The three-valued predicate

　　Dom\_Γ(P) ∈ { 1, 0, ⊥ }

is used throughout.

Dom\_Γ(P) = 1 if and only if there exists a legal normalization witness N^Γ\_P by which P's candidate counter-holding structure relative to A can enter the common normalized A space of Γ\_G without loss.

A legal normalization witness must satisfy seven items: the load-bearing A-side object is the same; the domain of definition is legal; it is frozen in advance; it neither manufactures nor destroys Anti; several permitted normalization witnesses must not give different grade conclusions; the D dimension requires an order embedding on the load-bearing A order together with a uniform Rest anchor; and the R dimension requires a structural isomorphism on a common legal result base domain, preserving the structures bearing on A's holding-direction.

Where a necessary condition is determinately violated, Dom\_Γ(P) = 0, recorded as exit from the current regulation domain, OUT\_Γ. Where there is no determinate failure while the necessary conditions are insufficient, Dom\_Γ(P) = ⊥.

The qualification to enter is adjudicated candidate by candidate. Within one comparison it may happen that Dom\_Γ(P) = 1 while Dom\_Γ(Q) = 0: this is not "that pair is undecided," but that under this regulation that pair cannot be completely compared at all. Only where both are 1 may they enter the comparisons of Sections Eleven through Thirteen under the current Γ\_G; where either is 0, the current regulation does not suffice to bear this complete comparison; where either is ⊥, the qualification to enter is undecided.

It must be recorded: OUT\_Γ is not the same as incommensurability. The former says that this one regulation will not do; the latter says that no permitted regulation will; see Section Twelve.

## Section Seven　Normalization on the A Side

### 7.1　Pairwise bridging is forbidden

The method of pairwise bridging — one bridge for P and Q, another for Q and R — must not be used. Every candidate entering one and the same Γ\_G must be normalized into one and the same A-side normalization space frozen in advance.

The reason is transitivity. If each pair uses its own bridge, then the first being not weaker than the second, and the second not weaker than the third, does not yield the first being not weaker than the third — the normalization spaces used in the two comparisons are simply not the same one. A common space is the precondition of transitivity, not a formal nicety.

7.2　The normalization requirement of the D dimension

The map

　　φ\_{P,u}: X^P\_{A,u}/∼\_P → C\_u

is required to be an order embedding on the load-bearing domain, that is,

　　x ≼\_P y ⟺ φ\_{P,u}(x) ≼^C\_u φ\_{P,u}(y),

and a uniform Rest anchor is required:

　　φ\_{P,u}(\[Y^P\_rest\]) = c^0\_u.

The original Rests need not be equal, but their normalized identities must be aligned to one and the same c^0\_u; otherwise each measures from its own starting point and the positions so obtained are not comparable.

A "distance" must not be manufactured out of pure order isomorphism. The normalization space must come from an ordered structure already present on A's common physical, state, result, or degree-of-freedom terms, or from a normalized quotient space already proved structurally identical; a number line must not be made up at will for the sake of ranking.

7.3　The normalization requirement of the R dimension

Normalization of the R dimension must guarantee four things: that the complete legal result base domains of P and of Q are isomorphic to one and the same D^C\_u; that A's holding-direction order is preserved; that Att, terminal classes, unbounded cofinality, and the other structures bearing ResultAnti are preserved; and that "a stricter restriction" is not forged out of an originally smaller base domain.

The last deserves particular care: to take a base domain that was narrow to begin with as "a strict restriction" is to pass a difference of domain of definition off as a difference of restriction.

## Section Eight　The D-Dimension Grade

What the D dimension grades is delivery counter-holding position, and it is not called a uniform "depth of counter-holding."

In the normalization space, where a delivery-type counter-hold stands,

　　d^Γ\_P(u) = φ\_{P,u}(\[Y\_P\]),

and

　　d^Γ\_P(u) ≺\_C c^0\_u,

that is, the normalized position after delivery runs strictly counter to the Rest anchor — which is precisely the normalized statement of DeliveryAnti standing.

The local grade order is defined as

　　d^Γ\_P(u) ≽\_D d^Γ\_Q(u)　⟺　d^Γ\_P(u) ≼\_C d^Γ\_Q(u).

Directional reading: the further a position runs counter to A's holding-direction in the common normalized order, the higher its delivery counter-holding position. The grade order therefore runs opposite in direction to the normalized order; this is no slip of the pen but the direct writing of that directional reading. No distance whatever is involved here: a position is a place in an order, and "further counter" means lying further toward the counter-holding side within the order, not a measured remoteness.

A numerical distance not supported upstream must not be introduced.

The reference implementation of the appendix represents a place in the normalized order by an integer rank, with the convention that a smaller rank runs further counter to the Rest anchor in the common normalized order, so that dominance in the D dimension runs opposite to the size of the rank. This is a convention of the implementation only, and agrees exactly with the definition ≽\_D ⟺ ≼\_C of this section.

## Section Nine　The R-Dimension Grade

What the R dimension grades is the strictness of a result restriction. It is not called a "result counter-holding distance" or a "directional depth"; nor is it properly called a position — position is the D dimension's term, meaning a place in the common normalized A order, whereas the R dimension's grade lies on no order at all: it is the restriction set itself, its height given by reverse inclusion.

For legal result restriction sets on which ResultAnti already stands, take within the normalized common result base domain

　　S^C\_P, S^C\_Q ⊆ D^C\_u,

and define

　　S^C\_P ≽\_R S^C\_Q　⟺　S^C\_P ⊆ S^C\_Q.

That is, the fewer legal results are left to A, the stricter the restriction and the higher the grade.

If two ResultAnti sets do not include one another, they are incomparable in the R dimension — even where intuitively one set seems "further from the terminal." Intuitive remoteness has no formal standing here: on the base domain there is no distance this form-text recognizes, only inclusion.

This is the substantive difference between the R dimension and the D: the positive values of D lie in a common normalized A order, which on the load-bearing domain may be a chain; whereas the positive values of R are result restriction sets, and reverse inclusion upon them is only a partial order, so that two positive grades not including one another are incomparable as such. The R dimension therefore must not be compressed into a chain of positions — that would quietly turn a partial order into a total one, so that incomparable cases could never arise. The reference implementation of the appendix accordingly divides the positive values of the two dimensions into two data types, and verifies separately an instance of incomparability within the R dimension.

## Section Ten　The Local State Alphabet and the Grade Profile

### 10.1　Local states

Local evaluation must strictly distinguish four states:

　　NA: that fixed dimension does not apply on that legal evaluation unit;  
　　0: that dimension is evaluable, while determinately no Anti of that type has been formed;  
　　v⁺: a real positive counter-holding grade is attained;  
　　⊥: it ought to be evaluable while the load-bearing facts are insufficient.

The four must be strictly distinguished and none may stand for another: NA, 0, v⁺, and ⊥ each have their own sense, and it must above all be kept in mind that NA ≠ 0, and that neither equals ⊥.

Moreover

　　NA, 0, v⁺ ∈ V\_G,　while　⊥ ∉ V\_G.

⊥ is a state of evaluation, not a grade value.

The rule of comparison: NA compares only with NA; a real positive value is higher than a determinate 0.

A mismatch between NA and non-NA is a determinate failure, not an undecidedness. The grade values of both are already determinate; they simply stand in no relation within the partial order: v⁺ is not higher than NA, and NA is not higher than v⁺, both directions failing to pass. Undecidedness belongs to ⊥ alone, that is, to the case where the load-bearing facts are insufficient. Hence where on one coordinate one side is NA and the other is v⁺ or 0, the dominance adjudications in both directions each come out 0, and the final relation is incomparability, not ⊥\_G.

NA must not be folded into 0 — that would forge "does not apply" into "zero counter-hold" and so manufacture a spurious strict dominance; see K-G1 of Section Fifteen.

### 10.2　The grade profile

The official grade value is the complete profile:

　　G^Γ\_D(P) = ( g^P\_D(u) )\_{u ∈ 𝒰\_Γ},  
　　G^Γ\_R(P) = ( g^P\_R(u) )\_{u ∈ 𝒰\_Γ}.

The grade profile of the whole Principle is

　　G^Γ(P) = ( G^Γ\_D(P), G^Γ\_R(P) ).

Each u is not an independent dimension: the official dimensions are D and R alone, and u is only the index of the profile.

## Section Eleven　Partial-Order Properties and the Joint Comparison

### 11.1　The partial order of each dimension

Comparison of D profiles is by pointwise dominance: G\_D(P) ≽\_D G\_D(Q) if and only if on every legal evaluation unit the local D value is not weaker. The R dimension likewise.

Theorem 11.1　The partial order of each dimension

≽\_D and ≽\_R are reflexive, transitive, and antisymmetric on the space of grade values.

This theorem is stated on the grade value space V\_G, that is, with each coordinate taking NA, 0, or v⁺; ⊥ is not a grade value and does not enter the universe of this theorem (Section Ten).

Proof. Reflexivity: on every coordinate the local value compared with itself passes — NA with NA passes, 0 with 0 passes, and a positive value with itself passes within its own order. Transitivity: suppose P is not weaker than Q on every coordinate, and Q not weaker than a third object O on every coordinate. Taking coordinates one by one, transitivity between positive values is borne by the two dimensions separately — between positive values of the D dimension it comes from the transitivity of the common normalized A order ≼\_C, and between positive values of the R dimension from the transitivity of set inclusion ⊆; both are partial orders and so each is transitive. Now the mixed cases: a positive value is higher than 0 and 0 is not higher than a positive value, so mixed cases of positive value and 0 are likewise transitive. The case of NA must be looked at separately: if on some coordinate one of P and Q is NA while the other is not, then the comparison on that coordinate has already failed, the premise "P is not weaker than Q on every coordinate" does not hold, and transitivity does not arise; so under a premise that does hold, P and Q on that coordinate must both be NA or both be non-NA, and likewise Q and O, whence all three on that coordinate are either all NA or none NA. Where all are NA, transitivity is plain; where none is NA, the case falls under the positive-value and 0 cases above. Once extended by 0 and NA, then, transitivity coordinate by coordinate still holds. So P is not weaker than O on every coordinate. Antisymmetry: if both directions pass, then on every coordinate each is not weaker than the other. At positive values of the D dimension, the antisymmetry of ≼\_C gives equality; at positive values of the R dimension, two-way inclusion of sets gives equality; at 0 and at NA they are equal directly. So the local values are equal coordinate by coordinate and the two profiles are the same. □

Note that antisymmetry gives only that the grade profiles are the same; it cannot entail P = Q. The identity layer of a Principle is not settled by grade. Define therefore grade equivalence

　　P ∼\_G Q　⟺　G(P) = G(Q),

and only after quotienting by grade equivalence is a genuine partial order obtained.

### 11.2　The joint partial order

Define

　　G(P) ≽\_G G(Q)　⟺　G\_D(P) ≽\_D G\_D(Q) and G\_R(P) ≽\_R G\_R(Q).

Strict dominance requires that it be not weaker overall and strict on at least one dimension.

Theorem 11.2　The product order

≽\_G is reflexive, transitive, and antisymmetric on the space of grade values.

Proof. The two components are each reflexive, transitive, and antisymmetric (Theorem 11.1), and a product order inherits all three componentwise. □

Score(P) = w\_D·D + w\_R·R, or any other weighted total score, must not be defined, nor may any single overall grade number be declared. The only conclusion permitted is this one sentence: P dominates Q on the complete grading domain.

## Section Twelve　Directional Adjudication, the Final Grade Relation, and Three Negations

12.1　Three-valued directional adjudication

No single comparison black box is written. Directional dominance is adjudicated first, separately:

　　Dir^Γ\_G(P, Q) ∈ { 1, 0, ⊥ }　and　Dir^Γ\_G(Q, P).

The notation must be distinguished from the regulation entry qualification Dom\_Γ(P) of Section Six: the latter adjudicates whether a single candidate can enter the common normalization space, the former whether one candidate dominates another in some one direction. Their senses are wholly different, and the same name is not used for both.

The single-direction rule: if there is any determinate coordinate on which "P is not weaker than Q" fails, then Dir(P, Q) = 0; if every coordinate passes determinately, it is 1; if there is no determinate failure while one or more coordinates remain undecided, it is ⊥.

"Passes determinately" here includes coordinates on which both sides are NA: that is a settled pass, neither undecided nor a failure. Whereas one side NA and the other non-NA is, by Section Ten, a determinate failure.

Refutation takes precedence: 0 takes precedence over ⊥. Where a determinate failure is already in hand, unknown coordinates must not lift the directional adjudication back to ⊥. This rule is no fastidiousness about notation — without it, manufacturing an undecidedness on any one coordinate whatever would launder an already determinate failure into an undecidedness; see K-G2 of Section Fifteen.

### 12.2　The final grade relation

Combine the two directional adjudications Dir(P, Q) and Dir(Q, P):

　　(1, 0) ⇒ P ≻ Q;  
　　(0, 1) ⇒ Q ≻ P;  
　　(1, 1) ⇒ P ∼\_G Q;  
　　(0, 0) ⇒ P ∥\_G Q;  
　　any other unclosed combination ⇒ ⊥\_G.

Here P ∥\_G Q says that both have entered one and the same legal complete grading domain while both directions have been determinately refuted.

12.3　The three negations are strictly distinguished

　　∥\_G ≠ incommensurability ≠ ⊥\_G.

∥\_G means that within a common grading domain neither dominates the other; incommensurability means that no permitted complete common regulation exists; ⊥\_G means that it ought to be adjudicable while the present facts are insufficient.

Mixed formulations such as "where comparison is impossible, record ⊥" must not be written: they press three different things into one.

### 12.4　Incommensurability

That some one Γ\_G fails at present does not entail incommensurability directly.

For one and the same scope of comparison S declared in advance, define the permitted regulation class Adm(S). Only where

　　there is no Γ′ ∈ Adm(S) into which P and Q can jointly and completely enter

is it called incommensurability.

This judgement requires an exhaustive examination of Adm(S). Merely "no other regulation has been found" does not suffice to conclude incommensurability; where exhaustion has not been completed, one can only stop at OUT\_Γ. The reference implementation of the appendix is designed accordingly: a conclusion of incommensurability is permitted only where the exhaustive enumeration of Adm(S) is passed in.

Two things must be recorded: finding a non-empty common subdomain is not the same as having achieved commensurability; and where the original task is a complete comparison, a new regulation must still cover that same complete object of comparison.

## Section Thirteen　Complete Comparison and Projected Comparison

Let the load-bearing domain of a complete regulation be 𝒰\_Γ. A complete comparison must cover the whole of 𝒰\_Γ.

Where comparison is possible only on U′ ⊊ 𝒰\_Γ, the conclusion must be written

　　P ≽\_{Γ\|U′} Q,

whose reading is this one sentence: P is not weaker than Q on the projected subdomain U′. It must not be upgraded to P ≽\_Γ Q.

Where some projection was from the outset an independently declared local comparison task, that projected domain is itself the complete domain of that local task, and its conclusion is a complete conclusion within that task.

Taking a favourable subdomain after the fact is forbidden.

## Section Fourteen　Non-Degeneracy and Non-Redundancy

The four claims of this section do not take "there exist certain grade profiles" as a premise. Every combination of grades must obtain an actual witness within a legal model satisfying the upstream Principle conditions; otherwise a hidden coupling remains possible — D and R both come from one and the same fixed duty, and it cannot be ruled out in advance, in theory, that only certain (D, R) pairings are realizable. What non-redundancy is to refute is precisely such a functional dependence, so "any combination is realizable" must not be taken as a premise; that would make the proof circular.

A legal minimal common model M\_G is therefore declared, its complete construction given in Appendix B. Only the three already standing Principles it provides and their grade profiles are cited here.

14.1　The witnesses the minimal model provides

On the single evaluation unit u of M\_G, the Rest anchor c₀ is fixed in the common normalized A order and two points d₁ ≺\_C d₂ ≺\_C c₀ are taken; the common normalized result base domain is D^C\_u = { 0, 1, 2 }, and A's holding-direction terminal is Att = { 2 }. Take two result restriction sets

　　S₁ = { 0 }, S₂ = { 0, 1 },

both proper subsets of D^C\_u and both excluding Att, so that the ResultAnti of each stands and both are real positive R grades; and S₁ ⊊ S₂.

Note that a restriction set must not equal the whole base domain: to permit A every legal result is to have formed no result restriction at all, and such a set cannot serve as a positive witness of ResultAnti.

Three already standing Principles are actually obtained within M\_G:

　　candidate　　Dom\_Γ　　D(u)　　R(u)　　fixed duty used  
　　P₁　　　　　 1　　　　 d₁　　　S₁　　　d\*₁₁  
　　P₂　　　　　 1　　　　 d₂　　　S₁　　　d\*₂₁  
　　P₃　　　　　 1　　　　 d₁　　　S₂　　　d\*₁₂

The Law(P), CounterHoldRole, and covering of J of each stand within M\_G; their DeliveryAnti is given by d\_i ≺\_C c₀ and their ResultAnti by the corresponding restriction set. They are not profiles assumed to exist, but three objects witnessed within that model.

### 14.2　Non-degeneracy of the D dimension

Witness W-ND-D: P₁ and P₂. The R values of the two are alike S₁, their D values are d₁ and d₂ respectively, and both are real positive grades. By the grade order ≽\_D ⟺ ≼\_C of Section Eight, d₁ ≺\_C d₂ gives P₁ strictly higher than P₂ in the D dimension — d₁ lies further counter to the Rest anchor in the common normalized order, so its delivery counter-holding position is higher. The D dimension therefore attains at least two different real positive grades, and non-degeneracy holds.

A difference between NA and 0 must not be used to counterfeit non-degeneracy: neither of those is a real positive grade.

### 14.3　Non-degeneracy of the R dimension

Witness W-ND-R: P₁ and P₃. The D values of the two are alike d₁, their R values are S₁ and S₂ respectively, and both are real positive grades. By the definition of Section Nine, S₁ ⊊ S₂ gives P₁ strictly higher than P₃ in the R dimension. The R dimension therefore attains at least two different real positive grades, and non-degeneracy holds.

### 14.4　D is not settled by R

Witness W-NR-D: P₁ and P₂. The R profiles of the two are exactly the same while their D profiles differ. If D were a function of R, then those with the same R profile would necessarily have the same D profile, contrary to this. So D is not settled by R.

### 14.5　R is not settled by D

Witness W-NR-R: P₁ and P₃. The D profiles of the two are exactly the same while their R profiles differ. If R were a function of D, then those with the same D profile would necessarily have the same R profile, contrary to this. So R is not settled by D.

### 14.6　Taken together

The three objects together bear the non-degeneracy and the non-redundancy of both dimensions: P₁ and P₂ give two different positive grades of the D dimension with R fixed, and P₁ and P₃ give two different positive grades of the R dimension with D fixed. D and R therefore each bear independently, neither may be dispensed with, and neither can stand in for the other.

The grade profiles of these three objects have been verified as a test item within the reference implementation of the appendix; see item fourteen of A.3.

## Section Fifteen　Bounds of Misjudgement

K-G1　Folding NA to zero. The erroneous practice is to map NA to 0. The consequence is to forge "does not apply" into "zero counter-hold" and so manufacture a spurious strict dominance. The erroneous implementation A of the appendix is just this, and the measurement shows the same case turning from a correct P ∥ Q into P ≻ Q.

K-G2　Propagating undecidedness upward. The erroneous practice is to adjudicate the whole as ⊥ whenever any ⊥ exists. The consequence is to conceal a two-way refutation already determinate. The correct practice is for directional adjudication to give precedence to determinate failure. The erroneous implementation B of the appendix is just this, and the measurement shows the same case turning from P ∥ Q into ⊥\_G.

K-G3　Passing a projection off as complete dominance. The erroneous practice is to take only a favourable subdomain, or a win on a single dimension, and declare complete dominance.

K-G4　Passing OUT\_Γ off as incommensurability. The erroneous practice is to conclude incommensurability directly from the failure of the current regulation, without checking whether the permitted regulation class contains another that would serve.

K-G5　Manufacturing grades by an arbitrary normalization map. If an ordinary monotone map were permitted to flatten an existing strict order, or if several legal maps gave different grade conclusions, then choosing the favourable one is forbidden. An order embedding or a structural isomorphism must be satisfied, and the grade conclusion must not change; otherwise the evaluation under that regulation is undecided or invalid.

Two situations must be kept apart. First, some map simply does not satisfy order embedding — for instance flattening several different positive grades uniformly into one value — and it is then not a permitted normalization from the outset, and should be rejected before grading rather than set against another map's conclusion. The reference implementation of the appendix accordingly provides an order-embedding check, and the measurement shows the flattening map failing it. Second, two normalizations both passing the basic structural conditions give different grade conclusions — and then, by the fifth of the seven items of Section Six, namely that several permitted normalization witnesses must not give different grade conclusions, the grade evaluation under that regulation is undecided.

## Section Sixteen　Conclusion

First, the counter-holding degree of a Principle has two fixed grading dimensions: D and R.

Second, within each dimension a partial order is formed.

Third, the complete grade profile uses product dominance.

Fourth, two Principles may be grade-equivalent, may stand in strict dominance, or may be incomparable.

Fifth, where no common legal complete regulation exists, the two are incommensurable.

Sixth, where information is insufficient, undecidedness is kept.

Seventh, no arbitrary total score, weight, or total order is erected.

Principles cannot be arranged in one uniform rank from low to high. They can only be compared, within a common regulation frozen in advance, along two fixed dimensions separately: only where every coordinate is not weaker in the same direction and at least one is strict is there strict dominance; where either direction is refuted severally by different coordinates or by the local partial order within a dimension, there is merely incomparability.

It must be recorded that the latter refutation has more than one source: it may be that the two dimensions run opposite; it may be that different evaluation units of the same dimension each win and lose; or it may be that two restriction sets within the R dimension do not include one another — reverse inclusion of sets is only a partial order to begin with, and can produce incomparability without recourse to the other dimension.

## Section Seventeen　Completion Conditions, Breakdown Conditions, and Interfaces

### 17.1　Completion Conditions

　　CPL-G1　The domain of objects graded is confined to Principle = 1, and the object graded is the counter-holding structure formed by the fixed duty.  
　　CPL-G2　The dimensions are fixed as D and R, with NA taken where inapplicable rather than the dimension deleted; the grade value spaces of the two dimensions stand apart, and the R dimension must not be compressed into a chain of positions.  
　　CPL-G3　The twelve items of the comparison regulation Γ\_G are frozen in advance.  
　　CPL-G4　Legal grade evaluation units are induced by the original Type and Read, with no re-dissection of upstream.  
　　CPL-G5　The normalization domain is three-valued, and OUT\_Γ is separated from incommensurability.

"""Minimal reference implementation for the grading protocol of the Principle grading form.

It carries no ontological proof. It only checks, on a finite reference model, that the
decision protocol, the partial-order axioms and the declared misjudgement bounds behave
as the text states.

Every integer rank below denotes a position in a common finite reference order obtained
AFTER a legal A-side normalisation has been performed. A rank is not a theory-defined
physical distance and not a universal scalar strength.
"""
from enum import Enum
from itertools import product


class Local(Enum):
    """Local state alphabet on one legal grading evaluation unit."""
    NA = "NA"              # the fixed dimension does not apply on this unit
    ZERO = "ZERO"          # evaluable, but no Anti of that type is formed
    POSITIVE = "POSITIVE"  # a genuine positive counter-hold grade is taken
    UNKNOWN = "UNKNOWN"    # should be evaluable, but the bearing facts are insufficient


class GammaState(Enum):
    IN_GAMMA = "IN_GAMMA"
    OUT_GAMMA = "OUT_GAMMA"
    UNKNOWN_GAMMA = "UNKNOWN_GAMMA"


class Cell:
    """One coordinate of a grade profile: a local state plus, when POSITIVE, a payload.

    The two fixed dimensions carry DIFFERENT kinds of positive value, and the reference
    implementation keeps them apart on purpose:

      D positive — a position in the common normalised A-order, held as an integer rank.
                   The rank follows the common A-order ≼_C: a SMALLER rank means a
                   position more counter to the Rest anchor, hence a HIGHER D grade.
                   D-grade dominance therefore reverses the rank order, exactly as the
                   text defines it: d_P ≽_D d_Q iff d_P ≼_C d_Q. The rank is a position
                   in a finite reference order obtained after a legal A-side
                   normalisation; it is not a physical distance.
      R positive — the legal result restriction set itself, held as a frozenset of the
                   results still allowed to A inside the common normalised result base.
                   Its order is reverse inclusion, which is only a partial order: two
                   restriction sets that neither contains the other are incomparable.

    Collapsing R onto a rank chain would silently turn a partial order into a total one
    and would never exhibit the incomparable case, so it is not done here.
    """
    def __init__(self, state, rank=None, allowed=None):
        self.state = state
        self.rank = rank            # D positive only
        self.allowed = allowed      # R positive only
        if state is Local.POSITIVE and rank is None and allowed is None:
            raise ValueError("POSITIVE requires either a D rank or an R restriction set")

    def __repr__(self):
        if self.state is not Local.POSITIVE:
            return self.state.value
        return f"D({self.rank})" if self.rank is not None else f"R({set(self.allowed)})"

    def __eq__(self, o):
        return (isinstance(o, Cell) and self.state is o.state
                and self.rank == o.rank and self.allowed == o.allowed)


NA, UNK = Cell(Local.NA), Cell(Local.UNKNOWN)
ZERO = Cell(Local.ZERO)


def POS(r):
    """A D-dimension positive grade: a position in the common normalised A-order.

    Smaller r is further counter to the Rest anchor, hence a higher D grade.
    """
    return Cell(Local.POSITIVE, rank=r)


def RPOS(allowed):
    """An R-dimension positive grade: the set of results still allowed to A.

    Fewer allowed results means a stricter restriction, hence a higher R grade.
    """
    return Cell(Local.POSITIVE, allowed=frozenset(allowed))


def d_positive_ge(a, b):
    """D positive against D positive.

    a is not weaker than b exactly when a sits no further from the counter side in the
    common normalised A-order, i.e. a ≼_C b. With smaller ranks lying more counter to
    the Rest anchor, this reads a.rank <= b.rank.
    """
    return a.rank <= b.rank


def r_positive_ge(a, b):
    """R positive against R positive: reverse inclusion.

    a is not weaker than b exactly when a allows no more than b does. When neither set
    contains the other, both directions return False and the two grades are incomparable.
    """
    return a.allowed <= b.allowed


def cell_ge(a, b):
    """Three-valued 'a is not weaker than b' on one coordinate of one dimension.

    Returns True / False / None, where None means undecided.
    NA compares only with NA. A genuine positive outranks an explicit zero. NA is never
    folded into zero. Note that an NA-versus-anything-else mismatch is a DECIDED failure,
    not an undecided one: both sides carry a settled grade value, they simply stand in no
    order relation, so neither direction passes. Undecidedness belongs to UNKNOWN alone.
    """
    if a.state is Local.UNKNOWN or b.state is Local.UNKNOWN:
        return None
    if a.state is Local.NA or b.state is Local.NA:
        return a.state is Local.NA and b.state is Local.NA
    if a.state is Local.POSITIVE and b.state is Local.POSITIVE:
        if (a.rank is None) != (b.rank is None):
            raise ValueError("cannot compare a D grade with an R grade on one coordinate")
        return d_positive_ge(a, b) if a.rank is not None else r_positive_ge(a, b)
    if a.state is Local.POSITIVE and b.state is Local.ZERO:
        return True
    if a.state is Local.ZERO and b.state is Local.POSITIVE:
        return False
    return True                      # ZERO vs ZERO


def profile_dom(pp, qq):
    """Directional three-valued dominance of one profile over another.

    Refutation first: one decided failure fixes the answer at 0, and later undecided
    coordinates may not lift it back to undecided.
    """
    if len(pp) != len(qq):
        raise ValueError("profiles must share the same declared grading domain")
    undecided = False
    for a, b in zip(pp, qq):
        r = cell_ge(a, b)
        if r is False:
            return 0
        if r is None:
            undecided = True
    return None if undecided else 1


def dom(P, Q):
    """Dominance over the full D/R profile pair. Same refutation-first discipline."""
    dD = profile_dom(P["D"], Q["D"])
    dR = profile_dom(P["R"], Q["R"])
    if dD == 0 or dR == 0:
        return 0
    if dD == 1 and dR == 1:
        return 1
    return None


def compare(P, Q):
    """Final grade relation from the two directional judgements."""
    a, b = dom(P, Q), dom(Q, P)
    if (a, b) == (1, 0): return "P≻Q"
    if (a, b) == (0, 1): return "Q≻P"
    if (a, b) == (1, 1): return "P~Q"
    if (a, b) == (0, 0): return "P∥Q"
    return "⊥_G"


def verdict(P, Q, gamma_P, gamma_Q, adm_exhaustive=None):
    """Separates current-regulation failure from incommensurability.

    Regulation entry is per candidate, as the text defines it: Dom_Γ(P) and Dom_Γ(Q) are
    judged separately, and only when BOTH are IN_GAMMA may the pair be compared under the
    current Γ. If either is OUT, the pair simply cannot be compared completely under this Γ.

    adm_exhaustive, when given, must be an exhaustive enumeration of the admissible
    regulation class Adm(S) over the same declared comparison range, each entry being the
    pair of entry states this regulation yields. Only such an exhaustive enumeration can
    support the verdict 'incommensurable'. When it is None, the caller has not established
    exhaustiveness and the verdict stops at OUT_Γ.
    """
    if GammaState.UNKNOWN_GAMMA in (gamma_P, gamma_Q):
        return "⊥_Γ"
    if gamma_P is GammaState.IN_GAMMA and gamma_Q is GammaState.IN_GAMMA:
        return compare(P, Q)
    if adm_exhaustive is None:
        return "OUT_Γ"
    if any(gp is GammaState.IN_GAMMA and gq is GammaState.IN_GAMMA
           for gp, gq in adm_exhaustive):
        return "OUT_Γ"
    return "incommensurable"


def is_order_embedding(phi, domain, le_src, le_dst):
    """Checks that phi is an order embedding, i.e. x ≼ y iff phi(x) ≼ phi(y).

    A normalisation that is merely monotone, and flattens a strict order, fails this and
    is therefore not an admissible normalisation at all — see the misjudgement bound on
    manufacturing grades by arbitrary mappings.
    """
    return all(le_src(x, y) == le_dst(phi(x), phi(y))
               for x in domain for y in domain)

## Appendix B　The Minimal Common Model M\_G

This appendix provides only the minimal witnesses required by Section Fourteen. It bears no ontological proof whatever and constructs no running base.

B.1　Settings of the model

Take a single legal grade evaluation unit u. The common normalized A space is a finite chain in which the Rest anchor c₀ is fixed and two points d₁ ≺\_C d₂ ≺\_C c₀ are taken; by the grade order of Section Eight, the D grade of d₁ is higher than that of d₂.

The common normalized result base domain is D^C\_u = { 0, 1, 2 }, and A's holding-direction terminal is Att = { 2 }. Two legal result restriction sets are S₁ = { 0 } and S₂ = { 0, 1 }: both are proper subsets of the base domain and both exclude Att, so the ResultAnti of each stands; and S₁ ⊊ S₂, so the R grade of S₁ is higher than that of S₂. Take also S₃ = { 1 }, which does not include and is not included in S₁, so that S₁ and S₃ are incomparable in the R dimension — a point verified separately by the reference implementation of Appendix A.

The fixed target Law A and the relation domain J do not change throughout the model, the regulation Γ\_G is frozen in advance, and the three candidates share one and the same A-side normalization space — no pairwise bridging is used.

B.2　The three candidates

The candidates P₁, P₂, and P₃ each acquire Principle by one fixed duty. The identity of that duty must be stated first: d*₁₁, d*₂₁, and d\*₁₂ are, respectively, just those established fixed duties through which P₁, P₂, and P₃ acquired their Law qualification, and not duties chosen separately. The DeliveryLimit, ResultLimit, DeliveryAnti, ResultAnti, and CounterHoldRole below are all borne by that same duty throughout, with no change.

This binding cannot be dispensed with. What upstream requires is not "P has a Law, and there is besides another duty counter-holding A," but that the same established duty through which P acquired Law qualification bear the counter-hold against A. If the duty the Law rests on and the duty bearing the counter-hold are not one and the same, what is obtained is not a Principle.

The three candidates severally:

　　P₁ acquires by d\*₁₁: its normalized position after delivery is d₁, and its result restriction set is S₁;  
　　P₂ acquires by d\*₂₁: its normalized position after delivery is d₂, and its result restriction set is S₁;  
　　P₃ acquires by d\*₁₂: its normalized position after delivery is d₁, and its result restriction set is S₂.

The Law(P) of each stands. As to CounterHoldRole, it is not passed over with the single phrase "RelPass holds" — by The Mathematical Form of Principle, RelPass is no atomic label, and one and the same fixed duty must satisfy each component item by item within one and the same joint evaluation and close the witness. Its minimal unfolding is therefore set out below.

B.2.1　The common evaluation framework

The three candidates uniformly take one and the same relational context j, one and the same joint evaluation η, and one and the same Bind witness family Π. Upon that framework it is declared:

　　JointProj = 1: the projections on the A side and the P side are legally obtained within the same evaluation;  
　　AHold = 1: A actually holds the degree of freedom at issue within that evaluation, its holding-direction terminal being Att = { 2 };  
　　RoleNonDegenerate = 1: the role realization used is non-degenerate;  
　　ΩWit = 1, Bind = 1: the witness closes, and the three candidates share one and the same frozen A-side normalization space.

J in this model consists of that one j, so "passing on every relational context of J" is here just passing on that j.

B.2.2　The components of the three fixed duties

For d*₁₁, d*₂₁, and d\*₁₂ it is declared severally:

　　DeliveryLimit = 1: each actually restricts the contribution delivered into A's generation, and does not merely write down a result value;  
　　ResultLimit = 1: each is a real restriction on the common normalized result base domain, that is, the set of legal results left is a proper subset of the base domain.

The values on the delivery side are

　　Y\_P = d₁ (for d*₁₁ and d*₁₂)　or　Y\_P = d₂ (for d\*₂₁),　Y\_rest = c₀,

and d₁ ≺\_C d₂ ≺\_C c₀, so the normalized positions of all three after delivery run strictly counter to the Rest anchor, whence

　　DeliveryAnti = 1.

The restriction sets on the result side are

　　S\_{d*₁₁} = S\_{d*₂₁} = S₁ = { 0 },　S\_{d\*₁₂} = S₂ = { 0, 1 }.

S₁ and S₂ are both proper subsets of D^C\_u = { 0, 1, 2 } and both exclude the holding-direction terminal Att = { 2 }, whence

　　ResultAnti = 1.

That one and the same fixed duty bears the delivery type and the result type at once is expressly permitted upstream — The Mathematical Form of Principle permits one duty both to restrict the contribution delivered into A and to restrict A's bearing result. No separate duty need be set up for each dimension here.

B.2.3　From components to Principle

The components above hold together within the same j, the same η, and the same Π, so each candidate's RelPass holds on that relational context; and J consists of that j, whence

　　CounterHoldRole(d\*\_i; P\_i, A, J) = 1.

The d\*\_i here and the duty on which Law(P\_i) rests are one and the same — precisely the one bound at the opening of B.2. The two conditions therefore share one and the same fixed duty, rather than each hanging on its own. The sufficient direction of The Necessity Form of Principle then gives Principle(P\_i; A, J) = 1.

This route runs from components to qualification, and does not take qualification as a premise; and the two items of qualification come from one and the same duty, rather than each condition going its own way.

B.3　The grade facts provided

By B.2, what is actually obtained within the model is:

　　G(P₁) = ( d₁, S₁ ), G(P₂) = ( d₂, S₁ ), G(P₃) = ( d₁, S₂ ).

So R(P₁) = R(P₂) while D(P₁) ≻\_D D(P₂); and D(P₁) = D(P₃) while R(P₁) ≻\_R R(P₃). These are exactly the two sets of witnesses required by Section Fourteen.

B.4　Declaration of scope

Whether any grade combination outside the model is realizable, this form-text does not adjudicate.
