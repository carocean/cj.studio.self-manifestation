# Master Diagram

Preface

An Exposition of the Foundational Theory

I　Form, Mathematics, Computation and Actual Occurrence

Form discloses how structures, the relations among structures and their transformations are organized; mathematics allows these relations to be expressed, derived and proved with precision. They can characterize a process that has already occurred, and can also extract common relations from concrete materials and phenomena, so that these may be re-created, approximated or reproduced after denoising in other materials or environments. That form and mathematics hold does not mean that the corresponding process has actually occurred in another system.

Let the system of original occurrence be A, and the system that undertakes reproduction be P. Over a history in which the Observer enters into Trace-Response with information from A, the Observer identifies the corresponding structures, relations and transformations, and writes them down as form, mathematics or rules of execution; P runs under its own material conditions; afterwards it must still be checked separately whether P forms the corresponding dynamics and whether it obtains the corresponding object qualification. Identification, implementation, reproduction of dynamics and the standing of objects are therefore distinct matters. Neither identical results nor similar trajectories automatically prove that the dynamics is the same.

Computation is the implementation of mathematical state transformation on an actual host. Its general process can be written as:

input → state → transformation → successor state → … → output.

One mathematical state transformation is:

Sₙ ──Tₙ(Iₙ?, Oₙ?)──→ Sₙ₊₁

Mₙ ≔ \[Sₙ ──Tₙ(Iₙ?, Oₙ?)──→ Sₙ₊₁\]  
Compₙ ⇔ Mₙ ∧ Dₙ

Sₙ is the complete state of the structures and structural relations sufficient to undertake the transformation at that step; Tₙ is a mathematical transformation operator, which may be single, composite or the identity; Iₙ? and Oₙ? are an optional input and output, the question mark not being part of a variable name; Sₙ₊₁ may equal Sₙ. Mₙ says that a legitimate mathematical transformation holds; Dₙ says that the device actually drives and implements that mathematical transformation; Compₙ says that this computation holds. Mₙ and Dₙ must refer to the same transformation: a legitimate transformation in one system may not be spliced together with the driving in another event to make one computation. The reading on which Dₙ refers back to the legitimate transformation of Mₙ is to observe the conditions and restrictions of *The General Form of Computation*. Computation does not require a change in visible output, a particular program, an encoding, a user's intention or an observer's read-out.

The device here means the physical host that actually undertakes the computation, and is not confined to digital computers. Continuous analogue computation can be genuine computation; being digital, being discrete and whether deviations are absorbed are not necessary features of computation. The brain can become a host of computation when it performs some mathematical operation that can hold, but the material dynamics of the brain cannot on that ground be reduced wholly to computation. The actual action of neurons, membrane permeability, ions, transmitters and other media does not lose its material source because it can be described mathematically.

II　Two Kinds of Realization of Hosts, and the Boundary of Computation

When a system P bears the occurrence of the dynamics under discussion, or of the dynamical objects upon it (laws, Principles and so on), P is called its host. P may be a computing host or a non-computing host. The former undertakes mathematical state transformations in actual implementation; the latter changes through the relations of action within its own material and environment. Different materials and different environments may still realize comparable dynamical processes, but whether they do depends on the concrete Trace-Response paths, temporal relations and successor conditions; the success of reproduction cannot be judged directly from an abstract phrase such as "the paths are the same."

Both kinds of host rest on the material interaction of signals and media; the difference lies not in whether physical causation exists or whether a mathematical expression can be written, but in whether the declared computational task and the legitimate mathematical transformation are actually implemented by that host. For an electronic analogue integrator whose components have been deliberately selected and connected according to a mathematically expressed relation, computational qualification can be judged by the operator and event implemented; for a capacitor charging naturally, even though its voltage trajectory can be expressed by an integral, it cannot be judged, on the strength of this external description alone, to be executing the declared computation of an integral. The two are contrasting examples for testing the difference between "description" and "implementation"; they do not show that the intention of human design alone suffices to decide, for any physical system, between computation and non-computation. In ontological analysis, the source of Trace-Response relations is likewise a claim of demarcation to be retained: one kind of host configures the implementation of its transformations according to mathematical relations; in the other kind of host, Trace-Response is formed by the material properties of the medium itself, and mathematical expression only describes this occurrence. This is not a distinction determined by the form of output, by discreteness or by the name of the material, nor does every physical process describable mathematically automatically belong to computation. For the concrete computational qualification under this demarcation, the formal criteria of mathematical transformation, actual implementation and the same event must still be checked; the ontological demarcation is retained as a theoretical claim, and its general sufficiency and the determination of concrete qualification are to be handled separately.

Nor can the relation between formal expression and a physical host simply be equated with hostlessness. A law described on paper or in a program may not have obtained bearing in the sense of the dynamics of the source system to which it refers; on the other hand, the device carrying the program is still undergoing real physical transformations, and a computation and a regularity of another level may hold there. What are called hostless laws, hostless Principles, hostless Probabilities and hostless Intentions mean that the dynamics and object duties referred to by the description do not correspondingly hold on the declared host; they do not assert that the text, the program or the computing device itself undergoes no real motion at all.

III　Identification, Thinking, Reasoning and Computation

The Observer's identification of a law in A occurs in the Observer's own history of sedimentation. Here one must distinguish the organization of objects that actually holds in A from the knowledge the Observer obtains: identification involves selection and correction, and does not mean that the standing of an object within the field depends on the Observer's naming. Information from A interacts with the Observer's existing experience, Imagery and Causal-Response relations; the Observer recognizes and generalizes relations, corrects them, breaks their hold and reorganizes them, and then expresses them as form or mathematics. An identified law is not an article moved from A to P; the process of identification may also be influenced by the Observer's selection, power of discrimination and existing history.

Thinking and reasoning can both take the form of a process of input, state, transformation and successor state; deduction from Causal-Response relations already established, and the recognition of new relations in new situations and in the advance of Imagery, remain different kinds of work. The brain's identification of a new law may involve both processes at once. Causal-Response derivation along existing relations, and a process of undergoing occurrence, hold-breaking, renewed occurrence and renewed identification in a new situation, can follow on from one another, but this difference of duty should not be erased merely on the ground of their common form of state transformation. A computing device may also arrive, by means of existing reproducible relations and rules of inference, at conclusions not previously written out explicitly; whether such derivation further involves the formation of new dynamics, identification or object qualification is to be examined from its real history, and cannot be decided from the output alone.

To liken thinking and the process of identification to "pan-computation" indicates only the common form of state transformation. Computation itself remains restricted to the actual implementation of mathematical state transformation on a device; a common shape of process cannot make computation, thinking and reasoning identical in all their material mechanisms and qualifications.

IV　Rule Computation and Generative Computation

Rule computation obtains results according to given structures, structural relations and rules of transformation, and can also run continuously along steps already laid down, forming a simulation of the motion of objects. "Rule computation" here is distinguished by the declared task and the duty of action; it does not say that every actual execution involving rules can undertake only simulation. It may be completed in one step or may run for many steps. Neither the number of steps, nor the complexity of the output, nor whether the program contains branches, is a criterion for history having become dynamics.

Generative computation is also the implementation of mathematical transformations by a host according to rules. What sets it apart from computation that only obtains results or trajectories according to given object rules is that, in the declared run, the sedimentable field state formed by existing history, or legitimately taken over, is read in by subsequent actual execution and bears a dynamical duty. "Legitimately taken over" here does not mean naming arbitrary external parameters as history: what is taken over must satisfy the conditions of formation, holding and moving in of the declared field state; if only numerical values are moved in without the corresponding qualification, their source cannot be made good by later invocation. Signals and media undergo Trace-Response according to the rules of the host, and legitimate execution can update structural relations such as connections, weights and permeability; only when the updated difference is legitimately retained, read in at a successor and actually takes part in Continuation does it constitute the relevant chain of bearing from execution to dynamics. If the relevant difference remains only in a log or shadow copy that is never read, or the present successor is wholly settled by a pre-written object rule, "history has been saved" cannot stand in for the bearing of dynamics. The declared run segment need not regenerate the whole of its history, nor rewrite the field state at every step. Retention and reading-in are necessary links of recognition; the full qualification of generative computation cannot be declared from these two surface phenomena alone. Generative computation is borne by rule execution on the host, and so contains the computational duty of rule execution rather than excluding it. But rule computation that obtains results by pregiven relations does not automatically acquire a generative duty because it uses the same kind of execution mechanism. The basis of the distinction is whether historical conditions obtain the declared dynamical qualification in legitimate implementation and reading-in, not whether mathematical rules are present.

Rule transformations can execute a directly preset object path, and can also undertake transformations that let history rewrite the medium. The two kinds of rule execution can be equally deterministic in algorithmic form; the difference lies in what they actually bear: whether they directly give the successor of the simulated object, or change the sedimentable medium so that history enters the successor. The latter kind of computation is not confined to simple local counting. Training aimed at reducing error can use algorithms such as backpropagation. An objective function gives an evaluation of error from the relation among samples, outputs and targets; backpropagation computes the corresponding gradients, and the actual update must further be carried into the weights through a learning rate, an optimization rule and the host's execution. If the field state left by the update is read in in later question-answering and produces a difference in Continuation, earlier samples, answers and evaluations may change the successor path by way of history. "Rerouting" here is a substantive change in the conditions on which the successor depends; it does not require a visibly different answer every time. This is the positive mechanism by which rule execution changes the sedimentable medium and lets past question-answering affect later question-answering through the conditions of the weights. Reducing error is the purpose of the adjustment, the update algorithm is the method of transformation, the weights are the field state that is adjusted and made available for later use, and historical rerouting is an actual consequence that may appear; none of the four substitutes for another. What must be verified separately here is: that the update has actually been implemented, that the difference is legitimately retained, that the successor actually reads in the relevant difference, and that this reading-in actually plays a part in the declared generative relation. A fall in error is not substitute evidence for these four, and the holding of the four does not directly certify the object qualification of a question-answer law.

If an object rule directly and wholly determines the successor at some node, so that the corresponding field state no longer obtains its due duty of being read in, that node may remain at rule simulation; but where a rule merely biases the run, it should not be judged, without examination, to strip the dynamics away. The presence of parameter updates, input changes or loops in a program text cannot by itself prove that generation has occurred.

V　Basic Rules, Dynamical Conditions and Actual Dynamics

Basic rules, dynamical conditions and actual dynamics are three determinations within the running of one and the same host that may not be confused: rules prescribe how Trace-Response occurs, conditions are the formed field states available to be drawn on this time, and dynamics is the action and transformation that actually occur. They are related to the same event, yet are not made identical by that relation.

Basic rules prescribe how signals and media within the declared field undergo Trace-Response under particular conditions, and how structures, relations and state transformations arise. Within one and the same declared mechanism whose field specification stays unchanged, such rules differ from field states that change with history; training has its own objectives and rules of execution, but the two kinds of rule can be actually linked through legitimate transformations of the same host, and they cannot be asserted to be mutually isolated merely because they are classified differently. A computing host implements these transformations by executing mathematical rules; a non-computing host implements them by the material action of its own materials. One simple realization of a field may deepen or shallow its grooves by the number of passages within a time window, but this is only an example of realization, not a general definition of every Sedimentation-Field.

Error reduction gives the target of adjustment, algorithms such as backpropagation compute the amount of update, and the computing host actually accomplishes the change of weights. Within relations of field-induction, the training method guides which histories come to be formed; the change of weights actually written is a transformation of the host, which may change the dynamical conditions read in by the successor. What must be distinguished here is the duty of the rules, rather than arbitrarily assigning placement in layers by the names of algorithms: the basic rules of the Sedimentation-Field prescribe Trace-Response and state change within the field, while the rules used in training prescribe the concrete method of guidance or update; in one realization the two may be connected through the same device and chain of transformations. Whether an adjustment of weights can obtain the qualification of historical formation within the field depends on whether that adjustment belongs to the legitimate relations of formation of the declared field, and on subsequent retention, reading-in and actual action; it can neither be denied because it uses gradient updates nor affirmed directly because a write has indeed taken place. Time-window counting is only a particular model of Trace-Response, not a general condition.

Dynamical conditions are the field states that can actually take part in the successor, including connections, paths, permeability, weights, initially non-uniform organization and changes retained through history. In a given run it must further be discerned which parts of them are actually read in. A quantity computed temporarily and not used for holding or for later reading-in does not become a sedimentary state because it took part in the computation. Organization already stored in the field but not read in this time cannot pass itself off as dynamical action actually exerted this time. But "not currently acting" does not mean "formed organization has lost its qualification"; the conditions of holding and re-entry are to be determined separately.

Actual dynamics is the action and transformation really occurring between signals and the host's medium under present conditions. Here one must tell material transformation in general apart from transformation that has obtained a duty of historical dynamics: the former is a fact of occurrence, while the latter further requires that the corresponding sedimentable history actually bear Continuation in legitimate execution. Rules prescribe the relation of implementation of transformations, dynamical conditions give the formed field states now able to take part, and dynamics is the action now occurring; one cannot infer directly from one real transformation that history has become dynamics. A legitimate update of weights by a computing device is a real transformation implemented by computation; whether this update can become a link of historical dynamics depends on whether its field state is held, read in and bears the successor, not on whether it uses counting, gradients or some other mathematical operator.

In one sentence: whether rule transformation gives rise to a transformation with a duty of historical dynamics, and whether that transformation with a duty of historical dynamics acts back upon rule transformation — this is the measure for weighing and telling apart whether dynamical transformation has occurred.

The spikes, membranes, ions and transmitter action of biological nervous systems, and the signals, connections, weights and mathematical operators of artificial networks, can be compared with respect to these duties; this does not license the assertion that the two materials or concrete mechanisms are wholly the same, but both are host media of generative dynamics.

VI　The Initial Sedimentation-Field, Potential and Historical Continuation

The starting point of a Sedimentation-Field is not required to be uniform, flat or of zero potential. Terrain has its heights and hollows from the outset, and water is already guided by the slope the first time it arrives; biological nervous systems and artificial networks alike may enter running with non-uniform initial organization. The initial state affects the first division of flow, and later flows can in turn change the existing division. The initial potential is neither the same as the potential later formed by history nor bound to determine the successor for ever.

Take a network of channels as an example: where water passes through channels it may erode, deepen or break open passages, and where it seldom passes channels may silt up or shallow; the altered network of channels then acts on the next flow of water. Frequently passed passages form a tendency to be passed more easily, and history may thus become an actual dynamical condition of what follows. This is a schematic of relations, not a direct transplantation of the law of erosion of water channels as a uniform rule for all neurons, weights or materials. The actual directions of deepening and shallowing, the time windows, the saturation relations and the mechanisms of holding must be determined according to the concrete medium.

Changes in a Sedimentation-Field may include Trace-Response, sedimentation, holding-tendency, flux, back-erosion and rerouting, shallowing, intervention and competition, holding and recovery. Law-dynamics, the counter-hold of Principle, Probability-dynamics and Intention-dynamics may also actually occur in the field, but they involve formed objects and their qualifications, and should not be ranked on the same level as the most basic Trace-Response of the medium, as "direct products of the host."

VII　The Host Layer, the Dynamics Layer and the Object Layer

One and the same actual system can be distinguished, by the duties it bears, into a host layer, a dynamics layer and an object layer. The host layer bears the material execution of legitimate transformations; the dynamics layer treats how conditions formed by history enter subsequent actual occurrence; the object layer treats the qualification and action of laws, and of objects such as Principle, Probability and Intention that hold in the corresponding structures. The three layers here are layers of duty within the generative relations declared by the Computation Group-Form; they are not an exhaustive classification of all that exists, still less do they mean that every run must have all three layers at once; a legitimate case that bears only historical dynamics and as yet has no law-object can remain in the first two layers. They are not three sets of matter cut off from one another, but layers of one and the same run under different questions.

In a computing host, computation really executes state change, and can also take part directly, within the present causal chain, in the actual Continuation of objects of an upper layer. But "taking part in execution" is not "bearing object qualification": the rules, computational power, number of instructions or output on the computation side cannot substitute for the formed structure of a law and the conditions of its actual action. Computation can give dynamical generation a host to bear it, but cannot on that account guarantee that a law has been generated. Dynamics without law qualification can also keep running.

A law, as formed organization, can exercise a holding-tendency and run again. A Principle must first hold as a law in its own duty, and then obtain counter-hold qualification relative to a target law, a relation domain and the same formed duty; Principle is not a basic object generated alongside laws without first becoming a law. The counter-hold of a Principle does not mean eliminating the target law, nor does it guarantee that the Principle itself prevails, that the successor is bound to reroute, or that a new law is bound to occur. Probability and Intention are likewise to be determined according to the qualifications and dynamical conditions of their respective group forms, and they do not lose their own structural differences because they contain the organization of a law.

Objects form in dynamics, and objects that have obtained a qualification of current action can in turn influence later dynamics through actual action within the field. This back-and-forth is not objects issuing commands apart from their material host. The formation of an object, the current action of the object and the concrete consequences to which that action leads are to be certified separately. The Observer's identification of an object also differs from the fact of the object's formation within the field; not being identified by an Observer does not mean that there is no dynamics or object formation at all in that field.

VIII　Computed Out, Run Out and Generated

"Computed out" stresses obtaining the corresponding result or final state under rule relations already laid down. "Run out" stresses executing step by step along the object's rules already laid down, so that the motion described presents a trajectory, a continuous picture or an observable effect. Both can have real device implementation, yet neither naturally turns into dynamical generation by having one step or many.

"Generated" stresses that the host, through legitimate execution, lets sedimentable history be formed or legitimately taken over and, in actual reading-in, constrain the successor; under further conditions of formation and qualification, new dynamical organization and objects may appear. It is not made a general requirement here that the present segment must write history anew, that every step must rewrite weights, or that every segment must give birth to a new law. Generative computation itself does not require that every segment give birth to a new law; existing dynamical organization that continues to act in a legitimate successor is likewise to be examined according to the corresponding working domain.

The three expressions distinguish duties of implementation and generative relations; they are not three mutually incompatible kinds of machine. A generative process contains rule transformations; a simulation may also have complex running and very high output precision. The basis of judgement is the concrete mechanism of formation, not the name, the number of steps or the expressiveness.

IX　Biological Nervous Systems, Artificial Neural Networks and GPT

Biological neural tissue realizes Trace-Response, sedimentation and successor response through its own material action, and can at the same time exercise computational capacity; one cannot conversely hold that all the dynamics of the brain is produced by computation. An artificial neural network constructs an executable system out of mathematized signals, connections, weights and relations of operation; its computing device really implements state transformations, and can also bear the updating and reading-in of dynamical conditions.

In an artificial network that uses error-driven training, input samples, model outputs and training targets form an evaluation of error through a loss function, backpropagation computes gradients, an optimization step fixes the amount of update, and the computing device actually rewrites the weights; only when the changed weights are legitimately retained and actually read in by later question-answering can earlier question-answering and evaluation affect the successor path through the historical field state. Not all training uses backpropagation, nor does every stretch of inference update weights in real time. Update, retention, reading-in and contribution to Continuation should each have their own evidence from running; if a legitimate link of bearing among them is missing, it cannot be judged, merely because the parameters once changed, that history has become dynamics. Even if this dynamical duty holds, whether a concrete question-answer law has formed remains an independent question of qualification at the object layer. The core here is that experiences of question-answering change, in history, the field state available to be read in; it is not required that the weights change only by local counting of the "frequent flow deepens" kind. How the update algorithm corresponds to formation, retention, reading-in and object qualification must still be verified in the particular realization.

Weights frozen after training can continue to be read as dynamical conditions formed by history; no longer being rewritten does not mean that earlier history no longer acts on the current run. In the same system, historical formation and initial weights legitimately moved in also differ in source: moving in can legitimately take over existing organization, but taking over cannot be written up as formation by the present run segment itself. Generative computation, current dynamics, formed law qualification and the present action of a law also do not require that a new write or a new law occur at every step.

The feedforward operations, matrix transformations and inference computation of GPT are indeed implementations by the host. Whether, concretely, its historical weights, current reading-in and the organization of question-answering constitute generative dynamics and a formed question-answer law must be determined under the conditions of the corresponding group forms. A question-answer law cannot be inferred directly from computational power, the number of parameters or fluent answers; nor can the possibility of dynamical formation be denied merely because mathematical computation takes part in weight updates or every step is executed by rules.

X　Field-Induction, Training, Legitimate Taking-Over and Intrusion

Field-induction is the relation of method that, by means of the actual relations of action of the field, guides the occurrence of history and modulates formed or later-formed potential. Training is one concrete method of field-induction, not another kind of ontological mechanism alongside field-induction; other possible methods of field-induction are not excluded because training is discussed here. Guidance carried out through input, targets, feedback, attraction and repulsion, flow paths or conditions of the medium may adopt different technical routes; which method is adopted, and whether qualification of dynamics or of objects has been obtained within the field, must be judged separately. The exact qualification of field-induction and the boundaries of its methods remain governed by the dedicated form-text on it, and cannot be replaced by any machine-learning algorithm or by the example of channel networks.

Error-driven training is an instance of field-induction: the training target specifies which error is to be reduced, the algorithm fixes the weight update, the host executes the transformation, and the changed field state is legitimately saved and made available for later reading-in; thereby past question-answering and evaluation may affect the path of later question-answering through the field state. The training target cannot substitute for the actual update, the parameter update cannot substitute for retention and reading-in, and the appearance of rerouting cannot substitute for certification that a law holds. Using the name "training rule" neither cancels its actual role of execution in a computing host nor automatically gives every item of training a qualification of generation within the field.

"What is often passed deepens; what is seldom passed shallows" describes one class of sedimentation model; it is neither the definition of all Sedimentation-Fields nor the only way of updating that field-induction can adopt. Error-driven weight updates can change actual parameters, so that earlier question-answering and evaluation change successor conditions through legitimate history; such implementation is not the same as pre-writing an object rule that wholly determines the successor of question-answering. Whether it constitutes the generation of history within the field, and whether it obtains a dynamical duty, must still be certified according to the actual chain of formation and reading-in of that field. How exactly its concrete operation enters the legitimate chain of formation of the field should be checked against the host rules declared in advance, the temporal position in running and the source of history; a weight-update mechanism should not be excluded in advance by a classificatory name.

The initial field state may be non-uniform; preloaded, copied or moved-in weights can, under legitimate conditions, serve as the starting point of a run. This differs from field-induction or other intervention actually occurring during running. Directly modifying the field state during running likewise requires discerning whether it belongs to the declared legitimate action, whether the required chain of generation has been preserved, and whether the corresponding duty of historical reading-in has been cancelled. The judgement of intrusion cannot rest only on "whether a value was written from outside," nor can the legitimacy of training rest only on "declaration in advance" in place of verification of the actual mechanism.

Directly presetting at the object layer a rule that wholly determines the next step, and changing the paths that can later be taken by way of weights and conditions of the medium, bear different generative duties. The former may make the reading-in of the field state at that point lose its effect; the latter can let historical change actually take part in the successor. Whether a difference caused by external rewriting causes distortion, and whether it leaves consequences, must still be judged by the concrete history, target and evidence from running; intrusion cannot be held unconditionally to be bound to distort.

Field-induction and intrusion cannot be told apart only by whether weights are modified. For the same difference in weights, it must be verified when it was written, by whom it was executed, within what declared field and boundary of running it was written, whether it belongs to taking-over at the starting point or to a change en route, and whether it continues to be read through the corresponding history. Declared rules can delimit attribution and permission, while the actual chain of generation determines whether the corresponding dynamical duty is obtained; neither can substitute for the other.

XI　Reproduction, Simulation, Distortion and Source

When relations in A enter P through the Observer's identification and purification, actions that were not included in the model but were actually drawn on by A's later motion may be omitted. Differences between P and A in medium, environment, structure, initial potential, signal expression and historical formation may all produce deviation. The clusters of Causal-Response occurring continuously in the source system may contain relations of action that, after the Observer's purification and encoding, did not enter P; the difference between analogue signals and digital representation provides an analysable case of such omission. But a continuous form does not guarantee completeness of Causal-Response, nor is digital representation bound to omit all relevant action; which relations really affect the successor must be checked against the representation, running and targets of A and P.

Simulating step by step with object rules may reproduce visible trajectories with high precision without reproducing A's historical dynamics. Rebuilding the dynamics in P by a sedimentation mechanism likewise does not guarantee that all relations of the source system can be replicated. Both routes must face the target of reproduction, the permitted differences and the actual history. The extraction and purification of rules, and external intrusion, may all become sources of distortion, and may all, under concrete conditions, produce no distortion.

Distortion must be judged relative to a target specified in advance, a legitimate control and the corresponding time window; differences in Continuation trajectory, differences in declared law qualification and differences in performance effects are not by nature one and the same verdict. If a difference has dissipated before it really takes part in Continuation, it does not necessarily change the target; if a difference falls on the part that bears the law, it may affect the law's qualification without changing the immediate readings of Continuation. After digital purification omits clusters of Causal-Response of the source system, the difference may propagate through historical reading-in and rewriting, and thereby widen the contrast between A and P; but "omission necessarily constitutes distortion" and "the contrast necessarily widens" hold only under conditions such as that the omitted component really enters the chain of successor action of the declared target, and a mechanism that may propagate cannot be written unconditionally as a conclusion about all digital reproduction. A difference may widen, narrow, cancel out or be cleared away by later running; one cannot assert unconditionally that "digital reproduction necessarily distorts" or that "the longer it runs, the more the contrast necessarily widens." Where the source system cannot be fully represented in the reproducing system, some attributions of source may themselves lack a determinable control.

Correcting distortion may involve signal encoding, medium structure, connections, initial states, training regimes and dynamical conditions. If the error comes from an object rule that bypasses the field state and wholly specifies the successor, adjusting the field state alone may be ineffective; if it arises from a difference in sedimentable dynamical conditions, retraining and adjusting historical paths may be effective. Which adjustment can align with the source system is decided by the concrete intervention and evidence from the successor.

XII　The Explanatory Boundary of Computation for Complex Systems

The interaction of objects and dynamics makes a complex system more than the mechanical unfolding of a set of predetermined object rules. Dynamical conditions are formed by history, and formed objects may in turn, in later actual action, change holding direction, counter-hold, rerouting and the environment of formation. Looking from outside only at computational rules, computational power, the number of program steps, outputs and current variables, one may be unable to determine uniquely from them the qualifications of dynamics and objects.

The back-and-forth relation in which object action returns to dynamics and the history of dynamics in turn changes the conditions of object formation constitutes the original theoretical claim that the future of occurrence is hard to grasp fully from object rules alone. What it stresses is that object rules are not the same as the whole of the dynamical conditions currently in force, and that appearances on the computation side do not suffice to substitute for the real source of objects. This original view concerns the back-and-forth relation between dynamics and object action, and the part of it that is stronger than the concrete working domains already proved remains a proposition for further research. At the same time, the conclusion that object qualification cannot be inferred directly from the computation side does not mean that all complex systems are in principle unsimulable, unpredictable or uncontrollable. Given a sufficiently complete system state, mechanism and legitimate observation protocol, some behaviour can still be predicted, reproduced or controlled; what cannot be crossed is the direct inference, from finite computational appearances, of the corresponding history of generation within the field and of object qualification. Whether a stronger identifiability exists must be examined in a concrete formal model.

The role of computation in complex systems is not shrunk by the distinction of levels into an irrelevant low-level operation. In a computing host, legitimate transformations are really implemented by computation, and computation can take part directly in the causal realization of the current event; but the causal participation of execution and the bearing of the qualification of a law, Principle, Probability or Intention are two kinds of judgement. The scale of computational power can change computational resources, the state space that can be run and the efficiency of execution, but cannot by itself serve as proof that an object holds.

XIII　The Theoretical Objects and the Engineering Interfaces

The Computation Group-Form studies mathematical transformation on hosts, the bearing of tasks, actual execution, generative computation, historical reading-in, layering, reproduction, and determination and distinction. The Law Group-Form treats the qualification of forming a law; the Law-Dynamics Group-Form treats the current action, cross-time dynamics, interaction and holding of formed laws; the Principle Group-Form studies the counter-hold qualification of a formed law relative to a target law, and its action; the Probability and Intention Group-Forms each take on their own structure, running and review. Between them there are upstream and downstream interfaces and results that can check one another, but the theorems of any one group cannot become proofs for another group merely by conceptual proximity. The ontological questions of computation also include the source of Trace-Response relations, the relation between thinking and reasoning, the routes by which field-induction is realized, the reproduction of continuous clusters of Causal-Response and the back-and-forth action between objects and dynamics. These questions connect with the computational theorems already established, and each also has room for further development.

The Field-Concretization Group-Form and the Field-Induction Group-Form are both closely related to the Computation Group-Form. Field concretization studies the concrete realization of Sedimentation-Fields, distinguishing different hosts and the kinds of dynamics they realize, including forms of realization such as single-celled organisms, animal brains and computing devices, and discerning their respective signals, media, Trace-Response relations, field states and modes in which dynamics occurs.

The Field-Induction Group-Form, on the basis of the realization of the corresponding Sedimentation-Field, guides the occurrence of history and the formation of holding-tendency through concrete methods including training, changing the dynamical conditions faced by later signal flow.

The Computation Group-Form explains how a computing host actually implements transformations and how it lets history bear dynamical duties, and distinguishes computing hosts from non-computing hosts.

Field concretization explains how different hosts realize Sedimentation-Fields and their dynamics; field-induction explains how history and holding-tendency are guided within an existing field mechanism. The three are connected through the actual relations of signals, media, field states, Trace-Response and legitimate Continuation.

XIV　The Ontological Position of Computation

Computation is the actual execution of mathematical state transformation on a device host, and is not the same as pure form or pure mathematics. Rule computation can obtain results and simulated trajectories according to given relations; generative computation, on the basis of legitimate implementation, has sedimentable history, actually formed or legitimately taken over, bear the corresponding dynamical duty in later reading-in. In a computing host, computation drives actual implementation; sedimentary dynamics and the history of its formation belong to the duty of the next layer; laws, and objects beyond them such as Principle, Probability and Intention, must satisfy their own conditions of holding.

That computation effects actual transformations on the host does not mean that it directly obtains object qualification; that dynamics is formed by history in the host does not guarantee that objects must appear; that an object already holds does not mean that it must currently be in action or must change the successor. Conversely, the execution of computation remains the material bearing on which these actual changes in a computing host depend.

Separating computational implementation, historical dynamics and object qualification avoids attributing complex intelligent performance directly to computational power, and also avoids setting real generation and computational implementation in sharp opposition. The role of computation at the host layer, the formation of dynamics in history and the holding of objects in further relations constitute three levels that carry on from one another yet may not be confused.

## Section One　Why the Thirteen Chapters Must Compose the Computation Group-Form

What the thirteen chapters study is one and the same object axis: computation, and its standing, bearing, execution, generation, layering, reproduction and identification. The actual implementation of one mathematical transformation on a physical host cannot be replaced by a legitimate formula on paper or by a result that happens to coincide; nor can the bearing of a given task be replaced by one actual execution. ## Section Three　The Five-Zone Structure

Zone One: standing and implementation (Chapters 1–4). Chapter 1 unfolds the two duties in the definition of computation — the legitimacy of the mathematical transformation and the actual driving by the physical host — onto one and the same event and one and the same state correspondence; Chapter 2 takes a task given in advance as its object and forces out the necessary structure of bearing, such as legitimacy of the reading scheme, input reachability and production by the host itself; Chapter 3, on the antecedent of bearing, forces out the witness of one and the same execution — initiation, enablement, no proxy evaluation, bearing of the actual transformation and read-out (the two clauses of Ex carrying H3 of Chapter 3); Chapter 4 gives the formal system of bearing and execution in a discrete local dynamical model. Chapter 1 is adjacent to Chapters 2 and 3 but is not merged with them: the question of the identity of one computation cannot be settled automatically by the bearing or the exercise of a task alone.

Zone Two: generation and layering (Chapters 5–8). Chapter 5, starting from the bearing of a one-step task and successive executions, gives the necessary structure of generative computation under the working domain, H and upstream antecedents listed in that chapter; Chapter 6 builds these into a formal model and counterfactual variational readings; Chapter 7, under its several declared working domains and antecedents, distinguishes the duties of S and D when history becomes dynamics, and analyses how the Continuation of a law further involves the object layer O; the criteria of Chapter 5's seven items are cited there as inherited items of proof, its general theorem not serving as an antecedent; the declaration of the exact minimum number of layers carries further conditions; Chapter 8, under declared conditions such as a medium that is a finite simple directed graph and the Trace-Response rule class, together with H3 of Chapter 3, proves respectively the existence of a witness of generation and the necessity of generation within any run segment containing the witness configuration. From Chapter 5 to Chapter 8 what is obtained advances from the necessary direction to a conditional sufficient direction, but does not constitute a general unconditional sufficiency theorem for all media.

Zone Three: cross-layer dissolution (Chapter 9). It routes the inferences "computation directly computes out the law," "computational power determines the object," "every step is a rule, so it is not generation," "dynamics is nothing but program state" and "where there is dynamics there is law" back to their respective layers; it retains the fact that a computing host executes transformations at the layer of realization, while not letting quantities obtainable on the computation side substitute for dynamical or law qualification (restricted to 𝒲₈; for the conditions carried by each branch see Section Eight).

Zone Four: sources of reproduction (Chapter 10). For reproduction distortion that has actually occurred, it examines separately the chains of formation by which a rule extraction does not follow the source dynamics, and by which intrusion and purification are retained in the host's history and change the successor; a source is neither always present nor necessarily unique, and sameness of effect in output does not automatically give uniqueness of source (restricted to 𝒲₉, carrying H-SRC1–3; see Section Eight).

Zone Five: determination and distinction (Chapters 11–13). Chapter 11 treats three-valued official verdicts on four properties; Chapter 12 distinguishes a host candidate C whose mathematical Trace-Response relations can be reset from a candidate N without that pathway of setting; Chapter 13 distinguishes two kinds of run: those in which historical conditions read in by what follows are formed by execution within the segment, and those in which they are not so formed within the segment. Determination, distinction of host type and distinction of formation within the segment are three different questions, and none of them is replaced by an observation made for either of the other two.

The five zones are not a chain of generation in five consecutive steps. Chapters 9 and 10 are different directions of review; Chapters 12 and 13 are different axes of distinction; chapter order is not the same as the necessary premises of proof.

## Section Five　One Computation, Bearing, Execution and Formal Read-Back

Chapter 1 gives the necessary structure of one computation at the event level:

Compₙ(𝔇,e,T) ⇒ N(𝔇,e,T)  
N ≡ e∈Evt(𝔇) ∧ ∃ρₑ\[Mₙ(𝔇,e,T;ρₑ) ∧ Dₙ(𝔇,e,T;ρₑ)\]

Mₙ expresses that the mathematical state transformation is legitimate, and Dₙ that the host actually drives and implements that transformation in the event; the two share the host, the event, the operator and the state correspondence. Chapter 1 shows, under the inheritance reading of H1, that Dₙ implies Mₙ, so that the conjunction can logically absorb Mₙ; but the two conceptual duties are not thereby merged. The necessary structure is an analysis of the initial stipulation of computation, and cannot be written up as an independent physical criterion proved to be "globally simplest."

Chapter 2 gives the necessary direction of bearing:

Bear(P,τ) ⇒ Leg(P,τ) ∧ Prov(P,τ) ∧ Prod(P,τ)  
N\_bear = Prov ∧ Prod

This necessary direction holds relative to the antecedents A1–A5 of the concept of bearing. Leg is the legitimacy condition that the reading schemes used in a claim of bearing must satisfy (Leg₁: types marked out in advance; Leg₂: the reading schemes do not evaluate by proxy); Prov says that every operand in the domain of definition has an executable declared delivery protocol; Prod says that, for every admissible execution of the delivery protocol, under the declared operating conditions, there is no external proxy evaluation during or after delivery, and the family of results produced by the host's own dynamics satisfies the acceptance protocol. This formula does not prove the sufficient direction; the determination of concrete legitimate reading schemes involves H1–H3 of that chapter.

Chapter 3 gives, on the antecedent that Bear holds, the necessary structure of one execution:

Exec(P,τ,T) ⇒ ∃r⊆T\[Ini(r) ∧ Run(r) ∧ Out(r)\]  
Run = En ∧ ¬Sub ∧ Ex\_α ∧ Ex\_β

This necessary direction holds relative to the antecedents E1–E6 of the concept of execution and the counting convention. Ex\_β for trivial tasks is treated as that chapter prescribes; the necessity of the two clauses of Ex carries H3 of Chapter 3, while start and stop, enablement, the same run and discrimination further involve H1 and H2 of that chapter and the H group of Chapter 2. Execution does not require that the result be computed correctly, still less can one infer back from it that the host as a whole is a "computing host." The formal predicates of Chapter 4's S\_Comp⟼M\_Comp take values in {1,0,⊥}; a true formal image gives only a reading of necessary structure in the declared model, provides no sufficiency of bearing or execution, and does not guarantee sufficient correspondence between model and real thing.

12.3 Scope of Registration of Member Fingerprints

The member body texts do not carry their own hashes. The full-text SHA-256 values of the thirteen chapters registered in Section Two are taken from the SHA256SUMS of the sealed package, and the SHA-256 of the Collection is likewise taken from that file; all of them are fingerprints of the sealed Chinese texts. The SHA-256/md5 values seen in the body texts of Chapters 7 to 13 refer to local files such as program files, standard output and run products, and do not serve as fingerprints of member form-texts. The independently frozen upstream originals of flb.py, collected in the appendices of Chapters 7–11 (with ljudge.py also collected in Chapters 7, 9 and 10), have not been obtained, and byte-level source certification is not complete: this is a gap in source evidence, and it does not affect the program-reproduction results of these chapters (Chapter 7 L1684).
