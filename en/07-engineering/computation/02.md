# The General Form of Computational Bearing

This chapter attempts a general proposition:

For a mathematical operation task τ given in advance, what structure must a host P minimally have in order to bear that task?

On this group form's initial stipulation of computation (working domain, item one), the essence of computation is that an input passes through a state, a transformation and again a state, and so on in sequence until the output; and this process is restricted to mathematical operations driven by a device. Computation transforms given structures and relations and obtains new structures and relations. For a mathematical operation to be done by a host, it must first be possible to hand it to the host, and then the host must carry it out by itself. What this chapter asks about is precisely the minimal structure of this: when a mathematical task given in advance is borne by a physical system, what must that system have?

Bearing, in this chapter, is a host's structural qualification for a task — a capacity or disposition. A calculator that bears addition still bears addition when it is switched off; it is simply not executing at the moment. Bearing does not require execution at the moment; present execution is another question (The Computation Group-Form, Chapter 3, *The General Form of Computational Execution*).

What is to be proved is the necessary direction:

Bear(P, τ) ⇒ Leg(P, τ) ∧ N\_bear(P, τ)

where Leg is the legitimacy condition that the reading schemes used in a claim of bearing must satisfy; it acts on the reading schemes and adds no property to the host. N\_bear is the structure the host itself must have.

Bearing is a host's structural qualification for a task, not a mark of computation; how it corresponds to the single computation Compₙ of *The General Form of Computation* (the bridging of event identity, host correspondence and attribution) is unanswered within the group. That a system bears some task does not thereby make it a computing host, nor does it mean that the system is executing that task at the moment; neither of these enters the necessary structure of bearing.

In this chapter we shall find, through persistent questioning, where the proof must go, and derive the general form of computational bearing.

Our objective is:

to take a task τ given in advance as the anchor and, under the three-layer stratification of host, dynamics and object, to obtain the minimal structure of a host's bearing a mathematical task, so that the mathematical operation in the definition of computation has a bearer that can be checked; to make this structure apply uniformly to every kind of bearing — exact and approximate, deterministic and stochastic, identity and constant, single-machine and distributed, sequential input and variable configuration, bearing that depends on external maintenance and bearing that depends on an external random source; and to separate bearing from description, bearing from execution, bearing from correctness and bearing from algorithm.

The result first.

The general form of the minimal structure of computational bearing

N\_bear(P, τ) = Prov(P, τ) ∧ Prod(P, τ)

Alongside it stands the legitimacy condition Leg that the claim of bearing itself must satisfy: the reading schemes of τ are defined on state types of the host marked out in advance (Leg₁), and in no input or output do the reading schemes evaluate on the host's behalf (Leg₂). Leg is likewise proved necessary in this chapter; it acts on the reading schemes and is not counted as part of the host's structure. The host's boundary, its entry and exit channels, its delivery protocols and its operating conditions must already have been declared.

That is: for every operand in the domain, there is a delivery protocol that hands it to the host through a declared entry and can be executed (Prov, input reachability); and, when delivery follows the declared delivery protocol — the protocol's own randomness being counted into the result, and however the choices the protocol leaves to the deliverer are made — the results produced by the host's own dynamics under the declared operating conditions, with no external process evaluating on the host's behalf after delivery, satisfy, as a family of results over the whole domain, the acceptance protocol declared by the task (Prod, self-production).

General Theorem (preview): relative to the antecedents A1–A5 of the concept of bearing, if a host bears a mathematical task given in advance, then necessarily its reading schemes satisfy Leg, every operand of the task can be delivered to the host by a declared protocol, and, for every admissible execution of the protocol, the host's own dynamics, with no external proxy evaluation, produces a family of results that satisfies the acceptance protocol. Whether the reading schemes are defined on state types marked out in advance, and whether they evaluate on the host's behalf in input and output, decide whether a claim of bearing has content; a task has the extension of its function as its identity, and the necessary structure of bearing contains no algorithm, except in the determination of "partial proxy evaluation"; for a non-trivial task, bearing implies counterfactual dependence of the result on delivery.

In One Sentence

To bear a mathematical task is, at the least, for every problem to be deliverable to the host by the rules, for no one else to compute on its behalf once the problem is delivered, and for the host itself to bring the answer to the standard the task requires.

Abstract

On this group form's initial stipulation of computation: the essence of computation is the passage from an input through a state, a transformation and again a state, and so on in sequence until the output, restricted to mathematical operations driven by a device; computation transforms given structures and relations and obtains new structures and relations, structures and relations being the whole expression of a state; two kinds of host of computation are known at present, computing devices and the brain when exercising the capacity to compute; and a law that has only a formal and mathematical description, without a host, is a hostless law.

Taking a mathematical task given in advance as its anchor, this chapter asks what, at the least, a physical system must have in order to bear that task. One antecedent runs through the whole inquiry: bearing requires that the evaluation of f be actually produced by the host itself (A1); the claim of bearing extends to the task as a whole (A4), and is made relative to declarations given before the runs (A5). Consequently, any arrangement that moves the work of evaluation outside the host empties "bearing" of content. A heating stone, paired with reading schemes assigned run by run, agrees with any task; so the reading schemes must be defined on state types marked out before, and independently of, the runs under test. A wire, paired with reading schemes that write the answer into the operand, also agrees with any task; so in no input or output may the reading schemes evaluate on the host's behalf — though they may be designed with the task in view. These two items are legitimacy conditions on the claim of bearing; they too are necessary conditions of bearing, and they act on the reading schemes, not on the host. A device whose noise happens to land on the right value, and a record that is correct for a single sample, still agree with the task under legitimate reading schemes; so the whole set of operands must be covered. The solar system satisfies coverage under the reading schemes of celestial mechanics, yet operands cannot be handed to it, and an adder with a broken key cannot be given operands containing a certain digit; so input reachability is required. A black box concealing a proxy evaluator satisfies coverage after delivery, yet the results are produced outside the box; while heat dissipation, clocks, external random sources and other external actions really do take part in the host's running without evaluating anything. What is required, therefore, is not "external actions must not vary with the input," but that, under the declared operating conditions, no external action evaluate on the host's behalf after delivery. If all that were required were that some delivery exist under which the results pass, a deliverer could pick, according to the answer, the microstate that happens to evolve to the correct result, and so make any noisy device bear any task; so delivery must follow a declared protocol, the protocol's randomness being counted into the result, and the results must pass however the unspecified choices that the protocol leaves to the deliverer are made. "The result must depend on the input" is not a necessary condition; for non-trivial tasks it follows as a lemma. "The result must be readable" is not set up separately; the acceptance protocol already decides the cases in which the read-out is ⊥. The original question thereby closes.

The general theorem of this chapter is in the necessary direction. The formal criteria for state types marked out in advance, and the formal criteria for "no proxy evaluation" at the three places of reading scheme, delivery and external action, are listed as Computational Bearing General Form-H1, H2 and H3; their verification text is The Computation Group-Form, Chapter 4, *The Mathematical Form of Computation*. They support determinations on specific claims; they do not support the reasoning of the general theorem. Whether a host is a computing host, whether it is executing at the moment, and whether some particular result is correct do not enter the necessary structure of bearing.

Degree of Answer

First edition. The initial question with which the general proposition enters the proof is this: on the initial stipulation of computation, the antecedents A1–A5 of the concept of bearing, a task τ given in advance and the task-side non-degeneracy condition alone, does "the evolution of the host agrees with the task" already amount to bearing? The subquestions actually generated in the course of the inquiry, and the standing of their answers, are as follows.

First: once the reading schemes are declared, does bearing already have content? Proved: it further requires that the reading schemes be defined on state types marked out in advance (Round One), and that in input and output the reading schemes not evaluate on the host's behalf (Round Two). The two are legitimacy conditions on the claim of bearing; their formal criteria are conditional (H1, H2), and the independent indispensability of the two is conditional on H2 (Section Eight).  
Second: under legitimate reading schemes, does agreement of individual runs with the task amount to bearing? Proved: coverage of all operands is further required (Round Three).  
Third: does coverage amount to bearing? Proved: input reachability is further required (Round Four).  
Fourth: do reachability and coverage amount to bearing? Proved: coverage must hold under the declared operating conditions and with no external proxy evaluation after delivery; external actions that vary with the input, that cannot be replaced by a fixed action, that bring the host up to standard only under one particular mode of maintenance, or that affect the result are all compatible with bearing, provided they do not evaluate f (Round Five). The formal criterion for external proxy evaluation is conditional (H3).  
Fifth: for which deliveries does coverage hold? Proved: delivery follows the declared protocol; the random components of the protocol are counted into the result distribution, and reliability is measured according to Π on this distribution; for the unspecified choices that the protocol leaves to the deliverer, the results must pass however those choices are made (Round Six). The formal criterion for proxy evaluation in delivery is conditional (H2).  
Sixth: must dependence of the result on the input, and readability of the result, be set up separately? Proved: the former is not a necessary condition, and for tasks that are not Π-trivial it follows as a lemma; the latter is not set up separately, and Prod needs no readability requirement beyond the acceptance protocol (Round Seven).  
Seventh: does bearing fix an algorithm, and can bearing be composed? Proved: a task has the extension of its function as its identity; apart from the clause on "partial evaluation" in the criterion of proxy evaluation, which is determined relative to some evaluation decomposition, N\_bear depends only on the values of the task function on the domain and contains no algorithmic component (Corollary One); the criterion for partial evaluation that does not depend on a decomposition is conditional (H2, H3). When the reading schemes at both ends are legitimate and N\_bear holds, Leg and N\_bear are closed under composition under four conditions — the end-point reading schemes do not evaluate on the host's behalf for the composite task, the interface is legitimate, support is covered, and the condition on error propagation and the composite protocol holds; for exact deterministic tasks only the first two are needed (Corollary Two).  
General conclusion: the necessary direction Bear ⇒ Leg ∧ N\_bear is proved relative to the antecedents A1–A5 of the concept of bearing; the determination of Leg and N\_bear on specific claims is conditional on H1–H3; minimality is the indispensability of each of the items forced (Section Eight; for Leg₁ and Leg₂, conditional on H2), and restricted minimality among admissible candidate structures is unanswered; the converse N\_bear ⇒ Bear does not hold (Corollary Five), and Leg ∧ N\_bear ⇒ Bear is unanswered.

Introduction

When people speak of "something computing some function," they often run several things together: whether the function can be used to describe the thing, whether the thing can be used to compute the function, whether it is computing it at the moment, whether this particular computation is correct, and which algorithm it uses. The first is description, the second is bearing, the third is execution, the fourth is correctness, and the fifth is algorithm.

Philosophical discussion in the late twentieth century pointed out long ago that if "computing some function" required only that there exist some reading scheme under which physical states agree with the function, then a stone or a wall would compute every function. The difficulty has not disappeared with the spread of computers; it reappears in another guise in present-day talk about artificial intelligence, where a system is said to have "computed" something as soon as its inputs and outputs seem to match. The initial stipulation restricts computation to mathematical operations driven by a device. For this restriction to have content, it must first be made clear what a physical system needs in order to bear a mathematical task.

That is the question of this chapter.

Preliminary Distinctions

1\. Bearing and some neighbouring matters

Bearing and description. That a process can be described by some equation does not make it bear the task the equation expresses. On the initial stipulation of computation, a law that has only a description and no host is called a hostless law; description is a matter on the Observer's side, bearing a matter on the host's side.

Bearing and execution. Bearing is a qualification; execution is one actual exercise of that qualification. A calculator that bears addition still bears addition when it is switched off.

Bearing and correctness. When an execution goes wrong, the bearer still bears its task; a permanently faulty adder bears another task τ′, or bears addition on a reduced domain.

Bearing and algorithm. One and the same task can be borne by summing circuits, table lookup, mental arithmetic and other means; bearing settles the task only as far as its extension on the domain (the determination of "partial proxy evaluation" requires an evaluation decomposition to be specified; see Corollary One).

2\. Objects and terms

The meanings of the following objects are stated here in advance. These statements only specify the objects; they carry no claim of necessity.

Task τ = (D, f, ρ\_in, ρ\_out, Π): a mathematical operation task given in advance. D is the domain of operands; f : D → V is a mathematical function or transformation on D, taking values in V; in a stochastic task f(d) may be a distribution on V; ρ\_in and ρ\_out are the declared reading schemes, which read states of the host as operands in D and as values in V respectively; Π is the acceptance protocol. τ is this chapter's point of entry into the inquiry, not a criterion for computation to hold: a task can be borne by many mechanisms, and can also be merely described formally.

Acceptance protocol Π: what τ requires of the results, written as an acceptance predicate Acc\_Π on families of results. Given a family of result distributions (μ\_d)\_{d∈D}, where μ\_d is the distribution of results for operand d as read out through ρ\_out (read-outs falling outside the definition of ρ\_out are written ⊥), Acc\_Π((μ\_d)\_{d∈D}, f) decides whether this family satisfies the task. An exact deterministic task requires μ\_d to be concentrated on f(d); a task with reliability requires μ\_d(f(d)) to reach a declared level; an approximate task requires the result to fall, with a declared probability, within a declared tolerance of f(d); a stochastic task requires that the declared distance between μ\_d and the distribution f(d) not exceed a limit; a task that tolerates finitely many errors may state its condition as a condition on the whole family, for example that the pointwise requirement be met except on at most k operands. Pointwise protocols are a special case of family predicates. How strong Π is belongs to the declaration of the task.

Host P: any physical system, its boundary marked out by declaration. P may be declared as one persisting instance, or as a class of systems prepared by a fixed protocol (for example, molecular computation that uses a fresh test tube for each run).

Declared items: the boundary B; the entry E\_in, through which operands are handed to P — it may consist of several channels, may be time-shared, and may be spread over several places; the read-out E\_out, together with the timing or conditions of read-out; the delivery protocols, that is, for each operand d, the procedure by which delivery through E\_in is carried out — a protocol may contain random components, and may also leave some choices to be decided by the deliverer, with no probabilities specified; the operating conditions Env, that is, the external conditions, with their ranges, that P needs in order to operate normally, such as supply voltage, clock frequency, temperature and its stability, mechanical support and shielding, together with the external apparatus that maintains these conditions.

State types: the types into which the physical states of P are divided by physical properties, for example voltage intervals, gear positions, water-level intervals, numeral representations.

Declared period Θ: the period over which bearing is being spoken of. The reading schemes, delivery protocols and operating conditions are fixed within Θ; they may change from one period to another.

Proxy evaluation: in some process of input and output, some link outside the host — a user of the reading schemes, a deliverer or a process acting on the host from outside its boundary — carries out the work of evaluating f on the operand, or part of that work, and whether the result passes depends on that work. Where and why it must be excluded is given by the Line of Argument; its formal criteria are given in H2 and H3.

Working Domain and Boundary of Inheritance

This chapter inherits the following as what is currently known:

First, the initial stipulation of computation. This group form takes the following stipulations about computation as its starting point, using them as initial theoretical conventions that are not proved in this form: the essential chain of computation runs from an input through a state, a transformation and again a state, in sequence until the output; computation is restricted to mathematical operations driven by a device; computation transforms given structures and relations and obtains new structures and relations, structures and relations being the whole expression of a state; computation enables a law to reproduce, within a host, the structures, relations and transformations of field dynamics and of the law; computation, thinking and reasoning are the same in essence, what is special about computation being its restriction to mathematical operations; there is a stratification into host layer, dynamics layer and object layer; the brain is a computing host only when exercising the capacity to compute; reproduction with the same kind of matter and environment (a vortex, for instance) is non-computational reproduction; and a law that has only a formal and mathematical description, without a host, is called a hostless law.

Second, the antecedents of the concept of bearing. Bear(P, τ) is delimited by the following antecedents, relative to which the necessity conclusions below hold:  
　A1 (actual production)　For P to bear τ, the evaluation of f must be actually produced by P, within the declared boundary, by its own dynamics; a merely coincidental mathematical correspondence between readings and f does not constitute production.  
　A2 (point of entry)　A task τ given in advance is the point of entry into the inquiry; τ is not taken as a sufficient criterion for computation to hold.  
　A3 (bearing separated from exercise)　Bearing is a host's structural qualification for a task and does not require running at the moment; an actual exercise of bearing is another matter.  
　A4 (task as a whole)　Bearing τ is a qualification for the task τ as a whole: τ has (D, f, ρ\_in, ρ\_out, Π) as its identity, the claim of bearing extends to every operand of D, and the family of results is judged as a whole by Π; a qualification that holds only for part of D is a qualification for another task, restricted to that part.  
　A5 (relativity to declaration)　A claim of bearing is made relative to its declarations: the reading schemes, the entry, the read-out and its conditions, the delivery protocols and the operating conditions are all declared items, given before, and independently of, the runs under test — neither on the basis of those runs nor on the basis of run-by-run prediction or simulation of them; design and simulation by category, not aimed at individual runs, are not excluded by this; the content of the claim is "if d is handed to P according to these declarations, P gives an acceptable result by its own dynamics," and its holding may not depend on anything outside the declarations.  
A1–A5 lay down the conceptual content of bearing: who produces the evaluation (A1), what the claim extends to (A4), and relative to what it is made (A5). They do not give the structural form of what satisfies these requirements: how the types of a reading scheme must be marked out for the scheme to count as given before the runs; which participations of the reading schemes, of delivery and of external action count as proxy evaluation and which do not; how the random components and the unspecified choices of delivery are each to be counted; and which external actions in the operating conditions are compatible with bearing. These are forced by the counterexamples of Rounds One to Seven, and Section Eight, under "overstrong substitutes," then checks that no item can be strengthened further. So Leg and N\_bear are the necessary forms in which A1–A5 are realized at the three places of reading scheme, delivery and production: their necessity follows from A1–A5, and their form and minimality are fixed by the inquiry. Which item follows from which: Leg₁ from A5; Leg₂ and external non-proxy from A1; coverage from A4; Prov from A4 and A5; the universal quantification over unspecified choices from A5. A1 alone excludes neither a device that, by its own dynamics, computes correctly for half the operands and, by its own dynamics, computes wrongly for the other half, nor an adder with a broken key that cannot be given operands containing a certain digit — in the former the evaluation is indeed produced by the host, and in the latter likewise wherever delivery is possible; the former is excluded by A4 (Round Three), the latter by A4 and A5 (Round Four).

Third, the physical-possibility premise PP: the trajectory at the operand level of any actual computation (the sequence of states read through the reading schemes, together with any finite number of observed physical variables) can physically be produced by a process that performs no operation. PP is an explicitly declared premise, not a proved physical theorem; it enters only Corollary Three, whose conclusion carries PP.

Fourth, the task τ and the declared items, as the task-side conditions of this chapter.

Task-side non-degeneracy condition: bearing is not a relation that holds for every physical system and every task. This is given by the initial stipulation of computation together with A1 — the initial stipulation restricts computation to mathematical operations driven by a device and distinguishes from it the cases that can be mathematically described but have no host; A1 requires that the evaluation be actually produced by the host, not a coincidental correspondence; if some account of bearing held for every system and every task, this restriction would come to nothing. Hence the non-degeneracy condition: no account under which bearing holds for every system and every task characterizes bearing. It only delimits the range within which the question enters, and contains none of the structure to be proved.

Apart from the inherited items above, the legitimacy condition on reading schemes, coverage, input reachability, external non-proxy and admissible execution of delivery protocols used in this chapter are structures or conditions put forward by this chapter. They are the forms in which A1–A5 are realized at the three places of reading scheme, delivery and production; their form is fixed by the inquiry of Rounds One to Seven, and their necessity is proved in each round from the corresponding antecedent; the initial stipulation and A1–A5 themselves do not contain these forms.

Division of labour between this chapter and its neighbours: Chapter 3, *The General Form of Computational Execution*, treats an actual exercise of bearing; Chapter 4, *The Mathematical Form of Computation*, gives the exact form of the structures of this chapter and is the verification text of H1, H2 and H3 (except for the clause of H3 on the norm for declaring the operating conditions Env, for which that chapter gives no criterion; not yet established within the group); Chapter 5, *The General Form of Generative Computation*, treats generative computation under dynamical conditions; the originally planned grading of levels of reproduction (not yet established within the group) uses this chapter's reading schemes and families of results as tools for the qualification of reproduction; on whether computation and non-computation can be told apart at the level of effects alone, the related result already established within the group is Theorem One of Chapter 12, *The Distinction Form of Computing and Non-Computing Hosts* (the passive observations listed there cannot separate that chapter's two candidates), while the rest of the research on distinction at the level of effects was originally planned and is not yet established; Chapter 11, *The Determination Form of Computation*, partly takes over the evidential standards for bearing and execution; the full determination of computing hosts versus natural physical processes is to be taken up by further work on *The Determination Form of Computation* and is not yet established within the group. The conclusions of this chapter are available to be cited by those chapters; their conclusions do not enter the premises of this chapter.

Line of Argument

Each round takes everything obtained so far as what is currently known, and asks the same question again: is a host's bearing τ already closed by what has been obtained so far?

One

What is currently known: the initial stipulation of computation, the antecedents A1–A5 of the concept of bearing, the task τ and the declaration of its reading schemes, the declared items and the task-side non-degeneracy condition. On these alone, the most direct account of bearing one can give is: the evolution of P, read out by the reading schemes of τ, agrees with f. That is, P has some stretch of evolution from a state read as d to a state read as f(d).

Said this way, is bearing already closed?

Take a stone that is heating up. It has an enormous number of microstates, and at each moment its state differs from that of the moment before. For every stretch of evolution s₀ → s₁ that actually occurs in it, one can write down a reading scheme of this sort: "that state at the moment s₀ is read as 3, that state at the moment s₁ is read as f(3)"; for each operand in D, pick a stretch of evolution and pair it with such a reading scheme. This set of reading schemes can be written into τ in full and declared beforehand. Then "the evolution of P agrees with f under the reading schemes" holds for this stone, and holds for any f. The same can be achieved another way: first observe several runs of the stone, then mark out the states those runs happened to pass through as "the type read as 3" and "the type read as f(3)" — formally types, but in fact tailored to the runs after they have been seen.

This shows that what has been obtained is not yet closed. If this counted as bearing, any physical system with sufficiently many distinguishable states would bear any task, violating the non-degeneracy condition, and the restriction in the initial stipulation that computation is limited to mathematical operations driven by a device would come to nothing. The trouble is not whether the reading scheme is declared — it is — but what unit the reading scheme takes and when it is marked out: it takes particular states in a particular run as its unit, or marks out types only after the runs have been seen, which amounts to pairing each stretch of evolution with its own reading scheme. The order of quantifiers is "for each stretch of evolution, there is a reading scheme."

By A5, the reading schemes are declared items, given before, and independently of, the runs under test; run-by-run reading schemes and after-the-fact typing are given on the basis of the runs under test, so the claim is not made relative to declarations prior to the runs — and this holds for every claim of bearing, not just for this stone. To exclude such cases, the reading schemes must be defined on state types of P, and the division into types must be fixed by physical properties, before and independently of the runs under test: a reading scheme reads a class of physical states (a voltage interval, a gear position, a numeral representation) as an operand or a value, and the class is delimited by its physical properties, not by which states the runs under test happen to pass through. This does not require types to be independent of all history: the numeral representations of a brain are formed by learning, the internal representations of a trained network are formed by training, and the voltage intervals of a circuit are fixed by manufacture; all of them are formed by prior history, before the runs under test, and once marked out, the same set is used for every run under test. The order of quantifiers thus becomes "there is a reading scheme that is used for every run under test."

This gives the first legitimacy condition:

Leg₁　The reading schemes are defined on state types marked out in advance — the domains of ρ\_in and ρ\_out are state types of P; the division into types is fixed by physical properties, before and independently of the runs under test, and may be formed by prior history (learning, training, manufacture).

Leg₁ has two parts, each with its own source. First, "marked out by physical properties" means exactly this: the reading scheme has a fixed rule of interpretation on the physical states of P, each type being given by a physically identifiable condition — a voltage interval, a gear position, a numeral representation or a table listing some identifiable microstates all fall within it. This is merely the unfolding of the phrase "a reading scheme is a reading scheme on the states of P": a rule not stated in terms of identifiable conditions on the states of P is not a reading scheme for P. It sets no lower bound on granularity, and does not require types to be "natural" or coarse: arbitrary enumeration, digital encoding, table lookup and representations formed by training all satisfy this clause. Second, "before and independently of the runs under test" follows from A5: the reading schemes are declared items. So Leg₁ is just "a reading scheme is a fixed rule of interpretation on the physical states of P" together with the independence requirement of A5; a reading scheme that violates Leg₁ is either not a reading scheme for the states of P, or violates A5. Setting no lower bound on granularity does not let arbitrary code assignment back in: under a reading scheme that is given before and independently of the runs and reads certain individual microstates as numbers, the host, across the random components of the protocol and all admissible executions, will in general not land exactly on those microstates, and Prod then rules it unacceptable; if the table was compiled on the basis of predictions of the runs under test, it violates A5. The formal criterion for "physically identifiable condition" belongs to H1.

Leg₁ adds no property to P; what it specifies is what kind of claim of bearing has content. It is a condition acting on the claim "P bears τ" itself.

Ask again: once the reading schemes are defined on state types marked out in advance, is bearing closed?

Two

What is currently known now includes Leg₁.

Take a wire that carries the level at its input end unchanged to its output end. Declare for it the following reading schemes: read the class of states "the input end carries two segments of code, for d and for f(d)" as d; read the class of states "the output end carries some two segments of code" as the value represented by the second segment. Both reading schemes are defined on state types marked out in advance and satisfy Leg₁. To hand the wire a state "read as d," the deliverer must write f(d) in along with it; the wire carries it over unchanged, and what is read out is exactly f(d). This can be done for any f. The same can be done on the read-out side: take another device that copies its input unchanged, read the state in which the input end carries the number r as r, and read the state in which the output end carries the number r as 2r; it then "bears" multiplication by 2 — the multiplying by 2 is done by the reader at the moment of reading.

What has been obtained is still not closed. Here the work of finding f(d) — all of it or part of it — is done by the user of the reading schemes in this input or output: the deliverer worked out f(d) at the time of delivery, or the reader applied part of f at the time of read-out. The initial stipulation says that computation is a mathematical operation driven by a device, and A1 requires that the evaluation of f be actually produced by the host; but here that share of the result is produced by someone outside the host. Leg₁ cannot stop this, because the trouble lies not in the domain of the reading schemes but in whether the use of the reading schemes takes on the evaluation.

To exclude such cases, it must be required that in no input or output do the reading schemes evaluate on the host's behalf. Specifically, two clauses: delivering an operand d requires only d itself and the declared reading scheme, not the finding of f(d) or any part of it; obtaining a value from an output state requires only applying ρ\_out to the type of the output state, not applying f or any part of it in addition.

This gives the second legitimacy condition:

Leg₂　The reading schemes do not evaluate by proxy — in every input and output, the content of delivery is fixed by d through ρ\_in alone and requires no evaluation of f, and the read-out is obtained by applying ρ\_out to the type of the output state alone and requires no application of f; the user of the reading schemes takes on no evaluation of f.

The necessity of Leg₂ comes from A1: bearing requires the evaluation of f to be produced by the host within the declared boundary; if the users of the reading schemes were allowed to take on all or part of the evaluation in input and output, that part of the evaluation would not be produced by the host; and the wire and the copying device show that this relaxation lets any system that can carry states over bear any function.

Leg₂ restricts evaluation in input and output; it does not prevent reading schemes from being designed with the task in view. Representations designed specifically for a task are all legitimate. A slide rule has logarithmic scales engraved for multiplication: delivering means setting the slide to the mark engraved "3," which needs only 3; reading means reading the number on the scale, which needs only the scale; and the multiplication is carried out by the addition of lengths. An analogue computer chooses scale factors for its quantities according to the ranges of D and V, bringing the quantities within the host's working range. A residue representation is adopted for modular arithmetic, a logarithmic representation for multiplication. These representations were chosen for the task at design time, but in each input and output, delivery uses only the operand, read-out uses only the scale or the code, and the evaluation falls on the host's dynamics. It is also legitimate to store a table of answers inside the host and have the host's dynamics fetch from it according to the operand: the table is part of the host's configuration, and the fetching is done by the host. What is wrong with the copying device is not that "the output reading scheme is related to the task," but that one and the same number is read differently on the input side and the output side, and this difference is exactly a part of f — each time the reader reads, one multiplication by 2 is applied. The scale factors of an analogue computer must be judged by the same standard: choosing scale factors for input and output separately is in itself legitimate; but if the ratio of the two scale factors happens to absorb a factor of f, that factor is applied in the read-out, which is proxy evaluation by the reading scheme, and what the host bears is the scaled task with that factor removed, not f itself.

Leg₁ and Leg₂ together are called the legitimacy condition on a claim of bearing, written Leg(P, τ). From here on, "bearing" always means a legitimate claim of bearing.

Ask again: once the reading schemes are legitimate, is bearing closed?

Three

What is currently known now includes Leg₂. The account of bearing at this point is: under legitimate reading schemes, the evolution of P agrees with f.

Take a device whose reading schemes are legitimate: the voltage intervals of its registers are read as numbers in binary. On one occasion, the initial state is read as 3 and the final state as f(3). But the final state on this occasion was settled at random by thermal noise; on another run, or with an initial state read as 4, the final state has nothing to do with f. Take also a page of records on which the answer is written correctly for the single sample d = 3.

Both of these "agree with f" under legitimate reading schemes, yet neither bears τ. τ is a function on D, and by A4, bearing τ means that for every operand in D the host gives the result f requires, the family of results being judged as a whole by Π; agreement on one occasion, or on one sample, speaks of something else. A1 states explicitly as well that a coincidental correspondence between readings and f does not constitute production; the occasion on which the noise landed on the right value is just such a coincidental correspondence.

To exclude such cases, bearing must hold over the whole domain and be judged by the acceptance protocol of the task: for each d in D, if P were in a state read as d and evolved from it, the results obtained, read out through ρ\_out, would form a result distribution μ\_d; and the family (μ\_d)\_{d∈D} composed of these distributions satisfies Acc\_Π. This is a counterfactual condition: it speaks of "what would happen if P were in a state read as d," and does not require every d actually to have been run. Written in terms of families of results and the acceptance predicate, it covers exact and approximate, deterministic and stochastic tasks, and tasks that tolerate finitely many errors.

This gives:

Coverage — the family (μ\_d)\_{d∈D}, where μ\_d is the distribution of results obtained by P evolving from a state read as d, satisfies Acc\_Π.

Coverage says that the results pass; it does not yet say who produces them.

Ask again: with legitimate reading schemes and coverage holding, is bearing closed?

Four

What is currently known now includes coverage.

Take an 8-bit adder with an intact circuit, its declared entry a keypad on which the "7" key is broken. The circuit would give the sum for every operand, so coverage holds; but no operand containing the digit 7 can be handed to it.

Take next the solar system. Take intervals of the planets' positions and momenta as state types, read as configurations by celestial mechanics; the types are marked out in advance by physical quantities, and the use of the reading schemes evaluates nothing on its behalf, so Leg is satisfied. Let D be the set of planetary configurations and f the transformation that advances them by a duration Δ under celestial mechanics. If the solar system were in a configuration read as d, it would evolve from there to a configuration read as f(d), so coverage holds. Yet no physical process can bring the solar system, through any entry, into a configuration read as an arbitrary d.

Both of these satisfy everything obtained so far, yet neither bears τ. The antecedent of coverage, "if P were in a state read as d," is for them only a supposition: the task cannot reach them, and the "given" in the initial stipulation's "transforms given structures and relations" has no way of taking place. The adder with the broken key bears addition on the domain of operands not containing the digit 7; it does not bear it on D. The solar system can be described by this set of equations; it does not bear the task the equations express — this is exactly the distinction between bearing and description drawn in the Preliminary Distinctions. Of the two cases, the broken-key adder does not depend on any choice in how the domain is taken, and is a direct witness; the solar-system case depends on the declared domain taking arbitrary configurations — if D were shrunk to the configurations the solar system will pass through of its own accord, the question would become a different one; see the entry "waiting for the moment" in Section Eleven.

By A4, the claim extends to every d; by A5, the content of the claim for d is "if d is handed to P according to the declarations, P gives an acceptable result." If for some d there is no executable declared delivery protocol, the claim has no content at d, and P bears at most the task restricted to the deliverable part. To exclude such cases, the task must be deliverable to the host: for each d in D, there is a delivery protocol through the entry E\_in that can be executed, whose execution brings P into a state read as d, or completes a delivery process read as d. The content of a delivery protocol is constrained by Leg₂: it is fixed by d through ρ\_in alone.

This gives the first structural item:

Prov　Input reachability — for all d ∈ D, the set Prov\_τ(d) of executable delivery protocols for d through E\_in is non-empty: Prov\_τ(d) ≠ ∅.

Input reachability requires only reaching, through the declared entry, the state type read as d; it does not require that the host's internal state can be set at will: a device with fixed ports satisfies it when operands are written into its registers through those ports. Delivery may be a process: sequential input, streamed input, an operand entering in several instalments. Delivery may come from several places: distributed input completes one delivery through several channels jointly. Once delivery has entered the host, it may change the host's configuration, connections or parameters: in a network whose gains are modulated by the input, or a general-purpose machine that takes programs as data, the content of delivery is fixed by the operand, and how it changes the host once it has entered belongs to the host's own dynamics. When part of an operand is prestored in the host and cannot be rewritten, that part belongs to the host's configuration, and the domain is restricted accordingly; when it can be rewritten, the channel that writes it belongs to the entry. When the host must be prepared afresh to enter a state read as d, delivery is legitimate if this preparation is the declared entry and its content is fixed by d alone; P is then declared as a class of systems prepared by this protocol.

Once input reachability holds, the antecedent of coverage, "P is in a state read as d," is realized by delivery. Coverage is then written: after d has entered P by delivery, the family of results obtained by P's evolution satisfies Acc\_Π.

Ask again: with legitimate reading schemes, input reachability and coverage holding after delivery, is bearing closed?

Five

What is currently known now includes input reachability and coverage after delivery.

Take a writing-pad black box, with a skilled human calculator sitting outside it. The reading schemes are legitimate; any d can be written on the entry card and handed in, so input reachability holds; the human calculator reads the card, works out f(d) and writes it at the exit, so after delivery the result is always f(d), and coverage holds. But the box itself is only a writing pad: from the state read as d to the state read as f(d), the production in between was completed by the person outside the box and written back.

What has been obtained is still not closed. If the box is taken as P, what produces f(d) is the human calculator outside the box, not the box; by A1, the producer of the evaluation must be the host itself. Round Two excluded evaluation by the users of the reading schemes in input and output; here the evaluator is a process acting on the host from outside its boundary after delivery, which Leg₂ does not reach. Coverage requires only that the results pass and says nothing about who produces them; what Round Three left open shows up here as a gap. A limit is needed: which actions entering from outside the boundary may be present while it can still be said that the host produced the result itself.

First consider which external actions really are compatible with bearing.

Power supply and clock enter from outside the boundary, and without them the host cannot run at all. An external cooling system adjusts its cooling intensity adaptively according to temperature: the device carries different heat loads when processing different operands, and the cooling changes with them; if the range of loads is wide, no single fixed cooling intensity can keep the device within a safe temperature for all operands. A precision analogue calculator reaches its declared accuracy only when the temperature is sufficiently stable; connect it to a cooler that makes the temperature fluctuate widely, and although the temperature stays within the safe range, the results fall outside the declared accuracy. A device executing a randomized algorithm obtains random numbers from an external physical random source, and its results vary with these random numbers.

All these external actions take part in the host's running: some vary with the operand, some cannot be replaced by a fixed action, some bring the host up to standard only under one particular mode of maintenance, and some directly affect the result; yet none of them evaluates f on the host's behalf. The cooler did not find the answer; the random numbers sent by the random source have nothing to do with f; that the precision calculator goes wrong when the temperature is unstable shows that it cannot operate normally under that condition, not that the cooler computed anything for it. So "whether the external action varies with the operand," "whether it can be replaced by a fixed external action," "whether the results pass under every external maintenance that keeps the temperature within the safe range" and "whether it affects the result" cannot serve as the limit: each of the four would misjudge some class of host above, which really does bear its task, as not bearing it.

What distinguishes the black box from these is what the external action does. The human calculator read the operand, carried out the work of finding f(d), and wrote its result in; take this work away, and the box cannot give f(d). There is a more hidden case as well: a "cooler" reads the temperature distribution inside the box, decodes the operand from it, works out f(d), and then, by means of the cooling, prints a temperature pattern representing the answer inside the box. It acts through the channel of cooling and reads only temperature, and in outward form it is no different from adaptive cooling; its difference from adaptive cooling likewise lies not in the channel or the readings, but in its having carried out the work of evaluating f, with the result depending on that work. The kind of external action, its channel, whether it varies with the input and whether it affects the result cannot separate the two classes of action; what can separate them is only whether the external action takes on the evaluation of f.

To exclude such cases, coverage must hold under the following condition: after delivery is complete and before read-out, no process acting on P from outside its boundary — whatever its channel, whether or not it varies with the operand, whether or not it affects the result — carries out the evaluation of f, or any part of it, on which the passing of the result depends. Call this condition external non-proxy. The necessity of external non-proxy comes from A1: whatever share of the result is produced by external proxy evaluation is not evaluation produced by the host; and the black box shows that if external proxy evaluation were allowed, any writing pad would bear any function.

This round reveals a second point not yet closed: under what external conditions is coverage assessed? This point is revealed by the precision analogue calculator and has a different source from the black box: if no conditions of assessment are given, coverage must hold under all external conditions, the precision analogue calculator fails to reach its accuracy when the temperature fluctuates widely and is judged not to bear its task, and coverage is then no longer a necessary condition of bearing. A host needs power, a clock, a stable temperature and the like in order to operate normally, and bearing is bearing under these conditions. The precision analogue calculator bears its task within the declared temperature stability; under widely fluctuating temperature it fails to reach its accuracy, and this fact shows what its operating conditions are, not that it does not bear the task. So coverage is assessed under the declared operating conditions Env: Env declares the external conditions, with their ranges, that the host needs in order to operate normally, together with the external apparatus that maintains these conditions; the maintaining apparatus must itself also satisfy external non-proxy.

External non-proxy and the operating conditions are not two further items besides coverage, but the assessment regime of coverage, written R\_B: under Env, with external non-proxy. That the results are produced by the host's own dynamics means precisely that "coverage holds under R\_B." When the host consists of several devices distributed across physical nodes, and other nodes carry out part of the evaluation of f, that is external proxy evaluation relative to a single node; when the boundary is declared to include all the nodes, the work between nodes lies within the boundary, and the combined host bears the task. Who the bearer is, is decided by the declaration of the boundary; external non-proxy ensures that the evaluation borne does not go beyond the declared boundary.

The operating conditions Env are a declared item (A5), and their declaration must itself be legitimate: Env specifies the conditions the host needs in order to operate and to meet the acceptance protocol, and may not be narrowed to exactly those conditions under which the results pass, so as to obtain bearing that way — otherwise "passes under the declared Env" could also be obtained by shrinking Env after the fact. The criterion for a legitimate declaration of Env belongs to H3.

Ask again: with legitimate reading schemes, input reachability and coverage holding under R\_B after delivery, is bearing closed?

Six

What is currently known now includes R\_B. Coverage now says: after d has entered P by delivery, under R\_B, the family of results obtained by P's own dynamics satisfies Acc\_Π. But which delivery does "d enters by delivery" refer to? Delivery can be physically realized in many ways: one and the same operand can enter through different modes of operation; one state type contains an enormous number of microstates, and which of them the entry brings the host to on each occasion is often not determined. For which of these realizations does coverage hold?

Take a noisy device: its reading schemes are legitimate, its entry is fully declared, and its processing of operands has, by and large, nothing to do with f. The state type read as d contains an enormous number of microstates; at the microscopic level evolution is deterministic, so every type always contains some microstates that happen to evolve to a state read as f(d). If coverage required only that "there exist one realization of delivery under which the results satisfy the acceptance protocol," then the deliverer would only need to pick out, for each d, one such microstate and hand it in, and the device would bear f — and would bear any f.

What has been obtained is still not closed. That the deliverer can pick out, for each d, exactly the right microstate depends on knowing beforehand which one will land on f(d) — and that knowledge is precisely an evaluation of f; the picker did that work for the host at the time of delivery. Leg₂ constrains the content of delivery to be fixed by d through the reading scheme; it reaches "hand in a state read as d," but not "which state of this class to hand in"; R\_B constrains external actions after delivery. Proxy evaluation appears once more, at the execution of delivery.

To exclude such cases, it must be specified what delivery is, and for which executions of delivery coverage holds.

Delivery follows the declared delivery protocol. The protocol specifies, for each d, how delivery through the entry is carried out; it is fixed by d and the reading schemes alone and contains no evaluation of f. A protocol may contain random components: the distribution of microstates within a type that the entry naturally produces, two modes of operation used alternately at a declared frequency, a low-probability intermittent-contact fault. These random components are part of the protocol and are counted into the result distribution μ\_d; the reliability requirement of the task is measured according to Π on this distribution. A protocol may also leave some choices with no probabilities specified, to be decided by the deliverer: which mode of operation to use, which microstate within the type to place the host in.

For these unspecified choices, coverage must hold however they are made. The reasons are as follows. The claim of bearing is made relative to the declarations: "if d is handed to P according to this delivery protocol, P gives an acceptable result." Unspecified choices are, by definition, not fixed by the declarations. If coverage held only under some of those choices, then whether the task is completed would depend on something outside the declarations — on how the deliverer actually chooses. The deliverer's ways of choosing fall into two classes, according to whether they rest on knowledge about the result, and the two are exhaustive. In the first, the choice rests on knowledge about the result — whether all or part of that knowledge, whether obtained beforehand or learned from trial runs — as in microstate selection: that is proxy evaluation at delivery, which the first half of this round has shown to empty bearing of content. In the second, the choice does not rest on knowledge about the result; it takes only two shapes. Choosing at random is a random component, and should be written into the protocol and measured by its distribution. Choosing by a deterministic rule (the rule may depend on d — for example, always use the first mode of operation): the acceptable results obtained under it really are produced by the host, but the rule is part of the mode of delivery and should be written into the protocol. Whether a deterministic rule depending on d tacitly contains an evaluation of f is decided by Leg₂ (formal criterion H2); one that does belongs to the first class. Once either of the latter two shapes is written into the protocol, it is no longer an unspecified choice, but a random component of the protocol or the single execution the protocol prescribes. So every way of choosing on which the claim of bearing actually depends should be declared in the protocol. The claim of bearing is made relative to the protocol it declares (A5): if coverage held only for some of the choices still left unspecified after declaration, then relative to the declared protocol the claim of bearing would not hold. Hence, for these choices, coverage must hold universally. The universality therefore comes from A5, not from non-proxy: non-proxy explains why "there exists an execution under which the results pass" is not enough (microstate selection); A5 explains why it must hold for every admissible execution. If a protocol allows two modes of delivery, both determined by d and neither evaluating f, one under which the device computes normally and one that often disables the input, then relative to this broad protocol bearing does not hold, and relative to a narrow protocol that allows only the reliable mode it can hold; both follow from A5. What this universality says is that the claim of bearing must hold relative to the protocol it declares; the host may still bear the task under a more specific protocol.

The range of this universality is marked out by the declared protocol; it does not extend to all physically possible operations outside the protocol, nor does it replace the probabilistic measurement of the protocol's random components. Suppose a calculator's input port has two modes of operation, one that occasionally makes poor contact and one that is reliable. If the protocol prescribes using the two alternately at some frequency, the distribution they compose is counted into μ\_d and reliability is measured by Π; it suffices that this passes, and the poor-contact mode need not pass on its own. If the protocol prescribes using only the reliable mode, measurement is made on that mode alone. Only when the protocol leaves "always use only the poor-contact mode" as a choice the deliverer may make must coverage hold for it as well — and in that case the protocol really cannot guarantee that the task is completed, and the host bears the task under a more specific protocol. The universality excludes selection; it does not require every run to be correct. Errors in single runs are absorbed by the reliability and error-tolerance declarations of Π.

Call one way of making the unspecified choices of a delivery protocol π an admissible execution of π; let μ\_{π,σ} be the distribution of results produced by P's own dynamics and read out through ρ\_out after delivery by the admissible execution σ of π under R\_B, the random components of π already counted into it. This gives the second structural item:

Prod　Self-production — for every family of delivery protocols (π\_d)\_{d∈D} with π\_d ∈ Prov\_τ(d), and every family of admissible executions (σ\_d)\_{d∈D}, σ\_d an admissible execution of π\_d, the family of results (μ\_{π\_d,σ\_d})\_{d∈D} produced by P's own dynamics under R\_B satisfies Acc\_Π.

If several delivery protocols are declared for the same d, the claim of bearing extends to each of them; the deliverer's choice among several protocols is also an unspecified choice.

"Produced by P's own dynamics" in Prod means that the result is obtained by applying ρ\_out to the state P reaches by its own dynamics under R\_B, with neither delivery nor any external process after delivery evaluating by proxy; non-proxy at the reading schemes is constrained by Leg₂. It is a structural condition at the host layer; it makes no further judgement on whether there is more dependence between the result and the host's dynamics. The case in which the reading scheme itself gives the same acceptable value for every state satisfies Prod, and is excluded by A1 (Corollary Five).

Ask again: with legitimate reading schemes, input reachability and self-production holding, is bearing closed?

Seven

What is currently known: Leg, Prov, Prod. Two matters remain to be tested: whether the actual production of which A1 speaks requires a further item, "the result depends on the input"; and whether readability of the result must be set up separately.

First the former. Suppose a further item is added: "the result must depend on the input to a degree above chance." Test whether it is necessary for each kind of bearing. A memory that holds its contents unchanged bears the identity task: what goes in is what is read out. If "the probability of reaching the target value when not constrained by delivery" is taken as the chance baseline, the memory already holds its contents, so this baseline can be 1, and the result cannot exceed it; yet the memory really does bear the identity task. The bearer of the constant task "output 0" has a result that does not change with the input at all; that it bears the constant task is equally real. So this item excludes genuine bearing and is not a necessary condition. Conversely, when the acceptance protocol is so weak that even noise unrelated to the input can satisfy it, a random device that is "above chance" on some baseline shows nothing more. This item therefore does not enter the structure of bearing.

The content of A1 in the necessary direction is realized by Leg₂ and Prod: the result is produced by the host itself under the declared operating conditions, with no proxy evaluation at any of the three places — reading scheme, delivery and external action. Whether Leg₂ ∧ Prod exhausts A1 belongs to the converse question, which is unanswered (Corollary Five). What can be derived as a lemma, however, is for which tasks bearing necessarily brings with it a dependence of the result on the input:

Definition　Π-trivial task — there is a distribution μ*, independent of d, such that the constant family (μ*)\_{d∈D} satisfies Acc\_Π.

Derived Dependence Lemma　If τ is not a Π-trivial task, the reading schemes of τ satisfy Leg, and P satisfies Prov and Prod, then for any family of admissible executions there are d₁, d₂ ∈ D whose result distributions differ; that is, the result depends counterfactually on delivery, and the evaluation in this dependence is not supplied by proxy evaluation in the reading schemes, in delivery or in external action.

Proof. Take any family of admissible executions. If the result distributions obtained were the same for all d, call the common distribution μ*; then the constant family (μ*)\_{d∈D} would be exactly a family guaranteed by Prod to satisfy Acc\_Π, and τ would be a Π-trivial task, contrary to the premise. So the result distribution varies with d. By Leg₂, the users of the reading schemes take on no evaluation; since the delivery protocol is fixed by d and the reading schemes alone and Prod holds universally over unspecified choices, delivery takes on no evaluation; by R\_B, external actions after delivery take on no evaluation. So whatever part of the dependence of the result on d is evaluation is borne by P's own dynamics; external actions may take part in the running, may vary with d and may affect the result, but they do not supply this evaluation. This completes the proof.

An exact deterministic task is not Π-trivial so long as f is not constant; for the bearer of the identity task, the dependence of the result on delivery follows directly from this lemma, with no probabilistic baseline needed. In an approximate task, if the tolerance regions of f(d₁) and f(d₂) intersect, distinguishable results are not required for this pair of operands, and the lemma gives dependence between operands whose acceptance sets are disjoint. Constant tasks, and tasks whose protocols are so weak that noise can satisfy them, are Π-trivial; for them N\_bear requires only input reachability and that the output region fall, by the host's own dynamics, into μ\*.

Now the latter. Must readability of the result — the result being read out by the same reading scheme — be set up separately? The result distributions in Prod are measured through ρ\_out, read-outs falling outside the reading scheme are written ⊥, and whether a read-out of ⊥ passes is decided by Acc\_Π — Π may tolerate some read-outs of ⊥ (error-tolerant protocols), and certain protocols of Π-trivial tasks even tolerate all of them being ⊥. That the reading scheme used for read-out is legitimate is guaranteed by Leg. So Prod needs no readability requirement beyond the acceptance protocol; it cannot be said that every result must be readable.

We now return to the same task. For a host to bear τ means: the reading schemes are legitimate and every item has been declared; every operand in D can be handed to the host by the declared protocol; and after it is handed over, however the choices left by the protocol are made, under the declared operating conditions and with no external proxy evaluation whatever, the family of results produced by the host's own dynamics satisfies the acceptance protocol of the task. The "given" in the initial stipulation's "transforms given structures and relations and obtains new structures and relations" falls on Prov, and the "transforms" and "obtains" fall on Prod; the "input" of the essential chain is delivery, and the "output" is read-out. What runs through all seven rounds is one and the same requirement: the evaluation is borne by the host, and is not moved onto the reading schemes, the deliverer or external processes. The task is closed, and no further items are forced.

## Section Eight　Proof of Minimal Structure and Classification by Load-Bearing Role

Classification by load-bearing role

Leg₁ (the reading schemes are defined on state types marked out in advance): a legitimacy condition on the claim of bearing forced by this chapter, referring back to Round One (run-by-run reading schemes and after-the-fact typing). Its formal criterion depends on H1; its witness of independent degeneration depends on H2.  
Leg₂ (the reading schemes do not evaluate by proxy): a legitimacy condition on the claim of bearing forced by this chapter, referring back to Round Two (the wire, the copying device). Its formal criterion depends on H2; its witness of independent degeneration depends on H2.  
Prov (input reachability): a structural item forced by this chapter, referring back to Round Four (the broken-key adder; the solar system, relative to the declared domain).  
Prod (self-production): a structural item forced by this chapter, composed from three gaps — coverage refers back to Round Three (noise landing on the right value, the single sample); the assessment regime R\_B (external non-proxy under the declared operating conditions) refers back to Round Five (the proxy-evaluating black box, the proxy evaluation that prints the answer by means of cooling; adaptive cooling, the precision analogue calculator and the external random source show that the limit is not "whether it varies with d," "whether it can be replaced by a fixed action," "whether the results pass under every safe maintenance" or "whether it affects the result"); the delivery protocol and the universality over its unspecified choices refer back to Round Six (microstate selection; the calculator with two modes of operation shows that the universality does not extend to the random components of the protocol or to operations outside the protocol). The formal criteria for external proxy evaluation and for proxy evaluation in delivery depend on H3 and H2 respectively.  
The Derived Dependence Lemma and the absorption of readability: local results obtained in Round Seven, not structural items.  
Antecedent items: the initial stipulation of computation; the antecedents A1–A5 of the concept of bearing (actual production; point of entry; bearing separated from exercise; task as a whole; relativity to declaration); the task and the declared items; the task-side non-degeneracy condition, given by the initial stipulation and A1, which only delimits the range of the question and contains no structure to be proved. The antecedent items serve only as what is currently known and take on no part of the core task still to be proved; A1–A5 do not give the structural form of N\_bear (working domain, item two).  
Condition items: H1, H2, H3 (Section Twelve); PP enters only Corollary Three.

Necessity and independent degeneration

Removing or weakening any one item, while keeping the remaining conditions in their original sense, leaves behind in each case a definite failure. What this test examines is the independence of the items of the general formula from one another: the counterexample must satisfy all the other items of the general formula and lack only the item removed; and it fails to be bearing precisely because it violates the antecedent that the removed item realizes (Leg₁ realizes A5; Leg₂ and external non-proxy realize A1; coverage realizes A4; Prov realizes A4 and A5; universality over unspecified choices realizes A5). A case that retains A1–A5 is, under the antecedents, a case of bearing; so a counterexample lacking only one item necessarily violates the antecedent that this item realizes, and this is exactly what it means to say that the item is indispensable.

Remove Leg₁, keeping everything else obtained: take a stone and a fixed heating protocol — for each d, a heating procedure s\_d determined by d alone is prescribed beforehand (for example, the heating duration encodes d, and this encoding contains no evaluation of f); the protocol has no random components and no unspecified choices, and under fixed operating conditions the evolution of the stone is deterministic, so for each d there is only one execution, and each execution reaches the same final state x\_d. The reading scheme rests on after-the-fact typing: having observed these runs, one uses a single reading scheme ρ, shared by the input and output sides, that reads the state after executing s\_d as d and reads x\_d as f(d), and keeps it fixed thereafter; take D finite and choose the trajectories so that this assignment is free of conflict — the states after delivery of different operands are pairwise distinguishable, the final states for different values of f(d) are pairwise distinguishable, and if one state is both the state after some s\_e and some x\_d, then e and f(d) are the same value. The other items hold: delivering d requires only executing s\_d, and s\_d is determined by d alone; read-out only applies ρ to the output state; one and the same state is read as the same value on both sides, so there is no case like the copying device, in which the difference between the reading schemes of the two sides is exactly f; so, by the two clauses of Round Two and the consistency of the two sides, Leg₂ holds; s\_d is executable for each d, so Prov holds; the single execution of the protocol reaches x\_d, read as f(d), with no external proxy evaluation and no execution available for selection, so Prod holds. Only Leg₁ is lacking — the types are marked out on the basis of the runs under test, which violates A5, the antecedent that Leg₁ realizes; so this is not bearing. Remove Leg₁, and any system whose evolution is deterministic and whose final states for different d are distinguishable agrees with any task, and bearing loses its content. This reading table was compiled after the runs had been seen, according to the values of f; beyond the consistency of the two sides and the holding of the two clauses, whether a prestored table still carries part of f over into the reading scheme is a matter whose formal criterion belongs to H2; so the independent indispensability of Leg₁ is likewise conditional on H2. The necessity of Leg₁ is proved from A5 (Round One) and does not depend on this witness.  
Remove Leg₂, keeping everything else obtained: a copying device paired with an output reading scheme that applies f (f(d) = 2d, the same number being read as twice itself on the output side). The other items hold: the reading schemes are defined on types marked out in advance, so Leg₁ holds; the content of delivery is only d, and the delivery protocol contains no evaluation of f, so Prov holds; after delivery the results pass under this reading scheme, with no external action and no selection, so Prod holds. Only Leg₂ is lacking — f is applied at read-out. The evaluation falls on the user of the reading scheme. That this witness lacks only Leg₂ is judged on the basis of the consistency of the representations on the input and output sides: the same number is read differently on the two sides, and the difference is exactly f. Output codes and scale factors chosen for a task can likewise make the reading schemes of the two sides differ (Round Two), and the formal distinction between such task-chosen representations and a reading scheme that applies f belongs to H2; so the independent indispensability of Leg₂ is conditional on H2. The necessity of Leg₂ is proved from A1 (Round Two) and does not depend on this witness. (The wire paired with a reading scheme that writes the answer into the operand cannot be used for this test: the content of delivery already contains f(d), the delivery protocol contains an evaluation, and the form of delivery that Prod requires fails along with it.)  
Remove Prov, keeping Leg and Prod in its universal form: the broken-key adder has legitimate reading schemes; on the deliverable operands Prod actually holds, and on the undeliverable operands Prod holds vacuously; only Prov is lacking — on these operands delivery is impossible.  
Remove the coverage of Prod, keeping Leg and Prov: the noisy device (legitimate reading schemes, deliverable, no external proxy evaluation, no selection — only the results do not pass); the permanently faulty adder, which does not give f(d) for some operands and bears another task τ′.  
Remove external non-proxy from the assessment regime of Prod: the proxy-evaluating black box, and the "cooler" that prints the answer by means of cooling. The other items hold: the reading schemes are legitimate, delivery is possible, the results pass after delivery, and delivery makes no selection; only external non-proxy is lacking.  
Weaken the universality of Prod over unspecified choices to existence: a device whose delivery protocol allows two prescribed modes of delivery, each determined by d alone and neither evaluating f, mode A making the family of results satisfy Acc\_Π and mode B making it fail, the protocol specifying no probability of using either, and the deliverer free to choose. The other items hold: the reading schemes are legitimate, and delivery does no proxy evaluation; both modes are executable, so Prov holds; under mode A the results pass, and external non-proxy holds. Only the universality is lacking, and this violates A5, the antecedent the universality realizes — the claim depends on the deliverer's choice at a point the declaration leaves undetermined. Weakened to existence, this would be counted as bearing. (The microstate selection of Round Six would also be wrongly admitted by this weakening, but its selection rests on knowledge about the result and also touches proxy evaluation at delivery, so it is not used as the witness for lacking only the universality.) A host that passes when delivered by a deterministic rule unrelated to f is not wrongly excluded by this: once the rule is written into the protocol, it is the single admissible execution.

Each overstrong substitute wrongly excludes a class of cases that do bear their tasks by A1–A5 — in each case the evaluation is produced by the host itself (A1), extends to the whole domain (A4), and holds relative to its declarations (A5) — so the strength adopted cannot be increased further:  
Strengthen external non-proxy to "external actions do not vary with d," or to "can be replaced by a fixed action independent of d": a device with a wide range of loads that can only be cooled adaptively is wrongly excluded.  
Strengthen it to "the results pass under every external maintenance that keeps temperature and other maintained quantities within the safe range": the precision analogue calculator, which reaches its accuracy only when the temperature is stable, is wrongly excluded.  
Strengthen it to "external actions do not affect the result": a device running a randomized algorithm that takes external random numbers is wrongly excluded.  
Extend the universality over unspecified choices to requiring that each random component of the protocol pass on its own, or to all physically possible deliveries outside the protocol: the calculator that uses two modes of operation alternately according to its protocol, with acceptable composite reliability, is wrongly excluded.

Non-mergeability

Leg is of a different kind from Prov and Prod: Leg acts on the reading schemes of the claim of bearing and adds no property to the host; Prov and Prod are structure of the host. Leg₁ and Leg₂ cannot be merged: the after-the-fact reading scheme of the stone is consistent on its two sides, and neither delivery nor read-out evaluates f, so it satisfies Leg₂ (subject to H2) while violating Leg₁; the reading schemes of the wire are defined on types marked out in advance, so they satisfy Leg₁ while violating Leg₂.

Prov and Prod cannot be derived from each other. Prod, taken in its universal form, holds vacuously for operands that have no delivery protocol, so Prod does not yield Prov: the broken-key adder satisfies Prod but not Prov. Leg ∧ Prov does not yield Prod: the noisy device has legitimate reading schemes and satisfies Prov but not Prod. They also differ in where they fail: one is failure of delivery, the other is failure of the production after delivery to meet requirements — two classes of failure of bearing (Corollary Four). The two could also be written together as one compound condition, "for each d, a delivery protocol exists, and production passes under all its admissible executions," with the necessary content unchanged and the count of items then being one; listed as two items, they correspond to two independent failure worlds (the broken-key adder, the noisy device) and to the two classes of failure of bearing (Corollary Four).

The three places within Prod — coverage, the assessment regime R\_B and the universality over unspecified choices — cannot be split into three independent structures: R\_B and the universality over unspecified choices are meaningful only for coverage; they specify under what conditions, and for which executions of delivery, coverage holds; apart from coverage, the two have no content that admits determination. So Prod is taken as the unit of the removal test, and each of its three internal places is shown non-redundant by a failure world (the fourth to sixth entries above).

The three places of "no proxy evaluation" — Leg₂ (the use of the reading schemes), the delivery protocol's being fixed by d and the reading schemes alone together with universality over unspecified choices (the execution of delivery), and external non-proxy in R\_B (external processes after delivery) — are the realization of one and the same requirement at three positions, and cannot stand in for one another: Leg₂ does not reach selection within a type (microstate selection satisfies Leg₂), nor external processes after delivery (the black box satisfies Leg₂); the constraint at delivery does not reach read-out or external processes after delivery; external non-proxy does not reach the reading schemes or delivery (neither the wire nor microstate selection involves any external action after delivery).

Relations of dependence: Prov and Prod both take Leg as an antecedent ("a state read as d" and "read-out" are both delimited by legitimate reading schemes); the coverage in Prod realizes its antecedent through the delivery protocols given by Prov. These are dependences of antecedent within a conjunction; the order of proof does not indicate an order in time or in causation.

Exclusion of proposed additions

None of the following opens a new constraint on the task, and none belongs to N\_bear:  
Readability of the result — not set up separately: Prod needs no readability requirement beyond the acceptance protocol (Round Seven).  
Dependence of the result on the input above chance — not necessary for identity and constant tasks; for tasks that are not Π-trivial it is derived by the Derived Dependence Lemma (Round Seven).  
Setting the host's internal state at will — devices with fixed ports, delivered to only through their ports, really do bear tasks (Round Four).  
Delivery that does not change the host's transformation dynamics — systems whose parameters are modulated by the input, and systems that take programs as data, really do bear tasks (Round Four).  
The host persisting as one and the same instance — a class of systems prepared by protocol really does bear tasks (Round Four).  
Reading schemes designed without regard to the task — the slide rule, logarithmic representation and residue representation really do bear tasks (Round Two).  
External actions that are independent of the input, can be replaced by fixed actions, pass under every safe maintenance, or do not affect the result — adaptive cooling, the precision analogue calculator and the external random source really do bear tasks (Round Five).  
Every random component of the protocol passing on its own, every run being correct — handled by the reliability and error-tolerance declarations of Π on the protocol's distribution (Round Six).  
The host being a computing device — bearing is not a mark of computation (opening of the chapter); the full determination of whether a host is a computing host is to be taken up by further work on *The Determination Form of Computation* and is not yet established within the group.  
A particular algorithm or particular physical realization — apart from the determination of "partial proxy evaluation," which requires an evaluation decomposition to be specified, N\_bear depends only on the values of the task function on the domain (Corollary One).

What minimality means: (1) Leg₁, Leg₂, Prov and Prod are each forced by a case that keeps everything obtained before it and yet is not bearing (Rounds One to Six); (2) removing any one item (with each of the three places within Prod tested) leaves behind a non-bearing case that would be counted as bearing (the independent degenerations above; for Leg₁ and Leg₂, conditional on H2); (3) each overstrong substitute above wrongly excludes a class of genuine bearing; (4) the proposed additions tested in Round Seven open no new constraint. This is indispensability relative to the items forced, and closure relative to the proposed additions tested. An unqualified "there is no weaker necessary condition" holds for no necessary condition whatever: take any condition Q that is not a tautology; then N\_bear ∨ Q is also a necessary condition of bearing and can be strictly weaker; a tautology is likewise such a necessary condition, merely one with no discriminating power. Whether, among admissible candidate structures whose components are facts about the host's delivery and production and which can exclude each failure case of Rounds One to Six, there is a set of components different from Leg ∧ N\_bear and weaker than it, is unanswered. In this sense, N\_bear gives functional and structural minimal necessity, and does not prescribe a unique physical realization.

## Section Nine　General Form and General Theorem

The general form of the minimal structure of computational bearing

N\_bear(P, τ) = Prov(P, τ) ∧ Prod(P, τ)

General formula: Bear(P, τ) ⇒ Leg(P, τ) ∧ N\_bear(P, τ)

Formal statement:

Let τ = (D, f, ρ\_in, ρ\_out, Π) be a mathematical task given in advance, P a physical system with declared boundary B, entry E\_in, read-out E\_out, delivery protocols and operating conditions Env, and Θ the declared period. Write the legitimacy condition Leg(P, τ) for the conjunction of the following two clauses:

(Leg₁) ρ\_in and ρ\_out are defined on state types of P, the types being marked out by physical properties, before and independently of the runs under test, and possibly formed by prior history;  
(Leg₂) in every input and output, the content of delivery is fixed by d through ρ\_in alone and requires no evaluation of f, and the read-out is obtained by applying ρ\_out to the type of the output state alone and requires no application of f.

General Theorem　If P bears τ within Θ, then within Θ:

(Leg) the reading schemes of τ satisfy Leg₁ and Leg₂;

(Prov) for any d ∈ D, Prov\_τ(d) ≠ ∅;  
(Prod) for every family of delivery protocols (π\_d)\_{d∈D} with π\_d ∈ Prov\_τ(d), and every family of admissible executions (σ\_d)\_{d∈D}, σ\_d an admissible execution of π\_d, the family of results (μ\_{π\_d,σ\_d})\_{d∈D} produced by P's own dynamics under R\_B satisfies Acc\_Π((μ\_{π\_d,σ\_d})\_{d∈D}, f).

Proof. From Rounds One to Seven. Suppose P bears τ within Θ. If the reading schemes violate Leg₁, then either they are not fixed rules of interpretation on the physical states of P — in which case they are not reading schemes for P, and the claim "P bears τ" cannot even be stated — or they are marked out on the basis of the runs under test, in which case the claim of bearing is not made relative to declarations given before and independently of the runs, contradicting A5; such reading schemes would moreover make any system with sufficiently many distinguishable states agree with any task, violating the non-degeneracy condition (Round One). If the reading schemes violate Leg₂, then the users of the reading schemes evaluate in input and output, and that part of the evaluation is not produced by the host, contradicting A1; the same move would moreover make any system that can carry states over bear any function (Round Two). Both contradict P's bearing τ, so Leg holds. If Prov fails, there is some d ∈ D with no executable declared delivery protocol; by A5, the content of the claim at d is "if d is handed to P according to the declarations …," and so the claim has no content at d; by A4, the claim extends to d, so P bears at most the task restricted to the deliverable part, contradicting its bearing τ (Round Four). If Prod fails, there are a family of delivery protocols and a family of admissible executions under which the family of results obtained under R\_B does not satisfy Acc\_Π; whereas, when P bears τ, the results must be produced by P under its declared operating conditions, through the declared delivery and its own dynamics. There are three cases. If, under the declared operating conditions and with no external proxy evaluation, the family of results simply fails to satisfy the acceptance protocol, then P's results on D, judged as a whole by Π, do not meet the task, contradicting A4 (Round Three). If the family of results passes only when some external process carries out an evaluation of f, then a share of the result is produced outside the boundary, contradicting A1 (Round Five). If the family of results passes only for some of the deliverer's unspecified choices, then completion of the task depends on something outside the declarations: if those choices rest on knowledge about the result, this is proxy evaluation at delivery; if they are unrelated to the result, whether random or by a deterministic rule, they should be written into the protocol, and once written in they are no longer unspecified choices; so, relative to the declared protocol, the claim of bearing does not hold, contradicting A5 (Round Six). All three cases contradict P's bearing τ. Hence Prov ∧ Prod. This completes the proof.

Declaration of strength: the general theorem is in the necessary direction (Bear ⇒ Leg ∧ N\_bear); its reasoning depends on the antecedents A1–A5 of the concept of bearing, not on the specific criteria of H1–H3. The determination, on a specific claim of bearing, of Leg₁, of Leg₂, of a delivery protocol's "containing no evaluation of f" and of external non-proxy depends on the formal well-definedness of the criteria H1, H2 and H3; wherever downstream work relies, in a load-bearing way, on "a given claim satisfies Leg," "a given delivery protocol contains no evaluation" or "a given external action does not evaluate by proxy," it must inherit the corresponding H along with it. The necessity of Leg₁ and Leg₂ does not depend on H2; the independent indispensability of the two (Section Eight) is conditional on H2. The converse N\_bear ⇒ Bear does not hold (Corollary Five); the converse Leg ∧ N\_bear ⇒ Bear has not been proved.

Symbols and read-back:

P: the host, any physical system; it may be declared as an instance, or as a class of systems prepared by a fixed protocol.  
B: the boundary; E\_in: the entry (possibly several channels, time-shared, spread over several places); E\_out: the read-out, together with the timing of read-out.  
Env: the operating conditions — the external conditions, with their ranges, that P needs in order to operate normally (power supply, clock, temperature and its stability, mechanical support, shielding and so on), together with the external apparatus that maintains these conditions.  
Θ: the declared period, within which the reading schemes, delivery protocols and operating conditions are fixed.  
τ = (D, f, ρ\_in, ρ\_out, Π): a mathematical task given in advance; V: the range; ⊥: a read-out falling outside ρ\_out.  
Acc\_Π: the predicate of the acceptance protocol on families of results.  
Prov\_τ(d): the set of delivery protocols declared for d that are executable through E\_in; each protocol is fixed by d and the reading schemes alone, contains no evaluation of f, may contain random components and unspecified choices, and its execution brings P into a state read as d or completes a delivery process read as d.  
σ: an admissible execution of a delivery protocol, that is, one way of making its unspecified choices; the random components of the protocol do not belong to σ, and are counted into the result distribution.  
Proxy evaluation: in some process of input and output, a link outside the host carries out the work of evaluating f on the operand, or part of that work, and whether the result passes depends on that work.  
R\_B: the assessment regime — under Env; after delivery is complete and before read-out, no process acting on P from outside B (including the maintaining apparatus in Env) evaluates by proxy. External processes may vary with d, may affect the result, and may be necessary conditions of P's operation.  
μ\_{π,σ}: the distribution of results produced by P's own dynamics under R\_B and read out through ρ\_out, after delivery by the admissible execution σ of protocol π; it takes values in V ∪ {⊥}, with the random components of π already counted in.  
Leg(P, τ): Leg₁ ∧ Leg₂.  
Prov, Prod, N\_bear: as above.  
Bear(P, τ): P bears τ within Θ.

Read-back by layer: Prov and Prod are both facts at the host layer — what states can be reached through the entry by protocol, and what results the host's own dynamics produces under its operating conditions after delivery. Leg is a condition on the side of the claim of bearing. N\_bear contains no term of Sedimentation-Field dynamics, of law or of any object layer; it does not determine whether the host is a computing device, whether it is executing at the moment, or whether some particular result is correct.

Read-back in words: if a host bears, within some period, a mathematical task given in advance, then necessarily: the reading schemes of the task are defined on state types of the host marked out by physical properties before the runs under test, and in no input or output do delivery or read-out evaluate on the host's behalf (this clause acts on the reading schemes); and every operand in the domain has an executable delivery protocol that hands it to the host through a declared entry; and, when each operand is delivered by its delivery protocol — the protocol's random components being counted into the result, and however the choices the protocol leaves to the deliverer are made — the results produced by the host's own dynamics, under its declared operating conditions and with no external process evaluating on its behalf after delivery, satisfy, as a family of results over the whole domain, the acceptance protocol declared by the task. "Any d" and "any family of admissible executions whatever" are universal quantifiers; Leg, Prov and Prod are conjoined, Leg acting on the reading schemes and Prov and Prod on the host; the family of results is a family of distributions over the whole domain, and the acceptance protocol acts on the whole family.

## Section Ten　Lemmas and Corollaries

Corollary One　Task identity, and the extensional dependence of N\_bear

The f in a task τ is a function from D to V, that is, the totality of its values on D; two rules written differently whose values on D agree everywhere are one and the same function. Hence two tasks τ and τ′ with the same domain, reading schemes and acceptance protocol, whose task functions are extensionally equal on D, are one and the same task, and P's bearing τ is the same thing as P's bearing τ′. This comes from the identity of functions in mathematics; it is a stipulation about task identity and is not derived from N\_bear.

What can be derived from N\_bear is something else, but it must be stated at two levels.

First level: apart from "proxy evaluation," N\_bear(P, τ) and Leg(P, τ) involve only D, the reading schemes, the delivery protocols, the families of results and Acc\_Π, and the task function appears only through its values on D.

Second level: if "the work of evaluating f" in "proxy evaluation" means finding f(d) itself — a deliverer writing in f(d), an external process writing back f(d), a table of the values of f broadcast from outside — it likewise involves only the values of f. The clause "or part of that work" is different: whether a piece of external work is "part of evaluating f" can be determined only relative to some evaluation decomposition, and one and the same piece of work may be a step in evaluating f under one decomposition and have nothing to do with f under another. This clause cannot be deleted: a reading scheme whose ratio of scale factors on the two sides absorbs a factor of f (Round Two), and an external node that works out a single intermediate quantity for one node, are both excluded only by it. So this clause introduces a dependence on the evaluation decomposition; a formal criterion for it that does not depend on the decomposition is listed under H2 and H3.

Proof. Check item by item against the formal statement of Section Nine: Leg₁ does not contain f; Prov does not contain f; in Prod, f appears in Acc\_Π((μ)\_{d∈D}, f), through its values on D; "proxy evaluation" in Leg₂ and R\_B appears through the values of f for the clause "finding f(d)," and depends on the evaluation decomposition adopted for the clause "or part of that work." This completes the proof.

Conclusion: the necessary structure of bearing contains no algorithmic component apart from the determination of "partial proxy evaluation"; until H2 and H3 give a criterion that does not depend on the decomposition, the determination of "partial proxy evaluation" must specify the decomposition adopted.

Accordingly, circuit summation and table-lookup summation face one and the same addition task; neither involves external proxy evaluation, and both satisfy the conditions of the same N\_bear; bearing settles which function is computed, but not by what algorithm it is computed. If objects are to be distinguished by algorithm or by the way a rule is written, that is a question of realization and execution configuration, handed to Chapters 3 and 4.

Corollary Two　Closure of Leg and N\_bear under composition (conditional)

Let P₁ satisfy Leg(P₁, τ₁) ∧ N\_bear(P₁, τ₁), with τ₁ = (D, f₁, ρ\_in¹, ρ\_out¹, Π₁); let P₂ satisfy Leg(P₂, τ₂) ∧ N\_bear(P₂, τ₂), with τ₂ = (D₂, f₂, ρ\_in², ρ\_out², Π₂); and let there be a coupling between P₁ and P₂. Let the composite task be τ\_c = (D, f\_c, ρ\_in¹, ρ\_out^c, Π\_c), the combined boundary being P₁ ∪ P₂ together with the coupling, and the combined operating conditions the union of the two hosts' operating conditions.

The composite task function f\_c is the target of the composite task. When f₁ and f₂ are deterministic functions and f₁(D) ⊆ D₂, f\_c = f₂ ∘ f₁. When f₁ and f₂ are stochastic tasks, f₁(d) and f₂(v) are target distributions on the respective ranges, and f\_c integrates over the intermediate value: f\_c(d)(A) = ∫ f₂(v)(A) f₁(d)(dv), with the support of f₁(d) required to lie within D₂; a deterministic function is the special case in which f₁(d) is a point distribution. The target f\_c and the response actually produced by the hosts — the result distribution μ — are not the same object: f\_c is obtained by composing two targets, μ by chaining the responses of two hosts through the coupling, and the two are compared by Acc\_Π\_c; C3 constrains the composition of responses. The measurable structure needed for the integral belongs to the measure-theoretic formalization of Chapter 4.

A response selection s for P₂: for each v ∈ D₂, specify a delivery protocol π\_v ∈ Prov\_τ₂(v) and one admissible execution σ\_v of it; write F\_s = (μ\_{π\_v,σ\_v})\_{v∈D₂} for P₂'s family of results under s. What P₂'s Prod says is precisely that, for every response selection s, F\_s satisfies Acc\_Π₂. This is an assertion about the whole family; when the acceptance protocol is a family protocol (for example, one tolerating failure on at most k operands), no single member of F\_s need satisfy any pointwise requirement. Write h for a run history of P₁ on d, and v(h) for its read-out, which takes values in V₁ ∪ {⊥}. The composite reading scheme ρ\_out^c is an output reading scheme defined on the combined host and declared along with τ\_c: the combined host declares a failure bypass and a failure output type Z\_⊥ marked out in advance; when the output state of P₁ belongs to a type outside the definition of ρ\_out¹ (read-out ⊥), the failure bypass brings the combined host into Z\_⊥, which lies outside the types to which ρ\_out^c assigns values, and the read-out is ⊥; in all other cases, ρ\_out^c coincides with ρ\_out² on the output states of P₂. The bypass is triggered only by the type of P₁'s output state, and does not require P₂ to execute on these histories. Accordingly the response is extended to ⊥: F̃\_s(v) = F\_s(v) for v ∈ D₂; F̃\_s(⊥) = δ\_⊥, the point distribution at the read-out ⊥.

If the following four conditions hold:

(C0) The end-point reading schemes do not evaluate by proxy for the composite task: delivering d by ρ\_in¹ requires no evaluation of f\_c or any part of it, and reading out by ρ\_out^c requires no application of f\_c or any part of it;  
(C1) The interface is legitimate: the coupling and the failure bypass lie within the combined boundary and do not evaluate by proxy, and the failure bypass is executable on every history on which P₁'s read-out is ⊥; an output state of P₁ read as the value v constitutes, through the coupling, one execution of some declared delivery protocol of P₂ for v, its content fixed by v through ρ\_in² alone, the unspecified choices in it belonging to the admissible executions of that protocol of P₂; conditional on "P₁ reads out v" and on P₁'s run history, the random components of the protocol and the exogenous values on the side of P₂ follow the declared distributions on which P₂'s Prod rests; a combined operating regime exists and is executable, satisfying the operating conditions of P₁ and of P₂ alike (the two hosts' operating conditions do not conflict); the reaction of the coupling on P₁ does not take P₁ outside its declared operating conditions; and P₁'s result distribution under the combined regime is the same as when it runs alone according to its declarations;  
(C2) Support is covered: for any d ∈ D, any delivery protocol of P₁ and any admissible execution, every read-out of P₁ that is not ⊥ falls within D₂;  
(C3) Error propagation and composite protocol: for any family of results of P₁ that satisfies Acc\_Π₁ — given by P₁'s distribution of histories P₁(dh \| d) on each d — and any family of response selections (s\_h) indexed by history, the composite family of results (∫ F̃\_{s\_h}(v(h)) P₁(dh \| d))\_{d∈D} satisfies Acc\_Π\_c, where each F\_{s\_h} satisfies Acc\_Π₂ (by P₂'s Prod). The failure histories of P₁ enter the composite result distribution as δ\_⊥ through F̃; whether they are within tolerance is decided by Acc\_Π\_c, not by C2;

then P₁ ∪ P₂ satisfies Leg(P₁ ∪ P₂, τ\_c) ∧ N\_bear(P₁ ∪ P₂, τ\_c).

Proof. Leg₁: by Leg(P₁, τ₁) and Leg(P₂, τ₂), ρ\_in¹ and ρ\_out² are defined on state types of P₁ and P₂ marked out in advance by physical properties, and these types are also state types of the combined host; ρ\_out^c coincides with ρ\_out² upon normal completion, the triggering of the failure bypass is conditioned on types outside the definition of ρ\_out¹, and the failure output type Z\_⊥ is declared in advance along with τ\_c — all three are marked out by physical properties, before the runs of the composite under test; the runs of the composite are formed by chaining P₁'s runs to P₂'s runs through the coupling, and since neither reading scheme is marked out on the basis of the runs of its own host under test, neither is marked out on the basis of their chaining. Leg₂: by C0; the coupling and the failure bypass do not evaluate by proxy (C1). Prov: for d ∈ D, a delivery protocol π ∈ Prov\_τ₁(d) of P₁ is executable through P₁'s entry and fixed by d through ρ\_in¹ alone, and by C0 contains no evaluation of f\_c; hence π ∈ Prov\_τc(d). Prod: take any family of delivery protocols of P₁ and any family of admissible executions, under the combined operating conditions. By C1, P₁'s result distribution under the combined regime is the same as when it runs alone, and by P₁'s Prod its family of results satisfies Acc\_Π₁; by C2, the values of the read-outs that are not ⊥ all lie within D₂. For a history h whose read-out is ⊥, by C1 the failure bypass brings the combined host into Z\_⊥, and the composite read-out is ⊥, that is, F̃\_{s\_h}(⊥) = δ\_⊥. For every other history h, by C1 the coupling, conditional on h, constitutes one admissible execution σ of some declared protocol π of P₂ for v(h), and the random components and exogenous values on the side of P₂ follow its declared distributions; so P₂'s response under h is just μ\_{π,σ}. Take a response selection s\_h that takes π and σ at v(h) and is arbitrary at the other values; then P₂'s response under h is the member of F\_{s\_h} at v(h), and by P₂'s Prod, F\_{s\_h} satisfies Acc\_Π₂. Only Prod's assertion about the whole family is used here: a single conditional response is merely one member of an acceptable family, and is not asserted to pass on its own. The composite result on d is ∫ F̃\_{s\_h}(v(h)) P₁(dh \| d), and by C3 the composite family of results satisfies Acc\_Π\_c. The coupling and the failure bypass lie within the combined boundary and do not evaluate by proxy, and processes outside the combined boundary do not evaluate by proxy for P₁ or for P₂, so R\_B holds for the combined host; here "partial proxy evaluation" is determined relative to the decomposition of f\_c through the intermediate value v, and a criterion that does not depend on the decomposition belongs to H2 and H3 (Corollary One, second level). This completes the proof.

C0 does not follow from Leg at the two ends. Take P₁ to be a wire bearing the identity task τ₁: ρ\_in¹ reads the class of states "the input end carries two segments of code (d, w), w being the code of f\_c(d)" as d, and ρ\_out¹ reads the first segment at the output end. Delivery must write in f\_c(d), but requires no evaluation of f₁, the identity, and read-out applies nothing beyond the identity, so Leg(P₁, τ₁) holds; P₂ legitimately bears τ₂, reading only the first segment at its entry. Delivery for the composite task, however, requires finding f\_c(d): Leg₂ fails for τ\_c, and P₁'s delivery protocol does not belong to Prov\_τc(d). The legitimacy of the reading schemes of a single task is judged only relative to the function of that task; the composite task changes the function, so legitimacy must be verified anew for it.

Nor are C1–C3 superfluous. An approximate output of P₁ may meet Π₁ and yet fall outside the domain of P₂ (C2 fails); or it may fall within D₂ and yet be amplified by f₂ beyond the composite tolerance (C3 fails). Two devices that each pass can therefore compose into a family of results that does not pass. The clause on conditional distributions in C1 cannot be dispensed with either: let the two stages share one fair random bit; when it is 0, P₁ is correct and P₂ errs on the correct input; when it is 1, P₁ errs; each succeeds pointwise with probability ½, but the chained probability of success is 0 — the random component on the side of P₂ does not follow its declared distribution conditional on "P₁ reads out v." The clause of C3 on response selections indexed by history is likewise required: f₁ maps d₁ ≠ d₂ to the same v, P₁ records the operand internally, and the coupling accordingly uses different admissible executions of P₂ on d₁ and d₂, whose responses to the same v differ; if the composite were tested only against a single response selection independent of d, it could be judged to pass when in fact it does not. Under family protocols all the more so: when Π₂ tolerates failure on at most one operand, every F\_{s\_h} passes, yet the place of failure can fall on different v depending on h, and after composition the number of failing operands can exceed what Π\_c tolerates; C3 must test this family of selections as a whole.

Special case of exact deterministic tasks: if Π₁ and Π₂ both require the results to be concentrated on the function values, with no ⊥ tolerated, and V₁ ⊆ D₂, then C2 and C3 (taking Π\_c to be the exact protocol) hold automatically, and only C0 and C1 are needed. Special case of pointwise reliability: if Π₁ and Π₂ require the probability of reading out f₁(d) and f₂(v) to be at least r₁ and r₂ respectively, and C0 and C1 hold with V₁ ⊆ D₂, then the probability that the composite result is read as f₂(f₁(d)) is at least r₁r₂: P₁ gives f₁(d) with probability at least r₁; P₂'s Prod holds for all admissible executions of its delivery protocols, the delivery formed by the coupling is one of them, and under a pointwise protocol every member of an acceptable family satisfies the pointwise requirement; so under this condition P₂ gives f₂(f₁(d)) with probability at least r₂. For approximate tasks, C2 and C3 must be verified in their own right — for example, by requiring the domain of P₂ to cover the Π₁-tolerance neighbourhood of f₁(d), and by carrying the tolerances of Π₁ and Π₂ over to Π\_c through the modulus of continuity of f₂ on that neighbourhood.

The necessary structure of bearing is not lost under composition: compound operations, pipelines, distributed computation and changes of encoding across periods (the reading scheme of one period connected to the reading scheme of the next through a conversion subtask) are not wrongly excluded by Leg and N\_bear so long as they meet the conditions above. Whether the composed system bears its task is, as for a single system, given by the determination of bearing; Corollary Two does not derive this.

Corollary Three　Intrinsic insufficiency (under PP)

Under the physical-possibility premise PP, any condition that depends only on the operand-level trajectory (the sequence of states read through the reading schemes, together with any finite number of observed physical variables), if it holds for some stretch of running of some bearer, holds equally for some process that performs no operation.

Proof. By PP, the operand-level trajectory of that stretch of running can be produced by a process that performs no operation; the condition depends only on this trajectory, so it holds equally for that process. This completes the proof.

Hence the finite observed trajectory of a single stretch of running is insufficient to determine bearing. PP says only that one trajectory can be produced by a non-operating process; it does not guarantee that a non-operating process can replicate the whole family of counterfactual runs over all deliverable inputs, or the results under all environmental interventions; Corollary Three does not reach conditions of this whole-family kind. The Prod in N\_bear is exactly such a whole-family condition: it is a counterfactual condition over all admissible executions of the delivery protocols, and it assesses to whom the evaluation belongs by "no proxy evaluation." This corollary carries PP and is available for research on indistinguishability at the level of effects; results of the same kind already established within the group are Theorem One of Chapter 12, *The Distinction Form of Computing and Non-Computing Hosts* (restricted to that chapter's candidates C and N and its working domain), and Theorems Four and Five of Chapter 13, *The Distinction Form of Intervention in Generative Computation* (restricted to that chapter's working domain 𝒲₇ and Z1–Z4, W1′; the part on equivalence of Theorem Five carries no H, while its part on determination of items carries the conditions listed in that chapter's 12.1, namely Generative Computation General Form-H1, Computational Execution General Form-H1–H3 and Computational Bearing General Form-H1–H3, the same-run (Multi) pair additionally carrying Computational Execution General Form-H1 and H2); neither takes this corollary as a premise.

Corollary Four　Classification of faults

When a bearer permanently ceases to give acceptable results on some operands, it no longer bears τ on the original domain, but bears another task τ′, or bears τ on a reduced domain. When a bearer errs in some run while its family of results still satisfies Acc\_Π, it still bears τ, and this error is not a failure of bearing; whether, at the execution layer, it is a failed execution or no execution at all is decided by Chapter 3. Failure of the entry (Prov fails) and failure of production (Prod fails) are two distinct classes of failure of bearing; failure outside the operating conditions (such as loss of accuracy when temperature becomes unstable) is not a failure of bearing, but a failure of the operating conditions to be met. For use by Chapter 3.

Corollary Five　The residue of trivial bearing

For a Π-trivial task, any system that satisfies Prov and whose output region falls of itself into μ\* under R\_B satisfies N\_bear. For example, take a stone whose entry is arbitrary placement, with an output reading scheme that reads any of its states as 0. Under R\_B, the states the stone reaches by its own dynamics are all read out through ρ\_out as 0, the family of results satisfies the acceptance protocol of the constant task "output 0," neither delivery nor external processes evaluate by proxy, Prod holds in the sense fixed in Round Six, and N\_bear holds. Yet the stone's result has nothing to do with its dynamics: replace its evolution by any other, and the read-out is still 0; the value 0 is given by ρ\_out for every state, and no share of the evaluation of f falls on the stone. By A1, it does not bear this task. So N\_bear alone does not yield Bear. Whether this output reading scheme at the same time violates Leg₂ — whether reading every state as the unique value of f counts as applying f at read-out — is decided by H2; whether Leg ∧ N\_bear yields Bear is unanswered. The residue of trivial bearing is most complete for Π-trivial tasks: any system that has an entry and whose output region falls into μ\* satisfies N\_bear. Tasks that are not Π-trivial also have trivial residues: a stone at rest, with its placement as state type, placing as delivery and reading in place as read-out, bears the identity task "identity of position." For tasks that are not Π-trivial, the Derived Dependence Lemma guarantees only that the result depends on delivery, not that the host has any complex dynamics. Both kinds of residue are handed to Chapter 11.

## Section Eleven　Boundary Cases

A digital adder bears 8-bit addition: voltage intervals are the reading schemes, keying in is delivery, the circuit produces the sum under the declared operating conditions, and N\_bear holds.  
Table-lookup addition: the same task, borne by storage and addressing; N\_bear holds (Corollary One).  
An analogue integrator: Π declares an approximation, and N\_bear holds under the approximate protocol.  
A slide rule bears multiplication: the logarithmic scales are designed for the task and belong to the host; delivery and read-out use only the scales, and Leg₂ holds.  
The scale factors of an analogue computer: input and output each take scale factors according to the range of their quantities, and Leg₂ holds; if the ratio of the two scale factors absorbs a factor of f, what the host bears is the scaled task with that factor removed (Round Two).  
Residue representation, logarithmic representation and other representations chosen for a task: f is not evaluated in input or output, and Leg₂ holds.  
A person who can do mental arithmetic bears two-digit addition: numeral representations are the reading schemes, defined on representation types during the period in which the person has mastered the numerals, and formed in advance by the person's history of learning; the numbers heard are the delivery; reliability is declared by Π; the production is in the person's brain. N\_bear holds.  
Identity storage and constant tasks: N\_bear holds; for the former the result depends on delivery (Derived Dependence Lemma), the latter belongs to the residue of trivial bearing (Corollary Five).  
Devices with fixed ports, streamed input, distributed input, general-purpose machines that take programs as data, networks whose parameters are modulated by the input, and molecular computation that uses a fresh test tube for each run: N\_bear holds (Round Four).  
Distributed computation: N\_bear holds when the boundary includes all the nodes (Round Five).  
A device with a wide range of loads that can only be cooled adaptively according to temperature: the cooling varies with the input and cannot be replaced by fixed cooling, but does not evaluate by proxy; N\_bear holds under the declared operating conditions.  
A precision analogue calculator that reaches its accuracy only when the temperature is stable: N\_bear holds under the declared temperature stability; the loss of accuracy under widely fluctuating temperature is a failure of the operating conditions to be met (Corollary Four).  
A device executing a randomized algorithm that takes random numbers from an external physical random source: the random numbers contain no evaluation of f, the external random source does not evaluate by proxy, and N\_bear holds.  
Feed-forward cooling that reads the operand at the entry in advance and increases cooling beforehand: it reads the operand without evaluating f, does not evaluate by proxy, and N\_bear holds for P.  
A calculator whose input port has two modes of operation, one of which occasionally makes poor contact: reliability is measured according to the declared protocol (alternate use, or use of the reliable mode only), and if it passes, N\_bear holds; if the protocol leaves "always use the poor-contact mode" as a choice for the deliverer, it does not hold under that protocol, and holds under a more specific protocol (Round Six).  
The stone under run-by-run reading schemes, or under after-the-fact typing based on the runs under test: the claim of bearing is not legitimate (Leg₁). A trained neural network, and a brain that has mastered the numerals: the types are formed by prior history, before the runs under test, and Leg₁ holds.  
The wire with a reading scheme that contains the answer, and the copying device under an output reading scheme that applies f: the claim of bearing is not legitimate (Leg₂).  
The broken-key adder with respect to 8-bit addition: Prov fails; it bears addition on the domain of operands not containing the digit 7.  
The solar system with respect to advancement under celestial mechanics, the domain taking arbitrary configurations: Prov fails.  
The noisy device and the single-sample record: Prod fails.  
The permanently faulty adder with respect to addition: Prod fails; it bears τ′ (Corollary Four).  
The proxy-evaluating black box: external proxy evaluation, and Prod fails; when the boundary is the box together with the human calculator, the bearer is the two combined.  
The "cooler" that reads the temperature distribution, decodes the operand and prints the answer by means of cooling: external proxy evaluation, and Prod fails; when the boundary is the box together with the cooler, the bearer is the two combined.  
A whole table of answers broadcast from outside, from which the host selects according to the operand: the values in the table were found outside the host, and the results depend on them, so this is external proxy evaluation; the boundary must include the source of the broadcast, or the table must be made part of the host's prestored configuration.  
Microstate selection: proxy evaluation at delivery; under universality over unspecified choices, Prod fails.  
Waiting for the solar system to pass through some configuration of its own accord and then starting the clock: the moment of starting must be found from the orbital relations of the solar system, and this finding rests on exactly the dynamics the task expresses; the delivery protocol contains an evaluation of f, and by Round Six it is not legitimate delivery. As a boundary case between computation and natural processes, its full determination as computation is to be taken up by further work on *The Determination Form of Computation* and is not yet established within the group.  
A fluid in an experimental tank: the initial flow field can be set and the flow velocity read out on a grid; under a declared task, reading schemes and delivery protocol it can satisfy N\_bear, that is, bear the time advancement of the fluid equations. Bearing is not a mark of computation; for whether the tank is at the moment a reproduction of physical dynamics or has been organized into an analogue computing apparatus, Chapter 12, *The Distinction Form of Computing and Non-Computing Hosts*, gives a distinguishing observation (restricted to that chapter's candidates and working domain); the full determination is to be taken up by further work on Chapter 11, *The Determination Form of Computation*, and is not yet established within the group. A natural vortex has no entry, and Prov fails. The initial stipulation lists reproduction of a vortex with the same kind of matter as non-computational reproduction; the necessary structure of bearing is compatible with this classification, and does not change the initial stipulation's division between computational and non-computational reproduction.  
The encoding of a reading scheme is itself complex, so that some class of functions has already been partly evaluated in the reading scheme: this is the proxy evaluation by reading schemes excluded by Leg₂; the exact line at which an encoding's share of the work of evaluation constitutes proxy evaluation belongs to H2; *The Determination Form of Computation* gives only the verdict of bearing ⊥\_H2 for this case (that form, 7.5), and the exact line is not yet established within the group.

The standing of each case: the positive and negative cases listed above all keep this chapter's conditions of entry, and are tests of the general theorem, not breaches of it; the grounds for classifying the two items of waiting for the moment and encoding-borne evaluation are as stated above, and their full determination as computation is to be taken up by further work on *The Determination Form of Computation* and is not yet established within the group.

## Section Twelve　Declaration of Gaps

Computational Bearing General Form-H1　Formal criterion for state types marked out in advance. Content: to give the formal conditions for the two requirements in Leg₁ — "each type is given by a physically identifiable condition on P (no lower bound on granularity)" and "before and independently of the runs under test (neither on the basis of those runs nor on the basis of run-by-run prediction or simulation of them)" — so as to distinguish legitimate physical typing (including arbitrary enumeration, table lookup, types formed by prior history such as learning, training and manufacture, and design simulation by category not aimed at individual runs) from typing based on the runs under test or on run-by-run prediction of them. Supports: the determination of Leg₁ on specific claims of bearing. Responsible: The Computation Group-Form, Chapter 4, *The Mathematical Form of Computation* (a task for a Mathematical Form). Present state: pending verification.

Computational Bearing General Form-H2　Formal criteria for proxy evaluation by reading schemes and in delivery. Content: to give the formal conditions for "delivery requires no evaluation of f, read-out requires no application of f" in Leg₂, including the condition of consistency between the representations on the input and output sides (distinguishing representations chosen for the task from representations that apply f at read-out); to give the formal conditions for a delivery protocol's being "fixed by d and the reading schemes alone, containing no evaluation of f," and the formal distinction between random components and unspecified choices in a protocol. In addition, to give a criterion, not dependent on an evaluation decomposition, for "read-out or delivery takes on part of f." Supports: the determination, on specific claims of bearing, of Leg₂ and of the delivery-protocol clauses in Prod, and the second level of Corollary One. Responsible: The Computation Group-Form, Chapter 4, *The Mathematical Form of Computation* (a task for a Mathematical Form); for the boundary case of the degree to which an encoding shares the work, *The Determination Form of Computation* gives only the verdict of bearing ⊥\_H2 (that form, 7.5), and the exact line is not yet established within the group. Present state: pending verification.

Computational Bearing General Form-H3　Formal criterion for external proxy evaluation. Content: to give the formal conditions for "a process acting on the host from outside its boundary after delivery carries out the evaluation of f, or part of it, and the result depends on that work," distinguishing it from external maintenance, external random sources, external synchronization and other actions that take part in the running without evaluating; to give a criterion, not dependent on an evaluation decomposition, for "an external process takes on part of f"; and to give the norm for declaring the operating conditions Env, including a criterion for the prohibition on narrowing Env, in order to obtain bearing, to exactly those conditions under which the results pass. Supports: the determination of R\_B on specific claims of bearing and the second level of Corollary One, that is, distinguishing physical maintenance of the host's operation by external action from evaluation of the target function. Responsible: The Computation Group-Form, Chapter 4, *The Mathematical Form of Computation* (a task for a Mathematical Form); for the clause on external proxy evaluation, that chapter's Definition 3.18 gives a candidate formal criterion; for the clause on the norm for declaring Env, that chapter gives no criterion, and it is not yet established within the group. Present state: pending verification.

All three support determinations, not the reasoning of the general theorem: the general theorem holds unconditionally, Leg and R\_B being understood in the sense stated in this chapter; the determination of whether a specific claim satisfies Leg, whether a given delivery protocol contains an evaluation, and whether a given external action evaluates by proxy carries the corresponding H.

Further matters (the conclusions of this chapter do not depend on them): the measure-theoretic formalization of Acc\_Π and of result distributions; the measure-theoretic characterization of the random components of delivery protocols; the evidential standards for bearing; the full determination of computation versus natural physical processes. The first two are given by Chapter 4 on finite-support models (that chapter's working domain W2), while the cases of infinite domains and continuous time are not yet established within the group; the evidential standards for bearing are partly taken over by Chapter 11; the full determination of computation versus natural physical processes is to be taken up by further work on Chapter 11 and is not yet established within the group.

## Section Thirteen　Breach Argument

Ways of breach

With Leg holding, every item declared, and the corresponding H conditions maintained, exhibit a host that bears τ but has no executable delivery protocol for some d ∈ D; or one that, for some family of admissible executions of its delivery protocols, under the declared operating conditions and with no external proxy evaluation, produces of itself a family of results that does not satisfy Acc\_Π, while it bears τ by A1–A5. Either case constitutes a breach of the general theorem.

Against the legitimacy condition: exhibit a host that bears τ by A1–A5 and whose bearing can hold only under reading schemes that violate Leg₁ or Leg₂.

Against minimality: prove that Prov can be derived from Prod together with Leg (under this chapter's universal formulation); or prove that one of the three places within Prod — coverage, R\_B and the universality over unspecified choices — can be removed with bearing still closed; or exhibit an external process or deliverer that really does carry out an evaluation of f on which the result depends, while the host by A1–A5 still bears τ on its own; or prove that some proposed addition opens a constraint on the task not treated in this chapter.

Against the local results: exhibit a bearer of a task that is not Π-trivial whose result, for some family of admissible executions, is the same for all d.

Not breaches: exhibiting a system that satisfies N\_bear but is not a computing host (the tank, the trivial residue), which shows that N\_bear is insufficient to determine a computing host, not that Leg ∧ N\_bear is not necessary; exhibiting a system that satisfies Leg ∧ N\_bear but does not bear τ, which answers the unanswered converse Leg ∧ N\_bear ⇒ Bear and breaches neither the necessary direction nor the indispensability of the components; exhibiting another physical realization of the same N\_bear; exhibiting a "bearing" that violates Leg, which falls among the illegitimate claims excluded by this chapter; exhibiting failure of a host outside its declared operating conditions.

## Section Fifteen　Closing

Compressed finally into three sentences:

That a task can be handed to the host by the rules is the first half of bearing;  
that, once it is handed over, no one computes on the host's behalf, and the host brings it by itself, under the conditions it should have, to the standard the task requires, is the second half of bearing;  
if the reading schemes, the deliverer or an external process does the problem for the host, there can be no talk of the host's bearing it.

Compressed again into one sentence:

For a host to bear a mathematical task is for every operand to be deliverable to it by the declared rules, and, once delivered, for a result that meets the task to be produced by the host's own dynamics, and not by anything else.
