# The General Form of Computational Execution

This chapter attempts a general proposition:

For a host P that bears a mathematical task τ to actually execute τ once within a time interval T, what must it minimally have?

Being able to do a task and doing it are two different things. *The General Form of Computational Bearing* has already given the first: what a host must minimally have in order to bear a mathematical task. Here we ask about the second: when a host that already bears a task actually executes that task once over some stretch of time, what must minimally be present for this to hold?

Execution is one exercise of bearing, not a mark of computation. That a host has executed some task does not thereby make it a computing host; whether the result of an execution is correct is another matter as well. Neither enters the necessary structure of execution.

In this chapter we shall find, through persistent questioning, where the proof must go, and derive the general form of computational execution.

Our objective is:

taking bearing as the antecedent, to obtain the minimal necessary structure of one actual execution, together with a proof of minimality and the ways of breach. The structure must accommodate executions that compute correctly and executions that compute wrongly, transformations in place and out of place, identity and constant tasks, no-operations that are actually executed, failed executions under internal data corruption and under output-stage faults, and free-running devices; and it must separate these from switching off, mere holding, the case in which the arithmetic part has stalled and the output is rewritten by noise, piecing together across instances, and the like.

Let us, then, give the result first.

The general form of the minimal structure of computational execution

Given that Bear(P, τ) holds within the declared period Θ and T ⊆ Θ:

　　Exec(P, τ, T) ⇒ ∃r ⊆ T: Ini(r) ∧ Run(r) ∧ Out(r)

where Run(r) = En(r) ∧ ¬Sub(r) ∧ Ex\_α(r) ∧ Ex\_β(r), and Ex\_β holds trivially when τ is a Π-trivial task.

That is: there exists a run r — a continuous stretch of actual process on one and the same instance — such that: at its starting point the event of the initiation part of a declared delivery protocol of τ occurs, and the host is in some state type read as d (Ini); from initiation to read-out, the part in which the bearing resides operates only under the enabling conditions, and when it stops midway its state is held by the host itself and the run is continued by the same instance (En); in this run there is no proxy evaluation at any of the three places — reading scheme, delivery, external action (¬Sub); the transforming part that bears τ is driven by this initiation (Ex\_α); for a non-trivial task, the part driven includes the part that carries the data (Ex\_β); for a free-running device, which has no initiation event, the two clauses become instead that the corresponding parts continue by their own dynamics under the enabling conditions; and the run obtains, at the declared read-out condition, a state read through the reading scheme, whether correct or not (Out). The three items are witnessed by one and the same run; this is the scope of the existential quantifier ∃r. The necessity of the two clauses Ex\_α and Ex\_β is conditional on Computational Execution General Form-H3.

The above is the result to be proved; the formal statement and proof are in Section Eleven.

In One Sentence

To execute once is, at the least, for this occasion really to set going the transformation that is borne — and, for a non-trivial task, for the part of the dynamics that carries the data to be set going with it (these two clauses conditional on H3) — and to carry it all the way to read-out; whether it is done right or wrong is another matter.

Abstract

*The General Form of Computational Bearing* has already shown that if a host bears a mathematical task given in advance, then necessarily its reading schemes are legitimate (Leg), every operand can be delivered by a declared protocol (Prov), and, after delivery, under the declared operating conditions and with external non-proxy, the host's own dynamics produces a family of results that satisfies the acceptance protocol (Prod). Bearing is a disposition and does not require running at the moment.

From here we ask what more one actual execution requires. Bearing does not yield execution: a switched-off calculator and a person fast asleep both bear their tasks without executing them. That the host is operating does not yield execution either: in a computer that is switched on, an idle register can be flipped by noise into a "correct" reading; so initiation is required (Ini). That the power is cut right after initiation while the display retains an old number, and that a person outside the box computes the answer separately and overwrites it, show that operation must take place only under the enabling conditions, that when it stops midway the state must be held by the host and the run continued by it (En), and that there must be no proxy evaluation on this occasion (¬Sub). That the initiation signal is lost while the adder logic is processing the previous queued instruction, whose result happens to be written to the display, shows that the transforming part that bears the task must be driven by this initiation (Ex\_α). That the control timing is set going while the adder logic stands completely still and the display is rewritten by noise shows that, for a non-trivial task, what is driven must include the part that carries the data (Ex\_β); whereas, on a device whose initiation and content are separate, an output-stage fault, an internal data path that corrupts the operands, and adder logic that operates but momentarily loses its response to the data all satisfy this clause and are failed executions. A run interrupted midway has no read-out; under the counting convention that "one execution is one complete run from initiation to read-out," reaching read-out (Out) is obtained, together with the necessary structure of "execution has occurred." The initiation, operation and read-out of two calculators can be pieced together into one, so binding to one and the same run is required. Correctness of the result, the source of the operands, the mechanism pathway, change of state, dependence of the read-out, response to the data, intention and observation are each tested and found not to require a separate item.

The general theorem is in the necessary direction. The necessity of Ini, En, ¬Sub, Out and same-run binding holds relative to the antecedents E1–E6 of the concept of execution listed in Section Two and to the declared counting convention; the necessity of Ex\_α and Ex\_β is conditional on Computational Execution General Form-H3. The determination of initiation and enablement, and of the same run, further depends on Computational Execution General Form-H1 and H2; the determination of reading schemes and of proxy evaluation inherits, along with the antecedent of bearing, Computational Bearing General Form-H1–H3.

Degree of Answer

First edition. The initial question on entering the proof is this: on the bearing of Chapter 2, the antecedents of the concept of execution and the task-side non-degeneracy condition alone, does "P executed τ once in T" already hold? The subquestions actually generated in the course of the inquiry, and the standing of their answers, are as follows.

First: does bearing yield execution? No; the host must be operating and obtain successors by its own dynamics (Section Three; this initial form is made precise by the En, ¬Sub and Ex of Sections Five to Seven). Proved.  
Second: does the host's operating yield execution? No; initiation is required (Section Four). Proved.  
Third: does initiation together with operation yield execution? No; operation must be under the enabling conditions, with no proxy evaluation on this occasion (Section Five). Proved.  
Fourth: do initiation, operation under the enabling conditions and absence of proxy evaluation yield the exercise of bearing? No; the transforming part must be driven by this initiation (Section Six), and, for a non-trivial task, what is driven must include the part that carries the data (Section Seven); for a free-running device the two clauses become continuation by its own dynamics. Conditional on H3.  
Fifth: must "executing τ once" reach read-out? Under the declared counting convention it must; the necessary structure of the occurrence of execution is given separately as Corollary One (Section Eight). Proved relative to the counting convention.  
Sixth: can the items be witnessed by different runs? No; they must be witnessed by one and the same run (Section Nine). Proved.  
Seventh: must correctness of the result, the source of the operands, a particular mechanism pathway, change of state content, dependence of the read-out, the read-out's being obtained by the transforming part driven on this occasion, response of the data-carrying part to the data, prior declaration or intention, or observation of the read-out be set up separately? No (Section Ten). Proved.  
General conclusion: the necessary direction. The necessity of Ini, En, ¬Sub, Out and the same run is proved (relative to E1–E6 and the counting convention); the necessity of the two clauses of Ex is conditional on H3; minimality is indispensability relative to the items forced (Section Fourteen), and restricted minimality within the family of admissible candidate structures is unanswered (U4); whether Ini ∧ Run ∧ Out yields execution is unanswered (U1).

Introduction

To say that a machine "is doing addition" can mean four different things: that it can do addition; that it is doing it now; that it has just finished one sum; or that the sum it did is right. The first is bearing, the second is the occurrence of execution, the third is one complete execution, and the fourth is correctness. A fifth is often mixed in as well: whether what it computes counts as real computation — that is determination.

In older ways of speaking, "execution" is often described as "running through the program" or "giving the correct answer." The former ties execution to one particular kind of realization, the latter ties it to the result. Yet a calculator that computes wrongly really has executed addition; a person who makes a mistake in mental arithmetic really has computed; an analogue device with no program, only wiring, is also executing its task. On the other hand, a calculator whose adder circuit has stalled completely and whose display has had a number written onto it by noise has not executed addition, even if that number happens to be right.

The minimal content of execution lies between these two groups of cases.

## Section One　Preliminary Distinctions

(1) Execution and some neighbouring matters

Execution and bearing. Bearing is a disposition; execution is one exercise of that disposition. A switched-off calculator bears addition without executing it.

Execution and the occurrence of execution. One execution runs from initiation to read-out; the occurrence of execution means that, as of some moment, execution has occurred — it is under way, or has taken place. A run interrupted midway may be a case in which execution has occurred without one execution having been completed; the two are constrained by different conditions: reaching read-out constrains only one complete execution, not the occurrence of execution (for the counting convention see E6 in Section Two).

Execution and correctness. Execution can go wrong: gate faults, internal data corruption, output-stage faults and running outside the specification conditions can all occur in execution. Whether the result is correct is a comparison of the read-out with f(d) according to Π.

Executing τ and executing τ′. A permanently faulty adder no longer bears addition; it bears another task τ′, or bears addition on a reduced domain (Chapter 2, Corollary Four); its runs are judged by what it bears.

Execution and determination. The structure of execution does not determine whether a host is a computing host, nor whether some process counts as computation.

(2) Objects and terms

The following definitions only specify the objects; the standing of each as necessary structure is proved in Sections Three to Ten.

Host P, task τ = (D, f, ρ\_in, ρ\_out, Π), reading schemes, acceptance protocol Acc\_Π, boundary B, entry, read-out (including the read-out conditions), delivery protocol, operating conditions Env, declared period Θ, proxy evaluation, Π-trivial task: all as in Chapter 2.

Initiation part and content part. The part of the events of a delivery protocol that makes the transformation of τ begin is called the initiation part — for example, pressing the "=" key, issuing an instruction, posing a problem; the rest is the content part. The initiation part is identified by "actually making the transformation begin in normal running," not by outward appearance; whether this event occurs on the present occasion and whether it actually sets going the transformation that follows are two different things. Where several tasks borne by one host share an initiation event (the "=" shared by addition, subtraction, multiplication and division on a calculator; the clock shared by the instructions of a processor), the task-selection event (the operation key, the opcode) belongs to the initiation part. Where delivery is spread over time (bit-serial input; doing mental arithmetic while still hearing the problem), the initiation part is the beginning of the delivery process. Where the initiation part contains several events, t₀ is taken as the moment of its completion; where the content part has been delivered in full before t₀ (the "2+3=" of a calculator), Ini is assessed on the state at t₀; otherwise it is treated as delivery spread over time. The initiation part of a combinational circuit (one that begins computing as soon as the operands arrive through the entry) is just the arrival of the operands, and the content part coincides with it; to declare as initiation part an "=" that afterwards merely gates the result onto the display is a misdeclaration. For a device declared as free-running, the initiation part is empty.

Enabling conditions and specification conditions. Among the operating conditions Env, those without which the part in which the host's bearing resides does not operate at all (power supply, clock, wakefulness) are called enabling conditions; those whose failure does not stop the host from operating, but within which it must stay in order to meet the acceptance protocol (temperature and its stability and the like), are called specification conditions. A maximal time interval over which the enabling conditions fail is called an enabling gap.

Run r. A continuous stretch of actual physical process on one instance of the host P (when P is declared as a class of systems prepared by protocol, one instance in that class), with a starting point t₀ and an end point.

Realizing part Real\_τ(P). The components and processes of P through which, in normal running under the declared protocols, the declared results are obtained: if only this part is retained (together with what enabling requires) and the rest is rendered inoperative, Prod still holds. Bypasses that act on the read-out without taking part in obtaining the result (debugging lines, monitoring pathways) are not included; components that only keep the rest operating, and themselves neither carry operands nor produce the read-out state (on-chip voltage regulation, temperature control), belong to what enabling requires and are not included — active maintenance that produces the read-out state for a constant task (a reference voltage source) is not of this kind. Redundant realizing parts each count as a separate realizing part; where there are several, the one used by the present run is meant. Where operands are dispatched to different pathways (a cache hit versus bit-by-bit computation, a fast path versus a general path, a memorized result versus long-hand working), the pathways together with the dispatching action count as one.

Reception and holding; transforming part. Within the realizing part, what merely takes in delivery or holds operands or results (input latches, the holding action of registers) is called reception and holding; the rest — what, in normal running, carries the held operands to the result by its dynamics — is called the transforming part, written Tr\_τ(P). The transforming part of an adder is its adder logic and timing; that of a lookup device is addressing and read-out; that of an in-place identity device is its control and write-back action; that of a brain is the neural process that bears mental arithmetic. Tr\_τ(P) may be empty.

Data-carrying part. The part of the transforming part that, in normal running, carries the operands and makes the result vary with them. Dispatch rule: for tasks outside the identity class, if the pathway dispatched to on the present occasion itself carries the operands and, by a transformation other than preservation-class obtaining, alone gives the result of τ on the present operands, then the dispatching action only decides which pathway gives the result, and, together with the transfer action that moves the operands to that pathway's entry, belongs to reception; the data-carrying part is then the part of that pathway that carries the operands. Otherwise — when the pathway dispatched to does not carry the operands (each pathway gives a constant), or merely transfers the operands unchanged (moving the selected one to the read-out when taking the maximum), or the dispatching action gives part of the result (determining the sign of the result by comparison in sign-magnitude addition and subtraction) — the dispatching action, together with the part of the dispatched pathway that carries the operands, is jointly the data-carrying part, of the same kind as the addressing of a lookup device.

Preservation-class obtaining and the identity class. Preserving, moving or copying the operands unchanged; selection and rearrangement at fixed positions independent of the values of the operands; and giving content independent of the operands (a constant, or a random value independent of the operands; selection may depend on such random components) — these are together called preservation-class obtaining; the term names a kind of dynamics. The identity class is a class of tasks delimited by the acceptance protocol: the tasks for which there is a family of result distributions, obtained by preservation-class obtaining, that satisfies Acc\_Π. Identity, transposition, projection, copying and positional permutation fall within it; Π-trivial tasks fall within it; and so do tasks whose target function is none of these but whose acceptance protocol tolerates preservation-class results — for example, D = {0, 10}, f(d) = d + 1, with a tolerance of 1 at each point, for which preserving the operand unchanged passes. Tasks whose target functions select according to values (taking the maximum, sorting, fetching with an operand as index) are not within it when the acceptance protocol does not tolerate preservation-class results. The kind of the target function, the kind of results the acceptance protocol tolerates, and the kind of dynamics of the realizing part are three different things; the identity class is delimited by the second. For tasks in the identity class, the action that, under the declared protocol, is set going by initiation and writes the result to the read-out position — transfer or write-back (MOV, the write of a store instruction, write-back in place), or the arithmetic logic actually applied in the realization together with its writing out — is the transforming part, and belongs to the data-carrying part; where the declared protocol contains no such action (static storage in which delivery is placement and read-out is reading in place; the stone bearing positional identity in Chapter 2, Corollary Five), the transforming part is empty.

P's actual condition on the present occasion, and the counterfactual. P's actual condition on the present occasion is the persisting condition of P during the present run, including damage, sticking and faults; transient external disturbances (high-energy particles, glitches) are not part of the persisting condition. The counterfactual "if there were no initiation on this occasion" removes only the initiation part that occurs at the starting point of r; the parts of the content part of the delivery protocol that do not coincide with it, and P's actual condition on the present occasion, are held fixed. "The evolution would be different" is reckoned by the declared state types of the transforming part and by its taking up of the present initiation and the operands; incidental differences that the initiation brings, through enabling pathways such as power supply, timing and crosstalk, to parts already operating are not counted.

Driving. To say that the present initiation drives some part is to say that the present initiation is one of the actual causes of that part's evolution on the present occasion — the relation forced in Section Six by E1 and the failure cases. When r contains no other event that independently triggers that part, the relation has a definite counterfactual criterion: under P's actual condition on the present occasion, if there were no initiation on this occasion (in the counterfactual sense above), the evolution of that part would be different. When r contains another event, not brought about by the present initiation, that independently triggers that part, the criterion no longer coincides with actual causation. Where both triggers actually set the part going (redundancy), removing the present initiation alone may leave the evolution as it was, and the criterion would wrongly exclude real driving. Where the other trigger has already set the part going and the present initiation arrives without effect (preemption), if one first takes the other trigger as not occurring and then compares, the evolution would be different, and the criterion would wrongly admit an initiation that had no effect. The general criterion under redundancy and preemption belongs to H3(c).

## Section Two　Working Domain and Boundary of Inheritance

The following serves as what is currently known.

First, *The General Form of Computational Bearing* v1.0 (hereafter Chapter 2): the general theorem Bear(P, τ) ⇒ Leg(P, τ) ∧ Prov(P, τ) ∧ Prod(P, τ); the delimitation of proxy evaluation — a link outside the host carries out the evaluation of f, or part of it, and whether the result passes depends on that work — together with non-proxy at the three places of reading scheme, delivery and external action after delivery, and the point that whatever takes the host together with that link as its boundary is another host; the antecedent A4 of the concept of bearing (bearing τ is a qualification extending to every operand in D); the operating conditions Env and the declared items; Corollary One (a task has as its identity the extension of f on D, and algorithm and configuration take no part in task identity), Corollary Two (when Leg ∧ N\_bear holds at both ends, Leg and N\_bear are closed under composition under C0–C3; the composite reading scheme includes a failure bypass for when P₁'s read-out is ⊥), Corollary Four (a permanent fault changes the task borne), Corollary Five (objects of the static-storage kind can bear a positional-identity task); and the Derived Dependence Lemma (when τ is not a Π-trivial task, the family of results of a host bearing τ varies with the operand, and the evaluation in this dependence is borne by the host's own dynamics). These are inherited together with Computational Bearing General Form-H1, H2 and H3, under conditional closure.

Second, the antecedents of the concept of execution. Exec(P, τ, T) means "P executes τ once in T," and ExecOcc(P, τ, t) means "as of moment t, P's execution of τ has occurred." The concept of execution is delimited by the following antecedents, relative to which the necessity conclusions below hold:

　E1 (exercise)　Executing τ is one exercise of P's bearing of τ: the transformation that P bears — making some operand in D into a result — actually takes place once according to the declared protocol of τ, and the part of the host's dynamics that bears τ is put to use on this occasion (for a device declared as free-running, the transformation proceeds continuously, and "once" is reckoned by the stretch under discussion). Bearing τ is a qualification extending to every operand in D (Chapter 2, A4); one exercise of it is an exercise on one of those operands. The operand on this occasion need not be handed in by a deliverer: it may have been computed by a previous step, or be garbage or uninitialized (S8).  
　E2 (continuous obtaining)　In execution (whether or not completed), the process from the starting point onwards is one run r proceeding continuously on one and the same instance, and the read-out state (if read-out is reached) is obtained by r continuously from its starting point: each successor is either obtained from the preceding state by the operation of the part of P in which the bearing resides, or held by P while operation is stopped. Transient external disturbances, or other processes on the same instance, may rewrite the states obtained or held, and the result may go wrong as a consequence; the rewritten states still count as states of r. Where the process continues after operation has stopped, the point of continuation must be a state of r held by P; where a reset or the like makes the process start again from an initial state not belonging to r, what follows is not a continuation of r.  
　E3 (no proxy evaluation)　Where a link outside P carries out the evaluation of f, or part of it, and the read-out's passing depends on that work, the run is not an execution by P. This is the same requirement as Chapter 2's delimitation of proxy evaluation, applied to a single run.  
　E4 (actual occurrence)　Execution includes some process fact that occurs because of this execution and would not occur without it; this fact need not be a change of state content: an executed identity, a write-back of the same value, an actually executed no-operation may leave the state content the same before and after.  
　E5 (independence of result)　The delimitation of execution does not include the result's passing: a wrong result, an internal fault or running outside the specification conditions does not by itself deprive a run of the qualification of execution.  
　E6 (counting)　"Executing once" means one complete run from initiation to read-out, undertaken by one run.

E1 says what is exercised: a transformation proceeding according to the declared protocol and taking some operand as its object, in which the dynamics that bears is put to use. It does not say what individuates this occasion's transformation, or from where in a run it is reckoned; nor does it say what relation "put to use on this occasion" comes to at the level of runs. The former is forced by the failure cases of Section Four: from operation and readings alone one cannot tell whether a stretch of process belongs to some transformation of τ, and the initiation event in the protocol that makes the transformation begin must be taken as the starting point of the occasion (Ini). The latter is forced by the failure cases of Sections Six and Seven: that the dynamics that bears is operating does not mean that it is put to use on this occasion; this occasion's initiation must actually have caused its operation (Ex\_α), and, for a non-trivial task, what it causes to operate must also include the data-carrying part (Ex\_β). The clause of Ini "the starting point is read as some d" is the direct spelling-out of E1's "taking some operand as its object," not a part that is forced.

E1–E5 are the content of the concept of execution; E6 is the declaration of a counting convention: an instance of τ is the making of one operand into one result in the range, and counting must say where one occasion is finished; this convention does not follow from the word "once." That a read-out happens to agree with f(d) does not thereby make a run an execution (E1, E2).

Third, the standard positive cases of execution. The following cases are executions, used as antecedents; the positive classifications in the proof of minimality (Section Fourteen) and in the boundary cases (Section Thirteen) hold relative to them.

　S1　A gate fault: the adder logic operates as usual but gives a wrong number, reaching read-out.  
　S2　On a device whose initiation and content are separate, the operands are changed to 0 by an internal data path before entering the adder logic, and the adder logic, once initiated, operates as usual on 0.  
　S3　The adder logic operates normally, and an output-stage fault makes the display show 0 for any input.  
　S4　On a device whose initiation and content are separate, the adder logic is initiated and the clock and internal operation proceed as usual, but a transient fault makes its action on this occasion independent of the data sent to it, and it finally gives 0.  
　S5　Overheating during running, the temperature leaving the specification conditions, and a wrong result.  
　S6　Clock gating or power gating of idle components midway through a computation; a static circuit whose clock is stopped and later continues; a hand-cranked calculator whose cranking stops midway; recovery from a checkpoint on the same instance; pencil-and-paper working continued overnight (pencil and paper within the declared boundary); power cut after the result has been latched in a non-volatile read-out position, the read-out condition arriving during the power cut.  
　S7　Midway through an operation a transient external disturbance flips a bit in the adder logic, and the adder logic operates as usual but gives a wrong number.  
　S8　Operating on garbage or an uninitialized register: the state of the register falls on some type of ρ\_in, its content unknown, or not deliberately given by a deliverer.  
　S9　An executed identity in place x ← x, a MOV, the write of a store instruction.  
　S10　A stretch of normal working of a free-running analogue integrator, including times when the input is exactly zero and the charge does not change.  
　S11　Unattended batch computation; spontaneous mental arithmetic.  
　S12　Runs of hosts that bear the same task through different realizations: circuit summation, table lookup, mental arithmetic, mechanical gears.  
　S13　The read-out position is rewritten by another process on the same instance, while the present run itself completes.  
The faults or errors in S1–S5, S7 and S13 do not, by E5, deprive the runs of the qualification of execution; the holding and continuation in S6 and S7 accord with E2; the unchanged state content in S9 accords with E4.

Fourth, the task-side conditions: Bear(P, τ) holds within the declared period Θ, and T ⊆ Θ; the declared items of Chapter 2, together with the finer declarations of the delivery protocol (initiation part and content part) and of the operating conditions (enabling conditions and specification conditions).

Task-side non-degeneracy condition: execution is not something that holds over every time interval merely because the host bears the task — there are cases in which Bear(P, τ) holds in T while P does not execute τ in T. It contains no component of the structure to be proved.

Apart from the inherited items above, operation, initiation, operation under enablement, absence of proxy evaluation on this occasion, driving, the data part's being driven, reaching read-out and the same run are all structures or conditions forced by the inquiry below; their necessity is proved in Sections Three to Ten.

Line of Argument

We proceed by persistent questioning, one step at a time. Each section takes everything obtained so far as what is currently known, and asks the same question again: is the characterization of P's executing τ once in T already closed by what has been obtained so far?

## Section Three　On bearing alone, does execution already hold?

The starting point is only this: P bears τ within Θ, and T ⊆ Θ; the general theorem and the declared items of Chapter 2; the antecedents E1–E6 of the concept of execution; the task-side non-degeneracy condition. On these alone, does "P executed τ once in T" already hold?

Take a calculator that bears 8-bit addition and is switched off throughout T; take a person who can do two-digit mental addition and is fast asleep throughout T. Both bear their tasks in T. If the switched-off calculator and the sleeping person also counted as executing, execution would hold for every bearer over every time interval, contrary to the non-degeneracy condition; these two are exactly cases of bearing without execution. Bearing does not yield execution.

Look again inside the switched-off calculator: its registers are not entirely without change, and thermal noise can flip a bit. So it cannot be said either that "whenever the host undergoes physical change in T, that is execution": leakage and bit flips while switched off are not the operation of the transforming part, and by E1 are not an exercise of bearing.

What has been obtained is not closed. E1 and E2 point to the same thing: the host is operating, and actually obtains successor states by its own dynamics.

To exclude switching off and sleep, it must be required that there is a stretch of process in T during which the enabling conditions of the part in which the bearing resides hold (the host is operating), and the host obtains successor states by its own dynamics — it is required only that there be such successors, not that every successor be so obtained; a successor may have the same value as the preceding state (E4).

This gives the initial form of operation: the enabling conditions hold, and successors are obtained by the host's own dynamics. This form does not enter the general formula directly: its first half is fixed as En in Section Five; its second half is made precise by the ¬Sub of Section Five and the Ex of Sections Six and Seven. The reason for taking only the enabling conditions, and not all the operating conditions, is given in Section Five. The sleeping person: if the declared enabling conditions include wakefulness, the case is excluded by this form; otherwise it is excluded by the initiation of Section Four.

## Section Four　With the host operating, is there already an execution?

The initial form of operation — the enabling conditions hold, and successors are obtained by the host's own dynamics — has been obtained.

Take a computer that is switched on and bears 8-bit addition, and is at the moment running some other program. The register that holds the output of addition is not currently taking part in any computation; under the reading scheme of τ, the two input registers of addition happen to be read as the operand d = (2, 3), and a bit of the output register is flipped by thermal noise so that it happens to be read as f(d) = 5. The enabling conditions hold, the host is running another program by its own dynamics (the initial form holds), the register has actually obtained a successor state, and the reading is even "correct."

This is not an execution of τ: no event of the delivery protocol of τ has occurred, the stretch of process belongs to no transformation of τ, and the adder circuit did not act on this register; the agreement of the reading is coincidence. What has been obtained does not exclude it, because what has been obtained asks only whether the host is operating and has successors, not whether those successors belong to some transformation of τ.

By E1, execution is the actual taking place of one transformation of τ according to the declared protocol. According to the declared protocol, a transformation begins with the part of the protocol's events that makes the transformation begin, and that part is the initiation part (Preliminary Distinctions); whether a process belongs to some transformation of τ must be settled by it. So it must be required that execution has a starting point: at some moment t₀ ∈ T, the event of the initiation part of a declared delivery protocol of τ occurs. Again by E1, this occasion is a transformation of some operand, so the starting state is read as some d. Where the content part has been delivered according to protocol, the state delivered is read as some d by Prov and Leg of Chapter 2; where the content part is not handed in by a deliverer (S8), its being read as d is given by the starting state's falling on a type of ρ\_in. Ini contains only the occurrence of the initiation event; whether it actually set going the transformation that follows is constrained by the Ex of Sections Six and Seven. For a device declared as free-running, the initiation part is empty, and any stretch of the running is reckoned from the state at that time.

This gives the first structural item:

Ini　Initiation — at t₀ the event of the initiation part of a declared delivery protocol of τ occurs (for a free-running device initiation is empty), and P is in some state type read as d; where the delivery protocol is declared as delivery spread over time, this becomes: from t₀ onwards, a delivery process read as d is carried out through the declared entry (Chapter 2, Prov).

Ini does not ask where the operand comes from: handed in by a deliverer, computed by a previous step, garbage or uninitialized — all are allowed (S8), so long as the starting state falls on some type of ρ\_in. A starting state outside the definition of ρ\_in (an illegal encoding, a metastable state) is read as no operand: the mechanism realizing τ may then still be initiated, operate and reach read-out, but no d is the object of this operation; it is one operation of the mechanism, not one execution of τ, and is excluded by Ini (Corollaries Two and Three). Whichever d it is read as, the present execution is an execution on that d; the operand actually received by the transforming part may differ from d (Corollary Two). Nor is which task is executed decided by some "configuration that assigns the operation"; it is decided by which borne task's declared protocol this initiation (together with the task-selection event) belongs to, and by the question under which task's reading scheme the starting point is read. A task is the extension of a function (Chapter 2, Corollary One); algorithm and configuration take no part in task identity.

## Section Five　Operating after initiation, is there already an execution?

Ini has been obtained. The initial form of operation in Section Three has two halves: first, the enabling conditions hold for some stretch of time; second, there are successors obtained by the host's own dynamics. With Ini added, each half leaves one point not yet closed: the first half does not ask whether the host is still operating at read-out, or by whom the state of the run is kept when it stops midway; the second does not ask whether the read-out's passing depends on evaluation outside the host. The two points do not depend on each other, so they divide into two branches, which close separately and then merge.

(a) The enabling half

Take a calculator: "=" is pressed (the initiation event occurs, and Ini holds; the host was operating beforehand, so the initial form holds), and the power is cut immediately; the volatile state is lost, and nothing continues afterwards; the non-volatile display retains the number from the previous occasion, which happens to be read as f(d).

This is not P's execution of τ: after the power is cut the volatile state is lost, the host does not thereafter continue from any held state, and it is not operating at read-out; the number at the read-out condition was obtained before t₀, not obtained continuously by r (E2). The initial form requires only that enablement hold for some stretch of time, and does not exclude this.

It must be required that:

En　Operation under enablement, and holding — on r, the part in which the bearing resides operates only while its enabling conditions hold; and, for each enabling gap J: (1) if r continues after J, the state r needs in order to continue — the state at the beginning of J, or a state r saved earlier at a checkpoint — is held by P itself during J, and after J the same instance continues from this held state (including returning to a checkpoint and redoing), the point of continuation being a state that r itself obtained after t₀, not replaced by an initial state, such as one produced by reset, that does not belong to r; (2) if r reaches read-out during J, the state of the read-out position at the beginning of J was obtained by r after t₀, and is held by P itself during J (the clock stopped after the result is latched); (3) where the read-out condition arrives while the enabling conditions hold, (2) does not apply. A state held during J that is rewritten by a transient external disturbance still counts as a state of r.

Necessity: outside the enabling conditions the part in which the bearing resides does not, by definition, operate, so the operation in r proceeds only under the enabling conditions. Suppose r continues after some enabling gap, but the state needed for continuation was not held by P, or the point of continuation is not a state r itself obtained; then the successors after continuation are not obtained continuously from the starting point of r, contrary to E2. Suppose r reaches read-out during an enabling gap, but the state of the read-out position at the beginning of the gap was not obtained by r after t₀, or was not held by P during the gap; then the read-out state is not a successor obtained by r, again contrary to E2.

En does not require the enabling conditions to hold at every moment: a processor that stops the clock or power of idle components midway through a computation, a static circuit whose clock is stopped and later continues, a hand-cranked calculator whose cranking stops midway, and pencil-and-paper working continued overnight (pencil and paper within the declared boundary) are all executions (S6) — when they stop, the state is kept by the host itself, and the same instance then carries on. Where the power is cut and the state is lost, after which the work is redone from an initial state, such as one produced by reset, that does not belong to r; or where the read-out condition arrives during an enabling gap and the read-out position holds no state obtained by r before the gap — En fails. Where the power is cut after the result has been latched in a non-volatile read-out position, and the read-out condition arrives during the power cut, En holds.

Why ask only about the enabling conditions, rather than require all the operating conditions to hold throughout? A computer overheats while running, the temperature leaves the specification, and it computes wrongly: it is operating, its adder circuit is working, only it errs outside the specification (S5). If all the operating conditions were required to hold throughout, it would be excluded. Chapter 2, Corollary Four, says that failure outside the operating conditions is not a failure of bearing — that is a statement at the level of bearing; at the level of execution, running outside the specification is still execution, only the acceptance protocol guaranteed by bearing does not apply there.

(b) The own-dynamics half

Take another calculator: "=" is pressed, the enabling conditions hold, and the adder logic operates as usual (the initial form holds), but a gate fault means it will give 6 on this occasion; a person outside the box sees the inputs 2 and 3, works out 5 separately, and writes it into the display register before read-out, overwriting the result of the adder logic.

This is not P's execution of τ: the acceptable result at read-out depends on the share of the evaluation of f done by the person outside the box; by E3 and Chapter 2's delimitation of proxy evaluation, this evaluation was not done by P — taking the box together with the human calculator as the boundary gives another host. Chapter 2's Prod concerns the family of results at the level of bearing; whether there is proxy evaluation in the present run is a fact at the level of runs. The initial form requires only that there be successors obtained by the host's own dynamics — and the adder logic does have them — so it does not exclude this. It is different when external noise rewrites the display to f(d): no link is evaluating on P's behalf; the agreement of the reading is coincidence, and bears only on whether the result is right or wrong (E2).

It must be required that:

¬Sub　No proxy evaluation on this occasion — in r there is no proxy evaluation at any of the three places: reading scheme, delivery, external action after delivery (proxy evaluation as in Chapter 2).

Necessity: by E3. Non-proxy at the three places is the application, at reading scheme, delivery and after delivery, of the same requirement already proved in Chapter 2; what shows up here is the third place, and the failure cases at the level of runs for the first two are of the same type (a reader who reads differently according to the answer; a deliverer who picks microstates according to the answer).

Why is Section Three's "successors obtained by the host's own dynamics" not set up as a separate item? What Section Three obtained is existential: the host has successors obtained by its own dynamics. It is too weak to exclude the cases of Section Four and of this section; and it says two things, each of which has a precise form. First, the successors are not obtained by some link outside the host evaluating on its behalf — the precise form of this half at the level of runs is ¬Sub; actions from outside the host that rewrite a state without evaluating (noise, transient disturbances) do not fall under it. Second, the successors are obtained by the host's own dynamics — the precise form of this half is given in Sections Six and Seven: what must be asked is not whether the host has some dynamics of its own, but whether the part of the dynamics that bears τ is set going by this occasion (Ex). If it were strengthened to a universal, requiring every successor to be obtained by the host's own dynamics, S7 would be excluded: midway through a computation, a transient external disturbance flips a bit in the adder logic, and the adder logic operates as usual but gives a wrong number. A transient external disturbance may intervene in a run without depriving it of the qualification of execution; if it does evaluation of f on the host's behalf, it is excluded by ¬Sub; if it means that the dynamics that bears is not set going at all, it is excluded by Ex; in all other cases it affects only whether the result is right or wrong.

Merging: the two branches do not depend on each other, and the condition for merging is that both are added at once — every case from here on must keep Ini, En and ¬Sub together. The initial form is thereby superseded: its first half by En; the first layer of its second half by ¬Sub; the second layer — whether the part of the dynamics that bears τ is set going by this occasion — is still not closed, and is taken up in Section Six.

## Section Six　Operating under enablement without proxy evaluation, has bearing been exercised?

En and ¬Sub have been obtained. The point left open where the branches merged in Section Five is this: the second layer of the second half of the initial form — the host's dynamics is operating, but whether what is operating is the part of the dynamics that bears τ, and whether it is set going by this initiation — has not yet been asked about.

Take a computer with an instruction queue. On this occasion an add instruction is issued (the initiation event occurs, and Ini holds), but the initiation signal of this instruction is lost before it reaches the control unit; at the same time the adder logic is processing operands left by the previous instruction in the queue, and its result is written to the position to be read out on this occasion, where it happens to be read as f(d). Enablement holds throughout, the adder logic operates by its own dynamics, and there is no external proxy evaluation.

This is not an execution of τ on the present occasion: the present initiation is idle — had this instruction not been issued, the adder logic would have operated just the same. What has been obtained does not exclude it, because Ini asks only whether the initiation event occurred, and En and ¬Sub ask only whether the host is operating under enablement with no evaluation done on its behalf; none of them asks whether its activity was set going by this initiation.

The same gap has a quieter form as well: an in-place identity instruction x ← x is issued, but it is lost before reaching the control unit; the register x simply keeps its original content. The read-out is exactly as it would have been had the instruction been executed. By E4, an executed identity (S9) and a holding with no execution can have exactly the same state content; the difference between them lies precisely in whether there is some fact that occurs because of execution and would not occur without it.

To exclude these cases, it must be required that the evolution of the transforming part Tr\_τ(P) that bears τ is driven by the present initiation — the present initiation is one of the actual causes of the present evolution of Tr; when r contains no other event that independently triggers Tr, this means: if there were no initiation on this occasion, the evolution of Tr would be different (for redundancy and preemption, see the Preliminary Distinctions and H3(c)). For a device declared as free-running, initiation is empty, and this clause is replaced by "Tr continues by its own dynamics under the enabling conditions"; if Tr does not continue under the enabling conditions (static holding, or being pinned by a fault), this clause fails.

This gives the first clause of exercise:

Ex\_α　Driving — under P's actual condition on the present occasion, the evolution of Tr\_τ(P) is driven by the present initiation (for a free-running device: Tr continues by its own dynamics under the enabling conditions, rather than being held statically or pinned).

What Ex\_α tests is the transforming part Tr, not the whole host. The initiation signal itself, and the input end that receives it, of course vary with initiation; what is asked is whether the borne transformation has been set going, not whether the initiation event occurred — the latter Ini has already asked.

"The present initiation" in Ex\_α means the initiation event that occurs at the starting point of r; whether it belongs to the declared protocol of τ is decided by Ini. In the counterfactual "if there were no initiation on this occasion," what is removed is the present initiation part, while the parts of the content part of the delivery protocol that do not coincide with the initiation part, and P's actual condition on the present occasion, are held fixed (Preliminary Distinctions). The determination of Ex\_α therefore depends on the initiation part being correctly identified: for a combinational circuit, what is removed is the arrival of the operands, the evolution of the transforming part differs accordingly, and Ex\_α holds; if "=" were misdeclared as the initiation part, then on removing "=" the adder logic would compute the result just the same, and Ex\_α would be judged to fail — a misjudgement that comes from the declaration. The conditions for identifying the initiation part go into H1; the formal statement that "the evolution would be different" does not count incidental differences through enabling pathways goes into H3.

Necessity: suppose P executes τ once while Ex\_α fails, that is, the present initiation is not an actual cause of the present evolution of Tr. By E1, the part of the dynamics that bears τ is put to use in this occasion's transformation; this part of the dynamics belongs to the transforming part (within the realizing part, outside Tr there is only reception and holding; see below). This occasion's transformation is individuated by the present initiation (Ini): if the operation of Tr does not have the present initiation as one of its actual causes, it is not being put to use on this occasion — the operation set going by the previous instruction in the queue belongs to the previous transformation. So the present evolution of Tr must have the present initiation as one of its actual causes, which is Ex\_α — contrary to the supposition. By E4, what is brought about need not be a difference in state content: for an executed identity, the occurrence of the write-back action is itself the event brought about. Within the realizing part, outside Tr there is only reception and holding; reception may take place along with initiation (latching when a key is pressed), but reception is not the operation of the transforming part, and not the use of the bearing dynamics that E1 speaks of. So when Ex\_α fails, what occurs in the present run because of execution is at most reception, which is not an execution. The free-running branch: there is no initiation event, and what is asked is whether Tr continues by its own dynamics on this occasion; if Tr does not continue under the enabling conditions — pinned by a fault, or merely held statically — then Tr has no operation at all in the present run, contrary to E1. For example, if the operational amplifier of an analogue integrator fails and the whole transforming part is pinned at some state, it does not execute over that stretch of time. The formal conditions for the division used here are H3(a). Judging "bringing about" by the counterfactual holds directly when r contains no other independent trigger; the general criterion under redundancy and preemption belongs to H3(c), which supports the determination of Ex\_α on specific runs and does not enter the reasoning on necessity here.

## Section Seven　With the transforming part driven, has bearing been exercised?

Ex\_α has been obtained.

Take a calculator: "2+3=" is pressed, and the control timing is set going by the present initiation and advances step by step (Ex\_α holds); but the adder logic stands completely still and undergoes no evolution at all in response to this initiation; the display register is disturbed by noise and shows 6. Enablement holds throughout, and there is no external proxy evaluation.

Take another calculator: the input latches take in 2 and 3, and the control unit is set going; the adder logic has stalled; the display keeps its old value.

Neither is an execution of addition. Addition is a transformation with the data as its argument; within the transforming part that bears addition, only the control timing is set going by this initiation, and the part that carries the data — the adder logic — is not set going at all. What has been obtained does not exclude these cases, because Ex\_α asks only whether the transforming part is set going, and the control timing's being set going satisfies it.

Consider next several cases that must be kept: S3 (the adder logic is initiated and operates normally, and a fault in the last output stage makes the display show 0 whatever the input); S2 (the operands are changed to a fixed 0 by a fault in the internal data path before entering the adder logic, and the adder logic is initiated and operates as usual on this 0); S4 (the adder logic is initiated, and the clock and internal operation proceed as usual, but a transient fault makes it lose its response to the data on this occasion — it operated, yet its action had nothing to do with the data sent in, and it finally gave 0).

In these three runs, the part of the dynamics that bears addition and carries the data was set going by this initiation and actually operated — it operated wrongly, and that is a fault in the way it operated. By E1, execution is one exercise of bearing; bearing addition, as a capacity, is constituted by the part of the dynamics that carries the data, and when this part of the dynamics is set going by the present initiation and operates, the capacity has been exercised; whether it operated correctly, and whether it responded to the data as it should, are the success or failure of this exercise, belonging to the side of the result, and by E5 they do not affect the qualification of execution. The line between S4 and the first case of this section is therefore clear: in S4 the adder logic is set going by the present initiation and operates; in that case the adder logic does not move at all.

The above concerns devices whose initiation part and content part are separate (sequential circuits, processors operating by instruction, mental arithmetic). In devices whose initiation part and content part coincide (combinational circuits), "being set going by the present initiation" just is the data-carrying part's evolving with the arrival of the operands. If the arrival triggers no event — in purely combinational logic arrival causes no write, and the evolution of the data-carrying part is simply its output following the values of its inputs — and, in addition, one of the following holds: what arrives on this occasion is fixed by a fault in the path; the data-carrying part has entirely lost its response to it; or what arrives on this occasion has the same value as the content already held at the input end — then its evolution on this occasion is independent of whether anything arrived, and the run is classed as initiated without exercise. If the arrival triggers a write event, then even if the value written does not vary with the operands and the state content does not change, by E4 the occurrence of this event is itself a difference in evolution, and the run does not fall into that class. The difference between the two kinds of device comes from what their initiation is: the adder logic of a sequential circuit is set going by the clock and the instruction, and operates just the same whatever it receives; a combinational circuit has no initiation besides arrival, and if arrival makes no difference to it, it has not moved on this occasion.

Accordingly, what separates the cases to be excluded from those to be kept is not whether the result is correct, not whether the read-out depends on the original operands, not whether what the transforming part receives is the original operands, nor (for devices whose initiation and content are separate) whether the data-carrying part responds to the data, but this: whether the part that carries the data has been set going by this initiation.

It must be required that, if τ is not a Π-trivial task, the evolution of the part of the transforming part that carries the data in normal running is driven by the present initiation (Preliminary Distinctions: the present initiation is one of the actual causes of its present evolution; when r contains no other independent trigger, this means that, under P's actual condition on the present occasion, if there were no initiation on this occasion, its evolution would be different). Where a non-trivial task is borne by a device declared as free-running (an analogue integrator, for instance), there is no initiation event, and the requirement must be rewritten consistently with the free-running branch of Section Six: the part that carries the data continues by its own dynamics under the enabling conditions (it may obtain successors of the same value; the intervention of transient external disturbances is treated as in Section Five), rather than being held statically or pinned by a fault or by external action. An integrator whose operational amplifier has failed, its capacitor merely keeping its original charge, is not executing; an integrator working normally whose input happens to be zero, its charge unchanged, has a data-carrying part that still obtains successors of the same value by its own dynamics, and is executing (S10). The two may be indistinguishable in their external read-out; their formal criterion goes into H3.

This gives the second clause of exercise:

Ex\_β　The data part is driven — if τ is not a Π-trivial task: (i) for a device that is not free-running, under P's actual condition on the present occasion, the evolution of the data-carrying part of Tr\_τ(P) (identified, where there are dispatched pathways, by the dispatch rule of the Preliminary Distinctions) is driven by the present initiation; (ii) for a free-running device, the data-carrying part of Tr\_τ(P) continues by its own dynamics under the enabling conditions, rather than being held statically or pinned. When τ is a Π-trivial task, this clause holds trivially: bearing a Π-trivial task does not require the result to depend on the operand, and the Derived Dependence Lemma of Chapter 2 holds only for non-trivial tasks.

Ex\_β is a strengthening of Ex\_α for non-trivial tasks: the data-carrying part is contained in Tr\_τ(P), and if the present initiation is one of the actual causes of its present evolution, it is one of the actual causes of the present evolution of Tr\_τ(P); so, for non-trivial tasks, Ex\_β yields Ex\_α (likewise in the free-running branch); for Π-trivial tasks there is only Ex\_α. Ex\_α and Ex\_β are together called exercise, written Ex; Ex together with En and ¬Sub makes up operation, Run.

Necessity: suppose τ is not a Π-trivial task and P executes τ once while Ex\_β fails: the present initiation is not an actual cause of the present evolution of the data-carrying part. First determine which part of the dynamics bears τ; there are two cases.

(a) τ is not in the identity class. By the division of the Preliminary Distinctions, within the realizing part, apart from the data-carrying part, what remains — reception, holding, control and timing — has dynamics that is at most preservation-class obtaining, or, where the dispatched pathway alone gives the result by a transformation other than preservation-class obtaining, does no more than select, giving no part of the result; the pathways not dispatched to on the present occasion (parallel lookup in a cache, speculative execution) are not, in normal running, parts through which the result on the present operands is obtained. From these parts alone, every family of results that can be obtained is a family obtained by preservation-class obtaining. That τ is not in the identity class means that Acc\_Π tolerates no such family of results; so every acceptable family of results must be obtained through the data-carrying part. What this step compares is the kind of results the acceptance protocol tolerates with the kind of dynamics of the realizing part, not the kind of target function: that f is not the identity function does not exclude an acceptance protocol that tolerates preservation-class results (the example f(d) = d + 1 in the Preliminary Distinctions); such a task belongs to the identity class and falls under (b). The Derived Dependence Lemma of Chapter 2 gives only that the result distribution varies with the operand; it does not give the attribution of dynamics needed here, and this step does not use it. The reasoning depends on the exhaustiveness of the dispatch rule: every selecting duty that depends on the operands and bears the distinction between results must already have been classed, by the Preliminary Distinctions, within the data-carrying part — for example, if two pathways give the constants 0 and 1, and the dispatching action selects between them according to the operand, the whole realizes a non-identity, non-trivial function, and this dispatching action belongs, by the Preliminary Distinctions, to the data-carrying part; if some such selecting duty were not classed within it, the conclusion above would fail. This exhaustiveness is declared by the Preliminary Distinctions, and its formal conditions belong to H3(a). So, at the level of bearing, what makes this bearing a bearing of τ is the part of the dynamics that carries the data.

(b) τ is in the identity class but is not a Π-trivial task. Here an acceptable family of results can be obtained by preservation-class obtaining, and the argument of (a) cannot attribute the bearing to any part. The data-carrying part is delimited directly by the Preliminary Distinctions as the action that, under the declared protocol, is set going by initiation and writes the result to the read-out position; in this case, "the part of the dynamics that bears τ is this action" comes from the delimitation of the identity class. Where preserving unchanged passes and the realization contains no such action (static storage), the transforming part is empty, and the host bears without being able to execute (Section Six).

Both cases give only an attribution at the level of bearing. To get from there to "this part must be set going by the present initiation in the present run," one further step is needed: E1 requires that what is exercised be the part of the dynamics that bears τ, and this requirement comes down, at the level of runs, to "the evolution of this part is driven by the present initiation." This step is H3(b), together with the division used (H3(a)); it is not derived from the Derived Dependence Lemma; the criterion H3(c) for driving on specific runs does not enter the reasoning here (Section Six). Under H3, when Ex\_β fails, what is set going on this occasion is at most parts that do not carry the data, pathways not dispatched to on this occasion, and reception; what is exercised is not the bearing of τ, contrary to E1. The transformation that has the operands as its argument can be applied wrongly (S2–S4), but here it has not been applied at all. In (b), this is exactly the latter of the two sides of Section Six's distinction between "an executed identity and a holding with no execution"; a device whose transforming part is empty cannot execute, by Section Six. The free-running branch: if the data-carrying part does not continue by its own dynamics on this occasion, then, under H3, this likewise contradicts E1.

The load-bearing point of this proof is the process fact that "the dynamics that constitutes the bearing is actually set going on this occasion," not a change of state content, nor response to the data: for an executed in-place identity x ← x, the state content may be the same before and after, the write-back action that carries the data is set going by the instruction, and both Ex\_α and Ex\_β hold (S9); in S4 the adder logic has lost its response to the data and is still set going by the present initiation, so Ex\_β holds. The data-carrying part's reacting to the data it receives is not made a necessary condition; it serves only as evidence for discrimination (Corollary Four).

## Section Eight　Initiated and operating, is there already one execution?

Ini and Run have been obtained.

Take a calculator: "=" is pressed, the adder logic is set going by the present initiation, and everything is operating; halfway through the computation, reset is pressed, and the read-out condition is never reached. Take a person doing mental arithmetic: halfway through, the person is called away.

In both, the transforming part and its data-carrying part really were set going by the present initiation, and Ini and Run both hold, but no result was delivered. By E6, one execution is one complete run from initiation to read-out; under this counting convention what has been obtained is not closed: a run interrupted midway satisfies Ini and Run, yet is not one complete execution.

That reaching read-out can take the weakest form depends on Ex's already being within Run: without Ex, the case of Section Seven in which "noise rewrites the display" could be excluded only by requiring the read-out to depend on the operands, and that requirement excludes S3.

It must be required that the run r obtain, at the declared read-out condition, a state that is read out through ρ\_out — a value in V, or ⊥ (for example, an error code displayed after a crash). It suffices that the read-out condition is reached; no one need observe it; the read-out may be wrong.

This gives the third structural item:

Out　Reaching read-out — r obtains, at the declared read-out condition, a state read out through ρ\_out (a value or ⊥).

Out does not require the read-out to retain dependence on the operands: the run with an output-stage fault, in which 0 is displayed for every input, reaches the read-out condition and is one complete failed execution (S3). Exercise is located in the Ex within Run, and is not loaded onto the read-out.

If an interrupted run is an execution that has occurred without being completed, its necessary structure is given by Corollary One; that it is one does not follow from Ini and Run holding.

## Section Nine　With every item witnessed, is there already one execution?

Ini, Run and Out have all been obtained.

Take two calculators of the same model, A and B. A is initiated and operates, its adder logic set going, and the power is cut midway; B had earlier computed on the same d, and its display still shows f(d). Put A's initiation, a stretch of A's operation and B's read-out together, and Ini, Run and Out each have a witness.

Take next a video recording that plays back the whole process of "key press — operation — display of f(d)," while the present device is not running at all. Take also a batch of molecular computations prepared by protocol, delivery taking place in test tube one and the read-out taken from test tube two, prepared separately (not pipetted from test tube one).

Each of the three items has a witness, yet no single run bears them all together. If "executing once" could be pieced together from different runs, different instances or playback recordings, any device could, by borrowing a read-out from elsewhere, "complete" an execution it never completed. By E6, one execution is undertaken by one run; by E2, where a reset or the like makes the process start again from an initial state not belonging to r, it is not a continuation of r.

It must be required that Ini, Run and Out be witnessed by one and the same run r: r is a continuous stretch of actual process on one and the same instance, and the read-out of Out is obtained within r; r proceeds continuously on one and the same instance from t₀ and is not restarted from an initial state, such as one produced by reset, that does not belong to r; when Run holds, the read-out state is then a successor obtained by r from its starting point through Run.

This gives the binding to one and the same run: ∃r \[Ini(r) ∧ Run(r) ∧ Out(r)\], and not \[∃r₁ Ini(r₁)\] ∧ \[∃r₂ Run(r₂)\] ∧ \[∃r₃ Out(r₃)\]. It is not a fourth structural item; it is the scope of the existential quantifier. This binding also supplies the other half of Out: the read-out is obtained within one and the same run r. The same run asks only about the individual identity of the process — the same instance, the same continuous stretch of process, not restarted by reset; by whom the state of the run is held when it stops midway is not part of this, and is constrained by En.

Within one and the same run the following are allowed: multi-core parallelism, pipelines, redundant pathways, distributed nodes (when the boundary includes all the nodes), and table lookup or cache reads during the run (the table and the cache belong to the host's configuration, and the reading is done by the host's dynamics). As one and the same run the following are not allowed: piecing one execution together across instances, across runs, across a playback and the present, or across a host and an external record. The same portion of reactants pipetted according to protocol is still the same instance; playback means replaying recorded states or images, with the host no longer operating — rerunning with the recorded inputs is a new run. Where the read-out position is rewritten by another process on the same instance while the present run itself satisfies every item, this is not piecing together but a failed execution disturbed on the read-out side (S13, E2).

## Section Ten　With one and the same run satisfying every item, must anything further be set up?

∃r \[Ini(r) ∧ Run(r) ∧ Out(r)\] has been obtained. Test one by one whether anything further must be set up.

Correctness of the result: runs with gate faults, internal data corruption (on devices whose initiation and content are separate) or output-stage faults, and runs outside the specification conditions, are all executions (S1–S3, S5). Not required.

Operands legitimately handed in by a deliverer: operating on garbage or uninitialized data, and operating after an internal data path has corrupted the operands on a device whose initiation and content are separate, are both executions (S8, S2). Not required: by E1, the operand of this occasion's transformation need not be handed in by a deliverer.

A particular mechanism pathway actually taking effect: Ex asks only whether the transforming part, and its data-carrying part, are driven by the present initiation, not which pathway, how many steps or what algorithm. Circuit summation, table lookup, mental arithmetic and mechanical gears each satisfy Ex by their own transforming part (S12). Requiring some particular pathway would mean identifying, at the level of runs, which pathway is the one that bears τ, pulling algorithm back into task identity, contrary to Chapter 2, Corollary One. Not required.

Change of state content: for an executed in-place identity and a write-back of the same value, the state content may be unchanged (S9, E4). Not required.

The read-out retaining dependence on the operands: a complete failed execution with an output-stage fault does not retain it (S3). Not required.

The read-out being obtained by the transforming part driven on this occasion: an output-stage fault, and a read-out position rewritten by another process on the same instance, are both failed executions (S3, S13); whether the result of the transformation is sent to the read-out belongs to the success or failure of this execution. Not required.

The data-carrying part responding to the data it receives: on a device whose initiation and content are separate, adder logic that operates but momentarily loses its response to the data is a failed execution (S4). Not required; it serves only as evidence for discrimination.

Prior declaration of "τ is to be computed," or the user's intention: a free-running analogue integrator and spontaneous mental arithmetic are both executions (S10, S11). Not required.

Observation of the read-out: unattended batch computation is execution (S11). Not required.

We return to the same task: if P executes τ once in T, there must be a run at whose starting point the initiation event of τ occurs; during which the host operates under the enabling conditions, with no proxy evaluation, and the transforming part that bears τ — for a non-trivial task, including its data-carrying part — is set going by this initiation (the two clauses of Ex conditional on H3; for a free-running device: continuation by its own dynamics); all the way to obtaining a read-out at the read-out condition. Whether the result of the transformation is sent to the read-out belongs to the success or failure of this execution. What has been obtained already excludes all the failure cases of Sections Three to Nine; none of the nine proposed additions opens a new constraint; and no run has been found that keeps everything obtained, is not an execution, and does not belong to the two undetermined cases of Section Thirteen. The original question is closed in the necessary direction, and is gathered below into the general formula and proved. Closed means that there remains no known point not yet closed; it does not mean sufficiency (U1).

## Section Eleven　General Form and General Theorem

The general form of the minimal structure of computational execution

　　Exec(P, τ, T) ⇒ ∃r ⊆ T: Ini(r) ∧ Run(r) ∧ Out(r)  
　　Run(r) = En(r) ∧ ¬Sub(r) ∧ Ex\_α(r) ∧ Ex\_β(r)　(Ex\_β holds trivially when τ is a Π-trivial task)

Formal statement:

Let P bear τ = (D, f, ρ\_in, ρ\_out, Π) within the declared period Θ (Chapter 2: Leg ∧ Prov ∧ Prod), with T ⊆ Θ, the declared items declared as in Chapter 2 and with the finer divisions of the Preliminary Distinctions, and the concept of execution delimited by E1–E6.

General Theorem　If P executes τ once in T, then there is a continuous stretch of actual process r ⊆ T on one instance of P such that:  
(Ini) at the starting point t₀ of r, the event of the initiation part of a declared delivery protocol of τ occurs (for a free-running device initiation is empty), and P is in some state type read as d (for delivery spread over time: from t₀ onwards a delivery process read as d is carried out through the declared entry);  
(En) on r, the part in which the bearing resides operates only while its enabling conditions hold; in each enabling gap, the state r needs in order to continue (the state at the beginning of the gap, or a state r saved earlier at a checkpoint) is held by P itself, and after the gap the same instance continues from this held state, which r itself obtained; where r reaches read-out during an enabling gap, the state of the read-out position at the beginning of that gap was obtained by r after t₀, and is held by P itself during the gap; a state held during a gap that is rewritten by a transient external disturbance still counts as a state of r;  
(¬Sub) in r there is no proxy evaluation at any of the three places: reading scheme, delivery, external action after delivery;  
(Out) r obtains, at the declared read-out condition, a state read out through ρ\_out (a value or ⊥), obtained within r (the same continuous stretch of process on the same instance from t₀, not restarted by reset);  
and, under Computational Execution General Form-H3:  
(Ex\_α) under P's actual condition on the present occasion, the evolution of Tr\_τ(P) is driven by the present initiation (for a free-running device: it continues by its own dynamics under the enabling conditions, rather than being held statically or pinned);  
(Ex\_β) if τ is not a Π-trivial task, the evolution of the data-carrying part of Tr\_τ(P) is driven by the present initiation (for a free-running device: that part continues by its own dynamics under the enabling conditions, rather than being held statically or pinned).

Proof. Suppose P executes τ once in T. By E6, there is a run r ⊆ T that undertakes this execution. By E1, r is the taking place of one transformation of τ according to the declared protocol; this occasion begins with the initiation part of the protocol, whose event occurs at the starting point of r; it takes some operand as its object, and the starting state is read as some d; so Ini holds (Section Four). By the meaning of the enabling conditions and by E2, the operation in r proceeds only under the enabling conditions, the state needed for continuation in an enabling gap is held by P, the point of continuation is a state r itself obtained, and the read-out position reached during a gap holds a state obtained by r after t₀ and held by P; so En holds. By E3, there is no proxy evaluation at the three places, so ¬Sub holds (Section Five). Under H3: by E1, the dynamics that bears is put to use in this occasion's transformation, and this occasion is individuated by the present initiation, so the present initiation is one of the actual causes of the present evolution of Tr (by E4, what is brought about need not be a difference in state content), and Ex\_α holds (Section Six); if τ is not a Π-trivial task, the part of the dynamics that bears τ is the data-carrying part (for tasks not in the identity class, by the division of the Preliminary Distinctions together with the delimitation of the identity class, the exhaustiveness of the division belonging to H3(a); for tasks in the identity class, by that class's delimitation), and by E1 and H3(b) it must be set going by the present initiation, so Ex\_β holds (Section Seven). By E6, r reaches read-out, so Out holds (Section Eight). By E6 and E2, the items are witnessed by one and the same r, and the read-out is obtained within r (Section Nine). This completes the proof.

Declaration of strength: the general theorem is in the necessary direction (Exec ⇒ ∃r\[…\]). The necessity of Ini, En, ¬Sub, Out and same-run binding is unconditional relative to E1–E6 and the declared counting convention; their determination on specific runs depends on H1 (initiation and enablement), H2 (the same run) and Computational Bearing General Form-H1–H3 (reading schemes and proxy evaluation). The necessity of Ex\_α and Ex\_β is conditional on H3(a)(b); their specific determination further depends on H3(c)–(e) and on H1's identification of the initiation part. Ex\_α and Ex\_β each contain two branches, the non-free-running and the free-running. On a device whose initiation and content are separate, a data-carrying part that operates under a severe fault but loses its response to the data satisfies Ex\_β; on a purely combinational device whose initiation and content coincide (arrival triggering no write event), Ex\_β fails in the same case (Section Seven). Whether the converse Ini ∧ Run ∧ Out ⇒ Exec holds is an open problem (Section Fifteen, U1); Section Thirteen contains two cases that satisfy every condition and whose classification is undetermined.

Symbols and read-back:

P, τ, D, f, V, ρ\_in, ρ\_out, Π, Acc\_Π, B, Env, Θ, proxy evaluation, Π-trivial task: as in Chapter 2.  
T: the time interval under discussion, T ⊆ Θ.  
r: a continuous stretch of actual process on one instance of P, r ⊆ T; starting point t₀; where read-out is reached, the end point is the moment of read-out.  
Initiation part: the class of events in a delivery protocol identified by "actually making the transformation begin in normal running" (including task-selection events; for delivery spread over time, the beginning of the delivery process); empty for a free-running device.  
Enabling conditions: the part of Env without which the part in which the bearing resides does not operate; specification conditions: the rest of Env; enabling gap: a maximal interval over which the enabling conditions fail.  
Real\_τ(P): the part through which, in normal running under the declared protocols, the declared results are obtained and Prod holds (dispatched pathways counting as one; components that only maintain enablement, and bypasses, excluded); Tr\_τ(P): the transforming part within it (where there are several, the one used on this occasion; possibly empty); data-carrying part: the part of Tr that, in normal running, carries the operands and makes the result vary with them (dispatch rule in the Preliminary Distinctions).  
"P's actual condition on the present occasion": the persisting condition of P during the present run, including damage, sticking and faults; held fixed when Ex is tested. The counterfactual removes only the present initiation part, holding fixed the parts of the content part of the delivery protocol that do not coincide with it.  
Driving: the present initiation is one of the actual causes of the present evolution of the part in question; when r contains no other independent trigger, it is judged by the counterfactual above; the criterion under redundancy and preemption belongs to H3(c).  
Preservation-class obtaining: preserving, moving or copying unchanged; selection and rearrangement at fixed positions; content independent of the operands. Identity class: tasks whose acceptance protocol tolerates a family of results obtained by preservation-class obtaining (Preliminary Distinctions).  
Ini, En, ¬Sub, Ex\_α, Ex\_β, Out: see the formal statement.  
Run: En ∧ ¬Sub ∧ Ex\_α ∧ Ex\_β (for Π-trivial tasks Ex\_β holds trivially); Ex: Ex\_α ∧ Ex\_β.  
Exec(P, τ, T): P executes τ once in T (one complete run from initiation to read-out, E6).  
ExecOcc(P, τ, t): as of moment t, execution of τ has occurred — under way or having taken place, not necessarily one complete execution (Corollary One).  
E1–E6: the antecedents of the concept of execution (Section Two); S1–S13: the standard positive cases of execution (Section Two).

Read-back by layer: every item of the general formula is a fact about running at the host layer in T — the initiation event of τ occurs, the host operates under the enabling conditions, nothing evaluates on its behalf, the transforming part (and, for non-trivial tasks, its data-carrying part) is driven by the present initiation (for a free-running device: continues by its own dynamics), and read-out is reached. Which task is executed is given by the protocol to which bearing and initiation belong (Chapter 2); whether the result is correct, and whether the host is a computing host, are not in the general formula. The order of the items in the tuple is the order of proof, not an order in time or in causation.

Read-back in words: if a host that bears a mathematical task executes that task once over some stretch of time, then necessarily: on one of its instances there is a continuous stretch of actual process at whose starting point the initiation event in the task's declared protocol occurs, the host being in a state that can be read as some operand; from initiation right through to read-out, the part of the host that bears this task is, whenever it operates, under the enabling conditions, and when it stops midway, the host itself keeps the state of the run and carries on from there, or returns to a checkpoint it saved earlier and redoes the work — likewise when it stops after the result has been latched and the read-out takes place while it is stopped; no reading scheme, delivery or external process evaluates on its behalf (transient external disturbances may intervene, so long as they do not evaluate on the host's behalf or deprive the dynamics that bears of being set going); the transforming part that bears the task is set going by this initiation (for a free-running device: it continues by its own dynamics, rather than being held statically or pinned), and (for a non-trivial task) what is set going includes its data-carrying part — whether it operates correctly, and whether it responds to the data as it should, does not matter; and this stretch of process finally obtains, at the read-out condition, a state that can be read through the reading scheme, whether the reading is right or wrong. "There exists a run" governs all the items, and the items must be witnessed by this one and the same run. The necessity of the clause "the transforming part that bears the task is set going by this initiation" (Ex\_α, Ex\_β) is conditional on H3.

## Section Twelve　Lemmas and Corollaries

Corollary One　Occurrence of execution

ExecOcc(P, τ, t) ⇒ ∃r ∃t₁ (t₀ \< t₁ ≤ t): Ini(r) holds at t₀, and Run holds on \[t₀, t₁\]

Run holding on \[t₀, t₁\] means: En holds on \[t₀, t₁\]; within \[t₀, t₁\] there is no proxy evaluation at any of the three places — reading scheme, delivery and external action (on a stretch of running that has as yet no result, proxy evaluation is identified by "its successors depend on this evaluation of f"); and the evolution of Tr\_τ(P) (for non-trivial tasks, including its data-carrying part) within \[t₀, t₁\] is driven by the present initiation (Preliminary Distinctions; for a free-running device: it continues by its own dynamics within \[t₀, t₁\], rather than being held statically or pinned). For delivery spread over time, Ini on the segment is identified by the part of the delivery already carried out within \[t₀, t₁\]. Here t₁ \> t₀, and Ex holding on \[t₀, t₁\] requires that the part in question has already undergone, within this interval, a stretch of evolution driven by the present initiation (for a free-running device: a stretch of evolution continued by its own dynamics): the initiation event alone, or a state of affairs after initiation in which only the control unit is advancing and the data-carrying part has as yet no driven evolution, does not satisfy it. For a free-running device, t₀ is taken as the starting point of the stretch under discussion.

That is: if, as of moment t, execution of τ has occurred, there must be a run and a moment t₁ no later than t such that the initiation event of τ occurs at t₀ and Run holds on the stretch \[t₀, t₁\]. For an execution under way, take t₁ = t; where t falls in an enabling gap, take t₁ as the beginning of that gap, and there must then already have been driven evolution of the kind described between t₀ and the beginning of the gap, or else execution has not yet occurred as of t; for an execution already terminated before t, take t₁ as the moment of termination, Run not being required to continue until t. For a non-trivial task, after initiation and before the data-carrying part is set going (with only the control unit advancing), execution has not yet occurred; if this stretch is terminated, it is classed as initiated without exercise (Corollary Three).

Proof. ExecOcc is the occurrence of execution on \[t₀, t₁\]; E1–E5 apply to it, E6 does not. The interval must contain a stretch of driven evolution: by E1, that execution has occurred means that the dynamics bearing τ has already been put to use on this occasion; by E4, this is a process fact that has already occurred. At the very instant of initiation, or after initiation with only the control unit advancing, the dynamics bearing τ (for non-trivial tasks, the data-carrying part) has as yet not been put to use at all on this occasion; so t₁ \> t₀, and \[t₀, t₁\] must already contain such a stretch of evolution. Ini: the argument of Section Four does not use read-out. En: in the argument of Section Five the clause on continuation does not use read-out; the clause on read-out does not apply. ¬Sub: by E3, in the sense for segments given above. Ex\_α, Ex\_β: the necessity proofs of Sections Six and Seven use only whether the dynamics that bears is set going in the present run, not read-out. The statements in Section Five that rely on read-out are used only for complete executions. Same-run binding by E2: E2 applies equally to uncompleted executions; the process of the execution from its starting point onwards is one run r proceeding continuously on one and the same instance, and \[t₀, t₁\] is a segment of r; E6 is not used here. The Ex part, and the identification of proxy evaluation on segments, are conditional on H3. This completes the proof.

Accordingly, runs that are interrupted midway, that never terminate or that terminate abnormally are not excluded by the necessary structure of Corollary One if, as of the interruption, they satisfy the items above; Corollary One is in the necessary direction, and it cannot be inferred from these conditions that execution has occurred.

Corollary Two　Five distinctions, and the operand actually executed on

In one execution five things can be distinguished: the task executed, τ (given by bearing, with the extension of a function as its identity); the algorithm realizing it (embodied in the structure of Tr, not prescribed by the general formula); the physical configuration (the construction of P within Θ); the run r; and the result (the read-out of Out). The operand that the starting point is read as, d (Ini), may differ from the operand actually executed on this occasion — the one actually received by the data-carrying part of Tr (S2). Where the starting point is read as no d (the state outside the definition of ρ\_in), the mechanism operates with no operand being executed on; it is an operation of the mechanism that realizes τ, not an execution of τ (Section Four). The correctness of the result is a comparison of the read-out with f(d) according to Π.

Proof. The general formula constrains only the facts of running on r; Ini asks only which d the starting point is read as, and Ex\_β asks only whether the data-carrying part is set going, not what it receives; by Chapter 2, Corollary One, task identity contains neither algorithm nor configuration. This completes the proof.

Rows may overlap; where they do, the first item that fails is taken as the ground of exclusion.

Corollary Four　Evidence for discrimination

The following three are not necessary conditions of execution; they can serve as evidence in determining Ex, but none of them yields Ex:  
(1) Operand-related change of state: under P's actual condition on the present occasion, and with transient external disturbances removed, the change of some variable in the data-carrying part itself varies with the operand. Its holding shows that the data-carrying part has, on the present occasion, operation that varies with the operand, but this operation may not have been brought about by the present initiation (the case of the previous instruction in the queue in Section Six), so it does not yield Ex\_β; its failing does not suffice, on a device whose initiation and content are separate, to deny execution (S4); on a device whose initiation part and content part coincide, if it fails and the present arrival makes no difference to the occurrence of any event in the data-carrying part (including a write of the same value that does not change the state content), Ex\_β fails (Section Seven); the mere absence of change at the level of values does not suffice to yield that Ex\_β fails (E4).  
(2) Dependence of the read-out on the operands at the delivery end. When it holds, the dependence may come from a bypass outside the realizing part (for example, a debugging line that carries the input unchanged to the display), so it does not yield Ex; when it fails, this does not suffice to deny execution (S3).  
(3) The data-carrying part's responding to the data it receives: intervening on the input it actually receives on the present occasion would make its subsequent state different. Its holding shows that the data-carrying part is in operation on the present occasion, but not that this operation was brought about by the present initiation, so it does not yield Ex\_β; its failing does not suffice, on a device whose initiation and content are separate, to deny execution (S4); on a device whose initiation part and content part coincide, if it fails and the present arrival makes no difference to the occurrence of any event, Ex\_β fails (Section Seven); the mere absence of response to the data does not suffice to yield that Ex\_β fails (E4).

Proof. Non-necessity: (1) and (3) fail in S4, and (2) fails in S3, while S3 and S4 are executions. Not yielding Ex: in the idle-initiation case of Section Six, the adder logic is processing the previous instruction, and (1) and (3) can hold while Ex fails; for (2), by the bypass cited. The sentence on coinciding devices: the initiation of a combinational device is the arrival of the operands, and "if there were no initiation on this occasion" means that the input end keeps its existing content. When (1) or (3) fails, the evolution at the level of values of the data-carrying part does not vary with the input received; if, moreover, the present arrival makes no difference to the occurrence of any event, then replacing the present arrival by the existing content leaves its evolution — the occurrence of events and their content — unchanged, and Ex\_β fails (Section Seven). Conversely, if the arrival triggers a write event, then even if the value written is independent of the operands and the state content does not change, by E4 the occurrence of this event is a difference in evolution, and the failure of (1) or (3) does not yield that Ex\_β fails. The formal statement of taking the existing content of the input end as the counterfactual baseline goes into H1 and H3. This completes the proof.

Corollary Five　Composition of executions (conditional)

Let a composite task τ\_c be formed by chaining subtasks τ₁, …, τ\_k (compound operations, pipelines, changes of encoding across periods), let P bear each subtask, and let the premises of Chapter 2, Corollary Two, together with conditions C0–C3, hold pairwise for successive subtasks (used iteratively). Chapter 2, Corollary Two, gives only that the necessary structure of bearing is not lost under composition; it does not give that P bears τ\_c, nor that the actual runs of the subtasks have been chained into one composite execution. If, in addition:  
(K0) P bears τ\_c within a declared period that contains the stretch of process; the input reading scheme, delivery protocol and initiation part of τ\_c are those of τ₁, and its output reading scheme and read-out condition are those of τ\_k (where the output reading scheme of τ\_c includes a failure bypass as in Chapter 2, Corollary Two, the part for normal completion is meant; in the chaining of K1 no intermediate read-out is ⊥, and the bypass is not triggered); Tr\_τc(P) contains Tr\_τ₁(P); and when τ\_c is not a Π-trivial task, neither is τ₁, and the data-carrying part of Tr\_τc(P) contains the data-carrying part of Tr\_τ₁(P);  
(K1) actual chaining: in one and the same continuous stretch of process on one and the same instance, the read-out state of the i-th subexecution becomes, through a coupling internal to P, the starting point of the (i+1)-th subexecution, and the initiation of the (i+1)-th subexecution occurs within this process (issued by P's own dynamics or by the declared protocol); changes of encoding across periods must satisfy this likewise, each subexecution falling within the declared period to which it belongs;  
(K2) no proxy evaluation at the chaining points: at each coupling there is no proxy evaluation by reading scheme, delivery or external action;  
(K3) each subexecution satisfies Ini, Run and Out on its own;  
(K4) En holds at the chaining points: at each chaining point, the part in which the bearing resides operates only under the enabling conditions, the state needed for continuation during a gap is held by P, and after the gap the same instance continues from the held state, which this stretch of process itself obtained;  
then this stretch of process satisfies Ini, Run, Out and same-run binding for τ\_c.

From K0 and K3 follows K5 (the witness of driving is preserved in the composite run): the initiation of τ\_c drives Tr\_τ₁(P) — and, when τ\_c is not a Π-trivial task, its data-carrying part as well. By K0, the initiation part of τ\_c is the initiation part of the first subexecution; by K3, this initiation is one of the actual causes of the evolution of Tr\_τ₁(P) (and of its data-carrying part) in the first subexecution; the first subexecution is a stretch of the composite run, and this relation of actual causation is a fact about that stretch of actual history, which does not change because other subexecutions are chained after it. K5 is not a separate condition; its determination, like the determination of Ex on each subexecution, carries H3(c).

Proof. Ini: by K0, the initiation event of τ\_c is the initiation event of the first subexecution, which occurs by K3; the starting point's being read as d is given by the Ini of the first subexecution. En: on the subintervals by K3, at the chaining points by K4. ¬Sub: on the subintervals by K3, at the chaining points by K2. Ex\_α, Ex\_β: by K5, the initiation of τ\_c is one of the actual causes of the present evolution of Tr\_τ₁(P) (and of its data-carrying part); by K0, Tr\_τ₁(P) is contained in Tr\_τc(P), and its data-carrying part in the data-carrying part of Tr\_τc(P); since the evolution of a part has this initiation as an actual cause, the evolution of the whole has it as one of its actual causes; so the evolution of Tr\_τc(P) (and, when τ\_c is non-trivial, of its data-carrying part) is driven by the present initiation. Out: take the read-out of the last subexecution (K0). Same run: by K1. This completes the proof.

What Corollary Five gives is that, when P bears τ\_c, the necessary structure of execution is not lost under such chaining, and the chained process is not excluded by the general formula. It cannot be inferred from this that the stretch of process is one execution of τ\_c.

## Section Thirteen　Boundary Cases

The following are cases, beyond the examples of Sections Three to Ten and the standard positive cases, that must be tested separately. "Excluded by such-and-such item" follows from the general theorem; "by Sk" means not excluded by the general theorem, the ground for its being an execution being that standard positive case or its like; "undetermined" means not excluded, with classification undetermined.

A combinational adder circuit (operating as soon as the operands arrive, "=" merely gating the result onto the display): not excluded; the initiation part is the arrival of the operands, not "=" (Preliminary Distinctions, Section Six). If "=" is declared as the initiation part, Ex is judged to fail under that declaration — a misdeclaration of the initiation part (H1).  
A combinational adder circuit (purely combinational logic, arrival triggering no write event) whose input channel is fixed by a fault, or whose adder logic has entirely lost its response to the input: excluded by Ex\_β (Section Seven); if arrival triggers a write event, it is judged separately as in Section Seven.  
A combinational adder circuit (purely combinational logic, arrival triggering no write event) whose input end already holds the present operands, the same values arriving again on this occasion: arrival makes no difference to the data-carrying part, and it is excluded by Ex\_β; if arrival triggers a write event, it is judged separately as in Section Seven; its difference from "an in-place identity of the same value is an execution" comes from what the initiation of each of the two kinds of device is (Section Seven). The formal statement of the counterfactual baseline of the input end when "arrival is removed" goes into H1 and H3.  
A clear (zeroing) instruction (likewise when the register was already 0): a Π-trivial task; the zero-writing action of the clear is set going by this instruction, and Ex\_α holds; by S9 and its like.  
A clear instruction in which the control unit is set going while the zero-writing part has stalled: Ex\_α is satisfied by the control unit, and the general formula does not exclude it (when the register was already 0 the read-out happens to pass); undetermined (U2).  
Static storage in which delivery is placement and read-out is reading in place (the stone bearing positional identity in Chapter 2, Corollary Five): the transforming part is empty, Ex\_α fails, and it bears without being able to execute (Section Six).  
An actually executed no-operation: the task borne is fixed by its declared protocol. Where it is declared as only advancing the control unit, with a read-out that contains no value of machine state, it is a Π-trivial task, the control unit is driven by this instruction, and Ex\_α holds; where the read-out contains the value of the program counter, the task is PC ↦ PC+1, non-trivial, the incrementing action is the data-carrying part, it is driven by this instruction, and Ex\_β holds; where it is declared as the identity task on the machine state, a further write-back action is needed to satisfy Ex\_β. None is excluded; by S9 and its like. A skipped no-operation: excluded by Ex.  
Pressing "=" causes a power glitch that rewrites the display while the adder logic has stalled: excluded by Ex\_β; the glitch passes through an enabling pathway, not through the transforming part, and does not make Ex\_β hold.  
The data-carrying part is set going by the present initiation, operates for a few clock cycles and then stalls midway, and the read-out is given by noise: Ex holds, and it is not excluded; by S1 and its like (it operated wrongly, which belongs to the success or failure of this execution).  
Pencil-and-paper working continued overnight with pencil and paper outside the declared boundary: during the gap the state is not held by P, so En excludes a complete execution, and it is classed as an execution terminated; with pencil and paper within the boundary, by S6.  
A bit-serial adder: Ini holds under delivery spread over time, and it is not excluded; by S12 and its like.  
A free-running constant device (such as a reference voltage source): active maintenance under enablement — its transforming part (such as a regulating feedback loop) continues by its own dynamics under the enabling conditions, maintaining the same value, and is not static holding — is not excluded by the general formula; its formal criterion for distinction from "the output pinned at that constant by a fault" goes into H3; undetermined (U2).  
Mental arithmetic 23+19: posing the problem is initiation; switching to long-hand working midway is still the same run, and no item is lost thereby — what is switched to is another pathway of the same transforming part (Ex does not ask about pathways); if pencil and paper are not within the boundary, they serve only for holding and do not evaluate by proxy (holding across an enabling gap requires pencil and paper to be within the boundary, En); a mistake midway, by S1 and its like; being called away, an execution terminated.  
Spontaneous mental arithmetic: initiation is issued by the host itself; by S11. Mental arithmetic in a dream: whether it satisfies the enabling conditions depends on whether the declared enabling conditions include wakefulness; for the evidential standard see U3.  
Live migration of a virtual machine, recovery from a checkpoint on another machine: when the source machine, the target machine and the transmission channel are all within the declared boundary and transmission does not evaluate by proxy, this is one and the same run on one and the same instance; otherwise it is piecing together across instances, excluded by same-run binding (H2).  
A cache read during running that returns a previously computed result: the cache is addressed by the present operands, and on a hit the pathway dispatched to on the present occasion is the addressing and read-out of the cache, which is set going by the present initiation; Ex\_β holds and it is not excluded (the cache, together with the dispatching action and the computing pathway, counts as one realizing part). On a miss, with the computing pathway stalled and the read-out given by noise: the pathway dispatched to on the present occasion is not set going, and it is excluded by Ex\_β; even if the parallel lookup of the cache is set going, it is not the pathway dispatched to on the present occasion and is not counted. The formal conditions for preemption and dispatch go into H3.  
Runs of a permanently faulty adder: judged by the τ′ borne, or by addition on a reduced domain (Chapter 2, Corollary Four).  
One step of dynamical transformation in generative computation: a step of transformation acting on the extended state (x, D) (x being the current structures and relations; D being Chapter 5's dynamical conditions, not the domain D of this chapter's tasks), as P's execution of a one-step task, satisfies every item and is not excluded by the general formula; whether the dynamical conditions hold is not in the general formula (Chapter 5).  
A fluid in an experimental tank, run for a stretch under a settable and readable arrangement: given that bearing in the sense of Chapter 2 holds, it can satisfy every item and is not excluded by the general formula; whether it is an execution, and whether it is computation, are undetermined (U5).

Apart from the permanent-fault entry (where bearing on the original domain no longer holds), each entry keeps the premises of the general theorem and is a test of it.

Necessity and independent degeneration

Removing or weakening any one item, while keeping the remaining items in their original sense, leaves behind in each case a definite failure. "The present initiation" in Ex means the initiation event that occurs at the starting point of r; whether it belongs to the protocol of τ is decided by Ini; when Ini is removed, Ex keeps its original sense with this event as its reference.  
Remove Ini: one and the same arithmetic unit bears addition and multiplication, multiplication being realized by shift-and-add through the adder logic used for addition; the instruction issued on this occasion is a multiplication instruction (2×2), the arithmetic unit is set going by this instruction and operates as multiplication, and the read-out 4 happens to be read as the f(2, 2) of addition. En, ¬Sub, Ex (with this multiplication instruction as reference), Out and same-run binding all hold — enablement throughout, no external evaluation, the arithmetic unit set going by this instruction, the read-out obtained within the same run; only the initiation event of addition is lacking; remove Ini, and this run would be counted as an execution of addition.  
Remove En: the adder logic is set going by the present initiation, operates for a few clock cycles, and then the power is cut; before the cut, the intermediate state is saved by an external memory outside the declared boundary (storing only, computing nothing), and after power is restored the same instance continues from this state as read back, and writes out the reading. Ini holds; the external memory only holds and does not evaluate, so ¬Sub holds; the adder logic is set going by the present initiation, so Ex holds; the read-out is obtained within the same continuous stretch of process on the same instance, not restarted by reset, so Out and same-run binding hold. Only En is lacking: the state needed for continuation during the gap is not held by P itself. Remove En, and this would be counted as an execution by P; by E2, it is a run of the larger system consisting of P together with the external memory. (The case of redoing from a reset initial state after a power cut cannot be used for this test: the reset restart makes the subsequent process not belong to r, and Out and the binding fail along with it.) In this example, that the continuation after external saving still belongs to the same run rests on the distinction of Section Nine: the same run asks only about the same instance, the same continuous stretch of process, not restarted by reset, while holding belongs to En and not to the same run; whether this distinction can be stably determined on specific runs is conditional on H2. So the independent indispensability of En holds under H2.  
Remove ¬Sub: the adder logic is set going by the present initiation and operates as usual (a gate fault meaning it will give 6 on this occasion), and a person outside the box works out 5 separately and overwrites the display register before read-out. Ini, En, Ex, Out and same-run binding hold — the read-out is obtained within this run on the same instance; only ¬Sub is lacking; remove ¬Sub, and this would be counted as an execution by P.  
Remove Ex\_α: on a Π-trivial task all conditions of exercise are lost — the present clear instruction is lost before reaching the control unit, and the previous clear instruction in the queue happens to clear the same register; Ini, En, ¬Sub, Out and same-run binding hold, Ex\_β holds trivially for a Π-trivial task, only Ex\_α is lacking, and it would be counted as an execution. For non-trivial tasks, Ex\_α follows from Ex\_β, and removing it produces no new failure; the idle initiation of Section Six and the identity instruction lost before reaching the control unit are still excluded while Ex\_β is kept (the data-carrying part is likewise not set going by the present initiation). So Ex\_α and Ex\_β are counted as one item, Ex, for independent degeneration: remove Ex (both clauses together), and all the failure cases of Sections Six and Seven would be counted as executions.  
Remove Ex\_β: the control unit is set going while the arithmetic part has stalled and the display is rewritten by noise (Section Seven): Ini, En, ¬Sub, Ex\_α, Out and same-run binding hold — the noise does not evaluate, the control unit is set going by the present initiation, and the read-out is obtained within the same run; only Ex\_β is lacking, and it would be counted as an execution.  
Remove Out: the run interrupted midway (Section Eight): Ini and Run hold, and the binding holds for the items present; only reaching read-out is lacking, and it would be counted as one execution.  
Remove same-run binding: the piecing together of two calculators (Section Nine): Ini, Run and Out each hold on some run; only the three's falling on one and the same run is lacking, and it would be counted as one execution.

Each overstrong substitute excludes a class of standard positive cases:  
Requiring the result to be correct: S1, S2 and S3 are excluded.  
Requiring all the operating conditions to hold throughout: S5 is excluded.  
Requiring the enabling conditions to hold at every moment: S6 is excluded.  
Requiring every successor to be obtained by the host's own dynamics: S7 is excluded.  
Requiring the state held during an enabling gap to be subject to no external action whatever: cases of the S6 and S7 kind in which a transient bit flip occurs during a gating gap are excluded.  
Requiring the read-out to be obtained by the transforming part driven on this occasion: S13 is excluded.  
Requiring the operands to be legitimately delivered: S8 is excluded.  
Requiring the read-out to depend on the operands: S3 is excluded.  
Requiring the state content to change: S9 is excluded.  
Requiring an operand-dependent change of state in the data-carrying part: S4 is excluded.  
Requiring what the transforming part receives to be the original operands: S2 is excluded.  
Requiring the data-carrying part to respond to the data it receives: S4 is excluded.  
Requiring a particular mechanism pathway: S12 is excluded.  
Requiring the read-out to be observed, or requiring the user's intention: S10 and S11 are excluded.

Non-mergeability

Ini and Run: the multiplication-instruction case satisfies Run (Ex with this multiplication instruction as reference) but not the Ini of addition; a power cut immediately after initiation satisfies Ini but not Run.  
Run and Out: interruption midway satisfies Ini and Run but has no Out; a power cut after initiation with an old number remaining satisfies "there is a read-out" but has no Run (En fails).  
Within Run: En and ¬Sub cannot be derived from each other (the overwriting case satisfies En but not ¬Sub; the external-saving case satisfies ¬Sub but not En); En and ¬Sub on the one hand and Ex on the other cannot be derived from each other (the arithmetic part stalled with noise rewriting the display satisfies En and ¬Sub but not Ex\_β; the overwriting case satisfies Ex but not ¬Sub; the external-saving case satisfies Ex but not En); the relation between Ex\_α and Ex\_β: for non-trivial tasks, Ex\_β yields Ex\_α, and Ex\_α does not yield Ex\_β (control set going while the arithmetic part has stalled satisfies Ex\_α but not Ex\_β); for Π-trivial tasks there is only Ex\_α, whose indispensability is shown by the clear case under removal of Ex\_α. The two are therefore two strengths of one condition on two classes of task, not two independent conditions.  
Same-run binding is not a fourth item: it adds no fact of running, and only specifies that the facts of the items fall on one and the same run.

Relations of dependence: Ex takes as its reference the initiation event that occurs at the starting point given by Ini; Out takes as antecedent that r obtains successors from its starting point through Run; Ex takes the division of Tr as the range of its test. Each item in turn takes the preceding one as an antecedent; they are not pairwise logically unrelated. The order of proof does not indicate an order in time or in causation.

Exclusion of proposed additions: the nine items tested in Section Ten — correctness of the result, the source of the operands, a particular mechanism pathway, change of state content, dependence of the read-out, the read-out's being obtained by the transforming part driven on this occasion, response of the data-carrying part to the data, prior declaration or intention, observation of the read-out — open no new constraint on the task and do not belong to the necessary structure; each, if added to the general formula, is one of the overstrong substitutes listed above.

What minimality means: (1) each item of the general formula is forced by a case that keeps the preceding items and is not an execution (Sections Three to Nine); (2) under E1–E6 and the corresponding H conditions, removing any one item (Ex\_α and Ex\_β counted as one item, Ex) leaves behind a non-execution that would be counted as an execution (the independent degenerations above); (3) none of the proposed additions tested in Section Ten opens a new constraint, and adding any of them excludes some standard positive case (the overstrong substitutes above). This is indispensability relative to the items forced, and closure relative to the proposed additions tested. An unqualified "there is no weaker necessary condition" holds for no necessary condition whatever: take any condition Q that is not a tautology; then ∃r\[Ini ∧ Run ∧ Out\] ∨ Q is also a necessary condition of execution and can be strictly weaker; a tautology is likewise such a necessary condition, merely one with no discriminating power. Minimality with discriminating power can be posed only relative to a family of admissible candidate structures — structures whose components are facts about running and which can exclude each failure case of Sections Three to Nine; whether there is, within this family, a different and weaker set of components is an unanswered question (U4). In this sense, the general formula gives functional and structural minimal necessity; it does not prescribe a unique physical realization, nor the specific construction of the transforming part.

## Section Sixteen　Breach Argument

Ways of breach

With the premises of the general theorem, E1–E6, the task-side conditions and the corresponding H conditions maintained: exhibit P executing τ once in T while no single run satisfies Ini, Run and Out together; or exhibit one of the standard positive cases S1–S13 lacking one of Ini, En, ¬Sub, Ex\_α and Out; or exhibit a standard positive case of a non-trivial task whose data-carrying part is not set going by the present initiation (for a free-running device: does not continue by its own dynamics). Any of these constitutes a breach of the general theorem.

Against minimality: prove that two items can be merged, or that one item can be derived from the others together with the antecedents (that Ex\_β yields Ex\_α is already given under non-mergeability, the two being counted as one item, Ex); or prove that one of the proposed additions excluded in Section Ten is in fact necessary; or exhibit a run that satisfies every item yet, by E1–E5, is not one exercise of bearing and does not belong to the two undetermined cases listed under U2, and further show that what it lacks is a new necessary distinction — a structural difference that, by E1–E6, can be proved to be possessed by every execution and is not possessed by it; this would show that the closure of Section Ten does not hold, one necessary difference not having been forced. Merely exhibiting a run that satisfies every item and is not an execution shows directly that sufficiency fails (U1); it breaches neither the necessary direction nor the indispensability of the components.

Against the local results: exhibit an execution that has occurred as of t for which there is no segment of a run, with t₀ \< t₁ ≤ t, that satisfies Ini and satisfies Run on \[t₀, t₁\].

Not breaches: a run that satisfies every item but has a wrong result (it is an execution, E5); a run that satisfies every item but whose host is not a computing host (not in the general formula); another physical realization of the same structure; running outside the specification conditions (it can be execution, S5); a run that is interrupted (not one execution, by E6); a misjudgement of Ex under a misdeclaration of the initiation part (an error of declaration, H1).

Chapter 11, *The Determination Form of Computation*: the evidential standards for execution (the first part of U3, partly taken over); the two undetermined cases (U2); the boundary of enablement, as in mental arithmetic in a dream. The protocol for determining the correctness of execution (the latter part of U3) is to be taken up by further work on that form and is not yet established within the group; a verdict of execution for the tank's runs (U5) has not been taken over by that form and is not yet established within the group.

The conclusions of the downstream forms do not enter the premises of the general theorem in reverse.

## Section Eighteen　Closing

Compressed finally into three sentences:

Bearing is being able to do it; execution is really doing it this time;  
really doing it this time is for the present initiation to set going the dynamics that bears the task — for a non-trivial task, together with the part that carries the data — and for the host itself, under the enabling conditions and with no one computing on its behalf, to carry it all the way to read-out (the clause on "setting going" conditional on H3);  
whether it is done right or wrong is the success or failure of this execution, not whether it counts as an execution.

Compressed again into one sentence:

For a host to execute once a task it bears is for the present initiation, within one and the same run, to set going the dynamics that bears the task, and for the host itself, under the enabling conditions and without proxy evaluation, to carry it all the way to read-out, right or wrong (the clause on "setting going" conditional on H3).
