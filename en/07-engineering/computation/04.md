# The Mathematical Form of Computation

## Section One　Purpose

### 1.1　Source of the structure

*The General Form of Computational Bearing* (hereafter Chapter 2) and *The General Form of Computational Execution* (hereafter Chapter 3) give two general theorems.

Chapter 2: if a physical host P bears a mathematical task τ given in advance, then its reading schemes are defined on state types marked out in advance, the reading schemes themselves do not evaluate by proxy, input is reachable, and, with external non-proxy, the host itself produces an acceptable family of results:

　　Bear(P, τ) ⇒ Leg₁ ∧ Leg₂ ∧ Prov ∧ Prod

Chapter 3: if a bearer executes τ once within an interval T, then there is a run r in which initiation occurs, operation proceeds only under enablement and, when it stops midway, its state is held by the host itself and the run is continued, there is no proxy evaluation at any of the three places — reading scheme, delivery and external action — the transforming part is driven by the present initiation, for a non-trivial task the data-carrying part is also driven by the present initiation, and read-out is reached; the items are witnessed by one and the same run:

　　Exec(P, τ, T) ⇒ ∃r ⊆ T: Ini(r) ∧ En(r) ∧ ¬Sub(r) ∧ Ex\_α(r) ∧ Ex\_β(r) ∧ Out(r)

(Π-trivial tasks are exempt from Ex\_β; the necessity of the two clauses of Ex is conditional on Computational Execution General Form-H3(a)(b).)

The concepts in the two theorems — state type, reading scheme, delivery protocol, proxy evaluation, realizing part, transforming part, data-carrying part, "driven by the present initiation" — are delimited in natural language. Determining when they hold on a given physical model requires criteria that can be computed item by item.

### 1.2　The object of formalization

Write S\_Comp for the structure established in the two chapters. The formal system M\_Comp is the formal representation of S\_Comp on discrete local-dynamics models with ports:

　　S\_Comp ⟼ M\_Comp

M\_Comp gives two levels of predicates on one and the same physical model:

　　Type level　Leg^form(P, τ) ∧ N\_bear^form(P, τ): takes its value over all operands, all delivery protocols and admissible executions, and all normal exogenous values;  
　　Token level　N\_exec(r) = Ini ∧ En ∧ ¬Sub ∧ Ex\_α ∧ Ex\_β ∧ Out: takes its value on the actual values of one run.

Predicates at both levels take values in {1, 0, ⊥}, ⊥ meaning that the matter cannot be determined under the declared model. Each formal predicate is the formal image of a structural item of S\_Comp. The sequence of definitions corresponds item by item to the structural items of S\_Comp (correspondence table 7.1); for some items a local correspondence has been proved, and each is annotated with the strength proved (Theorem 7.14) — none of these is a proof of semantic equivalence; the rest are candidate representations under formal conventions, and the read-back of their determinate values states the conventions and conditions relied on (7.1). That a formal image takes the value 1 says only that the corresponding necessary structure holds under the declared model; neither bearing nor execution can be inferred from it. Formal determinations hold relative to the declared model; sufficient correspondence between the model and an actual physical host is not guaranteed by these conditions.

## Section Two　Core Theses

M\_Comp consists of three main lines, all built on one and the same local-dynamics model.

Thesis One (the family quantifier of bearing)　The formal image of Prod is a universal in a fixed order:

　　Prod^form ⇔ R\_B^form ∧ ∀(π\_d)\_{d∈D} ∀(σ\_d)\_{d∈D}: Acc\_Π((μ^d\_{π\_d,σ\_d})\_{d∈D}, f)

The outer quantifier ranges over the delivery protocols for each operand, the next over the unspecified choices the deliverer may make, and the innermost makes a single judgement on the whole family of result distributions; the result distribution μ is obtained as a pushforward under the local dynamics. Random components of the protocol that have probabilities enter μ; choices without probabilities enter the universal; external non-proxy R\_B^form is a conjunct.

Thesis Two (the actual source of action in execution)　"The evolution of the transforming part is driven by the present initiation" is represented as follows: there is an actual path of events starting from the initiation part S and passing only through condition ports and content ports such that, when S is removed, every event on the path either does not occur or changes content, and this difference is transmitted at each step through that step's condition input or content input (written S ▷\_r W). Differences brought in through enable ports such as power supply, clock and gating are not counted. Ex\_α and Ex\_β require such events in the transforming part and in the data-carrying part respectively; the same run is bound by anchor assignment.

Thesis Three (finite solving and three-valued read-back)　On finite, completely declared discrete models, the predicates at both levels take unique values by finite procedures; where the model is insufficient for a determination the value ⊥ is taken, not 0; three-valued connectives are strong Kleene, so that, within one and the same declared model, a 0 or 1 already given does not change when other ⊥s are later determined. Refining the physical model itself — subdividing nodes, changing the exogenous representation, adding ports — is another matter: determinate values are stable only within the declared refinement family and family of exogenous representations (Proposition 7.11′). Each output is read back to a structural item of S\_Comp by the read-back rules.

The division of duties in bearing — realizing part, reception and holding, transforming part, data-carrying part — connects Theses One and Two: at the type level it is identified by minimal sets of actions that retain production, and at the token level it gives the range over which Ex is tested. Holding at the type level does not imply occurrence at the token level.

## Section Three　Basic Objects and the Sequence of Definitions

The definitions are given in the order of dependence: physical level, reading-scheme level, operand level, bearing level, duty level, execution level; each definition ends with a note of the structural item of S\_Comp to which it corresponds (correspondence table in 7.1).

### 3.1　Physical level

Definition 3.1 (host model)　A host model is 𝔐\_P = (𝒦, 𝕋, {𝒳\_i}, Port, {G\_i, F\_i, H\_i}, U, 𝔇).  
　(1) Components. A holding element is a physical unit that retains its state when undriven, or a group of atomic units selected by one and the same write-path strobe (a register). Component i is a holding element h\_i together with its upstream combinational evaluation network (extending upstream as far as the outputs of other holding elements or boundary ports); each output node of a purely combinational network is a holding element of its own. Combinational logic upstream of condition ports is absorbed into G\_i. Nodes without a write-path strobe, and active stages that regulate their state according to their input, are called driven nodes; their content additionally includes a declared gate-level node vector. 𝒦 is finite; 𝕋 is discrete, with step Δ; 𝒳\_i is the state space of h\_i, whose normally reachable subset is declared.  
　(2) Ports. The ports of component i fall into three classes: condition ports (those entering the write-path strobe: write enable, load strobe, write select), content ports (those determining the value written, including h\_i's own feedback) and enable ports (power supply, global clock, gating, watchdog). Port j is an enable port if and only if: in normal running the value of j does not vary with the task or the operand; j gates all transitions of i uniformly — outside the enabling range, E\_i = 0 whatever the other inputs, and within the range it does not select among different transitions of i; and j carries no event of the initiation part at the type level (where one signal carries both roles, as in start-on-power-up, it counts as both). The other strobe-type ports are condition ports. Every physical channel crossing the boundary B that can set a held state is represented as a port of some component; channels that change only rate or reliability are represented as exogenous terms. Delivered content that enters a condition port directly through a boundary port is represented as a write of a virtual entry component.  
　(3) Exogenous terms. U = U^per × U^tr. U^per = U^cfg × U^flt is the persisting condition (configuration and faults), constant within a run. U^tr is the transient term, divided into a disturbance component (acting on held states other than through ports, such as high-energy particles, glitches, crosstalk) and a stochastic-dynamics component (noise that is an argument of stochastic dynamics); the distinction between them is given by the declared exogenous representation. The declaration of the disturbance component is legitimate if and only if Prod^form still holds after the whole of it is replaced by the declared zero value (the other components keeping their joint law), and Prod^form fails once any one stochastic-dynamics component is also included in that replacement. There may be several ways of writing stochastic dynamics as "a deterministic function plus exogenous noise"; where a determination varies with the representation within the declared family of exogenous representations, ⊥ is recorded.  
　(4) Declared items 𝔇: the boundary B, the entry, the read-out and read-out condition R\_out, the family of delivery protocols (Definition 3.7), the operating conditions Env, and the initiation part S.  
　(5) Normal running: the operating conditions are within Env, U^flt takes its normal value, the exogenous values (together with the protocol's random components) lie in the support of their declared joint law, and delivery proceeds by some admissible execution of some protocol. The reference run r° of a run r is the normal run with the same configuration as r, U^flt at its normal value, and the same operand, exogenous realization and initial state.  
Correspondence: Chapter 2, "host, declared items"; Chapter 3, "P's actual condition on the present occasion."

Definition 3.2 (actual event)　On an interval I, E\_i = 1 if and only if, within I, the state of h\_i is determined by a driving path through a condition port or a content port: for a gated element, the interval over which the write path is strobed open (whether or not the content changes); for an ungated node, the interval over which it is driven by the output stage of its evaluation network; for an active stage, the interval over which it regulates h\_i according to its input. Maintenance solely by self-latching, passive energy storage or leakage, and a driving stage pinned by a persisting fault, do not count as E = 1. Events: for a driven node, the maximal intervals over which "E = 1 and the content (including the gate-level node vector) is unchanged"; for other components, the connected components of E = 1. Events e and e′ of the same component in two situations (a run and its counterfactual, or two paired runs) are identified if and only if their intervals overlap and e′ is the earliest of those overlapping e; an event that overlaps no event "does not occur" in the other situation. The events of a run r are those assigned to the present anchor (Definition 3.15) whose intervals meet \[t\_S, t\_end\]; t\_S is the moment at which the earliest event of the present initiation part begins (for S = ∅, the starting point t\_a of the interval anchor, Definition 3.14), and t\_end is as in Definition 3.15.  
Refinement family: a declared sequence of levels L₀ ≤ … ≤ L\_m, where L₀ is the declared model itself (together with its declared gate-level node vectors), and the subsequent levels successively add gate-level node vectors not yet declared, finer divisions into nodes and smaller steps; levels coarser than the declared model are not included in the refinement family. Stability on the refinement family is taken as agreement across all levels: a predicate that takes the same determinate value at every declared level takes that value; where any two levels differ (including where some level gives ⊥), ⊥\_M is recorded. A 0 obtained on a combinational network from "the content of every declared node is unchanged" further requires L\_m to be at switch level; otherwise ⊥ is recorded. Events invisible at coarser levels but visible at finer ones (such as those appearing only on gate-level node vectors) make predicates that depend on them differ between the two levels, and ⊥\_M is recorded; the refinement family is a declared item, declared before evaluation (working domain W3), and is not enlarged or reduced according to the results of evaluation; a difference at any level of the family is not overridden by the value at a finer level.  
Correspondence: the physical identification of Chapter 3's "successors obtained by the host's own dynamics."

### 3.2　Reading-scheme level

Definition 3.3 (internal representation and its legitimacy)　An atom is the smallest physical unit that can be set independently. An internal representation is R = (𝔯, ν): the atomic code 𝔯 is fixed solely by the physical state type of each atom itself (voltage threshold, polarity, unit and scale); the grouping code ν groups atoms into words and assigns them values. Canonical internal representation: inside the host, the native atomic code of each physical kind and a single uniform grouping code ν\* are used throughout, and every representation designed for the task (logarithmic scales, scale factors, BCD, offsets) is concentrated in the boundary maps: ρ\_in = κ\_in ∘ ν\* ∘ 𝔯, ρ\_out = κ\_out ∘ ν\* ∘ 𝔯. R is legitimate if and only if:  
　L-1　the set of state types of each word is the Cartesian product of the sets of state types of its atoms, and each atom can be rewritten while the other atoms stay unchanged;  
　L-2　the atomic code is given before and independently of the runs under test, and is uniform by physical kind;  
　L-3　a copy of the same physical quantity, from a single content input and preserving state types, is the identity atom by atom under R; between media of different kinds, copying follows the standard transduction correspondence (unique, with tolerance ε\_𝒮), and where this correspondence is not unique, or the determination is unstable among the available correspondences, ⊥ is recorded.  
Where there is no legitimate R compatible with the reading schemes, Leg₂^form is recorded as ⊥; for a physical kind with no native atomic code to rely on, determinations at the atomic level involving it are recorded as ⊥.  
Correspondence: Chapter 2, "state types," "representations designed for the task."

Definition 3.4 (coordinate map)　A map m : ∏\_{β∈𝒜\_in} A\_β → ∏\_{β′∈𝒜\_out} A\_{β′} is a coordinate map if and only if, for each output atom β′, either there is a fixed input atom ℓ(β′) such that m(y)\_{β′} = y\_{ℓ(β′)} (copying under native codes of the same kind; between different kinds, by the standard transduction correspondence), or m(y)\_{β′} is constant; the determination is made only on the normally reachable domain. The class of coordinate maps is written Coord.  
Correspondence: the deterministic branch of Chapter 3's "preservation-class obtaining" (preserving, moving or copying unchanged, selection and rearrangement at fixed positions, giving constants independent of the operands), and the same line on which Chapter 2's "reading schemes do not evaluate by proxy" relies.

### 3.3　Operand level

Definition 3.5 (task and acceptance protocol)　A task τ = (D, f, ρ\_in, ρ\_out, Π), with f : D → V (or D → Δ(V)), has the extension of f on D as its identity. The acceptance protocol Acc\_Π : Δ(V ∪ {⊥\_V})^D × Fn\_τ → {0, 1} is a family predicate on families of result distributions. Tolerance class (f taking values in V): with a declared metric δ\_V on V, Acc^{ε,p}((μ\_d), f) :⇔ ∀d: μ\_d({v : δ\_V(v, f(d)) ≤ ε}) ≥ p; the exact class and the pointwise-reliability class are special cases.  
　Triv\_Π(τ) :⇔ there is a μ*, independent of d, such that the constant family (μ*)\_d satisfies Acc\_Π.  
　Id(τ) (identity class) :⇔ for every protocol rule and every admissible-execution rule, there is a random coordinate selection (a coordinate map under each selection, with a selection law not depending on d) such that the family of results obtained by applying κ\_out ∘ ν\* ∘ m to the delivered states satisfies Acc\_Π.  
Constant maps are coordinate maps, so a Π-trivial task whose μ\* can be taken with support within the union of the image of κ\_out ∘ ν\* and {⊥\_V} belongs to the identity class; where the support must go beyond it, Triv\_Π and Id are determined separately.  
Correspondence: Chapter 2, "task, acceptance protocol, Π-trivial task"; Chapter 3, "identity class" — delimited by the acceptance protocol: the tasks for which there is a family of result distributions, obtained by preservation-class obtaining, that satisfies Acc\_Π. "For every protocol rule and every admissible-execution rule" is the precise form of "there is a family" when the delivered states vary with the protocol; the random coordinate selection is the branch of Chapter 3's preservation-class obtaining "a random value independent of the operands; selection may depend on such random components."

### 3.4　Bearing level

Definition 3.6 (Leg₁^form)　A family of state types 𝒯 is a measurable partition of the state space, declared by a description of physical kinds 𝒞\_phys and a history of formation Hist\_\< prior to the runs under test: Decl = Φ(𝒞\_phys, Hist\_\<). Types are not delimited by the states passed through by the runs under test (or by run-by-run prediction or simulation of them); design simulation by category, not aimed at individual runs, is not excluded by this. Leg₁^form :⇔ 𝒯 = 𝒯(Decl), and the same 𝒯 is used for every run under test within the declared period.  
Correspondence: Chapter 2, Leg₁.

Definition 3.7 (delivery protocol)　A protocol π ∈ Prov\_τ(d) consists of an intervention procedure through the entry, random components ξ\_π with their declared law (declared together with the joint law of the exogenous values U), and a set of unspecified choices Σ\_π ≠ ∅; σ ∈ Σ\_π is an admissible execution. Choices with declared probabilities belong to ξ\_π, those without declared probabilities to Σ\_π, and none belongs to both. A protocol rule is a map d ↦ π\_d, and an admissible-execution rule is a map d ↦ σ\_d. A selection rule declared in a protocol satisfies the load-carrying rule if and only if the position, within the type, of the selected physical starting point is constant in d (stated in physical coordinates declared in advance and independent of d), or Prod^form still holds after the rule is replaced by an unspecified choice within the same type; where it does not, Leg₂^form is recorded as ⊥. Prov^form :⇔ ∀d ∈ D: Prov\_τ(d) ≠ ∅.  
Correspondence: Chapter 2, Prov and the delimitation of delivery protocols.

Definition 3.8 (Leg₂^form)　Let the read-out chain Ch be the actions on the write chains and condition chains of the read-out content, together with the actions on the read-out-condition chains (Definition 3.9), and let Ch^W be the ancestor actions of the read-out content within it. Leg₂^form holds if and only if:  
　(i) R is legitimate (Definition 3.3);  
　(ii) the execution of each protocol falls within the realization set of d, and the type delivered is fixed by d alone through (κ\_in ∘ ν\* ∘ 𝔯)⁻¹; where the physical starting point obtained by delivery (including atoms not read by ρ\_in) satisfies the VInc of Definition 3.18, Leg₂^form = 0; where its value-level content changes and varies with d without satisfying VInc, and is neither a mere change of representation nor independent of d and f, ⊥ is recorded; where it contains only coordinate images of d, is only a change of representation, or is independent of d and f, this clause is passed; where a declared selection rule does not satisfy the load-carrying rule, ⊥ is recorded (Definition 3.7);  
　(iii) the read-out value is obtained solely by applying ρ\_out to the read-out type;  
　(iv) κ\_in and κ\_out are legitimate (for same-type tasks, i.e. D = V; the two codes are compared only on the read-out codes reachable in normal running):  
　　(κ-0) where κ\_out ≠ κ\_in and Ch^W contains an action within the host that is exactly a same-value conversion between the two codes, followed only by atom-by-atom copying and identity holding, the codes are legitimate; where the conversion is entangled with actions evaluating f and cannot be separated from them, ⊥ is recorded;  
　　(κ-1) where no non-reception action in Ch^W has Dep\* (Definition 3.12): κ\_out = κ\_in ∈ 𝒦\_std is legitimate, and κ\_out = κ\_in ∉ 𝒦\_std is recorded as ⊥; where κ\_out ≠ κ\_in and (κ-0) does not apply, Leg₂^form = 0 if and only if all of the following hold — no non-reception action in Ch has Dep\*, the model belongs to the class of coordinate models (the condition of Theorem 7.6), the subdivision of write actions has been declared, the only actions in Ch^W that reach read-out atoms through content links and holding are atom-by-atom identity copying and identity holding, every atom of the read-out is thereby taken from some input atom, κ\_out ≠ κ\_in still holds after the atom order of the read-out word is aligned with that of the input word, and Prod^form holds under κ\_out but fails when κ\_in is substituted for it; otherwise ⊥ is recorded;  
　　(κ-2) where Ch^W contains a non-reception action with Dep\*: κ\_out = κ\_in ∈ 𝒦\_std is legitimate; where κ\_out ≠ κ\_in and (κ-0) applies, (κ-0) governs; otherwise ⊥ is recorded.  
　(κ-0) is determined before (κ-1) and (κ-2); (κ-1) and (κ-2) are mutually exclusive according to whether a non-reception action in Ch^W has Dep\*.  
𝒦\_std is a closed standard list: standard discrete codes (binary, two's complement, offset binary, BCD, Gray code, seven-segment code), linear scale factors, logarithmic scales, residue representations. For tasks of different types (D ≠ V), the legitimacy of κ is recorded as ⊥. Leg^form := Leg₁^form ∧ Leg₂^form.  
Correspondence: Chapter 2, Leg₂ (reading schemes do not evaluate by proxy), and the example of "proxy evaluation by the reading scheme" (the copying device that multiplies by 2 at read-out).

Definition 3.9 (actions and links)　Component i has two kinds of action: write actions (the class of i's E = 1 events, which may be subdivided by the pattern of condition inputs that trigger them, the subdivision being a declared item) and hold actions (the E = 0 intervals with their holding law or dissipation law). For events e and e′, e → e′ is an actual link if and only if, at some time step within the interval of e′, content written by e and still held is, through a condition port or content port of e′'s component, an argument of that component's G or F (for a subdivided action, the G and F of the subdivided action to which it belongs); links are identified by port structure. Links through enable ports are called enabling links and are not actual links. A write chain is a sequence of actual links ending in the read-out write and its read-out holding interval; a condition chain consists of the links of a write chain that pass through condition ports. The read-out condition R\_out is declared as a predicate on the states of read-out-condition elements (a declared set of holding elements) and on synchronous boundary inputs; a read-out-condition chain is a sequence of actual links (through condition ports or content ports) ending in a write to a read-out-condition element. The read-out moment t\_out thus depends on the actions on the read-out-condition chains.  
Correspondence: Chapter 3, "through which the declared results are obtained."

Definition 3.10 (result distribution and Prod)　The result distribution μ^d\_{π,σ} is given by Equation (4.2). R\_B^form :⇔ in every normal run within Env and every admissible execution, each segment of the actual content source (Definition 3.18, write clause or hold clause) that acts on the host from outside the boundary after delivery is complete and before read-out, entering the read-out through a declared port, takes on no evaluation according to Definition 3.18 with the term ¬NC removed.  
　　Prod^form :⇔ R\_B^form ∧ ∀(π\_d) ∈ ∏\_d Prov\_τ(d) ∀(σ\_d) ∈ ∏\_d Σ\_{π\_d}: Acc\_Π((μ^d\_{π\_d,σ\_d})\_{d∈D}, f) = 1  
　　N\_bear^form := Prov^form ∧ Prod^form  
When some Prov\_τ(d) = ∅, the product is empty and Prod^form is vacuously true; N\_bear^form is not vacuously true because it contains Prov^form.  
Correspondence: Chapter 2, Prod and external non-proxy R\_B; the right-hand side Leg ∧ N\_bear of Chapter 2's general theorem.

### 3.5　Duty level

Definition 3.11 (realizing part)　Fix a configuration c. A retention set 𝔎 is a set of actions. "Retaining 𝔎": remove every removable event outside 𝔎 (Definition 3.16; events of delivered content and of the persisting condition are not removed), let the hold actions outside 𝔎 take the declared failure value (a constant independent of d, by default including the reset value), and retain everything that is connected to the chains only through enable ports. 𝔎 is a Prod-retaining set if and only if, for every assignment of failure values, Prod^form holds under c when 𝔎 is retained; it is minimal if none of its proper subsets is a Prod-retaining set.  
　Byp (bypass) and redundant-copy groups are declared items. Byp is legitimate if and only if there is a Prod-retaining set under c disjoint from Byp, and, for Id(τ) or Triv\_Π(τ), Byp does not contain Act\_S(c); where it is not legitimate, Real(c) is recorded as ⊥.  
　　Real(c) := ⋃{𝔎 : 𝔎 is a minimal Prod-retaining set under c} ∖ Byp  
　For Id(τ) or Triv\_Π(τ), Act\_S(c) is added to Real(c) — the actions on the read-out write chains and condition chains some condition input of which traces back, through the structure of condition links, to S. The resulting Real(c) (together with what enabling requires) must itself be a Prod-retaining set; otherwise Real(c) is recorded as ⊥. For S = ∅ (free running), Real(c) is recorded as ⊥. Where Prod-retention cannot be determined in the model, ⊥ is recorded. Real is obtained solely from the configuration c and normal running; the persisting faults U^flt of the present run do not change it, and duties in a faulty run are reckoned by the duties in normal running under the same configuration (Chapter 3: the realizing part is identified by normal running). At the token level, Real\_r := Real(U^cfg\_r).  
Proposition (attached to the definition): (a) actions irrelevant to the read-out belong to no minimal Prod-retaining set; (b) each redundant copy belongs to some minimal sets; (c) where every pathway is required for some d, the pathways together with the dispatching action belong to one minimal set. Proof: (a) a Prod-retaining set containing it is still a Prod-retaining set when it is removed; (b) and (c) because each copy and each pathway is required, on the corresponding operands, to retain Prod. ∎  
Correspondence: the realizing part of Chapter 3 — "if only this part is retained (together with what enabling requires) and the rest is rendered inoperative, Prod still holds; bypasses are not included; redundant parts each count separately; the pathways together with the dispatching action count as one." Taking "the results are obtained through them" as "belongs to some minimal Prod-retaining set" is a formal convention: Chapter 3 gives the properties of the realizing part in the five clauses above, not a unique construction; when Real(c) takes a determinate value it satisfies the five clauses (Proposition 7.3′), and whether other constructions satisfying the five clauses give the same set is not proved (8.3).

Definition 3.12 (duties, and dependence on the operand)　The value duty of an action a is the map m\_a that it induces under the atomic code (Equation (4.4)); its condition duty is the effect of its output, through outgoing condition edges, on whether downstream events occur; its timing duty is the variation of its write moment with the input, as used downstream. A duty belongs to the write action that produces the output in question; for outputs written before t₀, to the corresponding hold action. Paired runs (r₁, r₂) are two normal runs differing only in operand (d₁ ≠ d₂), with the same protocol rule and admissible-execution rule, the same configuration, U^flt normal and the same exogenous realization value ω. Let j range over the immediate downstream components of a.  
　Dep\_V^ω(a): there are paired runs with exogenous realization value ω in which the content inputs of a's events differ; Dep\_V(a) :⇔ ∃ω Dep\_V^ω(a);  
　Dep\_C(a): there are paired runs, a time step t and j such that E\_j(t) differs between the two runs, and, in some order of replacing, item by item, the entries of c\_j(t) whose values differ between the two runs by their r₂ values in place of their r₁ values, the step that replaces the entry coming from a changes G\_j;  
　Dep\_T(a): as above, and the write moment of a differs between the two runs;  
　Dep\*(a) :⇔ Dep\_C(a) ∨ Dep\_T(a) ∨ ∃ω \[m\_a^ω ∉ Coord ∧ Dep\_V^ω(a)\].  
Correspondence: the "dependence on the operand" on which Chapter 3's "transforming part" and "data-carrying part" rely.

Definition 3.13 (reception and holding, transforming part, data-carrying part)　At the type level, Recv consists of: (a) write actions whose value duty is a coordinate map, which have neither Dep\_C nor Dep\_T, and whose condition inputs trace back only to events of delivered content; (b) hold actions whose holding law is the identity and which have neither Dep\_C nor Dep\_T; (c) the entry stage of combinational devices (where initiation is the arrival of the operands); at the token level there is in addition Recv\_r(d) (below). Tr := Real ∖ Recv (control and timing belong to Tr; hold actions that change content according to a dissipation law belong to Tr).  
　Data₀ := {a ∈ Tr : a lies on some write chain, and Dep\*(a)}; Data₀^Id := Data₀ ∪ {a ∈ Tr : a lies on some write chain, m\_a ∈ Coord, Dep\_V(a), and some condition input of a traces back, through the structure of condition links, to S}. By cases, write  
　　Data₀^\* := Data₀^Id (for Id(τ)), Data₀^\* := Data₀ (for ¬Id(τ));  
the token-level formulas below use Data₀^\* throughout.  
　Token level: a dispatching action s is an action that, in the present run, selects between two or more alternative pathways; a pathway is the set of actions passed through from the output of s to the read-out write. Λ\_r is the pathway actually selected by s in the present run — where s has no event but the selection it holds still selects some pathway, that pathway is taken; where the selection output itself is indeterminate, Λ\_r is undefined. The routing exception Sel(s, r) holds if and only if ¬Id(τ), Λ\_r is defined, and (E1) Λ\_r itself carries the operands, (E2) Λ\_r contains an action whose value duty is non-coordinate and depends on the operands, and (E3) on the operands dispatched to Λ\_r, the content map of Λ\_r equals f and every atom of the read-out is given by writes in Λ\_r; where (E3) cannot be determined, ⊥ is recorded. Recv\_r(d) := ⋃\_{Sel(s,r)}({s} ∪ CondUp(s) ∪ {transfer actions moving operands to the entry of Λ\_r}), where CondUp(s) is the set of upstream actions that exist only to obtain the dispatching condition of s. Alt\_r is the set of actions belonging only to pathways not selected.  
　　Data\_r := \[(Data₀^\* ∩ Anc(Λ\_r)) ∪ ⋃\_{¬Sel(s,r)}({s} ∪ {b ∈ Λ\_r ∩ Tr : Dep\_V(b)})\] ∖ (Alt\_r ∪ Recv\_r(d))  
Anc(Λ\_r) is the set of write-chain ancestors of Λ\_r together with Λ\_r (the type-level ancestors under the present configuration; where Λ\_r is undefined, the ancestors in r). Where there are several dispatching actions, Λ\_r is the intersection of the pathways selected by each. Where there is no dispatching action, Data\_r = Data₀^\* ∩ Anc(read-out) ∖ Recv\_r(d). Tr\_r := Tr(U^cfg\_r) ∖ Recv\_r(d).  
Check (three cases of the identity class, single configuration, no dispatching action): (a) MOV: the transfer action copies the source word (m\_a ∈ Coord), its content input varies with the operand (Dep\_V), and its write enable traces back to S through condition links; it belongs to Act\_S and hence lies within Real (3.11); its condition inputs do not trace back only to delivered content, so it does not belong to Recv, and lies within Tr; it therefore belongs to Data₀^Id, and, being a write-chain ancestor of the read-out, belongs to Data\_r. (b) In-place write-back x ← x: the write-back action of RX is as in (a), and belongs to Data\_r; the state content may be the same before and after, and the event occurs all the same (Definition 3.2). (c) Static storage (delivery is placement, read-out is reading in place): there is no write action that S sets going; Act\_S = ∅, Tr = ∅, Data₀^Id = ∅, Ex\_α = 0 — it bears without being able to execute (Chapter 3, Section Six).  
Correspondence: Chapter 3's "reception and holding," "transforming part," "data-carrying part" and the dispatch rule — where the dispatching action only decides which pathway gives the result and gives no part of the result, it belongs to reception, and the data-carrying part is taken as the part of the dispatched pathway that carries the operands; otherwise the dispatching action together with the pathway belongs to the data-carrying part. (E1)(E2)(E3) correspond to these three items clause by clause; identifying "the pathway dispatched to" by actual selection is a formal convention.

### 3.6　Execution level

Definition 3.14 (initiation part and Ini)　The initiation part S is a set of events in the delivery protocol, which takes part in counterfactuals as a whole; t₀ is the moment at which S is completed, and t\_S the moment at which the earliest event of the present S begins; \[t\_S, t₀\] is called the initiation phase. Events of S in the initiation phase, and events brought about by them through actual links (such as the write of the task-selection state), belong to the present run and may form the front segment of an actual path (Definition 3.17); the operand reading is taken at t₀. Task-selection events (operation key, opcode) belong to S. Any other protocol event e belongs to S if and only if, in normal running, the first transition of the transforming part occurs only after e (or a declared alternative realization of it, the relation of alternative realization being declared as an equivalence relation) has occurred, and does not occur in normal running lacking e and all its alternative realizations; where the Tr obtained from the declared S does not pass this test, the declaration of S is invalid (⊥). The edges of a free-running clock do not belong to S. In combinational devices, S is the arrival of the operands. For a free-running device, S = ∅ and there is no initiation phase: t\_S = t₀ := t\_a, where t\_a is the starting point of its interval anchor (Definition 3.15).  
　Ini(r) :⇔ the events of S occur by t₀ on the instance on which r lies (one from each group of mutual alternative realizations), and there is a d such that the state at t₀ falls within the realization set of d; for delivery spread over time, from t₀ onwards a delivery process read as d is carried out through the entry. For S = ∅ only the latter clause is required.  
Ground for the clause on clock edges: Chapter 3 classes as not an execution the case in which the initiation signal is lost while the adder logic is processing the previous queued instruction (the case from which Chapter 3 is compelled to introduce Ex\_α); on clock-driven devices, if the edges of a free-running clock belonged to S, the transforming part would always be set going by S, and this classification could not stand.  
Correspondence: Chapter 3, "initiation part and content part," and Ini.

Definition 3.15 (same run and Out)　The process graph of an instance has events and holding intervals as nodes, and actual links and continuity of holding as edges. There are two kinds of anchor. Initiation anchors: for S ≠ ∅, the anchors are the successive completions of S, and the events of the present S are assigned to the anchor formed by their completion. Interval anchors: for S = ∅ (free running), the anchor is a declared interval I\_A = \[t\_a, t\_b\] (Chapter 3: for a free-running device, the stretch under discussion counts as one run), and the interval anchors of one instance are pairwise disjoint. The anchor-assignment function α assigns nodes to anchors (a declared item; under initiation anchors, for devices with at most one anchor in progress at any moment, the default is the nearest anchor in time found by tracing back along write-chain ancestors with no termination in between; under interval anchors, the default assigns to A the nodes of the instance whose intervals meet I\_A). The root set Root(A) of an anchor A: for an initiation anchor, the events of the present S; for an interval anchor, the events and holding intervals on the instance at t\_a. The process terminates at t if and only if at t the state of Tr (under interval anchors Tr is recorded as ⊥, Definition 3.11, and the states of all holding elements within the boundary are used in its place) is entirely lost with no continuation thereafter recovered from a checkpoint within the boundary, or at t it is put into an initial state not belonging to the process (recovery from its own checkpoint excluded). t\_end is the earlier of the moment t\_out at which the read-out condition is first reached and the moment of termination; for interval anchors t\_b is an additional upper bound. The run individual of anchor A is r\_A := the nodes of α⁻¹(A) up to t\_end; r\_A ∈ Run^ok if and only if its nodes all belong to Root(A) or are reachable from Root(A) (under interval anchors this clause is not imposed: the nodes assigned to A within the interval all belong to the present run, including exogenous events entering through declared ports and what they bring about), its splice edges through checkpoints, migration or enabling gaps all lie within the boundary and contain no segment that takes on evaluation, and there are no nodes after termination. Segments: r₁ ≈ r₂ :⇔ there is r\_A ∈ Run^ok of which both are segments; a segment is a non-empty subset of the nodes of r\_A. The two kinds of anchor share the anchor assignment, termination, t\_end, the run individual, the splice and termination clauses of Run^ok, ≈ and Out, and differ only in the root set, the reachability clause and the upper bound of t\_end; the determination of the same run is one and the same definition for both.  
　Out(r) :⇔ there is t\_out ∈ \[t₀, t\_end\] such that R\_out is reached at t\_out, t\_out lies within the run individual of r with no termination before it, the read-out holding element belongs to the same instance, and its type is read out through ρ\_out as a value in V ∪ {⊥\_V}. Whether the read-out is correct, and whether its content comes from the events of r, do not matter.  
Correspondence: Chapter 3, "same run" and Out; interval anchors correspond to Chapter 3's counting for free-running devices: "once" is reckoned by the stretch under discussion (Chapter 3, E1).

Definition 3.16 (removal semantics)　The removable set 𝒪(r): the events actually occurring in r that do not belong to S, to the content part of delivery, or to the persisting condition (events whose occurrence is fixed by U^per and independent of condition inputs). For Γ ⊆ 𝒪(r) ∪ S, the counterfactual r\[−Γ\]:  
　　E′\_i(t) = E^r\_i(t) ∧ G\_i(c′\_i(t), en′\_i(t), U^r\_i(t)) ∧ \[(i, t) lies within no event of Γ\]  
X′ is recomputed from t\_S by Equation (4.1) with E′ and the counterfactual inputs; exogenous values, delivered content and events of the persisting condition are kept as actual. Since E′ ≤ E^r, removal only makes events not occur, and does not make events occur that did not occur in r (the frozen reading, a formal convention). In contrast, Chapter 3's counterfactual r^{Exec}\[−Γ\] (take Γ away and let the rest evolve freely by the local dynamics) can contain events absent from r. For an event W of r, the ancestor domain Anc\_r(W) is the smallest set of nodes (component, time step) satisfying the following two conditions: it contains each time step of W; (i) it is closed under read dependence — if it contains a node, it contains all of that node's input nodes in the time-unrolled graph (together with the holding node of that component at the previous time step); (ii) it is closed under the E = 1 segments of r — if it contains a time step at which some component has E = 1 in r, it contains the whole connected E = 1 segment of that component in r that contains this time step. By (W1) it is finite. Write NB\_r(W): in r^{Exec}\[−S\], no time step with E = 1 that is absent from r appears on Anc\_r(W). The two counterfactuals r\[−S\] and r^{Exec}\[−S\] coincide on Anc\_r(W) under NB\_r(W) (Theorem 7.4); outside Anc\_r(W) they may differ; when NB\_r(W) fails they may differ (Example 7.5′), and Definition 3.20 distinguishes cases accordingly.  
Correspondence: Chapter 3's counterfactual clause, "what is removed is the present initiation part … and P's actual condition on the present occasion, are held fixed."

Definition 3.17 (actual source of action)　An actual path is a sequence of events e₀ ∈ 𝔈, e₁, …, e\_k = W (k ≥ 1, W ∉ 𝔈, no event repeated), each step of which is an actual link. 𝔈 ▷\_r W if and only if there are an actual path p and Γ ⊆ 𝒪(r) ∖ (p ∪ 𝔈) such that  
　　AC1　p runs from 𝔈 to W;  
　　AC2　in r\[−Γ\], each event on p occurs with its content unchanged (under the identification of Definition 3.2);  
　　AC3′　for each m = 1, …, k, e\_m does not occur, or changes content, in r\[−(Γ ∪ 𝔈)\], and Diff\_{e\_{m−1}→e\_m}: let j be the component of e\_m; at some time step t within the interval of e\_m, starting from j's inputs in r\[−(Γ ∪ 𝔈)\], first replace the entries at enable ports by their values in r\[−Γ\]; (a) if all the entries among the condition and content inputs whose values differ are then replaced at once by their values in r\[−Γ\], G\_j or F\_j changes; (b) there is an order of replacing these entries such that, at the step that replaces the entry coming from content written by e\_{m−1} and still held at t, G\_j or F\_j changes.  
Where identification is not injective on the events of p, AC2 and AC3′ for that p are recorded as ⊥. At the execution level, 𝔈 = S; Γ ≠ ∅ is used for overdetermination by non-initiation sources and for preemption of non-initiation sources by S — Chapter 3 delimits driving by "the present initiation is one of the actual causes of the present evolution of the part in question," the counterfactual criterion applying only when r contains no other independent trigger, and leaves the general criterion under redundancy and preemption to Computational Execution General Form-H3(c); witnesses with Γ ≠ ∅ are a formal choice for this relation of actual causation: AC2 requires the events on the path to occur as usual after Γ is removed, so a preempted initiation that had no effect does not obtain a witness through Γ.  
Correspondence: Chapter 3's "driving" (one of the actual causes), "the evolution of Tr\_τ(P) is driven by the present initiation," and "incidental differences that the initiation brings, through enabling pathways such as power supply, timing and crosstalk, to parts already operating are not counted."

Definition 3.18 (actual content source and proxy evaluation)　The read-out object is the read-out holding element at t\_out (where read-out is not reached, at t\_end; for the segment version, at t₁). Let W\_last be the last write event of that element up to the time of the read-out object (an event in the instance's process graph, whichever process it belongs to; where the element has not been written since the instance's initial state, the initial state). The source event W\_src := W\_last, falling under one of two clauses according to what it belongs to; the clauses are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. (Write clause) Where W\_last is an event of r, write W\_out := W\_last. (Hold clause) Where it is not, the read-out content is not given by a write of r; write W\_hold := W\_last — a write of an earlier run, a delivery or external write before t\_S, a write by another process on the same instance, or the initial state. The actual content source CS\_r is the set of events connected by actual links to W\_src and to its holding interval up to the time of the read-out object. In the hold clause, where W\_hold belongs to the present delivered content (delivery is placement in the read-out position), CS\_r and the segment criteria below are computed on r; where W\_hold does not belong to the present run (a write of an earlier run, an earlier external write, a write by another process on the same instance, or the initial state), CS\_r is computed backwards on the instance's process graph to the initial state (the process graph being finite, W1), and the segment criteria are not evaluated on r but take values as in the Sub clause below. In NC and CondF below, W\_out is read as W\_src. The output of a process outside the boundary entering CS through some declared port is called a segment η (a process that enters through several ports forms a segment at each port; value-level criteria are tested on the combination of its segments). out\_η is the value-level read-out of η's output through ρ\_out and κ\_out.  
　NC(η) (non-carrying): in r, replacing η's output by any other admissible value of that port (a declared finite set), with the other exogenous values and the delivery kept as actual, leaves the occurrence, content and read-out moment of W\_src unchanged.  
　VInc(η) (value-level increment): on the family of paired runs (ω fixed), out\_η varies with d and equals f(d) in every run (f non-constant), or out\_η contains the whole table of values (f(d))\_{d∈D} in every normal run; out\_η is not a pure copy image — there is no input word w at a fixed position not varying with d (an input of η within the boundary, or the delivered d) such that the value-level read-out of out\_η is identically equal to the value-level read-out of w; and, tracing back along CS, the first appearance of the values of f — the earliest event "whose output, on the family of paired runs, equals f(d) as a function of d" — is at the output of η.  
　FDep(η) (task counterfactual, only for models with f as a parameter): replacing f alone by some f′ in a declared candidate set, all else unchanged, changes the content with which η enters CS; for models that do not have f as a parameter, this term takes the value 0 and does not enter the determination of ⊥.  
　CondF(η) (condition port): η's output through a condition port varies with d on the family of paired runs; in the reference run r° the read-out passes, and when η's condition output is replaced by another admissible value (the moment of replacement no earlier than the completion of all of W\_src's ancestor events within the boundary) the read-out no longer passes; and the group of write actions within the host that it strobes (together with their content ancestors) does not by itself give a pointwise acceptable result — where this last clause fails, CondF is recorded as ⊥.  
　QDep(η) (acceptance dependence): there are a family of protocols (π\_d), a family of admissible executions (σ\_d), and an admissible replacement of the outputs of the process to which η belongs in each normal run (each output taking one value from the declared finite set of admissible values of that port, possibly differing from run to run; the other exogenous values and the delivery keeping their laws; the segments of the same process replaced simultaneously), such that the family of results obtained by Equation (4.2) with the actual outputs satisfies Acc\_Π, while that obtained with the replaced outputs does not.  
　η takes on evaluation :⇔ ¬NC(η) ∧ QDep(η) ∧ \[VInc(η) ∨ FDep(η) ∨ CondF(η)\].  
　Sub(r) :⇔ CS\_r contains a segment that takes on evaluation. In the hold clause, where W\_hold does not belong to the present run: if CS\_r contains no output from outside the boundary entering through a declared port, Sub(r) = 0; if it does, ¬Sub(r) is recorded as ⊥ (item (vii) below). In all other cases, ¬Sub(r) is recorded as ⊥ if and only if CS contains no segment that takes on evaluation and one of the following holds: (i) some segment that does not satisfy NC and enters through a content port neither satisfies VInc nor is a mere change of representation (output and input the same at the value level) or independent of both d and f — including cases in which the first appearance is not unique or is split between inside and outside the boundary; (ii) the result of a deliverer's segment varies with the deliverer's selection, and the selection rule does not satisfy the load-carrying rule; (iii) the completion moment required by some segment that does not satisfy NC and enters through a condition port cannot be determined, or Acc\_Π does not admit pointwise determination; (iv) CS contains an undeclared multi-driver merge node, or a channel that can set a held state but has not been declared as a port; (v) the atomic code of an interface required by the criteria is unavailable; (vi) one of NC, QDep, VInc, FDep and CondF takes the value ⊥ and has no determinate value under Section 3.7. In cases not listed above, ¬Sub(r) = 1. Item (vii): in the hold clause, W\_hold does not belong to the present run, and CS\_r contains outputs from outside the boundary entering through declared ports — these outputs fall outside the present run, and the replacements and paired runs required by NC, QDep and CondF cannot be defined on r; ⊥\_J is recorded.  
The user of the reading schemes and the deliverer are judged by the same criterion at their two places; the deliverer is compared by physical starting point (a starting point that already contains a value of f, rather than a coordinate image of d, is treated under VInc).  
Correspondence: Chapter 2's "proxy evaluation" — a link outside the host carries out the work of evaluating f, or part of that work, and whether the result passes depends on that work — and its three places. Taking "carries out the work of evaluation" as the position of the first appearance of the values of f is a formal convention; "whether the result passes depends on that work" is taken as QDep — whether replacing this work by any admissible value of the port would change whether the family of results passes. Where an external process finds a value of f that enters the read-out write through a port, but passing does not depend on it (for example, the process only adjusts the last digit within the tolerance), ¬QDep holds, and the process takes on no evaluation.

Definition 3.19 (enabling gap and En)　An enabling gap J: a maximal interval over which an enable port of some component in the realizing part Real\_r is outside its enabling range. En(r) holds if and only if, for each J:  
　(1) a component whose enable port is outside its range has no event within J;  
　(2) if r continues after it: the holding elements needed for continuation (those read by the first events after J) are held by the host itself during J — along the zero-disturbance holding trajectory (starting from the state at the end of that component's last event before J, H\_i iterated step by step with the disturbance component at its zero value and the other exogenous quantities at their actual values) the state type is unchanged, and within J there is no event driving them through a boundary port; after J, continuation proceeds from the held state (including returning to a checkpoint and redoing); the point of continuation is a state that r obtained after t₀, not replaced by reset or the like;  
　(3) if r reaches read-out during J: the read-out holding element is held by the host up to t\_out under the holding condition of (2), and its state at the beginning of J was obtained by r after t₀;  
　(4) where the read-out condition is reached under enablement, (3) does not apply.  
Rewriting of the held state by the disturbance component (during J or before J begins), and the subsequent holding evolution of the rewritten state, do not enter the holding tests of (2) and (3); "the held state" and "the state obtained by r" in continuation and read-out are taken as the actual state after rewriting. Dissipation laws (decay, drift) belong to H\_i itself, and change the type along the zero-disturbance trajectory as well, so they make (2) and (3) fail.  
Correspondence: Chapter 3's En — "a state held during a gap that is rewritten by a transient external disturbance still counts as a state of r"; such rewriting bears only on whether the result is right or wrong (see Chapter 3, E2, E5).

Definition 3.20 (Ex and N\_exec)  
　　Ex\_α(r) :⇔ ∃a ∈ Tr\_r ∃W ∈ Ev\_r(a): S ▷\_r W  
　　Ex\_β^D(r) :⇔ ∃a ∈ Data\_r ∃W ∈ Ev\_r(a): S ▷\_r W  
　　Ex\_β(r) :⇔ Triv\_Π(τ) ∨ Ex\_β^D(r)  
Ev\_r(a) is the set of events of a in r assigned to the present anchor. Cases of frozen versus free evolution: where NB\_r(W) fails at a witness W, the witness holds as usual, and its read-back as driving in Chapter 3's sense carries Computation Mathematical Form-H4 (Example 7.5′(a): a backup preempted by the present initiation); where the target set contains an event W that has no witness and at which NB\_r(W) fails, that W is not counted as "without witness," and Ex is not recorded as 0 because of it but as ⊥ (Example 7.5′(b): frozen removal cannot see differences transmitted through new events). Where Ex\_α has no witness in r, it is recorded as 0 (where the test of ▷ in it gives ⊥, or some target event falls under the ⊥ case of the preceding clause with no witness, ⊥ is recorded). Where Ex\_β^D has no witness in r: if it has a witness in r°, 0 is recorded; if it has no witness in r° either and one of the following holds, ⊥ is recorded: (1) there are a ∈ Data\_r, a time step t and an event e in r° whose source of action is S, such that e reaches a through a condition port, G\_a = 0 in r° and a has no event at t, and replacing the condition input coming from e by its value in r°\[−S\] gives G\_a = 1 (the initiation prevented a write that would otherwise have occurred); (2) Data₀ contains a hold action that changes content according to a dissipation law, while Data\_r contains no event whose source of action is S; (3) Tr contains an action with Dep\_C or Dep\_T that lies only on condition chains or read-out-condition chains (not on write chains); (4) some target event in Data\_r falls under the ⊥ case of the clause on frozen versus free evolution above; where one of the conditions above itself gives ⊥, ⊥ is likewise recorded. Otherwise 0 is recorded. For S = ∅ the formulas above do not apply: Ex\_α is recorded as ⊥, and Ex\_β, except under Triv\_Π, as ⊥; the run individual is given by the interval anchor of Definition 3.15, Ini (its latter clause), ¬Sub and Out are computed as usual, and En takes Real\_r as a parameter while Real\_r is recorded as ⊥ (Definition 3.11), and En takes its value by Section 3.7; N\_exec takes the value ⊥ or 0 because Ex\_α is recorded as ⊥.  
　　N\_exec(r) := Ini(r) ∧ En(r) ∧ ¬Sub(r) ∧ Ex\_α(r) ∧ Ex\_β(r) ∧ Out(r)  
　　N\_occ^{\[t₀,t₁\]}(r) := Ini(r) ∧ En\|\_{\[t₀,t₁\]} ∧ ¬Sub^{\[t₀,t₁\]} ∧ Ex\_α\|\_{\[t₀,t₁\]} ∧ Ex\_β\|\_{\[t₀,t₁\]}  
In the segment version, each item is restricted to events whose intervals meet \[t\_S, t₁\] (t₁ \> t₀), and AC is computed on r up to t₁; the object of dependence of ¬Sub^{\[t₀,t₁\]} is the read-out state already present by t₁, or the Tr events still continuing.  
Correspondence: Chapter 3's Ex\_α and Ex\_β (Π-trivial tasks exempt from Ex\_β), the general theorem and Corollary One, "occurrence of execution."

### 3.7　Three values

All predicates take values in {1, 0, ⊥}. ⊥ falls into four strata by source: ⊥\_M, model insufficiency (the model-adequacy check fails or has not been made, the exogenous representation is incomplete, physical identification is unavailable); ⊥\_D, invalid declaration (the declaration of a bypass, of the initiation part or of a protocol is not legitimate); ⊥\_J, criterion undecided (cases the definitions above prescribe as ⊥ that do not arise from the model or the declaration); ⊥\_C, computation not completed (outside the working domain, or not terminating within resources); the strata do not substitute for one another, and ⊥ is not used as 0. Conjunction is strong Kleene: if any term is 0, the result is 0; if no term is 0 and some term is ⊥, the result is ⊥. For predicates defined by cases on a condition, when the condition is ⊥, the informational meet of the two branches is taken: if the two branches have the same value, that value; otherwise ⊥; a choice among candidates some of which are ⊥ gives ⊥. The information order ⊑ is the flat partial order with ⊥ ⊑ 0 and ⊥ ⊑ 1.

## Section Five　Running and Solving

### 5.1　Running

Proposition 5.1　With the exogenous realization value fixed, Equation (4.1) determines the host's trajectory uniquely over discrete time steps; for any Γ ⊆ 𝒪(r) ∪ S, the counterfactual r\[−Γ\] is uniquely determined.

### 5.3　Solving

Type-level solving, SolveBear: first check the prerequisite items of model adequacy (the channel-declaration part of (i), and (ii) and (v) of 5.5; where they fail, each predicate is recorded as ⊥\_M and the procedure stops), and check the validity of the declarations that does not depend on the values of predicates (the declared forms of anchor assignment, refinement family, family of exogenous representations and family of failure values; where they are not legitimate, ⊥\_D is recorded and the procedure stops); the legitimacy of bypasses, the load-carrying rule and the initiation part is determined at the steps to which each belongs, and illegitimacy causes only the predicates that depend on it to be recorded as ⊥\_D; then, following steps (1)–(9) of Lemma 5.2, obtain the original values of the type-level predicates at the finest level L\_m and under one representation fixed from the family of exogenous representations; then recompute at the other levels of the refinement family and under the other representations of the family of exogenous representations ((iii) and (iv) of 5.5), and check the read-out superposition item (the latter part of (i) of 5.5); where the values at every level and under every representation equal the original values and the checks are passed, output the original values; where the value at some level or under some representation differs from the original value (including ⊥) or a check fails, record ⊥\_M (agreement across all levels, Definition 3.2). The output does not depend on which representation was fixed: when a determinate value is output, the values under all representations are the same. Composite predicates combine the outputs of their components by Section 3.7.

Token-level solving, SolveExec: after the same prerequisite checks, obtain the token-level predicates following steps (1)–(10). How Ex is obtained: for each event W in the target set (for Ex\_α, the events of Tr\_r; for Ex\_β, the events of Data\_r), enumerate the simple paths p from S to W on the graph of actual links; for each p, try in turn Γ = ∅, the set of non-initiation-source events writing to components on p, and general Γ ⊆ 𝒪(r) ∖ p, simulating r\[−Γ\] and r\[−(Γ ∪ S)\] to test AC1–AC3′; where Ex\_α has no witness, record 0 or ⊥ by Definition 3.20; where Ex\_β^D has no witness, construct r° and redo the test, then test the ⊥ clauses (1)–(4) of Definition 3.20 one by one; for each target event, check NB\_r(W) in addition: compute Anc\_r(W) (the two closures of Definition 3.16, terminating in finitely many steps), let r^{Exec}\[−S\] evolve freely up to the last time step of W, and compare the time steps with E = 1 on Anc\_r(W). How ¬Sub is obtained: first find the last write W\_last of the read-out holding element up to the time of the read-out object, decide the write clause or the hold clause according to whether it belongs to r, and obtain W\_src; traverse actual links backwards from W\_src to obtain CS, mark the segments, and judge each segment by Definition 3.18; in the hold clause where W\_hold does not belong to the present run, traverse the instance's process graph backwards to the initial state, testing only whether CS contains outputs from outside the boundary entering through declared ports — if so, record ⊥\_J; if not, ¬Sub = 1. Finally, check and combine as in SolveBear.

### 5.4　Working domain and solvability

Working domain: (W1) the components, actions, time steps and sets of reachable states are finite, and the time-unrolled graph (nodes (component, time step), edges the read dependences at a time step) is acyclic — that is, there is no zero-delay combinational feedback loop; physical feedback networks span different time steps in the unrolling and do not violate this condition; (W2) D is finite, each Prov\_τ(d) and Σ\_π is finite, random components and exogenous values have finite support, and the refinement family and the family of exogenous representations are finite; (W3) the five items of model adequacy, the initiation part, bypasses and redundant-copy groups, anchor assignment, the refinement family, the family of exogenous representations (including the disturbance component and the joint law) and the family of failure values have all been declared; (W4) the judgement of Acc\_Π on whole families, the retention tests, "the content map of the pathway equals f" in (E3), and the completion moments in the condition-port criterion are computable and terminate in the model; the existential quantifications in Π-triviality and in the identity class can be determined in the model; and the sets of admissible values for the condition-port criterion and for acceptance dependence, and the candidate set of f′ for the task counterfactual, are declared and finite.

Theorem 5.3　Under (W1)–(W4), SolveBear and SolveExec terminate in finitely many steps, and for each predicate return a unique value in {1, 0, ⊥\_M, ⊥\_D, ⊥\_J, ⊥\_C}, or an unsolvable status (model insufficient, declaration invalid, outside the working domain).  
Proof: Termination — every set traversed is finite: operands, protocols, admissible executions, exogenous supports and their products, subsets of actions (at most 2^{\|set of actions\|} candidate retention sets), assignments of failure values, events (including the nodes of the instance's process graph traversed backwards in the hold clause), simple paths over events (events being finite, simple paths are finite; the event level may have cycles), subsets of the removable set, the ancestor domain Anc\_r(W) of each target event (the least fixed point of the two closures, whose monotone iteration on a finite set of nodes terminates), families of paired runs, sets of admissible replacement values; the test on each element traversed terminates by (W4); a sum of finitely many terminating steps terminates. Uniqueness — by induction in the order of Lemma 5.2: the preceding predicates have all taken unique values, and the current definition is an explicit function of them and of the declared items. The parallel clauses in each definition all have a prescribed order of application or are mutually exclusive: the refinement family and the family of exogenous representations take values by agreement across all levels — where the values at every level and under every representation equal the original values, the original value is taken, and otherwise ⊥\_M (3.2, 5.3); κ is first judged by (κ-0), and where this does not apply it falls into (κ-1) or (κ-2) according to whether a non-reception action in the read-out chain has Dep\*, the items within each clause being mutually exclusive according to whether the two codes are the same and whether they belong to 𝒦\_std (3.8); anchors are first divided into initiation anchors and interval anchors according to whether S is empty (3.15); for the realizing part, S = ∅ is judged first, then the legitimacy of the bypass, then the union is taken and the retention test made (3.11); the choice of Λ\_r is mutually exclusive according to whether s has an event and whether the held selection strobes a pathway (3.13); for the actual content source, W\_src is first fixed by the mutually exclusive write and hold clauses, the hold clause is then divided exclusively according to whether W\_hold belongs to the present delivery, the segment criteria determine, by a composite formula, whether a segment takes on evaluation, and the recording of ¬Sub as ⊥ is considered only when no segment takes on evaluation (3.18); En branches according to whether the read-out falls within J, the disturbance clause fixing the object of the test first (3.19); for Ex, S = ∅ is judged first, then witnesses (where NB\_r(W) fails at a witness, the witness holds as usual, carrying Computation Mathematical Form-H4), then the NB\_r(W) clause for target events without witness, then the reference run, then the ⊥ clauses, and 0 for the rest (3.20). Strong Kleene connectives, finite quantification and the branching rules of Section 3.7 are total functions on {1, 0, ⊥}. So the same input yields only one value. ∎  
Outside the working domain, the procedure returns "outside the working domain" (⊥\_C) or "model insufficient" (⊥\_M). Finite reduction of infinite domains, and the correspondence on continuous-time models, are open problems (8.3). If it is further declared that the time of a single evaluation of each local function and of Acc\_Π, the length of the time unrolling, and the number of components are bounded by polynomials in the size of the model, then verification for a given (p, Γ) is polynomial-time, while the number of candidate paths and removal sets is exponential in the worst case.

### 5.5　Model adequacy

Five checks must be passed before and after evaluation; where they fail, the predicates concerned are recorded as ⊥\_M:  
　(i) every physical channel that can set a held state within the boundary, or can enter a read-out write chain, has been declared as a port; bypasses and redundant-copy groups have been declared; a read-out superposition action that can carry production on its own (there being a minimal Prod-retaining set containing it in which, apart from it, there are only hold actions and delivered content) belongs to one of these;  
　(ii) in normal running, G\_i = 1 coincides exactly with the physical identification of Definition 3.2;  
　(iii) the determinations at every level of the refinement family equal the original values (agreement across all levels, Definition 3.2); where any differs, ⊥ is recorded;  
　(iv) the determinations under every representation in the family of exogenous representations equal the original values; the disturbance component and the stochastic-dynamics component have been distinguished and legitimately declared, and the joint law of the protocol's random components and the exogenous values has been declared;  
　(v) for components whose write-select logic strobes different sources of content, the write actions have been subdivided by source.  
The channel part of (i), and (ii) and (v), do not depend on the values of predicates and are checked before evaluation; where they fail, every predicate is recorded as ⊥\_M. The read-out superposition item of (i), and (iii) and (iv), are checked after the original values have been obtained; (iii) and (iv) cause to be recorded as ⊥\_M only the predicates that are inconsistent across levels or representations, together with the predicates whose evaluation invoked inconsistent ones. Passing the five checks only allows predicates to output 0 or 1; it does not imply that there is no unmodelled decisive structure in the physical host.

## Section Six　Coupling Relations

### 6.1　With other formal systems

There are no coupling terms between M\_Comp and *The Mathematical Form of Field* v2.6 or *The Mathematical Form of Law* v1.5. The Computation Group-Form, Chapter 6, *The Mathematical Form of Generative Computation*, builds on the objects of M\_Comp, developing the extended state and equations under dynamical conditions, and does not redefine these objects.

### 6.2　Composition of hosts

Two hosts P₁ and P₂ are joined by a coupling into one host, bearing the composite task τ\_c = (D₁, f\_c, ρ\_in¹, ρ\_out^c, Π\_c). f\_c is as in Chapter 2, Corollary Two: when f₁ and f₂ are deterministic functions and f₁(D₁) ⊆ D₂, f\_c = f₂ ∘ f₁; for stochastic tasks, f\_c(d)(A) = ∫ f₂(v)(A) f₁(d)(dv), with the support of f₁(d) required to lie within D₂. The target f\_c and the result distributions actually produced by the combined model are compared by Acc\_{Π\_c}. The composite reading scheme ρ\_out^c is defined on the combined model: the combined model declares a failure bypass (distinct from the bypass Byp of Definition 3.11) and a failure output type Z\_⊥; when P₁'s read-out is ⊥\_V, the write action of the bypass brings the combined host into Z\_⊥, and ρ\_out^c(Z\_⊥) = ⊥\_V; in all other cases ρ\_out^c equals ρ\_out² on the read-out types of P₂. Chapter 2's corollary on composition takes Leg ∧ N\_bear at both ends as premise, and has four conditions: the end-point reading schemes do not evaluate by proxy for the composite task, the interface is legitimate, support is covered, and the error-propagation condition holds. Their formal versions are:

　C0　Delivery by ρ\_in¹ satisfies Definition 3.8(ii) on τ\_c, VInc being judged with f\_c in place of f; read-out by ρ\_out^c satisfies Definition 3.8(iii).  
　C1　The coupling and the failure bypass lie within the combined boundary, and none of their segments takes on evaluation; the failure bypass occurs on every run on which P₁'s read-out is ⊥\_V; a read-out state of P₁ read as v constitutes, through the coupling, one execution of some protocol of P₂ for v, the unspecified choices in which belong to the admissible executions of that protocol of P₂; and P₁'s result distribution in the combined model is the same as in its separate model.  
　C1b　Conditional on "P₁ reads out v" and on P₁'s run history, the conditional law of the random components and exogenous values on the side of P₂ equals some declared law on which P₂'s Prod^form rests.  
　C2　P₁'s result distribution has support within D₂ ∪ {⊥\_V}.  
　C3　For any acceptable family of results μ¹ of P₁, and for any response kernel μ²\_{d,·} ∈ ℛ\_d of P₂ chosen for each d, the composite family  
　　　μ^c\_d := ∫\_{D₂} μ²\_{d,v} dμ¹\_d(v) + μ¹\_d({⊥\_V}) · δ\_{⊥\_V}  
　satisfies Acc\_{Π\_c}. ℛ\_d consists of two parts: the response families (μ²\_v)\_{v∈D₂} with which P₂ on its own satisfies Acc\_{Π₂}, and the conditional response kernel v ↦ μ²\_{d,v} actually produced by the combined model on the operand d — conditional on "P₁ reads out v," integrated over P₁'s run histories on d. When P₁ fails on d, the composite read-out is ⊥\_V by the failure bypass of C1, so the second term is μ¹\_d({⊥\_V}) · δ\_{⊥\_V}; whether the mass of ⊥\_V is within tolerance is decided by Acc\_{Π\_c}. By the law of total probability, μ^c\_d, with the actual kernel substituted, is the actual composite read-out distribution of the combined host on d. Chapter 2's C3 takes families of response selections of P₂ indexed by P₁'s run history and then integrates over histories; integrating these responses over histories, for each d and at each v, gives exactly the actual conditional response kernel here, and the two are the same.

The response kernel must be indexed by d: C1b constrains only the law on the side of P₂ under each history, and when different operands yield the same read-out v through P₁, their distributions of histories may differ, so that the actual kernel varies with d.

C1b is the formal version of "under this condition" in Chapter 2's "the delivery formed by the coupling is one of them, … so under this condition P₂ gives f₂(f₁(d)) with probability at least r₂" (Chapter 2 writes r₂ for the lower bound of probability, which is the q₂ of Proposition 6.4; this chapter writes r for runs). C1b holds when the random sources of the two stages are independent: under independence the conditional law equals the marginal law, and the content of the coupling is fixed by v alone.

Proposition 6.1　Suppose P₂ satisfies N\_bear^form for τ₂ (that is, Prov^form ∧ Prod^form, Definition 3.10), C1b holds, and Acc\_{Π₂} is closed under value-wise mixtures (for each v, the mixture of the components at v of several acceptable families is still acceptable, the mixture weights possibly varying with v). Then, for each d, the actual conditional response kernel (μ²\_{d,v})\_{v} is defined on the support of μ¹\_d, and, completed outside the support by the components of some acceptable family of P₂, satisfies Acc\_{Π₂}. The exact class, the pointwise-reliability class and the tolerance class are all closed under value-wise mixtures. The assumption Prov^form cannot be dropped: when Prov\_{τ₂}(v) = ∅ for some v, Prod^form is vacuously true (Theorem 7.2) and provides no acceptable family covering D₂, so there is nothing with which to complete outside the support. Proof: Fix d. Example 6.2 (same read-out, different kernels)　f₁ maps d₁ ≠ d₂ to the same value v. P₁ reads out v with probability 1 on both d₁ and d₂, but its internal state records the operand, and the coupling delivers by P₂'s protocol A on d₁ and by protocol B on d₂; A reads out f₂(v) with probability 1, B with probability 0.9; Π₂ is pointwise reliability at level 0.9, both A and B are acceptable, and C1b holds. Example 6.3 (non-convex protocols)　P₂ has two admissible protocols, giving δ₀ and δ₁ respectively; Acc\_{Π₂} accepts only δ₀ or δ₁. Proposition 6.4 (pointwise reliability)　Suppose Π₁ and Π₂ require the probability of reading out f₁(d) and f₂(v) to be at least q₁ and q₂ respectively, P₁ satisfies N\_bear^form for τ₁ and P₂ for τ₂ (that is, Prov^form ∧ Prod^form), C1 and C1b hold, and V₁ ⊆ D₂. Together with the assumed R\_B^form, Prod^form follows by Equation (4.3). ∎

### 7.2　Theorems at the level of bearing

Theorem 7.1 (derived dependence)　If τ is not Π-trivial and Prov^form ∧ Prod^form holds, then for any family of protocols and family of admissible executions there are d₁, d₂ whose result distributions differ.  
Proof: By Prov^form and Σ\_π ≠ ∅, the families of protocols and of admissible executions are non-empty. Suppose, for contradiction, that the result distributions of some family were the same for all d; call the common distribution μ*; by Prod^form, the constant family (μ*)\_d satisfies Acc\_Π, that is, τ is Π-trivial — a contradiction. ∎  
This is the formal image of Chapter 2's Derived Dependence Lemma: for a bearer of a non-trivial task, the result varies with the operand.

Theorem 7.2 (read-back of the quantifiers)　The quantifier prefix of Equation (4.3) is ∀(π\_d) ∈ ∏\_d Prov\_τ(d) ∀(σ\_d) ∈ ∏\_d Σ\_{π\_d}, followed by a predicate on the whole family, in the same order as in Chapter 2's delimitation of Prod; when some Prov\_τ(d) = ∅, Prod^form is vacuously true; N\_bear^form is not vacuously true.  
Proof: Read off directly from Definition 3.10; vacuous truth from the universal over an empty product, non-emptiness from Prov^form. ∎

Proposition 7.3 (existence and retention of the realizing part)　Suppose τ is not Π-trivial, Prod^form holds under configuration c, the set of actions is finite, S ≠ ∅, and the declaration of bypasses is legitimate. Then there is a minimal Prod-retaining set under c which, with bypasses removed, is contained in Real(c). Where Real(c) takes a determinate value, Prod^form holds under c when Real(c) is retained and the other actions are rendered inoperative.  
Proof: The set of all actions is a Prod-retaining set: outside it there are no removable events or hold actions, the only assignment of failure values is the empty one, retaining it leaves the model under c unchanged, and Prod^form holds under c. The set of actions being finite, among its Prod-retaining subsets there is one of least cardinality, which is minimal. The latter assertion is the retention test of Definition 3.11. ∎

Proposition 7.3′ (defining properties of the realizing part)　Suppose Real(c) takes a determinate value. Then: (i) Prod^form holds under c when Real(c) is retained (together with what enabling requires) and the other actions are rendered inoperative; (ii) actions irrelevant to the read-out are not in Real(c); (iii) every declared redundant copy that belongs to some minimal Prod-retaining set is in Real(c); (iv) where every pathway dispatched by operand is required for some d, the pathways together with the dispatching action are all in Real(c); (v) declared bypasses are not in Real(c).  
Proof: (i) by the retention test of Definition 3.11 — a Real(c) that takes a determinate value has passed this test. (ii), (iii) and (iv) by (a), (b) and (c) of the proposition attached to Definition 3.11, together with Real(c)'s being a union of minimal sets. (v) by ∖ Byp. ∎  
The five clauses are the five clauses of Chapter 3's delimitation of the realizing part. Proposition 7.3 is existence; Proposition 7.3′ is Real(c)'s satisfying the upstream defining properties; neither yields "the set satisfying the five clauses is unique." Whether Prod-retention is monotone in retention sets is not proved (Computation Mathematical Form-H8): where it is not monotone, the retention test may fail and Real(c) is recorded as ⊥ (Definition 3.11), so the determinate values of Real do not depend on monotonicity.

### 7.3　Theorems at the level of execution

Theorem 7.4 (actual source of action and the counterfactual)　Take 𝔈 = S and Γ = ∅, and let W be an event of r. Suppose: time is discrete; NB\_r(W) — in the counterfactual of Chapter 3's sense, r^{Exec}\[−S\] (remove S, keep the content part and the actual condition fixed, and let the rest evolve freely by the local dynamics), no time step with E = 1 absent from r appears on the ancestor domain Anc\_r(W) (Definition 3.16); the content of holding elements changes only by writes or by dissipation laws; the dynamics is deterministic once the exogenous realization is fixed; E = 1 counts only setting of state through ports; events across situations are identified by overlap of intervals; r\[−S\] is as in Definition 3.16 (events only removed, never created). Then:  
　(1) on Anc\_r(W), the states and event indicators of the counterfactual r\[−S\] of Definition 3.16 and of r^{Exec}\[−S\] are the same node by node; nothing is asserted outside Anc\_r(W);  
　(2) W does not occur, or changes content, in r\[−S\] if and only if it does so in r^{Exec}\[−S\];  
　(3) S ▷\_r W (with Γ = ∅ as witness) if and only if there is a non-enabling chain of differences from S to W — each step passing through a condition port or content port, the event reached not occurring or changing content in r^{Exec}\[−S\], and the step satisfying the Diff condition of Definition 3.17 (Γ = ∅, the counterfactual taken as r^{Exec}\[−S\]).  
Proof: (1) By induction on Anc\_r(W) in the topological order of the time-unrolled graph ((W1): finite and acyclic). Take (i, t) ∈ Anc\_r(W), and suppose the two are the same at all its input nodes and at the holding node of i at the previous time step — by clause (i) of Anc\_r(W) these nodes all lie in Anc\_r(W), so the induction hypothesis applies. Then c′\_i(t), en′\_i(t) and the content inputs are the same, and the exogenous values are both the actual values, so the values of G\_i and F\_i are the same. In r^{Exec}\[−S\], E″\_i(t) = G\_i(·) ∧ \[(i, t) ∉ S\]; in r\[−S\], E′\_i(t) = E^r\_i(t) ∧ G\_i(·) ∧ \[(i, t) ∉ S\]; the two differ only when E^r\_i(t) = 0 and E″\_i(t) = 1, which NB\_r(W) excludes on Anc\_r(W). Hence E′\_i(t) = E″\_i(t), and, by Equation (4.1) and the holding law, X′\_i(t⁺) = X″\_i(t⁺). The induction uses only nodes within Anc\_r(W); differences outside Anc\_r(W) (such as new writes, in r^{Exec}\[−S\], of backup components with no ancestral relation to W) do not enter.  
(2) By (1) and the identification of events (Definition 3.2: take the earliest of those overlapping W). The interval of W belongs to Anc\_r(W) (clause (ii)), and so does the holding node at the time step before its segment (clause (i)). The parts, within the interval of W, of the events overlapping W are the same in the two counterfactuals by (1); for their extension to earlier time steps, each time step either has E = 1 in r and belongs to the same segment (clause (ii)), or is the holding node at the time step before the segment, which belongs to Anc\_r(W) with E′ = E″ ≤ E^r = 0, so that no extension reaches it. So the earliest of the events overlapping W in the two counterfactuals has the same starting point and the same content (the content of an event being constant over its interval); extension to later time steps does not affect identification or content. The judgement that W does not occur or changes content is therefore the same in both.  
(3) First prove that every event on the chain belongs to Anc\_r(W): by downward induction on the chain of differences e₀, …, e\_k = W from k; e\_k = W belongs to it; e\_{m−1} → e\_m is an actual link, and content written by e\_{m−1} and still held is an input of e\_m at some time step t, so the node of that component at t belongs to Anc\_r(W) (clause (i)); tracing back along holding nodes to the interval of e\_{m−1} (clause (i)), one finds by clause (ii) that the whole interval of e\_{m−1} belongs to it. The inputs, at each time step within its interval, of the component of e\_m used by Diff also belong to it by clause (i). So (2) holds for every event on the chain (with e\_m in place of W, all the segments used lying within Anc\_r(W)), and Diff has the same value on (r, r\[−S\]) and on (r, r^{Exec}\[−S\]) (by (1)). ⇐: each step of a non-enabling chain of differences is an actual link, so the chain is an actual path (AC1); with Γ = ∅, AC2 holds trivially; each event on the chain fails to occur or changes content in r\[−S\], and Diff holds (AC3′). ⇒: AC1 gives that each step is an actual link; AC3′ gives that each event differs in r\[−S\] and that Diff holds; replacing by r^{Exec}\[−S\] changes nothing. ∎  
This is the precise form — over discrete time steps, under NB\_r(W), and restricted to the ancestor domain of W — of Chapter 3's counterfactual clause, "if there were no initiation on this occasion, the evolution of Tr would be different," together with "incidental differences that the initiation brings, through enabling pathways such as power supply, timing and crosstalk, to parts already operating are not counted." Where NB\_r(W) fails (S preempting other sources), or non-initiation sources must be removed (overdetermination), Chapter 3's counterfactual criterion does not apply in the first place: Chapter 3 delimits driving by actual causation, the counterfactual criterion being used only where r contains no other independent trigger; witnesses with Γ ≠ ∅ are the formal image of the relation of actual causation in such cases (Computational Execution General Form-H3(c)).

Example 7.5 (a counterexample to weaker definitions)　S triggers, through a condition port, a write of the constant 0 to register RZ (RZ already held 0 before t₀), and the output of RZ is connected through a content port to the functional unit FU; S also opens, through the controller, the power gating of FU (an enable port); W is the present write of FU. p = (S, e\_K, e\_RZ, W) is an actual path, and W does not occur in r\[−S\]. If all that were required were "there is a non-enabling actual path and W differs," then S ▷ W would hold. But RZ outputs 0 in both r and r\[−S\], there is no difference at the step e\_RZ → W, and the difference in W arrives entirely through FU's enable port: this is exactly the incidental difference that Chapter 3 requires not to be counted. By AC3′, the Diff of that step fails, and S ⋫\_r W. If a step of difference required only that "the upstream output differs and there is a difference downstream as well," there would also be a counterexample: RZ held 1 before t₀ and is written to 0 on this occasion, while FU does not read RZ in the present mode — RZ's output differs between r and r\[−S\], and W does not occur either, but the step that replaces the RZ entry does not change FU's G or F, and the difference still arrives entirely through the enable port; without antecedent (a), a change in G could be manufactured midway through the telescoping chain after the enable entries are replaced first, while replacing all the non-enabling entries together does not change G — again a misjudgement.

Example 7.5′ (frozen removal versus free evolution)　(a) A preempted backup: S triggers, through a condition port, a write of the functional unit FU to the read-out (event W); there is also a backup controller BK, whose write enable, if S has not arrived, triggers the same write of FU at t₀ + δ, writing the same content; in r, S arrives and BK does not occur. In the free evolution r^{Exec}\[−S\], BK occurs and W occurs just the same with its content unchanged, so the literal counterfactual of Chapter 3, "if there were no initiation on this occasion, the evolution would be different," fails; in the frozen removal r\[−S\], the event of BK, being absent from r, does not occur, W does not occur, and S ▷\_r W. Chapter 3 delimits driving by actual causation: S preempts the backup and is an actual cause (Chapter 3, Preliminary Distinctions, "driving"); the 1 given by frozen removal agrees with this in direction, NB\_r(W) fails, and the read-back carries Computation Mathematical Form-H4. (b) Double prevention: the write enable of FU is T ∧ ¬Blk, where T is a trigger not brought about by S; register Blk is written to 1 by component P, whose write enable holds if S has not arrived. In r, S arrives, P does not write, Blk stays 0, and W occurs, triggered by T. Under frozen removal the event of P, being absent from r, does not occur, W occurs just the same, and there is no witness; under free evolution P writes 1, and W does not occur. Between S and W there is no actual path in r; the effect of S arrives entirely through an event that does not occur; Chapter 3 lays down nothing on whether "preventing a preventer" is driving. Frozen removal cannot give a credible 0 here, and Definition 3.20 records ⊥ (NB\_r(W) fails and there is no witness).

Theorem 7.6 (coordinate character of the read-out)　Suppose the internal representation is legitimate, the subdivision of write actions has been declared, and the model belongs to the class of coordinate models, that is, satisfies: the boundary inputs after delivery are only delivered content and synchronization, whose arrival time steps do not vary with d; the content of holding elements changes only by writes or by dissipation laws counted in value duties; each atom has at most one writer at any moment; once the exogenous realization is fixed, event conditions and written contents are determinate, and the exogenous law does not depend on d; the time-unrolled graph is finite and acyclic; the read-out agrees with the internal representation atom by atom; atoms outside the scope of delivery are set only to values independent of d; transient disturbances do not rewrite content; (read-out condition) the content written to read-out-condition elements does not vary with d. The read-out chain consists of the actions on the write chains and condition chains of the read-out content, together with the actions on the read-out-condition chains (Definitions 3.8, 3.9). If no non-reception action on the read-out chain has Dep\*, then, for each exogenous realization, the read-out moment t\_out does not vary with d, the value of the read-out atoms at t\_out, as a function of the values of the input atoms, is a coordinate map, and its law does not depend on d.  
Proof: Fix an exogenous realization ω and a pair of paired runs r₁, r₂.  
Step One (same skeleton): call a node (component, time step) divergent if the event indicators of its write actions differ between r₁ and r₂. Suppose, for contradiction, that there is a divergent node, and take one none of whose ancestors diverges (which exists, the time-unrolled graph being finite and acyclic). It is not the arrival of delivery itself (whose arrival time step does not vary with d), so its event indicator is given by G; the exogenous values and enable ports are the same in the two runs, so the two values of the condition inputs give G different values. Replacing the condition-input entries whose values differ one at a time, some step must change G, and that entry comes from some upstream action a, which therefore has Dep\_C, contrary to the premise. Here the divergent node is taken on components involved in the read-out chain (ancestor components of the read-out content and of the read-out-condition elements), and a, as an upstream action of it, also lies on the read-out chain; if a belongs to reception, reception actions do not have Dep\_C (Definition 3.13), which is likewise a contradiction. So the two runs have the same event skeleton on the read-out chain, and the write moments of the read-out-condition elements are the same.  
Step One′ (read-out moment): R\_out is a predicate on the states of read-out-condition elements and on synchronous boundary inputs. The write moments of the read-out-condition elements are the same (Step One), the content written does not vary with d (read-out condition), in between there is only holding (the content of holding elements changes only by writes or dissipation laws, and dissipation laws do not depend on d), and the arrival time steps of synchronous inputs do not vary with d (class of coordinate models); so R\_out is first reached at the same time step in r₁ and r₂, and t\_out does not vary with d.  
Step Two (composition): on the same skeleton and at the same t\_out, the value of a read-out atom is the content of the last write to its holding element. Recurse in the topological order of the time unrolling: the content written by each write action is m\_a^ω applied to its content inputs; a content input is either an input atom (read through 𝔯), or the held content of an earlier node, or a value independent of d. Where a belongs to reception, its value duty is a coordinate map or identity holding; where a does not belong to reception and its content inputs vary with d, m\_a^ω ∈ Coord follows from the absence of Dep\*; where the content inputs of a do not vary with d, the content it writes is independent of d. Since coordinate maps are closed under composition and parallel combination (each output atom of a composite map is either constant or takes some input atom through the inner map), the read-out is a coordinate map of the input atoms; the law of ω does not depend on d. ∎  
Example 7.6′ (the read-out moment carrying evaluation)　D = {0,1}², f(x, y) = x ⊕ y. The output register Z, on a fixed schedule independent of the input, holds 0 at time step 1 and 1 at time step 2; the read-out-condition element F is written to 1 by component T, whose write enable reads the delivered x and y through condition ports, and which writes at time step x ⊕ y; R\_out is the time step after F = 1, so t\_out = 1 + (x ⊕ y) and Z(t\_out) = x ⊕ y. Neither the write moments nor the content of Z vary with d. By Definition 3.9, T's write belongs to the read-out-condition chain; the action of the delivery entry, through condition ports, makes T's events differ between paired runs, so it has Dep\_C, does not belong to reception, and lies on the read-out chain — the premise of Theorem 7.6 is not satisfied, and the coordinate character does not follow; the Dep\* action required by Corollary 7.7 is precisely this timing action. If instead a process outside the boundary chose the read-out moment according to x ⊕ y, the arrival time steps of boundary inputs after delivery would vary with d, the model would not belong to the class of coordinate models, and Theorem 7.6 would not apply; whether that process takes on evaluation is judged by the condition-port criterion of Definition 3.18. If the content written to F varied with d while its write moment did not (R\_out reading content in order to time the read-out), the (read-out condition) clause would not be satisfied, and Theorem 7.6 would not apply either. At the token level, T belongs to Tr but does not lie on a write chain, and does not enter Data₀; where Ex\_β^D has no witness, ⊥ is recorded by ⊥ clause (3) of Definition 3.20, in agreement with the case "encoding the result in the completion moment" of 8.3(8), which takes ⊥. In all three cases the read-out moment cannot, within the premises of Theorem 7.6, carry an unrecognized evaluation.

Corollary 7.7　Under the conditions of Theorem 7.6, if Prov^form ∧ Prod^form holds and τ is not in the identity class (¬Id(τ), Definition 3.5: there is no random coordinate selection with a selection law independent of d such that the resulting family of results satisfies Acc\_Π), then the read-out chain contains a non-reception action with Dep*. If it is further assumed that the actions on the read-out chain all belong to the realizing part, then Tr contains a Dep* action; if it is further assumed that the actions in Tr with Dep\* all lie on write chains, then Data₀ ≠ ∅.  
Proof: Suppose, for contradiction, that no non-reception action on the read-out chain has Dep*. By Theorem 7.6, for any protocol rule and admissible-execution rule, under each exogenous realization the read-out atoms are a coordinate map of the input atoms, and the law of this map does not depend on d — this is a random coordinate selection with a selection law independent of d; the read-out value is κ\_out ∘ ν* applied to it, and its family of results is the actual family of results, which satisfies Acc\_Π by Prod^form. So Id(τ), a contradiction. The last two sentences follow from Tr = Real ∖ Recv and the definition of Data₀. ∎  
Corollary 7.7 is the formal image of the attribution at the level of bearing in Chapter 3, Section Seven (a): "the dynamics that bears is exercised" must fall on the part of the dynamics that depends on the operand. The further step at the level of runs needed to get from there to "this occasion is not an execution" (Computational Execution General Form-H3(b)) is not a formal proposition.

Theorem 7.8 (Ex\_β ⇒ Ex\_α)　Suppose τ is not Π-trivial, the configuration has only one declared value, and every dispatching action on the present occasion that does not satisfy the routing exception belongs to the realizing part and selects pathways according to the operand. Then Ex\_β^D(r) = 1 implies Ex\_α(r) = 1.  
Proof: It suffices to prove Data\_r ⊆ Tr\_r. Under a single configuration, Real\_r = Real and Tr\_r = Tr ∖ Recv\_r(d). By Equation (4.5), Data\_r is the union of three parts with Alt\_r ∪ Recv\_r(d) removed: Data₀^\* ∩ Anc(Λ\_r) ⊆ Data₀^\* ⊆ Tr (Data₀ and Data₀^Id are both taken from Tr by definition); a dispatching action s that does not satisfy the routing exception belongs to the realizing part, and its condition duty or value duty depends on the operand, so it does not belong to reception, and s ∈ Tr; Λ\_r ∩ Tr belongs to Tr in the first place. Removing Recv\_r(d) from both sides gives Data\_r ⊆ Tr\_r. ∎

Theorem 7.9 (same run)　≈ is an equivalence relation on the (non-empty) segments of Run^ok.  
Proof: Reflexivity and symmetry by definition. Transitivity: r₁ ≈ r₂ in r\_A and r₂ ≈ r₃ in r\_B; r₂ is non-empty; take a node x of it; α(x) = A and α(x) = B, and α is a function, so A = B. ∎  
Anchor assignment is taken as a function so that different runs sharing a register file are not joined into one; delimiting the same run by undirected connectedness would join them.

Theorem 7.10 (occurrence of execution)　N\_exec(r) ⇒ N\_occ^{\[t₀,t\_out\]}(r), where N\_occ is the conjunction of the restrictions of Ini, En, ¬Sub and Ex to \[t₀, t\_out\].  
Proof: N\_exec contains Out; the read-out condition is reached at t\_out with no termination before it, so the events of r all lie within \[t\_S, t\_out\]; the restriction of each item tests the same object as the original item. ∎

### 7.4　Theorems on the three values

Theorem 7.11 (monotonicity in truth-value information)　Under one and the same declared model, the same declared items, and the same refinement family and family of exogenous representations, refining the ⊥ of any atomic determination to 0 or 1 leaves unchanged every predicate built from strong Kleene connectives, finite quantification and the branching rules of Section 3.7 that already took a determinate value.  
Proof: Strong Kleene connectives are monotone in the information order ⊑; finite conjunction and disjunction preserve monotonicity; when the condition is ⊥, the branching rules take the informational meet of the two branches, and the branch taken after the condition is refined is not lower than that meet in the information order. Induction on the structure of formulas gives the result. ∎  
So, within one and the same declared model, a 0 or 1 already given does not change when other ⊥s are later determined. Theorem 7.11 does not concern changes to the model itself: subdividing nodes, changing the exogenous representation or adding ports changes the atomic judgements themselves, rather than merely refining the ⊥ of some atom.

Proposition 7.11′ (stability under model refinement within the declared families)　The determinate values output by SolveBear and SolveExec are the same at every level of the declared refinement family and under every representation of the family of exogenous representations; under model refinements outside the declared families, determinate values are not guaranteed.  
Proof: By Section 5.3, the original values are recomputed at every declared level and under every declared representation, and where the value at any level or under any representation differs from the original value (including ⊥), ⊥\_M is recorded (agreement across all levels, Definition 3.2, 5.5(iii)(iv)). So when the procedure outputs a determinate value v, the values at every declared level and under every declared representation equal v. This conclusion uses only the rule of agreement across all levels, not any precedence among levels. Refinements outside the families are not within the tests of the solving procedures; new events of the kind in Example 7.5′, and new ports, can change atomic judgements. ∎

Theorem 7.12 (formal realization)　Within the working domain (W1)–(W4), every formal predicate takes a unique value by the solving procedures, trajectories and counterfactuals are uniquely determined, and solving terminates in finitely many steps; after the output map ψ, every possible output o ∈ O of every predicate has exactly one basic read-back rule.  
Proof: The first three items are Proposition 5.1 and Theorem 5.3. The last item: ψ is a function from results of solving to O, so the output of each predicate falls uniquely in O. Predicates fall into three classes — type-level top-level predicates and their components (A), token-level top-level predicates and their components (B), and intermediate predicates (C); the three classes are mutually exclusive and exhaustive (the top-level predicates and their components are listed by name, and all others are intermediate predicates). Going cell by cell through the 3 × 6 table of (class, output): (A, 1) only R1; (B, 1) only R2; (A, 0) and (B, 0) only R6; (C, 1) and (C, 0) only R7; (A, B, C, each stratum of ⊥) only R3. The cells to which R1, R2, R6 and R7 apply are pairwise disjoint, and none contains a ⊥ cell; R3 contains only ⊥ cells. So each cell has exactly one basic rule. ∎

### 7.5　Semantic correspondence

Problem 7.13 (semantic fidelity)　Does each formal predicate, within its domain of application, preserve the judgemental meaning of the corresponding structural item of S\_Comp — for example, is Leg₂^form = 1 exactly the holding of the formal image of "the reading schemes do not evaluate by proxy," and is Ex\_β = 1 exactly the holding of the formal image of Chapter 3's Ex\_β?  
This problem is not solved as a whole. What has been proved are the following local correspondences.

Theorem 7.14 (local correspondences proved)　The following items hold under the conditions listed. The square brackets in each item state the strength of the correspondence proved: same quantifier order, formal implication, preservation of properties, conditional structural correspondence, preservation under composition, conditional equivalence; only those marked as conditional equivalence are two-way equivalences, and only under the conditions listed. This theorem does not prove that any formal predicate is semantically equivalent to the corresponding structural item of S\_Comp.  
　(1) \[same quantifier order\] The quantifier prefix of Prod^form has the same order as Chapter 2's delimitation of Prod, and Prod^form is vacuously true when some Prov\_τ(d) = ∅ (Theorem 7.2); that Prod^form as a whole is equivalent to the upstream Prod is not proved here.  
　(2) \[formal implication\] Where τ is not Π-trivial and Prov^form ∧ Prod^form holds, for any family of protocols and family of admissible executions there are d₁, d₂ whose result distributions differ (Theorem 7.1, the distributional part of Chapter 2's Derived Dependence Lemma); one-way, and not concerning the attribution of mechanism.  
　(3) \[preservation of properties\] Where Real(c) takes a determinate value, it satisfies the five clauses of Chapter 3's delimitation of the realizing part (Proposition 7.3′); that Real(c) is the same as the upstream realizing part (the set satisfying the five clauses being unique) is not proved, that is, 8.3(3′).  
　(4) \[conditional equivalence\] Under the conditions listed in Theorem 7.4 (discrete time steps, NB\_r(W), deterministic dynamics, events only removed and never created, and so on), S ▷\_r W with Γ = ∅ as witness is two-way equivalent to Chapter 3's counterfactual chain of differences stated with r^{Exec}\[−S\] (Theorem 7.4(3)); the identity of the two counterfactuals on which it relies holds only on the ancestor domain Anc\_r(W) of W (Theorem 7.4(1)); nothing is claimed outside the conditions, Example 7.5′ being such a case outside them.  
　(5) \[conditional structural correspondence\] Under the conditions of Theorem 7.6 (the class of coordinate models, including the read-out-condition clause; the read-out chain including the read-out-condition chains), where Prov^form ∧ Prod^form holds and ¬Id(τ), the read-out chain contains a non-reception action with Dep\*; under the further assumptions listed in Corollary 7.7, there is such an action in Tr and on write chains as well (Corollary 7.7, the formal image of the attribution at the level of bearing in Chapter 3, Section Seven (a)); this is an existence conclusion carrying model conditions, and does not claim that the action obtained is the data-carrying part of which Chapter 3 speaks.  
　(6) \[formal implication\] Under the conditions of Theorem 7.8 (single configuration and so on), Ex\_β^D(r) = 1 ⇒ Ex\_α(r) = 1; one-way, the converse not claimed.  
　(7) \[preservation under composition\] Under C0–C3 and the R\_B^form of the combined model, P₁'s Prov^form ∧ Prod^form gives the combined model's Prov^form ∧ Prod^form for τ\_c (Proposition 6.6, the N\_bear part of Chapter 2, Corollary Two; the acceptability on the side of P₂ enters through C3, without a separate assumption of P₂'s N\_bear^form); it does not yield the R\_B^form or Leg^form of the combined model (the note after Proposition 6.6).  
Proof: Each item is the theorem or proposition cited; the strength in square brackets matches the form of the conclusion cited one to one: (1) is a comparison of strings of quantifiers, (2) and (6) are one-way implications, (3) is the holding of the five properties, (4) is an "if and only if" under the conditions listed, (5) is an existence statement, and (7) is the preservation of one and the same predicate before and after composition. ∎  
The other correspondences — the legitimacy of reading schemes in Definitions 3.3 and 3.8, the construction by union of minimal sets in 3.11, the dispatch rule in 3.13, the anchor assignment in 3.15, witnesses with Γ ≠ ∅ in 3.17, and the way first appearance and acceptance dependence are taken in 3.18 — are candidate representations under formal conventions; their determinate values are read back with the conventions relied on stated as in 7.1, and are not used as if the upstream criteria had been verified. The completeness of the read-back rules in Theorem 7.12 is a correspondence at the level of rules, and does not imply an affirmative answer to Problem 7.13.

### 8.4　Ways of breach

Any of the following constitutes a breach of M\_Comp: some predicate taking two values under the same solving input; some formal predicate giving, on some positive case, negative case or boundary case of Chapters 2 and 3, a determinate value (0 or 1, not ⊥) contrary to the literal classification of the source text, not obtained by a formal convention carrying a condition; some load-bearing structural item of S\_Comp having no correspondent in the correspondence table; the actual properties of some relation not agreeing with those listed in Section 3.8; a predicate taking the value 0 where the model lacks structure; some theorem of Section Seven having a counterexample under the premises listed. Taking the value ⊥, a model's not faithfully characterizing the host, and another physical realization of the same structure do not constitute breaches. The minimal form of a breach is a finite event model (components, ports, G, F, H, exogenous values, S and declared items) on which a determinate value contrary to upstream is computed step by step from the definitions, or else the pointing out of a missing step in a proof.
