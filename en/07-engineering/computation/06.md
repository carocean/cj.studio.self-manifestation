# The Mathematical Form of Generative Computation

## Section One　Purpose

### 1.1　Source of the structure

*The General Form of Generative Computation* v1.0 (hereafter Chapter 5) proved the necessary structure of generative computation for a computing host P bearing a one-step task τ\_Φ and for a stretch of running σ on it. Under the task-side conditions, the working domain and the six stipulations G1–G6 of generative computation:

　　(1) GenComp(P, σ) ⇒ ∃π ∃r \[LegalPart(π) ∧ RunOf(r, P) ∧ N₆(σ, π, r)\]  
　　(2) H2 ∧ GenComp(P, σ) ⇒ ∃π ∃r \[LegalPart(π) ∧ RunOf(r, P) ∧ N₇(σ, π, r)\]

where N₆ ≔ Step ∧ Carry ∧ Sed ∧ ¬Intr ∧ Hold ∧ Multi and N₇ ≔ N₆ ∧ Gov; the two clauses of Ex in Step are conditional on Computational Execution General Form-H3, the clause of Sed(i) on persistent field states is conditional on C0 of *The General Form of Law* v2.5, and Sed(ii), for the deployment-coupling kind, is conditional on Generative Computation General Form-H3; the determination of each item carries Generative Computation General Form-H1, Computational Execution General Form-H1–H3 and Computational Bearing General Form-H1–H3. Their contrapositives give the conditions of non-generative computation:

　　(1′) ∀π ∀r \[LegalPart(π) ∧ RunOf(r, P) ⇒ ¬N₆(σ, π, r)\] ⇒ ¬GenComp(P, σ)  
　　(2′) under H2, ∀π ∀r \[LegalPart(π) ∧ RunOf(r, P) ⇒ ¬N₇(σ, π, r)\] ⇒ ¬GenComp(P, σ)

The seven items are given in natural language and in the formal statements of the appendix: the step partition is delimited by executions; the dynamical condition D belongs to some minimal μ-sufficient set for x′; D is a persistent field state of a qualified Sedimentation-Field, there is a sedimentation mode taking D as its object, and every change of D in σ accords with the field protocol; there is no evaluation from outside the boundary at junctions; the carried-over components and D are held by the host at junctions; between the output carrier and the state carrier of the next step there is only storage or transfer, and the carried-over projection is not rewritten; the steps belong to the same run. Through Generative Computation General Form-H1 (the determination of the extended state and the step partition, individuation, preservation under transformations of the partition, the demarcation between junctions and steps) and Generative Computation General Form-H2 (the characterization of the generation kernel and AdmVar, the derivation of D ∈ ActArg^μ from ActualHold, the decomposition of a composite D, the comparability condition), Chapter 5 registered the formal criteria needed to determine when these items hold on concrete hosts, and took this chapter as the verification text for the formal level of the clauses of H1 and of (a), (b3), (c), (d) in H2; H2(b1) and (b2) are not among what this chapter proves (8.2).

### 1.2　The object of formalization

Write S\_Gen for the structure established in Chapter 5. The formal system M\_Gen is the formal representation of S\_Gen on the discrete local-dynamics models with ports of *The Mathematical Form of Computation* v1.0 (hereafter Chapter 4):

　　S\_Gen ⟼ M\_Gen

M\_Gen takes Chapter 4's host model 𝔐\_P as its base, adds the declaration of the extended state, state carriers and output carriers, the Sedimentation-Field and its protocol, generation kernels and the admissible counterfactual family, and gives three levels of predicates on one and the same model:

　　Atomic level　the formal images of the seven items Step, Carry, Sed, ¬Intr, Hold, Multi, Gov on a given step partition π and run r;  
　　Candidate level　W₆(π, r), W₇(π, r): the conjunction of the seven items (six for W₆) on one and the same (π, r);  
　　Overall-verdict level　V₆, V₇: existence taken over the declared candidate family.

All predicates take values in {1, 0, ⊥}. A formal image taking 1 means only that the necessary structure holds under the declared model, and GenComp does not follow from it; taking 0 means only that, under the declared model, the formal image of the necessary structure does not hold; reading back to upstream structural propositions must go through the read-back rules of Section Seven and carries the conditions listed in Section Eight. Formal determinations hold relative to the declared model; sufficient correspondence between the model and the actual host is not guaranteed by these conditions.

## Section Two　Core Theses

Thesis One (determination of the seven items on host primitives)　Whatever in the seven items of Chapter 5 concerns "who holds," "who evaluates," "whether there is only storage or transfer," "whether it accords with the protocol" or "whether it is the same run" can be written as finite conditions on the primitives already defined in Chapter 4 — events, port reads, actual links, actual sources of action, last writes. Step calls Chapter 4's N\_exec directly; "the whole of σ belongs to one and the same run," which Multi requires, has no object under the letter of Chapter 4 (Chapter 4's run individuals do not extend past the first read-out after the anchor, Proposition 7.21), so this chapter makes a process-run extension r^σ on Chapter 4's instance process graph, stated explicitly as an extension (Definition 3.3(2), Generative Computation Mathematical Form-H6); Carry, ¬Intr, Hold and Sed(iii) are given by the authoritative carrier lineage, the evaluation duty of events outside the boundary (Chapter 4, Definition 3.18), storage-only or transfer-only copy paths, and the records of protocol operations; Sed(i) and (ii) are upstream field qualifications, entering M\_Gen as inputs with provenance.

Thesis Two (characterization of Gov by pivot sets)　On a finite set of read-in variables, D belongs to some minimal μ-sufficient set if and only if there is a set of variables C not containing D such that, with C fixed, the legitimate fibre diameter of the generation kernel exceeds the declared resolution, and with D fixed in addition it no longer exceeds it (Theorem 7.1). Hence H2(b) of Chapter 5, "ActualHold yields D ∈ ActArg^μ," is rewritten at the formal level as a proposition of the existence of a pivot set, Rep\_ε; the rewriting is not a proof, Rep\_ε is H2(b) of Chapter 5 itself, and its semantic level is still pending verification.

Thesis Three (declaration completeness)　The task-side conditions of Chapter 5 require the complete variable domain of the extended state to be declared before σ, and instantaneous quantities not to be carried over across steps. The test of this condition in an individual case is DeclEq: starting from the cut before the initiation of the present step, replace the undeclared host state by template values, and compare the two runs in the occurrence of events, their time intervals, the values written and the sources of what is read in. DeclEq takes only 1 or ⊥. The formal image of Step is taken as the conjunction of execution and declaration completeness, so that, where undeclared state carried in across steps is read in before being rewritten in the present step, and its value differs from the template value, Step does not take a determinate 1 (Lemma 7.7).

Thesis Four (the three-valued overall verdict and its reliability)　V\_j takes strong Kleene existence over the candidate family. V\_j = 1 certifies only that a formal witness of the seven-item (or six-item) necessary structure exists in the declared model; V\_j = 0 requires that every candidate have a determinate failing component and, in addition, either that the coverage of the candidate family has been certified or that the candidate family contains the family Cand\_gen finitely generated by formally legitimate partitions; where the candidate family is empty and its coverage has not been proved, V\_j is recorded as ⊥. Reading ¬GenComp from V\_j = 0 must go through (1′) and (2′) of Chapter 5 and carries premises (1)–(5) of Theorem 7.15: the premises of Chapter 5's general theorem (including the task-side conditions and the working domain), the conditions carried by Chapter 5's general theorem, the reverse-representation conditions, the candidate-coverage condition, and, for j = 7, H2 of Chapter 5 in addition; where the candidate family contains Cand\_gen, the candidate-coverage condition is turned by Proposition 7.27 into the listed clauses of reverse representation, none of which is proved.

The four theses rest on one and the same declared model: Thesis One gives the atoms, Thesis Three qualifies Step among them, Thesis Two characterizes Gov among them, and Thesis Four composes the atoms into the overall verdict.

## Section Three　Basic Objects and the Sequence of Definitions

The definitions are given in order of dependence: the declaration level, the level of steps and runs, the level of primitives, the level of the seven items, the level of declaration completeness, and the overall-verdict level. Each definition is annotated at its end with the structural item of S\_Gen to which it corresponds; those marked \[S\] are formal representations of structural items of S\_Gen, and those marked \[aux.\] are purely formal auxiliary objects, which have no counterpart in S\_Gen and are not read back on their own (7.1). Objects taken from Chapter 4 keep their notation and meaning.

### 3.1　Declaration level

Definition 3.1 \[S\] (declared generative model)　The declared generative model  
　　𝔐\_Gen = (𝔐\_P, 𝔇, τ\_Φ, Σ\_s, Car, F, Prot\_F, Pers(F), Op, K, Q, AdmVar, ε\_role, σ, t\_end, Tmpl, ℙ^σ)  
consists of the following items, all except σ and ℙ^σ declared before and independently of σ:  
　(1) 𝔐\_P and 𝔇: the host model and declared items of Chapter 4, Definition 3.1 (boundary B, entries, read-out and read-out condition R\_out, the family of delivery protocols, operating conditions, initiation part S).  
　(2) τ\_Φ: a one-step task, with operand (x, D, u) and result (x′, D′), having as its identity the extension of its function (or kernel) on its operand domain Dom\_τ (Chapter 4, Definition 3.5).  
　(3) Σ\_s: the division of the extended state. s = (x, D), x = (x\_carry, x\_input, x\_inst): carried-over components, input slots, instantaneous quantities; D is a composite quantity whose variable domain Dom(D) contains all persistent components of F and the derived quantities updated along with them; c is the exogenous control configuration, belonging neither to x nor to D. To which of x\_carry, x\_input, x\_inst, D, c each host state component belongs is given junction by junction according to the declared protocol.  
　(4) Car: the state carriers In\_k and output carriers Out\_k of the steps, and the final-state carrier Fin, declared component by component, together with the lineages of storage-only or transfer-only copying. The authoritative carrier Auth(v, t) of component v at time t is the holding element that, according to the lineages of Car, carries the authoritative content of v at t.  
　(5) F, Prot\_F, Pers(F): the Sedimentation-Field, the field protocol (modes and operations such as sedimentation, shallowing, freezing, thawing, role masking), and the set of persistent field states.  
　(6) Op: the records of protocol operations. Each record o gives a time, a mode, an operation type, a read source, a write target (the authoritative carrier of D) and the write value required by the protocol; Op lists all the updates of D that Prot\_F, under the declared mode, should carry out in σ.  
　(7) K, Q: the generation kernel K\_k(x′ \| x, D, u; c) and the joint kernel Q\_k(x′, D′ \| x, D, u; c) of step k (Section Four, Equation (4.2)).  
　(8) AdmVar: the admissible counterfactual family of Definition 3.9′ of *The Mathematical Form of Law* v1.5, taken on the input set A\_k actually read by K\_k; ε\_role ∈ \[0, 1): the declared resolution, the measure of difference being total variation TV.  
　(9) σ = \[s₁, t\_end\]: the stretch of actual process under discussion, t\_end being its declared end point. The cut-off time of a run individual in Chapter 4, Definition 3.15, is written t\_end^{Comp} in this chapter, distinct from t\_end.  
　(10) Tmpl: the template table, giving a template value, by default its reset value, for every holding element of the host not belonging to the retention set (Definition 3.12(D7-T)).  
　(11) ℙ^σ: the process-level exogenous law on σ \[aux.\], used by Definition 3.7′ (its support fixes the legitimate object domain, and its distribution fixes QDep). The slot lattice Z^σ and the space of exogenous realizations Ω^σ are given in Definition 3.7′(1). ℙ^σ consists of the following two items:  
　　(a) the joint law ℙ^{Env,σ}(dU) of U on σ. U is realized only once, and is shared by all the deliveries within σ.  
　　(b) the family of conditional kernels κ\_ζ(dξ\_ζ \| U, h\_ζ), the slots ζ ∈ Z^σ taken in temporal order. h\_ζ is the run history before ζ, determined through Equation (4.1) by the operand, the protocol rules and admissible executions, U and the random components of the preceding slots. Where, according to h\_ζ, there is a deliverer, the kernel is indexed by the protocol π\_ζ selected at that time; where there is none, the point law on the placeholder value ∅\_ζ is taken.  
　　ℙ^σ\_{d,ρ,ς}(dU, dξ) := ℙ^{Env,σ}(dU) ∏\_{ζ in temporal order} κ\_ζ(dξ\_ζ \| U, h\_ζ). The support is finite (Chapter 4, (W2)), and h\_ζ is a deterministic function of the preceding quantities, so this law is uniquely determined by (a) and (b).  
　　Mechanism compatibility (a restriction added in this chapter):  
　　(c1) for every ζ with a delivery and every reachable (U, h\_ζ), κ\_ζ(dξ\_ζ \| U, h\_ζ) = ℙ^{π\_ζ,Env}(dξ \| U\_ζ). U\_ζ is the component of U involved in that delivery, and the right-hand side is the conditional law, given U, of the joint law declared for π\_ζ in Chapter 4. By (c1), given U\_ζ and the selected protocol π\_ζ, the conditional law of ξ\_ζ no longer depends on the rest of the history; correlations between the random components of different deliveries can be expressed only through the shared U or through the influence of history on the selection of protocols, and must be declared as such.  
　　(c2) for every protocol π reachable under some legitimate operand, protocol rule and admissible execution, and the slot ζ in which it lies, ℙ^{Env,σ}\|\_{U\_ζ} = ℙ^{π,Env}\|\_U. This is the unconditional marginal law before the selection of a protocol. Where different protocols reachable at the same slot declare different marginal laws of U, (c2) cannot hold. When (c2) holds, the supports of U on both sides are the same, and the right-hand side of (c1) is defined on every U\_ζ reachable at the process level.  
　　Where (a) and (b) are not declared, or are declared but do not accord with (c1) and (c2), ℙ^σ is invalid. When ℙ^σ is invalid, every object depending on this law or its support — the legitimate object domain of Definition 3.7′, the admissible family, the family of normal runs ℱ\_ω, reference runs, the witness domain 𝒬^σ and the result distribution — obtains no determinate normal qualification; VInc^σ, CondF^σ, QDep^σ and the segment criteria that depend on these objects are recorded as ⊥\_M, and propagate according to the order of precedence in Section 3.6 (following Chapter 4, Equation (4.2), "where only marginal laws are available, μ is recorded as ⊥\_M"). Run primitives and structural criteria that do not depend on this law — r^σ, RunOf, ▷^σ, NC, CS and the clauses of the seven items that do not go through segment criteria — are unaffected. Conditional independence is a common special case, and must also be written as a declaration.  
　　Mechanism compatibility is not an equivalent characterization of Chapter 4's joint law. Where protocols are selected dynamically according to history, and hence according to U, the conditional law of U\_ζ given the event "π\_ζ is selected" is in general not equal to ℙ^{π\_ζ,Env}\|\_U. Example: U is uniform and two-valued, π₀ is selected when U = 0 and π₁ when U = 1; given that π₀ is selected, the conditional law of U is the point law on 0. Hence the joint law conditional on the selection event is in general not equal to Chapter 4's declaration. Where the selection event is independent of U\_ζ (for instance, where the protocol depends only on the object d at the starting point of σ, and not on states evolved afterwards through U or through earlier random components), (c1) and (c2) imply that the joint law of (ξ, U) for that delivery equals Chapter 4's declaration; where the protocol depends on the host state at the time of delivery, that state may depend on U, and the case is not included.  
Correspondence: Chapter 5, the task-side conditions (the sixth item), and the Appendix, "One. Basic Objects and Types." The record format of Op, Tmpl, the choice of lineage for Auth, ℙ^σ and its mechanism compatibility are formal conventions \[aux.\].

### 3.2　Level of steps and runs

Definition 3.2 \[S\] (covering, junctions, and legitimate and individuable partitions)　π = ((s\_k, e\_k))\_{k=1..n} is a step partition of σ, s\_k and e\_k being events in the instance process graph, and In\_{n+1} ≔ Fin. The time interval I(e) of an event e is the maximal driven interval of Chapter 4, Definition 3.2; write t⁻(e) := min I(e) and t⁺(e) := max I(e). Step boundaries are taken as point values:  
　　τ⁻\_k := t⁻(s\_k), τ⁺\_k := t⁺(e\_k); step segment \[τ⁻\_k, τ⁺\_k\]; junction J\_k := (τ⁺\_k, τ⁻\_{k+1}) (k \< n), final segment J\_n := (τ⁺\_n, t\_end\].  
　　Cover(σ, π) :⇔ σ = \[s₁, t\_end\] = ⋃\_{k=1}^{n} (\[τ⁻\_k, τ⁺\_k\] ∪ J\_k), the interiors of the parts being pairwise disjoint, end points being allowed to coincide; and (I) I(s\_k) ⊆ \[τ⁻\_k, τ⁺\_k\] and I(e\_k) ⊆ \[τ⁻\_k, τ⁺\_k\] (k = 1, …, n); (Id) the initiation and write events of different steps are pairwise distinct.  
Events at shared end points are assigned by their identity: e\_k is the read-out event of step k, s\_{k+1} is the initiation event of step k+1, and one and the same event belongs to only one step ((Id); the initiation and read-out of the same step may be one and the same event). (I) reads this assignment as requiring the whole interval of an initiation or write event to lie within its own step segment; within the same step the intervals of s\_k and e\_k may overlap, and Cover does not constrain the order of events within a step, which is determined by Step.  
Taking the step boundaries of persisting events as "from the beginning of the initiation event to the end of the read-out event," and requiring (I), is this chapter's reading of persisting events; Chapter 5 neither prescribes it nor prescribes against it; its correspondence with Chapter 5's Cover is listed in the persisting-event step-boundary clause of Generative Computation Mathematical Form-H2-Step (8.3). Where all events are instantaneous (t⁻ = t⁺), (I) holds automatically, and this definition is the letter of Chapter 5's Cover. In Definitions 3.7 and 3.8, "at e\_k," "at s\_{k+1}" and the interval \[e\_k, s\_{k+1}\] are read as at τ⁺\_k, at τ⁻\_{k+1} and \[τ⁺\_k, τ⁻\_{k+1}\] respectively; in Definition 3.12, the time t\_out of the read-out event e\_k is read as τ⁺\_k; in Definition 3.7′, "the time of the event identified with e\_k" is read as the t⁺ of that event. These point values fix only the times at which numerical values are read; to which step an end-point event belongs still follows the attribution by identity above, and events within steps at the two ends of a junction interval are not counted as rewriting within the junction (Definition 3.8).  
　　LegalPart(π) :⇔ Cover(σ, π) ∧ ∀k: the event s\_k belongs to P's control or to the declared protocol (an operation of Prot\_F or a delivery protocol in 𝔇), and e\_k is the event at which the result of step k is written or latched into Out\_k.  
LegalPart constrains only the covering of segments and the initiation and write events; it does not yield that each step is an execution. The final segment is taken as left-open and right-closed: Chapter 5's "the final segment is empty when e\_n = t\_end" is this reading; if it were taken as open, no case with τ⁺\_n \< t\_end would cover t\_end.  
Correspondence: Chapter 5, the Cover and J\_k of Appendix "One" and the LegalPart of "Two"; the reading of step boundaries for persisting events is a formal convention \[aux.\].

Definition 3.3 \[S\] (executions, runs and candidate families)  
　(1) Whether the step segment \[s\_k, e\_k\] is one execution is determined at Chapter 4's token level: take the run individual r\_k anchored at the initiation part of s\_k (Chapter 4, Definition 3.15), and evaluate N\_exec(r\_k) according to Chapter 4, Definition 3.20. The step partition delimited by executions, π\_Exec, takes as step boundaries the initiations and read-outs of the run individuals in σ on which N\_exec takes 1; for it to be a step partition of σ, the interiors of the intervals of these run individuals must be pairwise disjoint, and no later initiation may be earlier than the preceding read-out (Chapter 5's working domain, coinciding end points being assigned as in Definition 3.2). Where they overlap, the case does not fall within the working domain and π\_Exec does not constitute a step partition (pipelined overlapping execution, 8.2 H1(b)).  
　(2) The process run individual r^σ (an extension, not belonging to Chapter 4, Definition 3.15). Chapter 4's run individuals all end at the first arrival of their read-out condition after the anchor; where, under the same declaration, a multi-step σ contains actual nodes after the first read-out following the anchor, no run individual of Chapter 4 can contain the whole of σ at once (Proposition 7.21). So the run Multi requires is given, on one and the same 𝔐\_P and one and the same instance process graph, by the following clauses:  
　　(E1) Nodes: all events and holding intervals in the instance process graph whose intervals intersect \[s₁, t\_end\], each taken as its segment on \[s₁, t\_end^σ\]. The segment v\|\_J of a node v on an interval J is the part of that same node restricted to I(v) ∩ J (when I(v) ∩ J ≠ ∅); of a node straddling s₁ or t\_end^σ, only its segment within the interval is included.  
　　(E2) Cut-off: t\_end^σ := min(t\_end, the time of termination). The read-outs of the steps within σ do not cut r^σ off.  
　　(E3) Termination: as in the termination clause of Chapter 4, Definition 3.15, with all holding elements within the boundary in place of Tr. The process terminates at t if and only if at t the states of these elements are all lost without recovery from a checkpoint within the boundary, or are set to an initial state not belonging to the process. The node segment in which the moment of termination itself lies belongs to r^σ.  
　　(E4) The reachability clause is not imposed: exogenous events entering through declared entries, and whatever they bring about, are all nodes of r^σ.  
　　(E5) r^σ ∈ Run^ok^σ if and only if its splice edges through checkpoints, migration or enabling gaps all lie within the boundary and contain no segment that takes on evaluation, and there are no nodes after termination (following Chapter 4's splice and termination clauses).  
　　(E7) RunOf(r^σ, P) :⇔ r^σ ∈ Run^ok^σ, and all nodes of σ belong to r^σ.  
　　The cross-step initiation set S^σ\_all: the events, among the successive occurrences on the instance of the initiation part S declared by 𝔇 (valid as checked by Chapter 4, Definition 3.14), whose intervals intersect \[s₁, t\_end\]; it depends only on 𝔐\_P, 𝔇, the identity of actual events and σ, not on any candidate partition. Where the S of 𝔇 is invalid, S^σ\_all is undefined, and predicates depending on it are recorded as ⊥\_D.  
　　Segment inclusion: for sets A, B of node segments, A ⊑ B :⇔ every segment v\|\_J of A is a subsegment of some segment v\|\_{J′} of the same node in B (I(v) ∩ J ⊆ I(v) ∩ J′). The step segment Seg\_k of step k is the set of segments, on \[τ⁻\_k, τ⁺\_k\], of the nodes in the instance process graph whose intervals intersect \[τ⁻\_k, τ⁺\_k\]; it is the formal image of \[s\_k, e\_k\] in Chapter 5's "\[s\_k, e\_k\] ⊆ r."  
　　Inheritance and extension: the instance process graph, the termination clause, the splice clause, the removal semantics of Chapter 4, Definition 3.16 (the removable set taken as in Definition 3.4(5)), the ▷ of Chapter 4, Definition 3.17, and the segment criteria of Chapter 4, Definition 3.18 (evaluated on r^σ according to this chapter's Definition 3.7′) follow Chapter 4; the type of anchor, the cut-off clause (E2), the reachability clause (E4) and the reading by segments are extensions. r^σ is not a run individual of Chapter 4; Chapter 4's ≈ and the strength of correspondence of its Theorems 7.9 and 7.14 do not extend to r^σ; this chapter uses only segment inclusion ⊑.  
　　The run individual r\_k of each step is still obtained from 𝔐\_P's assignment of initiation anchors, and is used only for Step. Where r^σ terminates within σ (including being set to an initial state not belonging to the process) before t⁺(e\_k), Seg\_k ⊑ r^σ fails (Lemma 7.23). The correspondence between r^σ and Chapter 5's "the whole of σ belongs to one and the same run on one and the same instance of P" is listed as Generative Computation Mathematical Form-H6 (8.3).  
　(3) The candidate family Cand is a finite declared set of pairs (π, r), in which each π satisfies the declaration of LegalPart(π) and r is r^σ; whether Cand covers all legitimate (π, r) in the sense of Chapter 5 is recorded by the coverage certificate CovCert ∈ {1, ⊥} (CovCert taking 1 requires an independent proof of exhaustiveness).  
　　The generated candidate family \[aux.\]:  
　　· Π\_pre′: all π satisfying the point-value order of Lemma 7.24, (I) and (Id), with the initiation and write events of each step taken from the finite set of events within σ and after t\_end;  
　　· Π\_gen′ := {π ∈ Π\_pre′ : LegalPart(π) ≠ 0};  
　　· Cand\_gen := Π\_gen′ × {r^σ}.  
　　Π\_gen′ is finite and can be computed in finitely many steps (Proposition 7.25). Cand ⊇ Cand\_gen does not by itself make CovCert take 1: CovCert records only a proof of coverage of all legitimate (π, r) of Chapter 5.  
Correspondence: Chapter 5, the Exec\_Φ, working domain and π\_Exec of Appendix "Two," and the RunOf of "One"; r^σ is an extension; Cand, CovCert, S^σ\_all, ⊑, Π\_pre′, Π\_gen′ and Cand\_gen are \[aux.\].

### 3.3　Level of primitives

Definition 3.4 \[aux.\] (reading, holding, linking, copying, source)　The following items are all obtained from Chapter 4's host model, events and dynamics (Chapter 4, Equation (4.1)), and are not inputs to the determination procedure:  
　(1) Read facts and event reads. Reads are of two kinds, distinguished by the time of evaluation in Chapter 4, Equation (4.1):  
　　Condition read CRd(i, t): the set of holding elements drawn on through condition ports when G\_i is evaluated at time step t; where the component's own state enters G\_i through self-feedback, it is counted. By Equation (4.1), E\_i(t) = G\_i(·) is evaluated at every time step, so CRd(i, t) is defined at every time step whether E\_i(t) takes 1 or 0; only at time steps at which the enable port of i lies outside the enabling range does G\_i draw on no condition port, and CRd(i, t) = ∅.  
　　Content read FRd(i, t): the set of holding elements drawn on by F\_i through content ports when E\_i(t) = 1; when E\_i(t) = 0, F\_i is not evaluated, and FRd(i, t) = ∅.  
　　Read fact Rd(i, t) := CRd(i, t) ∪ FRd(i, t).  
An event read Reads(e, j, p) is the restriction of read facts to the interval of an event: where e belongs to component i and has interval I\_e, Reads(e, j, p) holds if and only if, for some t ∈ I\_e, j ∈ CRd(i, t) (p being a condition port) or j ∈ FRd(i, t) (p being a content port). Condition reads at time steps with no write event belong to the read facts, not to any event read. Enable ports are not counted as reads; the set of holding elements drawn on by G\_i through enable ports at time step t is written as the enabling source ERd(i, t) \[aux.\]; it does not belong to the read facts, does not enter the comparison of D7-d, and is used only in Lemma 7.7 and the remarks after it.  
　　The holding law draws on no other element. In Chapter 4, Equation (4.1), when E\_i(t) = 0, X\_i(t⁺) = H\_i(X\_i(t), U\_i(t); Δ), and the arguments of H\_i are only the state of h\_i itself and the exogenous values. This is the structure of Chapter 4's host model: every physical channel that can set a held state is represented as a port of some component (Chapter 4, Definition 3.1(2)); the intervals in which the state of h\_i is determined by a path through condition ports or content ports are exactly the intervals in which E\_i = 1 (Chapter 4, Definition 3.2). If the content of some element j changes the state of h\_i while h\_i has no event, this action is, by Chapter 4, Definition 3.2, an event of i, and j enters FRd(i, t); a model that declares it as E\_i = 0 and writes it into H\_i does not pass checks (i) and (ii) of Chapter 4, 5.5, and predicates depending on it are recorded as ⊥\_M. So, on models that pass the checks of 5.5, Rd(i, t) already contains every other element drawn on by i at t.

Definition 3.5 \[S\] (Step)

Definition 3.6 \[S\] (host holding and Carry)　A component v ∈ x\_carry ∪ {D} is held by P at junction J\_k:

Definition 3.8 \[S\] (storage-only or transfer-only passage, and Hold)　  
　　Link\_I(A, B) :⇔ A = B and A undergoes no rewriting within I except self-refresh, or there is a sequence of events e₁, …, e\_m within the interval I, joined end to end in time, such that e₁ copies the content held by A, taking it as its only content source (the value duty of Definition 3.4(4) being the identity or a copy; the content source of A being its last write event or the value held at the cut, Definition 3.4(6)), adjacent events are linked by Copy with the written content continuously Held in between, and e\_m writes into B; and moreover, from the start of I to e₁ for A, and from after e\_m to the end of I for B, there is no rewriting except self-refresh. Self-refresh means copying that takes the content held by the element itself as its only content source (Definition 3.4(4)); copying in the content of another element is rewriting, even if it is Copy. Each "no rewriting except self-refresh" above concerns the interior of I: the start and end events of I (such as the write of the read-out event e\_k into Out\_k, and the initiation event s\_{k+1}) belong to the adjacent steps by the end-point assignment of Definition 3.2, and are not counted. Where e₁ carries out a non-copying evaluation on the content of A, it does not constitute Link even if the value written is the same as that of A.  
Link is a relation between holding elements (positions), relative to a time interval I; Chapter 5's Link(A, B) takes I as the corresponding junction; changes of D through protocol operations are not Copy, are not handled by Link, and are dealt with by the G\_k of (b) below and by Sed(iii). For each k:  
　　(a) for every component v of x\_carry and D: Link\_{\[e\_k, s\_{k+1}\]}(Out\_k(v), In\_{k+1}(v)), where, on the components of D, the clauses "no rewriting except self-refresh" do not count protocol operations in Op as rewriting;  
　　(b) pr\_{x\_carry, D}(the content held by In\_{k+1} at s\_{k+1}) = G\_k(pr\_{x\_carry, D}(the content held by Out\_k at e\_k)) (what is held at e\_k including the components written at that time and those held earlier and not rewritten, such as D when it is not written at e\_k; for k = n the left-hand side is the content held by Fin at t\_end), where G\_k is the composition in temporal order of the updates of D in Op falling within J\_k, and is the identity on x\_carry; x\_input is obtained by legitimate delivery, x\_inst is recomputed within the subsequent execution, and c evolves separately, none of them entering this equation;  
　　(c) every event within J\_k that writes into a holding element on the lineage of x\_carry is a Copy within that lineage.  
　　Hold(σ, π) := ⋀\_k \[(a) ∧ (b) ∧ (c)\]  
Where complete value evidence is unequal, where there is a complete event model in which the Link path fails, or where there is non-Copy rewriting within the lineage, it takes 0; where the events, ports, values or interpretations of Op needed are missing, ⊥\_M or ⊥\_D is recorded. Equality of the end-point values does not exempt rewriting in between.  
Correspondence: Chapter 5's Hold(a)(b)(c).

### 5.4　Working domain and solvability

Working domain: (WG1) 𝔐\_P and every initiation anchor within σ satisfy Chapter 4's working domain (W1)–(W4), and the instance process graph on \[s₁, t\_end\] is finite; where Definition 3.7 replaces the f of Chapter 4, Definition 3.18, by a component of the result function of τ\_Φ or by the update function of Prot\_F, the computability and termination that (W4) requires of f hold for the function evaluated by proxy; (WG2) the operand domain of τ\_Φ, the legitimate configurations and control values, and the declared exogenous choices are all finite, and the Dom\_x₀, each Inp\_ζ, Ξ\_ζ, 𝒰^σ, the family of rules 𝓡, the admissible executions and the sets of admissible port values of Definition 3.7′ are all finite; (WG3) the A\_k of every step is finite, AdmVar\_k is finite, and K\_k is given as a finite table of rational numbers; (WG4) Cand, Op and the lineages of Car are finite.

Theorem 5.3　Under (WG1)–(WG4):  
　(1) (Termination) SolveGen terminates in finitely many steps.  
　(2) (Uniqueness of output) For each predicate, SolveGen returns exactly one value in {1, 0, ⊥\_M, ⊥\_D, ⊥\_J, ⊥\_C}, or an unsolvable status (model insufficient, declaration invalid, outside the working domain); unsolvable statuses are mapped by the output map ψ of 7.1 to ⊥\_M, ⊥\_D, ⊥\_C.  
Proof: (1) We give step by step the premises on which termination rests. Step (1) calls SolveExec at finitely many anchors; these satisfy (WG1), so the calls terminate by Chapter 4, Theorem 5.3; r^σ, Run^ok^σ and S^σ\_all are finite tests on a finite instance process graph; the removal families of ▷^σ are taken from a finite set of events, and each counterfactual is an iteration over finitely many time steps. Steps (2), (6), (7) and (10) test paths, attribution and condition ancestors on finite sets of events and finite lineages ((WG1), (WG4)); the Z^σ, Ω^σ and 𝒟^σ\_0 of Definition 3.7′(1)(3) are finite ((WG1), (WG2)), each legitimacy test consists of finitely many runs on a finite conditional support, and the mechanism compatibility of ℙ^σ is finitely many equalities. Step (3) tests Cover, J\_k and LegalPart on the finitely many candidates given in Cand ((WG4)); Π\_gen′ is computed in finitely many steps by Proposition 7.25, and Cand ⊇ Cand\_gen is a test of inclusion between finite sets; the procedure generates no partitions outside Cand\_gen. Step (4) makes two runs under each of finitely many legitimate assignments ((WG2)), the length of the runs being finite by (W1). Step (8) calls ▷^σ and the segment criteria of Definition 3.7′, the function evaluated by proxy satisfying the rider of (WG1); the removal families Γ needed for the negation of ▷^σ are taken from a finite set of events, and their exhaustion is a finite test; in actual solving, where a test is not completed, ⊥\_J is recorded by Definition 3.4(5), not 0. In Definition 3.7′, the admissible families form a set of maps from a finite set to a finite set, and Λ concerns finitely many outputs each with finitely many values, so 𝒬^σ is finite; VInc^σ and CondF^σ are finite tests on finite families; the tolerance class of Acc^σ is a finite weighted sum, and non-tolerance classes without a declared determination procedure take ⊥\_C directly, without entering computation; QDep^σ is an exhaustive search over 𝒬^σ. The scale is exponential; only finiteness is claimed. The certificates used by steps (9), (11) and (12) — cert\_FQ, cert\_SD, the coverage certificate of AdmVar, and CovCert — are inputs; the procedure checks only whether their provenance identifier, the version of the declaration they depend on and their range of coverage are complete (Section 3.6), recording ⊥\_M where any is missing, and does not check their truth. The bijection test of step (9) pairs the finite sets Op and W\_D one by one by temporal order and operation type; step (11) enumerates the at most 2^{\|A\_k\|} subsets of A\_k and the finitely many pairs of AdmVar\_k ((WG3)); step (12) is a combination over a finite family. The prerequisite check (i) is a test of finitely many items.  
(2) By induction in the order of Lemma 5.2: the preceding predicates have already taken unique values, and the current definition is an explicit function of them and of the declared items; the clauses of each definition apply in the order of Section 3.6, exactly one clause deciding the value, and the strong Kleene connectives, existence over finite families and the branching rules of Section 3.6 are total functions on {1, 0, ⊥}; the outputs of the subprograms of Chapter 4 that are called are unique by Chapter 4, Theorem 5.3. Where the prerequisite check does not pass, or a called subprogram returns an unsolvable status, predicates depending on it take that status; ψ is a function, so the value after mapping is unique too. ∎  
Outside the working domain, the procedure returns the unsolvable status "outside the working domain" or "model insufficient," mapped by ψ to ⊥\_C or ⊥\_M. Finite reduction of infinite operand domains and of continuous-time models is an open problem (8.4).  
The two parts of Theorem 5.3, together with read-back, concern three different strengths: (1) termination of the evaluation process on finite input; (2) a unique three-valued output or unsolvable status under the branching rules of Section 3.6; (3) correct read-back of the output to the true structure of the host. Theorem 5.3 gives only (1) and (2), both of which concern only the declared model and the certificates given. (3) is not given by Theorem 5.3: the read-back of V\_j = 1 depends on Theorem 7.14 and Generative Computation Mathematical Form-H1, and the read-back of V\_j = 0 on premises (1)–(5) of Theorem 7.15; the truth of the certificates, the agreement of the declaration with the host, and the completeness of Cand (the truth of CovCert) all lie within these premises. The termination and output of the solving procedure are not evidence for these premises, and these premises are not determined by the solving procedure.

7.2　Characterization of Gov by pivot sets

Theorem 7.1 (pivot sets)　Suppose that the set of read-in variables A\_k of step k is finite, D ∈ A\_k, ν\_k ∈ AdmVar\_k, and ε\_role ∈ \[0, 1). Then  
　　D ∈ ActArg^μ\_k(x′\_k) ⇔ ∃C ⊆ A\_k ∖ {D}: Δ\_k(C) \> ε\_role ∧ Δ\_k(C ∪ {D}) ≤ ε\_role.  
Proof: (⇒) Suppose D belongs to a minimal μ-sufficient set M. Take C = M ∖ {D}. M is minimal, so its proper subset C is not μ-sufficient, and Δ\_k(C) \> ε\_role; C ∪ {D} = M is μ-sufficient, and Δ\_k(C ∪ {D}) ≤ ε\_role. (⇐) Let C be as on the right-hand side. C ∪ {D} is μ-sufficient; since A\_k is finite, among the μ-sufficient subsets of C ∪ {D} there is one of least cardinality, M, and M is a minimal μ-sufficient set. If D ∉ M, then M ⊆ C; by the monotonicity of Equation (4.3), Δ\_k(C) ≤ Δ\_k(M) ≤ ε\_role, contradicting Δ\_k(C) \> ε\_role. So D ∈ M, and D ∈ ActArg^μ\_k(x′\_k). ∎  
Where A\_k is infinite, this theorem makes no claim; where the existence or obtainability of minimal sets cannot be guaranteed, Gov is recorded as ⊥\_C (Definition 3.11).

Corollary 7.2 (comparability)　If there are v, w ∈ AdmVar\_k, both agreeing with ν\_k on A\_k ∖ {D}, such that TV(K\_k(· \| v), K\_k(· \| w)) \> ε\_role, then D ∈ ActArg^μ\_k(x′\_k).  
Proof: Take C = A\_k ∖ {D}. v, w ∈ Var\_k(C), so Δ\_k(C) \> ε\_role; C ∪ {D} = A\_k, Var\_k(A\_k) = {ν\_k}, and Δ\_k(A\_k) = 0 ≤ ε\_role. The conclusion follows by Theorem 7.1. ∎  
This is the formal image of Chapter 5's comparability lemma: the counterfactuals vary only D, the other read-ins and c being fixed at their actual values at that step.

Proposition 7.3 (the formal level of Chapter 5's H2(b))　Write  
　　Rep\_ε :⇔ for every step k in σ, if D has, in the generation of x′\_k, the duty of which ActualHold speaks, then there is C ⊆ A\_k ∖ {D} such that Δ\_k(C) \> ε\_role and Δ\_k(C ∪ {D}) ≤ ε\_role.  
Where A\_k is finite and the forward read-back and reverse-representation conditions of Gov (Generative Computation Mathematical Form-H1-Gov, H2-Gov) hold, Chapter 5's H2(b), "ActualHold in the generation of x′ yields D ∈ ActArg^μ(x′)," is equivalent to Rep\_ε.  
Proof: Under the read-back conditions assumed, Chapter 5's D ∈ ActArg^μ(x′\_k) has the same value as the predicate of the same name in Definition 3.11; by Theorem 7.1, the latter is equivalent to the existence of a pivot set. Substituting into the consequent of H2(b) gives the result. ∎  
The antecedent of Rep\_ε contains ActualHold, which in *The Necessity Form of Law* v1.3 is the name of an intermediate duty and carries no independent truth value apart from the system of duties of law; Rep\_ε is simply the pivot-set rewriting of Chapter 5's H2(b) itself, with the same truth value as H2(b), and Proposition 7.3 does not prove it. What Proposition 7.3 gives is this: once H2(b) is determined on some host, the object of the determination is a proposition of the existence of a pivot set that can be tested on a finite declared model. By the stages of Chapter 5, Section Four, passing from the antecedent of Rep\_ε to its consequent goes through (b1) and (b2) to a pivot set under the declaration of the duty, and then through (b3) to a pivot set under the declaration of the present step (K\_k, AdmVar\_k, ε\_role); the C of Proposition 7.30 is a pivot set shared by the two levels, and Corollary 7.2 is a route that does not go through the declaration of the duty (7.9). Proposition 7.5′ (composite granularity and component granularity)　Suppose that at step k:  
　(Q0) D = (D\_1, …, D\_m), each D\_j being actually read by K\_k;  
　(Q1) A\_k is finite, and AdmVar\_k is finite;  
　(Q2) the two granularities use the same joint value family AdmVar\_k, the same kernel K\_k, the same actual point ν\_k ∈ AdmVar\_k and the same ε\_role; the candidate set at composite granularity is A\_k (D being a single element), and the candidate set at component granularity is A\_k^♭ := (A\_k ∖ {D}) ∪ {D\_1, …, D\_m}; for C ⊆ A\_k, Var\_k(C) is the same as the Var\_k^♭ obtained on A\_k^♭ after expanding the D in C into all its components. Then D ∈ ActArg^μ\_k(x′\_k) ⇔ ∃j: D\_j ∈ ActArg^{μ,♭}\_k(x′\_k).  
Proof: The proof of Theorem 7.1 uses only the finiteness of the candidate set, the monotonicity of Var in the conditioning set, and ν\_k ∈ AdmVar\_k, and so holds equally for A\_k^♭; both directions below use it.  
(⇒) By Theorem 7.1, there is C ⊆ A\_k ∖ {D} with Δ\_k(C) \> ε\_role and Δ\_k(C ∪ {D}) ≤ ε\_role. C contains no component of D, so Δ^♭(C) = Δ\_k(C) \> ε\_role. Let C\_i := C ∪ {D\_1, …, D\_i} (i = 0, …, m); by (Q0), C\_i ⊆ A\_k^♭, and by (Q2), Δ^♭(C\_m) = Δ\_k(C ∪ {D}) ≤ ε\_role. By Theorem 7.1 (at component granularity), D\_i ∈ ActArg^{μ,♭}\_k.  
(⇐) By Theorem 7.1 (at component granularity), there is C′ ⊆ A\_k^♭ ∖ {D\_j} with Δ^♭(C′) \> ε\_role and Δ^♭(C′ ∪ {D\_j}) ≤ ε\_role. Let C be the set of non-D components of C′, so C ⊆ A\_k ∖ {D}. 7.3　Well-definedness of abstract generation kernels

Theorem 7.6　Suppose that the set of host microstates 𝔐 is finite, α: 𝔐 → 𝒮 is the read-out to declared type states, and the one-step output kernel on microstates is κ(· \| m).  
　(1) The abstract kernel K(· \| s) := κ(· \| m) (α(m) = s) is well defined on the image of α if and only if, for each s, the microstates in α⁻¹(s) have the same output kernel.  
　(2) If the distribution of n-step outputs is required to be determined by the abstract state, a sufficient condition is that, for each s, the microstates in α⁻¹(s) have the same joint kernel of (α(m′), y), the microstate dynamics is Markovian, and the control history is frozen. Requiring only the same output kernel is not enough to guarantee that the distribution over two or more steps is determined by the abstract state.  
Proof: (1) K is a map on 𝒮 if and only if it takes a single value on each fibre. (2) Sufficiency: if the joint kernel is the same on fibres, the conditional distribution of (α(m\_{t+1}), y\_t) depends only on α(m\_t); composing step by step by the Markov property, the distribution of n-step outputs depends only on α(m₀). An example of insufficiency: 𝔐 = {a, b, a′, b′}, α(a) = α(b) = s, α(a′) = s₁, α(b′) = s₂; a and b both output 0 in one step, and pass to a′ and b′ respectively; a′ outputs 0 and b′ outputs 1. The one-step output kernel is the same on the fibre {a, b}, yet the distribution of two-step outputs varies with the microstate within the fibre. ∎  
Theorem 7.6 gives the conditions under which K\_k can be used as a single-valued kernel on type states; it does not prove that any host satisfies these conditions.

Lemma 7.7 (read before write)　Suppose that 𝔐\_P satisfies (WG1) and passes the checks of 5.5 (including (i) and (ii) of Chapter 4, 5.5), that the dynamics is deterministic, and that the G, F, H of each component do not depend on hidden state outside the model; that Ret\_k and Tmpl have been declared, and that the domain of legitimate assignments has been fully enumerated. For step k:  
　(i) If, for every legitimate assignment ω, in the actual run under ω every h ∈ ℋ\_k either has an actual value at the cut equal to Tmpl(h), or does not appear in any read fact or enabling source within \[t\_S, t\_out\], or has been rewritten by an event within the present run before it first appears, within \[t\_S, t\_out\], in some read fact Rd(i, t) or enabling source ERd(i, t) (Definition 3.4(1)), then DeclEq\_k = 1.  
　(ii) If there are a legitimate assignment ω and h ∈ ℋ\_k such that the actual value of h at the cut is not equal to Tmpl(h), and, in the actual run under ω, before any rewriting of it within the present run, h has appeared in some read fact Rd(i, t), t ∈ \[t\_S, t\_out\] (condition reads not requiring E\_i(t) = 1), then DeclEq\_k = ⊥ (a witness of difference, ⊥\_D).  
Proof: Fix a legitimate assignment ω; the two runs spoken of below are both under ω. Write Ξ := {h ∈ ℋ\_k : actual value at the cut ≠ Tmpl(h)}; the elements of Ret\_k are not in it. For a time t, write Υ\_t for the set of elements of Ξ not yet rewritten by an event before t in the actual run; in the proof of (i), "appears in" covers both read facts and enabling sources; write Diff\_t for the set of holding elements whose contents differ between the two runs at t. Changes of state fall into two branches: where E\_i(t) = 1, the content of h\_i is written by F\_i (an event write); where E\_i(t) = 0, h\_i evolves by H\_i, which depends only on the state of h\_i itself and on exogenous values (Chapter 4, Equation (4.1); the last paragraph of Definition 3.4(1)), the two runs taking the same exogenous realization. H\_i need not be the identity.  
(i) By induction on t, the induction hypothesis being: before t the two runs are the same on the four axes; Diff\_t ⊆ Υ\_t; no element of Υ\_t has previously appeared in any read fact. When t is the cut, Diff = Ξ = Υ, and the hypothesis holds. Since the condition of (i) holds for the fixed ω, Υ\_t is disjoint from ⋃\_i \[Rd(i, t) ∪ ERd(i, t)\], and so is Diff\_t. Hence: the condition arguments (CRd(i, t)) and enabling arguments (ERd(i, t)) of each G\_i at t take the same values in the two runs, the exogenous values are the same, and E\_i(t) is the same; where E\_i(t) = 1, the content arguments of F\_i (FRd(i, t)) take the same values, the content written is the same, and the successor of h\_i has the same value on both sides; where E\_i(t) = 0, the successor has the same value on both sides when h\_i ∉ Diff\_t, and when h\_i ∈ Diff\_t the successor either still differs or is merged into the same value through H\_i. So Diff\_{t+1} is contained in the set of elements of Diff\_t not written at t, and hence in Υ\_{t+1}: evolution under H can eliminate differences but cannot produce them; only event writes make elements leave Υ. An element j read at t is not in Υ\_t: if j has already been rewritten within the present run, its source in the two runs is the same event (the events being the same, identified by Chapter 4, Definition 3.2); if j has not been rewritten, then j ∉ Ξ, and the sources are both values held at the cut, with the same value at the cut. Axis (d) is the same. Advancing the induction to t\_out shows that the two runs are identical on all four axes. The condition of (i) holds for every ω, and ω was arbitrary, so the four axes are identical for every ω, and DeclEq\_k = 1.  
(ii) Take the ω given by (ii), and in the actual run under it take the earliest time t\* at which some h ∈ Υ\_{t*} appears in some Rd(i, t*); the condition of (ii) guarantees that t\* exists. If the two runs already differ on the four axes before t\* (for instance, if some element of Υ has changed the events of some component through an enabling source), the four axes are not identical under this ω, DeclEq\_k = ⊥, and the conclusion is reached. Otherwise, before t\* the two runs are the same on the four axes, the events are the same, and h has not been rewritten in either run. At t\* the source of that read in both runs is the value of h held at the cut (evolution under H\_h in between does not change the source, Definition 3.4(6)), and its value at the cut is the actual value in one and Tmpl(h) in the other, which differ; by D7-d, there is a difference on axis (d), whether or not H\_h has made the contents at the time of reading the same. The four axes are not identical under this ω, and by Definition 3.12 DeclEq\_k = ⊥. ∎  
The condition of (i) is a universal condition over all legitimate assignments, aligned with "for every legitimate assignment ω, the four axes are all the same" in Definition 3.12; (ii) needs only one ω. Satisfying "rewritten before read" only under the assignment actually taken is not enough to obtain 1: for example, an undeclared h has actual value 1 at the cut and template value 0, the declared input is x ∈ {0, 1}, h is written to 0 in the first beat when x = 0, and y reads h in the second beat; under the actual assignment x = 0, h is rewritten before being read, but under the legitimate assignment x = 1, h is read without having been rewritten, and the sources of the two reads are values held at the cut that differ, so by (ii) DeclEq\_k = ⊥. Where enabling sources are not involved, the conditions of (i) and (ii) are the negations of each other. There is one further case to which neither clause applies: some h ∈ Ξ is drawn on before rewriting only through an enabling source and does not appear in read facts. DeclEq\_k is then computed directly by Definition 3.12: where the two cut values put the enable port of some component inside and outside the range respectively, thereby changing events, there is a difference on axis (a), and ⊥ is taken; where both are inside the enabling range, or both outside, and the events are unchanged, D7-d does not compare enabling sources, and 1 can be taken. Example: an undeclared power-gating register PG has actual value 1 at the cut and reset value 0, and PG gates the functional unit FU through FU's enable port; in the template run FU cannot write, the events differ, and DeclEq\_k = ⊥, while PG appears in no read fact.  
(ii) gives only DeclEq\_k = ⊥, and hence Step\_k ∈ {0, ⊥}; it yields neither Step\_k = 0 nor N\_exec(r\_k) = 0. Lemma 7.7(ii) gives ⊥ for every undeclared state read before rewriting whose value differs from the template, whether or not that state carries content across steps; auxiliary states unrelated to execution also make DeclEq\_k = ⊥. This strictness keeps DeclEq from producing false 1s, at the cost that the reverse representation of Step (Generative Computation Mathematical Form-H2-Step) cannot be derived from the definition; a weaker reliable criterion is listed as an open problem (8.4(3)).  
Cases in which a difference at the cut merges through H before reading belong to this strictness. Example: an undeclared element h has actual value 1 at the cut and template value 0, E\_h = 0 and H\_h(h) = 0, and at the next time step y reads h through F\_y; at the time of reading the content of h is 0 in both runs and the writes are the same, but the sources of the two reads are values held at the cut with values 1 and 0 respectively, so by D7-d there is a difference on axis (d), and DeclEq\_k = ⊥. Here the cut content of h has not entered the present step; ⊥ is conservative — neither a false 1 nor a negation; it belongs, together with the auxiliary states mentioned in the preceding paragraph, to the cases the weaker criterion of 8.4(3) is meant to handle. If axis (d) were instead compared by content at the time of reading, this case could obtain 1; that is a different criterion, whose freedom from false 1s must be proved separately (8.4(3)).

### 7.7　Semantic correspondence

Proposition 7.19 (condition of the same run)　Write  
　　SameRun(σ, π, r) := RunOf(r, P) ∧ ⋀\_k \[Seg\_k ⊑ r\] ∧ \[W\_D ⊑ r\] ∧ ⋀\_k \[J\_k ⊑ r\], r taken as r^σ,  
where W\_D and J\_k denote, respectively, the set of event nodes within σ writing into Auth(D, ·), and the set of segments on J\_k of the nodes whose intervals intersect J\_k. Proof: Multi = 1 gives RunOf(r^σ, P) and Seg\_k ⊑ r^σ. Lemma 7.23 (step-segment inclusion)　Suppose π satisfies Cover, and every e\_k is an event node actually present in the instance process graph. Then Seg\_k ⊑ r^σ ⇔ t⁺(e\_k) ≤ t\_end^σ.  
Proof: (⇐) By Cover, s₁ ≤ τ⁻\_k; moreover τ⁺\_k = t⁺(e\_k) ≤ t\_end^σ, so \[τ⁻\_k, τ⁺\_k\] ⊆ \[s₁, t\_end^σ\]. Any segment v\|\_{\[τ⁻\_k, τ⁺\_k\]} of Seg\_k is non-empty, so the interval of v intersects \[s₁, t\_end^σ\], and v\|\_{\[s₁, t\_end^σ\]} is a node segment of r^σ (E1) containing it. (⇒) Suppose t⁺(e\_k) \> t\_end^σ. By (I) of Cover, I(e\_k) ⊆ \[τ⁻\_k, τ⁺\_k\], so the segment of e\_k in the step segment is the whole of e\_k, containing the moment t⁺(e\_k); the segment of e\_k in r^σ is e\_k\|\_{\[s₁, t\_end^σ\]}, which does not contain t⁺(e\_k). So Seg\_k ⊑ r^σ fails. ∎  
Where termination occurs exactly at t⁺(e\_k), t\_end^σ = t⁺(e\_k), and the right-hand side holds (E3: the segment in which the moment of termination lies belongs to r^σ). Where no actual event node corresponds to e\_k, the clause of LegalPart "e\_k is the event at which the result of step k is written or latched into Out\_k" fails or cannot be determined, so π ∉ Π\_gen′ or LegalPart = ⊥ (Proposition 7.25); in the latter case Multi takes ⊥ by strong Kleene.

Lemma 7.24 (point-value characterization of Cover)　Suppose the initiation and write events of π are taken from the instance process graph. Then  
　　Cover(σ, π) ⇔ τ⁻\_1 = s₁ ∧ τ⁻\_1 ≤ τ⁺\_1 ≤ τ⁻\_2 ≤ τ⁺\_2 ≤ … ≤ τ⁺\_n ≤ t\_end ∧ each step satisfies (I) ∧ (Id).  
Proof: The point-value part. (⇐) \[τ⁻\_1, τ⁺\_1\], J\_1 = (τ⁺\_1, τ⁻\_2), …, \[τ⁻\_n, τ⁺\_n\], J\_n = (τ⁺\_n, t\_end\] join end to end in turn, and their union is \[s₁, t\_end\]; the closed segments share end points only where τ⁺\_k = τ⁻\_{k+1}, and the open segments J\_k are disjoint from the others. (⇒) If some τ⁺\_k \< τ⁻\_k, \[τ⁻\_k, τ⁺\_k\] is not an interval; if some τ⁻\_{k+1} \< τ⁺\_k, the interiors of the step segments of steps k and k+1 intersect; if τ⁺\_n \> t\_end, the union exceeds σ; if τ⁻\_1 ≠ s₁, the starting point of σ is not covered or the union exceeds σ. (I) and (Id) are conjuncts of Definition 3.2 independent of the point-value part. ∎  
Cover depends only on times, intervals and the identity of events, all three determinate in the model, so Cover ∈ {0, 1} and does not take ⊥. Where all events are instantaneous, (I) holds automatically, and this lemma is the characterization of Cover written in terms of event times.

Proposition 7.25 (finite exhaustion of formally legitimate partitions)　Assume (WG1). Candidate partitions take a uniform type domain: n ≥ 1 steps, with the initiation and write events of each step taken from the finite set E\_σ of events within σ and after t\_end. Proof: (2) (⇒) When LegalPart ≠ 0, Cover ≠ 0, that is, Cover = 1, so by Lemma 7.24 the point-value order, (I) and (Id) hold; the attribution of each initiation and write is not 0 (a strong Kleene conjunction with a 0 is 0). Proposition 7.28 (extreme values of type completion)　In Definition 3.7′(5), for each d, the infimum and supremum of μ(G\_d) over Comp\_type(μ̃^d) are L\_d^{min} and L\_d^{max} respectively, and both are attained.  
Proof: In Comp\_type, the masses of the atoms containing ↑ are distributed independently over their possible completion values; μ(G\_d) is the known passing mass L\_d plus the mass each atom distributes onto passing values, and is linear in the distribution. A linear function on a product of simplices attains its extreme values at vertices, that is, where each atom puts its whole mass on one completion value: for the lower bound, an atom for which a failing completion is feasible contributes 0, and otherwise (where it can only pass) its whole mass, giving L\_d^{min}; for the upper bound, an atom for which a passing completion is feasible contributes its whole mass, giving L\_d^{max}. The support is finite, so the extreme values are attained. ∎  
Different d are different runs, whose ↑ atoms are completed separately, with no cross-object constraint; so the whole-family criterion of the tolerance class takes extreme values object by object. This proposition gives only the internal extreme values of Comp\_type, not their correspondence with physically possible completions.

7.9　The resolution window of bearing qualification and its transfer between declarations

The direction needed by Chapter 5's H2(b) falls into three stages (Chapter 5, Section Four, written (b1), (b2), (b3)): (b1) ActualHold yields DynamicRole (a question of how Chapter 5's G2 is read); (b2) DynamicRole yields the duty-level formal criterion, that is, at the generation node where the duty resides, some component of D belongs to ActArg^{μ,r} under the declaration of the duty (A, AdmVar^r, ν, K^r, ε^r) (H\_RepD of *The Mathematical Form of Law*); (b3) passing from the declaration of the duty to the declaration of the present step (A, AdmVar\_k, ν, K\_k, ε\_k). (b3) is a mathematical question between two sets of declarations, for which Proposition 7.30 and Corollary 7.2 give conditions; (b1) and (b2) are not proved, and remain the part of Chapter 5's H2(b) pending verification. The step from components to the composite D uses the (⇐) direction of Proposition 7.5′, which does not need (Q0).

Proposition 7.29 (resolution window)　Suppose A is finite, D ∈ A, and ν ∈ AdmVar. Then D ∈ ActArg^μ(ε) ⇔ ε ∈ ⋃\_{C ⊆ A∖{D}} \[Δ(C ∪ {D}), Δ(C)).  
Proof: For fixed C, the right-hand side of Theorem 7.1 is Δ(C ∪ {D}) ≤ ε \< Δ(C); take the union over C. ∎  
This proposition is an equivalent rewriting of Theorem 7.1, not a new sufficient condition; it writes the range of resolutions at which D obtains bearing qualification as a union of finitely many half-open intervals, which can be computed directly. In Example 7.4 the window of D is \[0, 1/20).

Proposition 7.30 (transfer between declarations)　Suppose the two declarations have the same finite candidate set A (aligned, when D is composite, by expansion as in (Q2) of Proposition 7.5′) and the same actual point ν ∈ AdmVar\_k ∩ AdmVar^r; and that there is a determinate map g such that, at the points common to the two families, K\_k(· \| v) = g#K^r(· \| v) (the present step's output being x′\_k = g(y), where y is the node at which the duty resides; when g is the identity, the two are the same node). Suppose that at the duty level there is C ⊆ A ∖ {D} with Δ^r(C) \> ε^r ≥ Δ^r(C ∪ {D}). If  
　(S1) Var\_k(C ∪ {D}) ⊆ Var^r(C ∪ {D}), and ε\_k ≥ ε^r;  
　(S2) there are v, w ∈ Var^r(C) ∩ Var\_k(C) with TV(g#K^r(· \| v), g#K^r(· \| w)) \> ε\_k;  
then D ∈ ActArg^μ\_k(x′\_k).  
Proof: By (S1), the points of Var\_k(C ∪ {D}) all lie in the duty family, and their present-step kernels are the pushforwards of the duty kernels under g; total variation does not increase under determinate maps, so Δ\_k(C ∪ {D}) ≤ Δ^r(C ∪ {D}) ≤ ε^r ≤ ε\_k. By (S2), Δ\_k(C) \> ε\_k. By Theorem 7.1, D ∈ ActArg^μ\_k(x′\_k). ∎  
(S1) is a relation between the two declarations, and can be checked point by point on a finite domain. (S2) says that the lower bound of the pivot set C is still witnessed, under the present step's declaration, by a pair of legitimate states common to both; it is the form, on a witnessing pair, of one half of the conclusion, and is checkable but not independent of the conclusion. The other route is Corollary 7.2: if the present step's legitimate family has a pair of states differing only in D, agreeing with ν on A ∖ {D}, whose kernels differ by more than ε\_k, then D belongs to ActArg^μ\_k(x′\_k), without needing (S1) and without depending on the declaration of the duty. Corollary 7.2 says only "when the present step's family has such a witnessing pair, D obtains qualification at the present step"; it does not prove that ActualHold necessarily brings such a witnessing pair with it, which still goes through (b1) and (b2). The following counterexamples show, respectively, that when the corresponding safeguard is removed, transfer no longer holds in general (they do not prove these conditions necessary for every transfer theorem): for (S2) failing, see Example 7.4 (D belongs at duty resolution 0 but not at the present step's ε = 1/10); for the family inclusion of (S1) failing, see Example 7.5(2); for the resolution relation of (S1) failing, see Proposition 7.31; where the node map erases the difference — for instance, the two values of D make y take two point values, and x′ = g(y) merges them — D belongs at the duty node but not at the present step; in this case D does not enter the generation of x′, so the case is not a counterexample to H2(b) and shows only that the node correspondence cannot be dropped.

Proposition 7.31 (bearing qualification is not monotone in resolution)　There are a finite model and ε₁ \< ε₂ such that D ∈ ActArg^μ(ε₂) while D ∉ ActArg^μ(ε₁); and there are also cases with D ∈ ActArg^μ(ε₁) while D ∉ ActArg^μ(ε₂).  
Proof: The latter is Example 7.4 (ε₁ = 0, ε₂ = 1/10). The former: A = {D, Z}, AdmVar = {(1, 0), (0, 1), (0, 0)} (coordinates (D, Z)), ν = (0, 1), K(· \| 1, 0) = (1, 0, 0), K(· \| 0, 1) = (6/13, 5/13, 2/13), K(· \| 0, 0) = (4/7, 3/7, 0). Var({D}) = {(0, 1), (0, 0)}, Δ({D}) = 2/13; Var({Z}) = {ν}, Δ({Z}) = 0; Δ(∅) = TV(K(· \| 1, 0), K(· \| 0, 1)) = 7/13. At ε₂ = 1/5, {D} and {Z} are both minimal μ-sufficient sets, and D ∈ ActArg^μ; at ε₁ = 0, {D} is not sufficient, only {Z} is a minimal μ-sufficient set, and D ∉ ActArg^μ. ∎  
In the former example, Z = 1 determines the state uniquely through the joint constraint, so at exact resolution Z is sufficient on its own and D is squeezed out. Hence qualification can be lost whether the present step's declared resolution is coarser or finer than the duty's resolution; Proposition 7.30 combines family inclusion with ε\_k ≥ ε^r, and Corollary 7.2 does not depend on their relative size; neither substitutes a single threshold relation for item-by-item checking.

Each condition is an unproved proposition; until it is proved or superseded by a stronger definition, the related conclusions hold only under that condition. The construction level and formal level of H6 have been given in the main text and are not conditions pending verification; they are listed under H6 only to state the objects on which the semantic level depends.

　Generative Computation Mathematical Form-H1 (forward read-back)　Content: for X ∈ {Step, Carry, Sed, ¬Intr, Hold, Multi, Gov}, the formal image of X taking 1 yields that Chapter 5's X holds (seven items, written H1-X). Of these, H1-Step contains the conditions carried by Chapter 4's R2, as well as Computational Execution General Form-H3, and contains the forward read-back of LegalPart (LegalPart(π) = 1 of Definition 3.2 yields Chapter 5's LegalPart(π)); H1-Multi governs the remaining clauses of Multi, the forward read-back of RunOf being undertaken by the forward side of H6; H1-¬Intr is that ¬Intr(σ, π, r^σ) = 1 on r^σ yields Chapter 5's ¬Intr(σ, π); H1-Sed contains the truth of the provenance of cert\_FQ and cert\_SD; H1-Gov is that the declared K\_k and AdmVar\_k are the same as the host's generation kernel and admissible counterfactual family. Supports: R1, R2, Theorem 7.14. Assigned to: the formal correspondence is the responsibility of further work on this chapter; host-side evidence is to be taken up by further work on Chapter 11, *The Determination Form of Computation*; not yet established within the group. Present state: pending verification.  
　Generative Computation Mathematical Form-H2 (reverse representation)　Content: for each X listed above, Chapter 5's X holding yields that its formal image takes 1 (seven items, written H2-X). ### 8.4　Conjectures and open problems

　(1) Semantic fidelity (Problem 7.20).  
　(2) Transmission of individual-case event consistency to execution duties: when two runs are identical on all four axes, does it follow that their evaluation duties are the same? The rule for the values of Step does not presuppose this (Definition 3.5: evaluation duties are determined by N\_exec), and none of the conclusions of Section Seven depends on this problem.  
　(3) A weaker reliable criterion for DeclEq: a criterion that does not take ⊥ on auxiliary states that read no cross-step content, or on states whose cut differences have already merged through H before reading, while still being free of false 1s.  
　(4) The general unique determination of Chapter 5's H1(a); the general case of H1(c); the equivalence of the bijection of Sed^{(iii)} with Chapter 5's Legal(σ, F).  
　(5) Finite reduction of infinite operand domains, infinite sets of read-in variables and continuous-time models.  
　(6) No determinate value given by M\_Gen is contrary to the literal classification of the same case by Chapter 5. At the known places of possible contrariety — redundant or overdetermining sources of action where the removal families of ▷ are not exhausted, the deployment-coupling kind, and cases where the coverage of the admissible counterfactual family cannot be determined — the definitions all take ⊥; the general form is not proved.  
　(7) The unconditional form of candidate coverage: whether, without depending on the clauses of reverse representation listed in Proposition 7.27, the generated candidate family covers the witnesses of Chapter 5's necessary structure.  
　(8) A general determination procedure for non-tolerance-class acceptance protocols over the set of type completions in Definition 3.7′; a process-level compatibility criterion conditional on the selection event, beyond mechanism compatibility.

### 8.5　Ways of breach

Any of the following constitutes a breach of M\_Gen: some predicate takes two values on the same solving input; some formal predicate gives, on Chapter 5's standard positive cases GS1–GS10, Chapter 5's classification of runs, the paired constructions of Corollary Two or other positive cases, negative cases or boundary cases, a determinate value (0 or 1, not ⊥) contrary to the literal classification of the source text, not obtained through a formal convention carrying a condition; DeclEq takes 0; V\_j takes 0 when some candidate takes ⊥, or when Cand is empty, CovCert has not taken 1 and Π\_gen′ ≠ ∅; some load-bearing structural item of S\_Gen has no correspondent in the correspondence table; the actual properties of some relation disagree with those listed in Section 3.8; a model lacks structure and a predicate takes 0; some theorem, lemma or proposition of Section Seven has a counterexample under the premises listed. Taking the value ⊥, a model's not faithfully characterizing the host, errors in the provenance of certificates, and another physical realization of the same structure do not constitute breaches of M\_Gen. The minimal form of a breach is a finite declared generative model (components, ports, G, F, H, exogenous values, S, Σ\_s, Car, Op, Tmpl, AdmVar and K) on which a determinate value contrary to upstream is computed step by step according to the definitions, or the indication of a missing step in a proof.

## Appendix A　The constructions of the ten items of Example 7.9

Each of the following items adds undeclared components to the host 𝔅 of Example 7.8, the other components and the declaration being unchanged; the classes of reading follow Definition 3.4(1). The holding laws H of all the components added (Q, BAD, Z, PROD, T, A, B, K, PR, MD, KEY, VAL, VLD) are the identity: their states do not change when not written, changing only through events; none of them has an enable port.  
　Notational convention: each item also adds a step-counting component NS: cond GO, cont NS; G = \[GO = 1\]; F = NS + 1; initial value 0; H the identity; NS is declared as part of the step sequencer and belongs to the retention set Ret\_k (Definition 3.12(D7-T)), not entering ℋ\_k. Wherever a component is said below to "write v at step j," that component is: cond GO, NS, cont the elements occurring in v; G = \[GO = 1 ∧ NS = j − 1\]; F = v. This write occurs in the same beat as TX and TW (one time step after initiation) and is evaluated before NS is incremented, so it occurs only once, at step j. The initial value of every added component equals its template value; where no template value is listed, the reset value 0 is taken. The reading components (BAD, PROD, PR) and the rewriting of TX are as listed in each item.  
　(1) Gating auxiliary register: Q writes 1 at step 1, strobed by GO, template value 5; BAD: cond Q, X, G = \[Q = 1 ∧ X = 2\], F = 1; TX gains an additional content port BAD and adds its value. At every time step of step 2, Q ∈ CRd(BAD, t) (a condition read, Definition 3.4(1)), actual value 1. In another variant the trigger value 1 is changed to 7, so BAD never fires in the actual run, E\_BAD(t) = 0, there is no event of BAD, and hence no event read; but Q ∈ CRd(BAD, t) still holds at every time step.  
　(2) Gated taking on of evaluation: initial state W = 2. Z writes \[((W + 1)·M) mod 3 = 0\] = 1 at step 1, template value 0; PROD: cond PH, Z, cont W, M, G = \[PH = 5 ∧ Z = 0\], F = (W·M) mod 3; TX is changed to F = (X + PROD + U) mod 3. In the initiation beat of step 2, Z ∈ CRd(PROD, t), actual value 1; PROD writes in the template run, and does not write in the actual run.  
　(3) Whole-table precomputation: T writes Σ\_{w=0}^{2} ((w·M) mod 3)·3^w = 21 at step 1, template value 99; when T \< 99 and W ≤ 2, TX uses the W-th ternary digit of T in place of W·M. At step 2, T ∈ FRd(TX, t), actual value 21. On W ∈ {0, 1, 2}, the table lookup gives the same value as W·M, so the contents written in the two runs are the same; the difference lies only in the sources of what is read in.  
　(4) Product term precomputed in the previous step: Z writes ((W + 1)·M) mod 3 = 1 at step 1, template value 9; when Z \< 9, TX uses Z in place of W·M. At step 2, Z ∈ FRd(TX, t), actual value 1.  
　(5) Joint precomputation in two registers: A and B each write ((W + 1)·M) mod 3 = 1 at step 1, template value 9 each; when A = B \< 9, TX uses A in place of W·M. At step 2, A, B ∈ FRd(TX, t), actual value 1 each.  
　(6) Initiation beat reading a precomputation from the previous step: K writes ((W + 1)·M) mod 3 = 1 at step 1, template value 9; PR: cond PH, cont K, W, M, G = \[PH = 5\] (in the same beat as initiation), F = K (when K \< 9) or (W·M) mod 3; TX is changed to F = (X + PR + U) mod 3. In the initiation beat of step 2, K ∈ FRd(PR, t), actual value 1. The cut is taken before the earliest event of the initiation part, so this read in the initiation beat lies within the comparison interval.  
　(7) Data spanning two anchors: MD writes W mod 2 = 0 at step 1, template value 0; B writes MD at step 2, strobed by GO, template value 5; TX adds 1 when B = 1. At step 2, MD is read by the F of B, with actual value 0 equal to the template value; B is rewritten only at the same time step at which it is read by TX, so what is read is the cut value 5, equal to the template value; these two places produce no difference. At step 3, B ∈ FRd(TX, t), actual value 0. Neither B = 0 nor B = 5 triggers the addition of 1, so the contents written in the two runs are the same; the difference lies only in the sources of what is read in.  
　(8) Constant auxiliary state with a defect in the successor: Q writes 1 at step 1, template value 5; TX adds 1 when (Q, X) ∈ {(0, 1), (1, 2)}. At step 2, Q ∈ FRd(TX, t), actual value 1.  
　(9) Data-dependent auxiliary state: as (8), with Q writing X mod 2 = 1 at step 1.  
　(10) Undeclared cache: KEY: cond GO, cont X; G = \[GO = 1\]; F = X (recording the X of step k one time step after its initiation). VAL: cond DONE, cont X; G = \[DONE = 1\]; F = X. VLD: cond DONE; G = \[DONE = 1\]; F = 1. After the read-out of step k, VAL and VLD write the result of that step and 1 respectively, the template value of VLD being 0; when VLD = 1 and KEY = X, TX takes VAL as the result, and otherwise evaluates as usual. At step k + 1, the F of TX selects the result by VLD, VLD ∈ FRd(TX, t), actual value 1.
