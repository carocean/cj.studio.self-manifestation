# The Determination Form of Computation

One. Purpose

This chapter is a Determination Form (an auxiliary form, of the inversion-and-identification group). Its use: for determination instances frozen in advance, under a legitimate and operable determination protocol, to invoke the definitions, necessary directions, formal systems and read-back rules already closed in Chapters 2 to 10, and to determine whether four kinds of property — bearing, execution, rule computation relative to a target, and generative computation — hold on an instance; and to prove that a verdict of 0 means the property certainly does not hold (within the domain of determination, carrying the declared conditions), and that where correctness cannot be guaranteed the verdict is ⊥, with the reason written down. In the present upstream state the range of the four verdicts is {0, ⊥} (Theorem Three).

The four kinds of property and the four verdicts are:

　　Det\_Bear(χ) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}: on the declared task τ, whether P bears τ;  
　　Det\_Exec(χ) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}: within the declared period T, whether P executes τ;  
　　Det\_RuleComp\_L(χ) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}: relative to the declared target L, whether P's execution within T is rule computation;  
　　Det\_Gen(χ) ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}: whether the declared run segment σ is generative computation.

The four verdicts answer four different questions; they are not combined into one overall verdict on "whether it is computation," they do not vote on one another, and they do not fill in for one another.

All four kinds of property take the upstream definitions and formal systems, with no constituent items added or removed; the condition ledger has no new H (7.2); the criteria take no qualification of law, Principle, Probability or Intention as input.

Declaration of the domain of determination: determination instances appear as declared models, and the declaration is frozen before the run under determination. Whether an actual host agrees with its declared model and rule text belongs to instantiation and external connection, and is declared as the conditions of the domain of determination, Dom (4.2).

Premises of determination:  
(1) The definitions of the four kinds of property: bearing, execution and generative computation are given by the antecedents or prescriptions of Chapters 2, 3 and 5 respectively; rule computation relative to a target is given by the conjunction of execution with the definition of object rules of Chapter 8, object rules having two readings (3.1).  
(2) The observation set: 3.3.  
(3) Forward relations: bearing, execution and generative computation are given by their respective necessary directions, and object rules are read from the rule text (3.4); the sufficient directions of the three original properties are unanswered in Chapter 2, and are U1 in Chapters 3 and 5.  
"Computing host" has no closed definition upstream (Chapter 2: "Bearing is a host's structural qualification for a task, not a mark of computation"; Chapter 3: "Execution is one exercise of bearing, not a mark of computation"; item 6 of the initial stipulation lists only the two kinds, brain and computing device), and is not a property determined here; for the verdicts on bearing and execution for natural processes and settable matter, see 7.5.

Initial stipulation. This form takes the following stipulations about computation as premises: they are the initial stipulation adopted by this form, are not proved in this form, and do not become proved theorems by being cited. Item numbers are marked when cited:  
　Initial stipulation, item 1: each execution node of rule transformation is a logical rule: it is the rule, at this node, of the law the program expresses, prescribing how the next step goes in order to form this law.  
　Initial stipulation, item 2: skipping the host layer and simulating laws directly by computation necessarily yields result computation.  
　Initial stipulation, item 3: generative computation contains rule computation; rule computation takes place in the host layer of generative computation.  
　Initial stipulation, item 4: rule computation, even if executed node by node on a device for very many steps, does not automatically become generative computation because the number of steps increases.  
　Initial stipulation, item 5: result computation is also called rule computation; the difference between generative computation and result computation lies in whether its transformation is dynamical transformation or rule transformation.  
　Initial stipulation, item 6: relative to non-computing P, the computing P known at present are of two kinds: one is the brain, only when it is exercising its capacity to compute, though this must not be misunderstood as computation driving and generating the dynamics of the brain; the other is the computing device.  
　Initial stipulation, item 7: the difference between simulating a law in P by setting transformation rules and growing a law out of dynamics in the host is the difference between a computer program and the feed-forward network (FFN) in GPT.

Three. Declaration of the Properties and the Observation Set

### 3.1　Properties

　Bear(P, τ): bearing in the sense of Chapter 2.  
　Exec(P, τ, T): execution in the sense of Chapter 3, with bearing as antecedent.  
　GenComp(P, σ): generative computation in the sense of Chapter 5.  
　RuleComp\_L(P, τ, T): rule computation relative to the declared target L,

　　RuleComp\_L(P, τ, T) :⟺ Exec(P, τ, T) ∧ Obj\_L(R\_P)

　R\_P: the rule text executed by P when executing τ within T (Y\_impl, frozen with Δ). Where the rule text is changed within T, determination is made separately on each sub-period in which the rule text is unchanged. A task has the extension of f on its domain as its identity, and algorithm and configuration do not enter the identity of the task (Corollary One of Chapter 2), so being an object rule is a property of the rules executed, not of the task; "their τ being object rules" in Corollary One of Chapter 7 and "a clause in τ\_Φ" in Chapter 8 both refer to the rules executed.  
　Obj\_L(R\_P): R\_P contains an object-rule clause on the declared structural positions, relations or entries of L. Object rules are as defined in 5.5 of Chapter 8: "a clause in τ\_Φ whose expression of action takes as argument the name — not the field state — of some structural position, relation or entry, prescribing or biasing Continuation or sedimentation at that place." The conjunction is given by the upstream texts: Corollary One of Chapter 7, "what is computed out and what is run out are rule computations in which S holds alone, their τ being object rules; the host layer of generative computation is also rule computation, its τ being the basic rules of the host"; Theorem Two (B) of Chapter 9, "whether what is executed is the Trace-Response rule of the host or an object rule can be read from the rule text." The rule computation of the host layer spoken of in item 3 of the initial stipulation is the latter kind, and holds in every generative computation; RuleComp\_L is the former kind, relative to the target L.

　The upstream texts admit two readings of Obj\_L, which diverge on clauses that only bias (the two readings are written Obj\_L^incl and Obj\_L^full; in the program outputs they are marked 含 and 全):  
　　Obj\_L^incl: R\_P has an object-rule clause on L, including those that only bias (the definition in 5.5 of Chapter 8);  
　　Obj\_L^full: R\_P has on L an object rule that wholly determines Continuation there — the clause takes names as arguments, and makes K\_x(D) = K\_x(D′) for all legitimately comparable field states D, D′ (Corollary 4.4 of Chapter 8; "their τ being object rules" in Corollary One of Chapter 7).  
　The final paragraph of Corollary 4.4 states that a clause which only biases "does not cancel the dynamical reading-in at that place," so the two readings differ. Determination is made under both readings in parallel (4.7); where they have the same value, the verdict does not depend on the reading.

　Φ(∅) (the duty of generation of Chapter 7) is not a property under determination: between it and GenComp there is only the conditional correspondence of Theorem Eight of Chapter 8.

### 3.2　Determination instances

χ = (P, Δ, T, σ). P is the host within the declared boundary; T is a period; σ is a run segment of P; Δ is a legitimate declaration: the task τ or one-step task τ\_Φ, reading schemes, delivery protocol (including random components and unspecified choices), operating conditions Env and assessment regime R\_B (Chapter 2: under Env, with external non-proxy), initiation part, enabling conditions, declared period Θ; on the generative side there are in addition the Sedimentation-Field F and its protocol, the division Σ\_s of the extended state and candidate step partitions; on the rule side there are in addition the target L and its declared structural positions, relations and entries. Δ is frozen before the run under determination, and during determination it may not be enlarged, reduced, restricted, changed in domain or declared again.

### 3.3　The observation set

Y = (Y\_decl, Y\_impl, Y\_run, Y\_cov)

　Y\_decl: Δ and its time of declaration.  
　Y\_impl: the port model and the implementation of delivery and read-out; the rule text and its clauses (whether each clause's expression of action takes names or field states as arguments, and whether its effect is to fix or to bias); the classification of state components; the update rule of each component; the sedimentation modes and their objects of action; write channels; coupling declarations.  
　Y\_run: records of run events; exhaustion of the whole family of deliveries and of unspecified choices and random components; the counterfactual with initiation removed; renaming replay.  
　Y\_cov: model adequacy (5.5 of Chapter 4); the consistency check of reading out the rule text; candidate coverage certificates (CovCert of Chapter 6; the certificate records only coverage, the conditions required for the coverage to be true being in C9). Y\_cov decides only whether the determination method can sign off, and is not a constituent item of any kind.

Operand-level trajectories, output sequences or empirical frequencies may not be used as criteria. Grounds: Corollary Three of Chapter 2 (carrying PP), the finite observed trajectory of a single run does not suffice to determine bearing; Lemma One and Theorem One (1) of Chapter 9, a judgement that takes only computation-side obtainable quantities must err on some class of instances; Theorem Six of Chapter 10, the source is not determined by the pointwise records on the host side at the evaluation points, and certifying the source requires obtaining information on construction and temporal position beyond these records (this chapter takes Y\_decl, Y\_impl and Y\_run). Three pairs of instances give comparisons of the same type (Appendix B, item E-2): an adder and a recorder that plays back recorded sums slot by slot give the same output on the observed delivery sequence, the bearing verdict for the former being ⊥ and for the latter 0; a normal calculator and a calculator whose arithmetic part stalls on the second run while the display keeps the previous value have the same input and display sequences, the execution verdict for the former being ⊥ while the latter gives a conditional negative reading 0; F\_LB and τ\_C, which fixes Continuation at a1 and m1, take the same route in all 100 runs under the same field state and the same random source, the verdict on rule computation relative to the target being 0 for the former and ⊥ for the latter.

Four. Construction of the Criteria

### 4.1　Verdicts and discipline

Det\_X(χ) = (v, Code, Cond, H, Read), X ∈ {Bear, Exec, RuleComp\_L, Gen}, v ∈ {1, 0, ⊥}: Code is the path or reason code; Cond, the conditions of the domain of determination used; H, the upstream conditions carried by the path; Read, the structural readings and conditional negative readings.

Six rules of the discipline of determination: a formal reading is not a final verdict; a declaration's failure to qualify is not a structural zero; not having found positive evidence is not a negative verdict; 0 is not signed while coverage is not closed; mismatched evidence does not manufacture 0; conditions propagate only along actual paths.

4.2　The domain of determination and conditions

The domain of determination Dom consists of conditions at the level of the determination method:  
　Dom1　Δ is legitimate and is frozen before the run under determination (time of declaration and version can be checked);  
　Dom2　The declared model is faithful: the five items of model adequacy pass (5.5 of Chapter 4); the declared classification of states, initiation part and run individuals agree with the host; the records of run events cover all run individuals within T;  
　Dom3　Reading out of the rule text is faithful: the table of clauses in Y\_impl agrees with the rules executed by the host;  
　Dom4　The determination protocol Π\_CJ (4.4) is observed.  
None of the items of Dom is an H or a constituent item of any kind. Dom2 takes the matters that clause (a) of Generative Computation General Form-H1 (the unique identification of extended states, variable domains and step partitions; its clause (b) — when pipelined overlapping execution and continuous free running fall within the working domain — is not included here, see the entry condition of 4.8), Computational Execution General Form-H1 (the identification of initiation and enablement) and H2 (the identification of one and the same run) are meant to answer, as conditions of agreement with the declaration; these H are not thereby closed; nor is Dom2 derived from the check of 5.5 of Chapter 4 — passing the check only allows the predicate to give output, and does not imply that there is no unmodelled structure in the host.

The upstream conditions H carried by a path are of two kinds:  
　Implication conditions: the conditions that the necessary direction used carries in its own right. The Ex path carries Computational Execution General Form-H3 (a)(b), its counterfactual branch being taken only under the conditions of C5 (including no overdetermination); the Gov path carries Generative Computation General Form-H2, and carries the conditions required for the per-candidate readings and for the coverage to be true (C9, G-E of 4.8); Sed(ii) under deployment coupling carries Generative Computation General Form-H3; the two clauses D ∉ Pers(F) and configuration constant in item 3 of G-D carry, through the persistent-field-state clause of Sed(i) of Chapter 5, C0 of *The General Form of Law* v2.5 (its upstream status being inheritance pending verification, treated by the rule of final verdict below in the same way as an H not closed).  
　Identification conditions: the formal criteria required to identify the failed item on the instance. The typing of reading schemes carries Computational Bearing General Form-H1, proxy evaluation by reading schemes and in delivery carries H2, and external proxy evaluation carries H3; where the failed item is a direct negation of the corresponding clause by the frozen declaration, implementation or run records, they are not carried (Lemma One and the note after it).

Rule of final verdict: where a path gives 0 and all the H it carries are closed, the final verdict is 0; where some H carried is not closed, the final verdict is ⊥, with reason ⊥\_HOpen, and Read reports the conditional negative reading 0 and the H carried. In the present upstream state, none of Computational Bearing General Form-H1–H3 (pending verification), Computational Execution General Form-H1–H3 (pending verification) and Generative Computation General Form-H1–H3 (pending verification) is closed.

Lemma One (proxy evaluation read from the declaration)　If the frozen implementation defines delivery or read-out as the evaluation of f (the delivery function outputs f(d), or the reading scheme reads a state as the value of f applied to some quantity), then Leg₂ does not hold; if the frozen declaration states that after delivery a process outside the boundary evaluates f and the result depends on this work, then Prod does not hold. Neither carries Computational Bearing General Form-H2 or H3.  
Proof: Chapter 2 delimits Leg₂ by "delivery requires no evaluation of f, read-out requires no application of f," and delimits production in Prod by "neither delivery nor any external process after delivery evaluates by proxy," proxy evaluation being "a link outside the host carries out the evaluation of f, or part of it, and whether the result passes depends on that work." What the premise gives is precisely the negation of these clauses, and the failure follows from the clauses themselves. H2 and H3 are the formal criteria of these clauses, used to make an identification where it is not stated directly (whether the encoding shares part of the work of evaluating f, whether an external action evaluates); any acceptable criterion must give the same conclusion where the clause is directly negated. □  
The classification of "the wire with a reading scheme that contains the answer" and "the proxy-evaluating black box" in Section Eleven of Chapter 2 is this case.  
Note after Lemma One (the general form of direct negation)　The argument of Lemma One is not restricted to Leg₂ and Prod: where the frozen declaration, implementation or run records directly give the textual negation of some necessary clause, the failure follows from the clause itself, without carrying the formal criterion used to identify that clause where it is not stated directly — any acceptable criterion must give the same conclusion where the clause is directly negated. The places at which this chapter accordingly carries no H are, one by one: B1, the reading scheme being listed run by run or typed by the records of the run under determination, which is the textual negation of "before, and independently of, the runs under test" in Leg₁ (the stone under run-by-run reading schemes of Section Eleven of Chapter 2), carrying no Computational Bearing General Form-H1; B2 and B3, that is, Lemma One, carrying no H2 or H3; B5, random components and unspecified choices being listed separately and exhausted one by one according to the delivery protocol of the frozen declaration, the distinction between the two being given directly by the declaration, carrying no formal distinction between the two in H2; ¬Sub in X2 and G-C, evaluation outside the boundary being read directly from the run records or the declaration, carrying no Computational Bearing General Form-H2 or H3. Where identification must be made by a formal criterion these paths are not taken: where whether the typing depends on the run under determination must be identified by H1, B1 is not taken; where proxy evaluation must be identified by H2 or H3, it falls under B2′ or B3′.

### 4.3　Order of determination and entry

Order of determination: bearing → execution → rule computation relative to a target; bearing on τ\_Φ → generative computation. Where the entry is 0, the downstream is 0 (Theorem One). An entry of ⊥ does not block the downstream from signing 0: the negative paths downstream are obtained by argument by cases, and do not presuppose that the entry is judged 1 (Theorem Two).

### 4.4　The determination protocol Π\_CJ

　C1　Freezing and version: Δ and the implementation are frozen before determination and their version recorded; where they are changed during determination, the verdict is void.  
　C2　Acceptable declaration: where there is no legitimate declaration, or the time of declaration is not earlier than the run under determination, the instance is not within Dom1, and the verdict is ⊥\_D; a declaration's failure to qualify does not manufacture 0.  
　C3　Model adequacy: the five items of 5.5 of Chapter 4; where they do not pass, the relevant verdicts are ⊥\_M.  
　C4　Exhaustion: on a finite declared model, the family of results is exhausted operand by operand, unspecified choice by unspecified choice, random component by random component; the executability of the delivery protocol is checked operand by operand.  
　C5　The counterfactual with initiation removed: obtained under the conditions of Theorem 7.4 of Chapter 4 (discrete time steps, NB\_r(W), deterministic dynamics, events only removed and not generated), where it has the same value as the counterfactual condition of Chapter 3; in addition there must be no overdetermination — no event in r that is not caused by the present initiation and independently triggers the transforming part or its data-carrying part. Chapter 3 delimits driving by actual cause, and "if there were no initiation on this occasion, the evolution would be different" coincides with it only when there is no other independent trigger in r; where two routes of triggering both actually set that part going (redundancy), removing the present initiation alone can leave the evolution as it was, while the present initiation can still be a driver (the Preliminary Distinctions ("driving") and Section Six of Chapter 3; the note after Theorem 7.4 of Chapter 4). NB\_r(W) excludes preemption, not overdetermination; whether there is overdetermination is read from the declared model and the records of run events. The two clauses together are the conditions of application of the counterfactual criterion of Chapter 3; the general criterion under redundancy and preemption (Computational Execution General Form-H3 (c)) is not used in this chapter. Outside these conditions, or where overdetermination is read off or it cannot be read off whether there is overdetermination, this path is not taken.  
　C6　Binding to one and the same run: Ini, Run and Out are checked on one and the same run individual; on the generative side, execution, field-state transformation and carry-over are checked by the records of events of one and the same run (the host-side evidence of H6 of Chapter 6); the comparison projections π\_cmp at the two places of comparison are the same (for π\_cmp see the fifth item of Section One of Chapter 7, and C₈ of Chapter 8).  
　C7　Reading out the rule text and renaming replay: the arguments of the expression of action are read out clause by clause; replay is carried out under an automorphism ψ of the medium, and the Continuation at x is compared with the image of the original Continuation (4.7).  
　C8　Failed items independent of the step partition: items 3–5 of Part Five of the Appendix (Formalization) of Chapter 5 are read from the implementation and the run records.  
　C9　Coverage: a failure that depends on the step partition must hold for all legitimate π and r in the sense of Chapter 5, and the candidate family examined must cover them. Entering this path requires one of two things: the coverage certificate of Chapter 6, CovCert = 1, or Cand ⊇ Cand\_gen (Definition 3.3 (3) and Proposition 7.25 of Chapter 6). Both give only formal coverage: CovCert is read according to the strength of its source (R7 of Chapter 6), and its being true is Generative Computation Mathematical Form-H3; Cand ⊇ Cand\_gen is only the exhaustion of formally legitimate partitions, and coverage of the witnesses of Chapter 5 requires (a)–(d) of Proposition 7.27 of Chapter 6. The conditions required for the coverage to be true are carried along with the path (G-E, G-F, 7.2), and are not conditions of the domain of determination; where neither is present, this path is not taken.  
The consequences of the failure of each item go into Section Six.

### 4.7　Det\_RuleComp\_L

For ∗ ∈ {incl, full}, determine Det^∗ once each:  
　R1　Det\_Exec = 0 ⇒ Det^∗ = 0, with the conditions of the execution branch.  
　R2　Obj\_L^∗ does not hold ⇒ Det^∗ = 0, carrying Dom3: R\_P has, on the declared structural positions, relations and entries of L, no clause taking their names as arguments (∗ = incl), or no clause taking names as arguments and wholly determining Continuation there (∗ = full).  
　R3　Obj\_L^∗ holds while the execution branch has not judged 0 ⇒ Det^∗ = ⊥ (⊥\_ExecSuff), Read reporting the clauses seen and the renaming witness.  
Consistency check: if R\_P is read as having no clause at all taking names as arguments on L, while renaming replay mismatches at some position of L, both Det^incl and Det^full are judged ⊥ (⊥\_Cov: the reading of the text disagrees with the run).  
Final verdict: where Det^incl = Det^full, that value is taken; where they differ, the verdict is ⊥ (⊥\_Reading), Read listing the values of the two readings side by side. The two readings differ only when L has only biasing clauses: then Det^full = 0 and Det^incl is ⊥ or 0.

Lemma Two (renaming mismatch is a witness of object rules)　Let ψ be an automorphism of the medium, with x ∈ L a fixed point of ψ; apply ψ to the field state and the history, and replay the same rules. If the expression of action of R\_P at x takes only the field state (together with the structure of the medium) as arguments, then after renaming the Continuation distribution at x equals the image under ψ of the original Continuation distribution. Hence, where after renaming the two are unequal, R\_P has at x a clause taking names as arguments.  
Proof: for an expression of action that reads only the field state and the structure of the medium, its value transforms together with its arguments under automorphisms of the medium, and the Continuation distribution transforms accordingly (the argument of Corollary 4.1 of Chapter 8 does not use the other conditions of 𝔗, only that the expression of action contains no names). The result follows by contraposition. □

Five. Proofs of the Validity of the Criteria

### 5.1　Entry

Theorem One (entry)　For any determination instance:  
(i) Exec(P, τ, T) ⇒ Bear(P, τ);  
(ii) RuleComp\_L(P, τ, T) ⇒ Exec(P, τ, T);  
(iii) GenComp(P, σ) ⇒ Bear(P, τ\_Φ), and each one-step transformation of the extended state in σ is one execution of τ\_Φ by P.

Six. Declaration of Misjudgement Bounds

No statistical rate of misjudgement is introduced. The discipline of determination is: where correctness cannot be guaranteed, fall back to ⊥.

The domain of negative verdicts: by Theorem Two, misjudgement of a final verdict 0 within Dom is zero; misjudgement of a conditional negative reading 0 is zero when the H carried holds.

The domain of positive verdicts: empty (Theorem Three).

Where the two cannot both be secured:  
(1) The positive domain of determination is empty. The necessary directions given upstream do not imply that the four kinds hold (the sufficient direction of bearing is unanswered in Chapter 2, those of execution and generative computation are U1 of Chapters 3 and 5, and rule computation relative to a target has execution as a conjunct). The standard positive cases S1–S13 and GS1–GS10 are descriptions of cases, give no sufficient criterion from observable evidence to the holding of the kind, and take bearing as antecedent (Corollary Three of Chapter 3: "the general formula does not yield that it is an execution"; Chapter 5: "The standard positive cases are used only as anchors for testing the domain of application and overstrong conditions"); in determination they serve only as regression anchors.  
(2) Negative incompleteness. Outside the subdomain of Theorem Five, instances of ¬X can be judged ⊥. Those that can be named: instances failing model adequacy (⊥\_M) or without legitimate declaration (⊥\_D); the two cases of U2 of Chapter 3 (classification undetermined); reading schemes whose encoding shares part of the work of evaluating f (⊥\_H2); negative verdicts of the Ex path (⊥\_HOpen, carrying Computational Execution General Form-H3 (a)(b)); overdetermined runs (⊥\_Cov, C5); σ not within the working domain of Chapter 5, or whose working domain cannot be read off (⊥\_Cov, 4.8); failures of generative computation depending on the step partition (⊥\_Cov); the Gov branch (carrying Generative Computation General Form-H2, Generative Computation Mathematical Form-H2-Gov and the conditions for coverage to be true); deployment coupling (⊥\_H3).  
(3) Misjudgement caused by failure of the protocol. Declared model disagreeing with the host (failure of Dom2): passing the check of 5.5 of Chapter 4 does not imply that there is no unmodelled structure in the host, and an unmodelled channel can make the 0 of B4 or B5 wrong. Rule text disagreeing with execution (failure of Dom3): renaming replay can expose it only when there is a mismatch, and symmetric naming clauses can make the 0 of R2 wrong. The counterfactual with initiation removed may differ in value from that of Chapter 3 outside the conditions of Theorem 7.4 of Chapter 4 (Example 7.5′); under overdetermination, even where the two have the same value, it does not coincide with the driving of Chapter 3; in both cases C5 does not take this path, and overdetermination left unread because the declared model or the event records are incomplete belongs to failure of Dom2. The coverage certificate or Cand ⊇ Cand\_gen gives only formal coverage (C9), the conditions required for coverage to be true being carried with G-E and G-F, and while they are not closed these two paths have final verdict ⊥\_HOpen. Where the declaration is changed during determination (failure of C1), the verdict is void. What these failures breach are the corresponding conditions of the domain of determination, not the Determination Theorem.  
(4) Where the reading is undetermined. On object rules that only bias, the two readings of Obj\_L differ, Det\_RuleComp\_L is judged ⊥\_Reading, and the values of the two readings are listed side by side in Read.

The real boundary of determination is the determinable domain and the conditions carried, not a statistical confidence interval.

(ii) the range of the four verdicts is {0, ⊥} (Theorem Three);

(iv) on the subdomain listed in Theorem Five, negative completeness holds;

    def Decay(self):

        self.t_sed+=1
