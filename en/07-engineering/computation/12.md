# The Distinction Form of Computing and Non-Computing Hosts

## Section One　The Single Task and the Form-Kind

### 1.1 The single task

Are computing hosts and non-computing hosts merely two names for one and the same kind of host?

What is to be proved is not that the two are defined in different words, but that there exists a legitimate, executable common observation specification q such that a qualified host satisfying C, connected to it, can read out only 1, and a qualified host satisfying N, connected to it, can read out only 0, the two sets of read-outs being disjoint. The read-out here is the theoretical read-out over all admissible results of the declared model; the actual read-out given by one actual run agrees with it under the condition that the model conforms to the real thing (9.6).

　　Out\_C(q) ∩ Out\_N(q) = ∅

The transformations in both kinds of host are Trace-Response between signal and medium. The difference between the two lies in where the Trace-Response relation comes from: the Trace-Response relation of a computing host is set by mathematical expressions, while the Trace-Response relation of a non-computing host is the very interaction of matter in the medium with the signal. This chapter proves that this difference can be read out by one observation, while the passive observations listed in Section Seven, which look only at bearing, output, medium and records, do not suffice to read it out in general.

The "computing host" and "non-computing host" here refer to the two candidates C and N of Sections Four and Five. C characterizes hosts whose Trace-Response relation can be set anew according to mathematical expressions; N characterizes hosts that have no such route of setting, expressions serving only as description. They are read from the initial stipulation (2.3), but this chapter has not proved that every computing host spoken of in the initial stipulation satisfies C and every non-computing host satisfies N: a hard-wired device that can realize only one task and must be manufactured anew to be reset lies outside the observable range of C (12.3 (7)(8)). So the general theorem is a distinction between these two candidates, not a classification of all hosts.

### 1.2 What is distinguished, and why

The initial stipulation divides the reproduction of law in a host into two routes: in a computing host, dynamics occurs when the signal flow passes and a computational transformation is really carried out through the rules; in a non-computing host, dynamics occurs when the signal flow passes through the host medium and interacts with matter in the medium in Trace-Response (initial stipulation, item 5). Both routes lead to the dynamics layer: these motions all really come from the host — if the host is a computing device, from computation; if it is non-computing matter, from that matter (initial stipulation, item 3); the Sedimentation-Field need not depend on computation to be realized (initial stipulation, item 2).

So what this chapter distinguishes is two sources of implementation of dynamics at the host layer, characterized by the two candidates C and N of Sections Four and Five (whether the two can fully represent these two sources, see 12.3 (8)). It does not classify devices, nor does it hold that either source is closer to real dynamics. The qualifications of the dynamics layer and the object layer are obtained by their own conditions of holding, not decided by the kind of host; this relation of levels is given by the separation and non-skipping of layers in *The General Form of Computational Layering* and by the dissolution of cross-layer misplacement in *The Dissolution Form of Cross-Layer Misplacement in Computation*, cited by this chapter in 10.2, 10.9 and Section Fourteen. The theorems of this chapter do not take as a premise that a dynamics layer has already formed on a non-computing host.

### 1.3 Form-kind

The Distinction Form, an auxiliary form, standing as a text of its own (Canon Chapter 1 v2.2, Part Four, Chapter Twelve). The purpose of the Distinction Form is to prove that an observation exists which separates two candidate structures; its entry conditions are that the definitions of the two candidates are already separately closed and that the forward mappings of both candidates are already given (the forward mappings of this chapter are written under the common observation specification, and the two sets of read-outs are given by them, see 8.5); its standard composition is declaration of the candidate pair, construction of the distinguishing observation, proof of distinguishing validity, distinction theorem, interfaces; its completion conditions are that the distinguishing observation is explicitly written and its proof of validity completed.

The Distinction Form answers only whether there is an observation that can separate the two; giving a verdict of "computing" or "non-computing" on an arbitrary host is the task of a Determination Form, to be taken up by further work on *The Determination Form of Computation*, and is not yet established within the group (12.3 (1)). Theorem One of Section Seven is the argument from the obstacle to distinguishing observation: it holds for each observation in the passive observation set 𝒴₀, showing by the witness pair of 7.1 that these observations do not suffice to separate the two candidates in general, so that a distinguishing observation must go beyond 𝒴₀. It does not by itself bear the completion conditions, does not study bounds on the extension of observation sets, and draws no conclusion about observations outside 𝒴₀; research on indistinguishability at the effect level on general observation sets belongs to the original plan and is not yet established within the group.

### 1.4 Results

Below is a summary of the results of this chapter; the formal statements and proofs are in Sections Seven and Nine.

Theorem One: on the six passive observations — the bearing verdict, the acceptance verdict, whether the medium is continuous, the material of the Trace-Response elements, expression records and their declared claims, and whether it is set up for evaluation — there exist a computing host and a non-computing host whose outputs are identical.

Theorems Two to Four: under the common antecedent K0 (3.3), completeness of declaration (W1), freezing of the operating conditions (W2), the signals delivered to the host being kept unchanged (Y5), the initial running states of the two compared rounds being obtained by the same initialization protocol (Y6) and the other conditions of 𝒴^adm, when the expression records are reset once — rewriting the records, rerunning the setting programs, and seeing by the frozen protocol which expression the output conforms to — a host satisfying C (with τ′ ∈ 𝒯) necessarily reads out 1, and a host satisfying N necessarily reads out 0. The verdict is made over all admissible results in the declared model, not over a particular sampled run. So there exists a common observation specification q with Out\_C(q) = {1}, Out\_N(q) = {0}, the two being disjoint.

Corollary Two: for observations in 𝒴^adm, under the condition that the actual output falls within the set of admissible results of the declared model (W3), when one actual intervention is made on the frozen test deliveries, the verdict given by the actual output agrees with the theoretical verdict above: a host satisfying C reads out 1, and a host satisfying N reads out 0. W3 is a declared condition of connection, not proved by finitely many experiments; one actual observation cannot by itself certify the robust qualification of an unknown host.

In one sentence: both kinds of host are Trace-Response between signal and medium; within the two candidates C and N and the working domain 𝒴^adm (including W1 and W2, together with the common antecedent K0; on the side of C, τ′ ∈ 𝒯 being required in addition), a host that changes accordingly when its expression is rewritten belongs to C, its Trace-Response relation being set by the expression; a host that does not change when its expression is rewritten belongs to N, its Trace-Response relation being the interaction of matter itself. Whether the two candidates can represent the two kinds of host has not been proved (12.3 (8)).

### 2.2 Initial stipulation

This form takes the following stipulations about computation as premises: they are the initial stipulation adopted by this form, are not proved in this form, and do not become proved theorems by being cited. Item numbers are marked when cited:

　Initial stipulation, item 1: the essence of computation is a process proceeding from input through state, transformation and state again, in turn, until output; computation is restricted to mathematical computation driven by a device.  
　Initial stipulation, item 2: computation drives the dynamics of the Sedimentation-Field, and the dynamics of the Sedimentation-Field generates laws and Principles; this is layering; the Sedimentation-Field need not depend on computation to be realized.  
　Initial stipulation, item 3: the motions of the dynamics layer and the object layer all really come from the host: if the host is a computing device, from computation; if it is non-computing matter, from that matter.  
　Initial stipulation, item 4: directly changing channels or rewriting weights from outside history, not through the signal flow, is intrusion.  
　Initial stipulation, item 5: the occurrence of dynamics is of two kinds: in a computing host, dynamics occurs when the signal flow passes and a computational transformation is really carried out through the rules (former clause); in a non-computing host, dynamics occurs when the signal flow passes through the host medium and interacts with matter in the medium in Trace-Response (latter clause). Host rules are the Trace-Response relation between signal and host medium.  
　Initial stipulation, item 6: relative to non-computing P, the computing P known at present are of two kinds: one is the brain, only when it is exercising its capacity to compute, though this must not be misunderstood as computation driving and generating the dynamics of the brain; the other is the computing device.  
　Initial stipulation, item 7: reproduction can also take place without computation: for example, reproducing a vortex with the same kind of matter and environment; what is used for reproduction need not be the same kind of matter and environment, and with different kinds too there must be examples of successful reproduction.  
　Initial stipulation, item 8: computation, thinking and reasoning are in essence one and the same thing, all proceeding from input through state, transformation and state until output, that is, the running of a law in a host; the difference is that computation is restricted to devices of mathematical computation, while the rest are dynamical fields driven by other matter.  
　Initial stipulation, item 9: what an artificial neural network simulates is the Trace-Response action between signal flow and medium, merely mathematized into rules and handed to a computing device for processing; the host of generative computation is in essence still how Trace-Response occurs between signal flow and medium.  
　Initial stipulation, item 10: form and mathematics prescribe the holding and proof of structures, relations and transformations: structures, relations and transformations are extracted from concrete material (phenomena), in order that they can also be reproduced in other material, or approach reproduction after denoising.

2.3 Two things read from the initial stipulation

First, the common basis. Items 5 and 9 of the initial stipulation show that the transformations in computing hosts and in non-computing hosts are both Trace-Response between signal and host medium.

Second, where the difference lies. A computing host mathematizes phenomena into rules, and the device transforms according to the rules (initial stipulation, item 1: computation is restricted to mathematical computation driven by a device; item 8: computation is restricted to devices of mathematical computation; item 9: mathematized into rules and handed to a computing device for processing); the transformation of a non-computing host is the interaction of the signal with matter in the medium, driven by the dynamics of matter (the latter clause of item 5; item 8: dynamical fields driven by other matter). Item 10 of the initial stipulation gives the two directions between mathematical expressions and phenomena: extracting expressions from phenomena; reproducing in other material by expressions.

So, in the initial stipulation, the distinction between computing and non-computing falls on the origin of the Trace-Response relation: whether the Trace-Response relation is set by mathematical expressions. The two candidates of Sections Four and Five are these two cases.

### 2.4 Outside the working domain

Whether the dynamics layer and the object layer above the host are computation; the qualifications of law, Principle, Probability and Intention in the host; whether a reproduction is distorted; to which kind an arbitrary host belongs; whether computation holds on a given event — these all lie outside the working domain, and the theorems of this chapter make no assertion about them.

## Section Three　Notation and the Common Antecedent

### 3.1 The host system

　　𝒮 = (P, m, Env, Π, ℛ, 𝒫)

P: the host.  
m: the composition and arrangement of the medium, including every component inside P from the input port to the read-out; it is the configuration of the host.  
s₀: the running state of the medium at the start of a test run, for example the initial charge on a capacitor. It differs from the configuration m: setting programs change the configuration, while the initial running state is obtained by the declared initialization protocol at the start of each run.  
Env: the declared operating conditions (Chapter 2).  
Π: the declared delivery and reading-scheme protocol (corresponding to delivery and reading schemes in Prov and Leg of Chapter 2). The Π of this chapter is not the acceptance protocol Π in the tasks of Chapter 2; the acceptance protocol is written Acc\_τ in this chapter (3.4). Delivery means the physical signal sent to the input port of P according to Π; the correspondence from a test delivery to this signal is written as the delivery mapping Δ\_Π.  
ℛ: the expression records within the declared boundary, that is, records carrying mathematical expressions in any form: program texts, wiring lists, circuit designs, instructions, notes.  
𝒫: the programs within the declared boundary that take records in ℛ as input; each program is annotated with its object of action — acting on m, or acting only on delivery.

### 3.2 Host rules

k\_P: the host rules of P, that is, the Trace-Response relation between signal and host medium (initial stipulation, item 5).

### 3.3 The common antecedent K0

Given Π and Env, the set of admissible results 𝓜(m, s₀, u) of P for one delivery u is determined by the configuration m, the initial running state s₀ and this delivery; 𝓜 is non-empty; after delivery, the result is not evaluated by proxy outside the declared boundary. Where the results of the host are random, the distribution of results on 𝓜 is likewise determined by these three. The result obtained in one actual run is an element of 𝓜.

Grounds: items 5 and 9 of the initial stipulation — the transformations in both kinds of host are Trace-Response between signal and medium, that is, the action of the medium on the signal under the operating conditions. The clause "no external proxy evaluation after delivery" has the same meaning as the clause of non-proxy in Prod of Chapter 2; this chapter takes it as a declared condition, not derived from bearing: the observation package of this chapter requires only robust acceptance (Y3), not bearing. K0 is the common antecedent of the declared models of this chapter, taken alike for both candidates; when it does not hold, what fails is the antecedent (Section Thirteen).

### 3.4 Task and acceptance

The task τ takes the definition of Chapter 2; ext(τ) is the extension of f on D (Corollary One of Chapter 2). For a finite sequence of test deliveries D\_test = (u₁, …, u\_k), a result family μ = (y₁, …, y\_k) is the sequence formed by taking one result on each delivery; Acc\_τ(μ) ∈ {1, 0} is the verdict given by the acceptance protocol of τ on this whole sequence of results.

The set of admissible result families: 𝓜(𝒮; D\_test) = 𝓜(m, s₀, u₁) × … × 𝓜(m, s₀, u\_k), that is, all the result families that can be formed by taking any result from the set of admissible results on each delivery.

Robust acceptance: Acc\*\_τ(𝒮; D\_test) = 1 if and only if every result family in 𝓜(𝒮; D\_test) satisfies Acc\_τ; otherwise 0.

Three levels are distinguished here: the distribution of results, the admissible result families and the actually sampled results. The verdicts of this chapter are all made at the level of admissible result families — robust acceptance holds for all admissible result families, so whichever result an actual run happens to draw, that result is accepted. That finitely many actual runs happen to be accepted does not amount to robust acceptance, and cannot stand in for it.

## Section Four　Candidate One: The Computing Host

### 4.1 Definition

　　C(𝒮; τ, 𝒯) ≔ C1 ∧ C2 ∧ C3

C1 (mathematized rules)　𝒯 is a set of tasks, τ ∈ 𝒯; every member shares with τ the delivery and reading-scheme types of Π; the extensions of the members are pairwise distinct; every member is given in the form of an expression record.

C2 (setting entry)　𝒫 contains programs that read the records of members of 𝒯 and act on m before delivery. These programs together are written Set.

C3 (transformation according to rules)　For every τ′ in 𝒯: after running Set with the record of τ′, under Env and Π, starting from the initial running state obtained by the declared initialization protocol, P robustly accepts τ′, that is, Acc\*\_τ′ = 1: all admissible result families satisfy Acc\_τ′. That one or finitely many runs happen to be accepted does not satisfy C3.

### 4.2 Reading

The expression comes before the host rules, and determines the host rules through the setting entry:

　　expression → m → k\_P

C1 is taken from items 10 and 1 of the initial stipulation: the rules are mathematical structures, relations and transformations extracted from phenomena, and what the host does is mathematical computation. C2 and C3 are taken from item 5 of the initial stipulation (a computational transformation really carried out through the rules), item 8 (computation restricted to devices of mathematical computation) and item 9 (mathematized into rules and handed to a computing device for processing): "the device transforms according to the rules," applied to the host system, means that there is an entry that takes rules as input and determines the medium, and that after setting by it the output of the host conforms to that rule. Set takes records as input, so it can be executed for every member of 𝒯.

### 4.3 Forms of the setting entry

Programmable devices: Set is the loading program; when the program is stored in memory, the record is at the same time in m, and loading is setting.  
Analogue computing apparatus: Set is wiring according to the wiring list and selecting components.  
Hard-wired devices: a hard-wired device that can realize only one task, whose task can be changed only by manufacturing it anew, does not necessarily satisfy C2. If the declared host system contains a manufacturing or setting program that configures the medium according to expression records, and this program satisfies C2, it can satisfy the definition of C; if what is declared is a single device already made, without such a setting entry, it does not belong to C. In either case its task family has only one member, so it does not satisfy the condition of readability τ′ ∈ 𝒯 on the side of C in 8.4, and the resetting observation of this chapter does not cover it (12.3 (7)). Whether it is a computing host in the sense of the initial stipulation is left to 12.3 (8). A device that can be rewired in place according to records belongs to the class of analogue computing apparatus above.  
The brain when exercising its capacity to compute (initial stipulation, item 6): Set is reading or learning the rules of operation; 𝒯 consists of the rules by which it can operate. A person doing mental arithmetic reads in an instruction and sets their own operation according to it; once the instruction is rewritten, their results conform accordingly to the new expression. The ionic and neurotransmitter dynamics of the brain itself are not set through expressions, and belong to the side of Section Five. This agrees with item 6 of the initial stipulation (the brain is a computing P only when exercising its capacity to compute).

4.4 The candidates are defined at the host layer

Both candidates are defined by the origin of the host rules k\_P, not by whether there is a target dynamical process above the host. If the candidates were defined as "hosts that drive a target dynamical process through computational execution" and "hosts that drive a target dynamical process through material interaction," then to judge which candidate a host belongs to, one would first have to judge whether there is a target dynamical process on it and what drives it, and the candidates would use the qualification of the dynamics layer (contrary to 6.3); moreover, computing devices that bear mathematical tasks but generate no dynamics layer would fall outside both candidates, whereas the initial stipulation lists computing devices as computing P (initial stipulation, item 6), without making it a condition that they generate dynamics. The two sources of dynamics spoken of in item 5 of the initial stipulation differ precisely in the origin of the host rules; the dynamics layer connects upward from here (initial stipulation, item 3), and need not be written into the definition.

## Section Five　Candidate Two: The Non-Computing Host

### 5.1 Definition

　　N(𝒮) ≔ N1 ∧ N2

N1 (material Trace-Response)　k\_P is the interaction of matter in the medium with the signal, given by m under Env (the latter clause of item 5 of the initial stipulation).

N2 (no setting entry)　𝒫 contains no program acting on m; if there are expression records in ℛ, they are only descriptions of P — extracted from the phenomena of P, or written about P (the direction of extraction of item 10 of the initial stipulation) — and are not in the part of m that determines k\_P. There may be programs that read records and act only on delivery, but the signals they send to the input port of P do not change with the content of the records (for example, programs that only record, time or trigger).

### 5.2 Reading

The phenomenon comes before the expression:

　　k\_P → expression (if any)

There is no route from the expression to k\_P. The latter clause of item 5 of the initial stipulation, the dynamical fields driven by other matter spoken of in item 8, and the reproduction with the same kind of matter and environment in item 7 all belong to this class: the transformation is given by the dynamics of matter itself.

## Section Six　The Two Candidates Are Each Closed

### 6.1 What each reads

C reads ℛ, 𝒫, the object of action of Set and the admissible result families after setting by Set. N reads the objects of action of 𝒫 and the standing of the records in ℛ.

### 6.2 Each has a determinate truth value

On 𝒮 and 𝒯 whose declaration has been frozen, the three clauses of C and the two clauses of N all have determinate truth values.

### 6.3 What each does not use

Neither candidate uses the name "computing host," uses the other candidate, uses any qualification of the dynamics layer or the object layer, or uses any reading of law, Principle, Probability or Intention.

### 6.4 Hence the candidates are closed

By 6.1 to 6.3, the definitions of the two candidates are already separately closed.

6.5 Mutual exclusion in definition is not observable separation

C2 requires that 𝒫 contain a program acting on m, and N2 excludes such programs, so the two are mutually exclusive in definition. Yet two mutually exclusive definitions can still have the same outputs on all passive observations (Theorem One of Section Seven). Whether they can be separated by observation requires a separate proof.

### 6.6 Neither ranks above the other

The two are two origins of the Trace-Response relation, and also two sources of implementation of dynamics. From the distinction it does not follow that either is more fundamental, stronger or more real.

## Section Seven　The Obstacle: Passive Observations Cannot Separate the Two

### 7.1 The witness pair

W\_C: an operational-amplifier feedback network whose feedback element is selected by a wiring program according to an expression record: when the record is integration, a capacitor C₀ is connected; when the record is proportion, a resistor R₀ is connected. The delivery is a current I(t), with amplitude and time window as declared; the read-out is the output voltage. ℛ contains the wiring list and the task expressions; 𝒫 contains the wiring program that selects and connects the feedback element according to the expression, and it acts on m.

　　𝒯 = { τ\_int, τ\_prop },　τ\_int : V(T) = (1/C₀)·∫₀ᵀ I dt,　τ\_prop : V(T) = R₀·I(T)

The operators of the two tasks differ — one is integration, the other proportion — not merely the parameters of one and the same operator.

W\_N: a bare capacitor of capacitance C₀, not selected according to any expression, charged by a current source, its terminal voltage read by a voltmeter. ℛ contains a note "V = (1/C₀)·∫ I dt," a description of how it charges; 𝒫 includes only connection, delivery and reading, and does not act on the capacitor.

The declared initialization protocol ι of the two is the same: before each delivery begins, the energy-storing element connected in the feedback network or at the read-out end that bears the output is discharged to 0 V. In W\_N and in W\_C in the integration configuration this element is the capacitor C₀; in W\_C in the proportion configuration the feedback element is the resistor R₀, which has no energy-storing state bearing the output, so ι has no effect on it, and the initial running state is determined by the proportion configuration itself. The declared operating conditions of both are taken as a bounded-error model: leakage makes the relative error of the integrated output no greater than 10⁻³, and the absolute value of the sum of offset and read-out noise is no greater than 0.005 V. The declarations of both are frozen before the test runs (Dom1 and C2 of *The Determination Form of Computation*), and the declared models of both are taken as passing the check of model adequacy cited by C3 of that form (5.5 of Chapter 4); the bearing verdict of 7.3 (ii) is made under these conditions of the domain of determination.

W\_C satisfies C (for τ\_int and 𝒯): C1 is given by the way 𝒯 is written; C2 by the wiring program; C3 by the output of the feedback network under the declared operating conditions — when the feedback element is the capacitor C₀, the output is (1/C₀)·∫ I dt; when it is the resistor R₀, the output is R₀·I(T).

W\_N satisfies N: the terminal voltage is given by the polarization of the dielectric and the accumulation of charge (N1); 𝒫 does not act on the capacitor, and the note is only a description (N2).

### 7.2 The passive observation set 𝒴₀

(i) the robust acceptance verdict Acc\*\_τint under the frozen protocol of τ\_int;  
(ii) the bearing verdict Det\_Bear of *The Determination Form of Computation* on τ\_int;  
(iii) whether the medium is continuous;  
(iv) what material the Trace-Response element bearing the integration is made of;  
(v) whether there are expression records, the content of the records, and the declared claims attached;  
(vi) whether the apparatus is set up in order to evaluate f.

7.3 Theorem One (passive observations cannot separate)

For each observation in 𝒴₀, the outputs of W\_C and W\_N are the same. Hence no observation in 𝒴₀ can separate C from N.

Proof.  
(i) Under the declared operating conditions, all admissible outputs of both give V(T) = (1/C₀)·∫ I dt within the tolerance (for the error bound see 9.2), so Acc\*\_τint = 1.  
(ii) Neither host's deliveries contain an evaluation of f; the reading schemes of both are fixed readings of voltage, applying no f at read-out; the entry is reachable; none of the cases of constant reading schemes, recording and playback, or sending out the result directly at delivery occurs. None of the negative criteria B1–B6 of *The Determination Form of Computation* is triggered; the reading schemes are direct readings of voltage, involve no encoding-borne evaluation, and do not fall into ⊥\_H2; both lie within the domain of determination (7.1), and do not fall into ⊥\_D or ⊥\_M; the sufficient direction of bearing is unanswered in Chapter 2, so within the domain of determination the bearing verdicts of both are ⊥\_Suff.  
(iii) The media of both are continuous.  
(iv) In both, the element bearing the integration is the dielectric of a capacitor.  
(v) The ℛ of both contains the expression of τ\_int. If the side of W\_N additionally attaches the declared claim "𝒯 can be set," then reading only the records and claims, its output is the same as that of W\_C; whether the claim is true cannot be seen by reading alone.  
(vi) Both are set up in order to obtain ∫ I dt. The reproduction with the same kind of matter and environment in item 7 of the initial stipulation is likewise set up intentionally.  
All six are the same. This completes the proof.

### 7.4 Reading back

Chapter 2 says "Bearing is a host's structural qualification for a task, not a mark of computation"; the half "not a mark of computation" is exemplified on one and the same task by a witness pair: the two have the same passive observations on the side of bearing (7.3 (ii)), while one is a computing host and the other a non-computing host. Continuous versus discrete is not the boundary: the continuous feedback network is a computing host. Material is not the boundary: the same dielectric bears the integration on both sides. Whether an expression can be written is not the boundary: the charging of the bare capacitor can be fully described by an expression. Whether it is intentionally set up is not the boundary either.

Item 6 of the initial stipulation gives another group of cases with the same medium: the brain when exercising its capacity to compute and the brain's own ionic dynamics have the same medium.

Theorem One shows that the passive observations listed are not sufficient to be a general distinguisher of the two candidates — on this witness pair they cannot separate the two. It does not show that there is no particular pair of hosts on which these observations can separate the two.

## Section Eight　The Resetting Observation

8.1 The common observation specification and the observation package

　　q = (τ, τ′, Π, Env, ι, D\_test, Acc\_τ, Acc\_τ′)

q is the common observation specification: ι is the declared initialization protocol, and the other items are as before. q contains no host, and applies alike to both candidates. Connecting a host system to the specification q gives an observation package:

　　Y = (𝒮, q)

What is compared between the two candidates is: under one and the same q, when qualified hosts satisfying C and satisfying N respectively are connected, what each can read out (8.5).

### 8.2 The operation I(τ → τ′)

Rewrite every record of τ in ℛ as a record of τ′; rerun, as declared, every program in 𝒫 that reads these records and acts on m; programs that act only on delivery are not rerun, and Π is frozen; apart from this, perform no operation on m, Π or Env. Then run P according to Π on each delivery in D\_test. The set of admissible result families of the host after the operation is written 𝓜′(D\_test); one actual run gives one result family μ′ in it.

### 8.3 Read-out

　　Read(Y) = 1　　if after the operation Acc\*\_τ′ = 1  
　　Read(Y) = 0　　if after the operation Acc\*\_τ = 1  
　　Read(Y) = ⊥　　if neither holds

By Y2 of 8.4 and the non-emptiness of 𝓜′ (K0), the first two clauses cannot hold at once, so Read takes only one value.

Read is the theoretical read-out: it makes its determination over all admissible result families after the operation, not over a particular run, and bears the proofs of Theorems Two to Four.

The actual read-out: one actual run according to 8.2, giving the result family μ′,

　　Read\_obs(Y) = 1　　if Acc\_τ′(μ′) = 1  
　　Read\_obs(Y) = 0　　if Acc\_τ(μ′) = 1  
　　Read\_obs(Y) = ⊥　　if neither holds

By Y2, the first two clauses cannot hold at once.

The two read-outs have different duties. Read requires knowing all admissible results of the declared model, given by the declared model and its certificates (for example an upper bound on error); Read\_obs requires only the actual deliveries and readings. That μ′ happens to satisfy one of the acceptance protocols does not suffice to fix Read: finitely many readings cannot certify all admissible results — one output mechanism always falls within the domain of acceptance, while another falls outside it only with extremely small probability, and finitely many tests can give exactly the same records. The connection between the two is borne by W3 (8.4), the proposition of connection being Corollary Two (9.6).

### 8.4 The admissible working domain 𝒴^adm

Y1　τ and τ′ are tasks in the sense of Chapter 2, sharing the delivery and reading-scheme types of Π, with ext(τ) ≠ ext(τ′); the reading schemes are frozen, not changed with τ′.  
Y2　Mutually exclusive acceptance: on D\_test no result family satisfies both Acc\_τ and Acc\_τ′. For example, on each delivery the difference between the two functions is greater than twice the tolerance.  
Y3 (robust baseline acceptance)　Before the operation is applied, P robustly accepts τ: for D\_test, all admissible result families of the host in the declared model satisfy Acc\_τ (Acc\*\_τ = 1). That finitely many actual runs happen to be accepted may not stand in for this condition.  
Y4　The operation consists only of what is listed in 8.2.  
Y5　Freezing of delivery: for each delivery in D\_test, the physical signal sent to the input port of P is the same before and after the operation, that is, the delivery mapping Δ\_Π is not changed by the rewriting of the records.  
Y6　Sameness of the initial running state: in the compared runs before and after the operation, at the start of each test delivery the running state of the medium relevant to the declared output is obtained by one and the same frozen initialization protocol: when the configuration is unchanged, the initial states of the two runs are the same; when the configuration is changed by a setting program, the initial state is the result of the same protocol acting on the new configuration. The initialization protocol does not read expression records, does not evaluate on the host's behalf, and does not undo or replace the change made to the configuration by the setting program: it decides only the initial running state s₀, and does not touch the configuration m.  
W1 (completeness of declaration)　The declared boundary of 𝒮 contains every program that takes expression records as input and sets k\_P in m.  
W2 (freezing of operating conditions)　Env and Π are unchanged before and after the operation.  
W3 (the model conforms to the real thing)　Before and after the operation, the output of each actual run of the host belongs to the set of admissible results 𝓜(m, s₀, u) given by the declared model. W3 is used only for the actual read-out Read\_obs, is not a condition of 𝒴^adm, and is not used by Theorems Two to Four. It is a declared condition of connection, and cannot be proved automatically by finitely many experiments.

The condition of readability on the side of each candidate: for C, τ′ ∈ 𝒯; for N, no further condition.

W1, W2 and W3 are of the same kind as the conditions of the domain of determination of *The Determination Form of Computation*: whether the declared model agrees with the actual host belongs to instantiation and external connection. When W1 or W2 does not hold, the output can disagree with the candidate; when W3 does not hold, the actual read-out can disagree with the theoretical read-out; what fails then is a condition of the working domain.

8.5 Forward mappings and sets of read-outs

　　F\_C: defined on {(𝒮, q) ∈ 𝒴^adm : 𝒮 satisfies C (for τ and 𝒯), τ′ ∈ 𝒯}, taking the value Read(𝒮, q).  
　　F\_N: defined on {(𝒮, q) ∈ 𝒴^adm : 𝒮 satisfies N}, taking the value Read(𝒮, q).

Fixing the common specification q, the sets of read-outs of the two candidates are:

　　Out\_C(q) = { F\_C(𝒮, q) : (𝒮, q) in the domain of F\_C }  
　　Out\_N(q) = { F\_N(𝒮, q) : (𝒮, q) in the domain of F\_N }

C and N are mutually exclusive (6.5), and one and the same 𝒮 cannot satisfy both, so the domains of F\_C and F\_N are disjoint. What the distinction compares is not the values of the two functions on one and the same host, but the sets of read-outs of the two sides under one and the same frozen specification q. Whether a set of read-outs is non-empty cannot be assumed from the way it is written; it must be given by qualified instances (9.2).

### 8.6 The observation can be executed

What can be executed is the actual read-out Read\_obs: rewriting records; rerunning programs as declared; delivering and reading by the frozen protocol; giving the verdict by the frozen acceptance protocol. All of these can be done in the declared environment of implementation, read no unobservable quantity, and do not read the label "computing host."

The theoretical read-out Read is not obtained by these operations alone: robust acceptance is a determination on the declared model, decided by the sets of admissible results given by the declared model, for example by proving through an upper bound on error that all admissible outputs fall within the tolerance (9.2). The actual read-out agrees with the theoretical read-out under W3 (Corollary Two); whether W3 holds belongs, like whether the declared model agrees with the actual host in *The Determination Form of Computation*, to the conditions of the domain of determination.

8.7 The observation reads a present intervention

What the operation does is: rewrite the records now, rerun the existing programs now, and see now whether the results change accordingly. It does not read how the medium was formed in the past. What W1 requires is that the declared boundary contain the existing setting entries.

8.8 The observation does not presuppose membership

The operation I uses one and the same definition for every host system: rewrite all the expression records within the declared boundary, and rerun all the programs within the declared boundary that read these records. Its definition contains no cases divided according to membership of a candidate, and does not select records or programs according to membership; the read-out rests only on the frozen acceptance protocols. Membership appears only in the proof of Theorem Two, to derive the outputs of the two sides.

The only thing related to membership is W1: if the declared boundary omits a setting entry, a host satisfying C will read out 0. What W1 requires is completeness of the declared boundary, not the declarer's statement of membership. The following cases show the role of each condition (Appendix B records their runs on the model):  
　(a)　In the declaration of a non-computing host, add a program that reads expression records and acts on the medium but does not set the Trace-Response relation according to the expression (for example, heating the capacitor upon reading a record, where heating does not change the output). This system satisfies neither N2 nor C3, and lies within neither candidate; it reads out 0, and the theorems make no assertion about it.  
　(b)　A host satisfying C omits a setting entry from its declaration: W1 does not hold, and it reads out 0. This is precisely the case W1 excludes.  
　(c)　A member of the declared 𝒯 cannot in fact be realized: C3 does not hold, the system does not satisfy C, and it is not within the condition of readability on the side of C.  
　(d)　In front of the bare capacitor, add a conversion program that reads expression records: when the record is integration, the delivery is passed on unchanged; when the record is changed to proportion, the input is replaced by another current waveform, so that the output of the capacitor satisfies the proportional task. Neither the capacitor nor its Trace-Response relation has changed, yet the read-out will be 1. If this conversion program is outside P, it changes the signal sent to the input port of P, Y5 does not hold, and this observation is not within 𝒴^adm; if it is inside P, it is a program acting on m according to records, the system does not satisfy N2, and it satisfies C when C3 holds. In neither case is it a counterexample to Theorem Two.  
　(e)　The bare capacitor starts the round after the operation from a different initial voltage: there is no setting program, the delivered signals are the same, the operating conditions are the same, and neither the capacitor nor its Trace-Response relation has changed, yet the output no longer conforms to the original task, and the read-out is not 0. Here the initial running states of the two rounds differ, Y6 does not hold, and this observation is not within 𝒴^adm. It does not show that the capacitor has become a computing host; it shows only that the two compared rounds must start from the same initial running state.  
　(f)　A random device satisfying N: under the same configuration, initial state, delivery and operating conditions, each run independently outputs 0 or 1 with probability one half each; τ accepts only 0, τ′ only 1. The first round actually outputs 0 and the second round actually outputs 1; reading by the actual results, it would read out 1. But the set of admissible results of this device is {0, 1}, which contains a result not accepted by Acc\_τ, so robust Y3 does not hold, and this observation is not within 𝒴^adm; after the operation the set of admissible results is still {0, 1}, and by 8.3 it reads out ⊥. That the distributions of results of the two rounds are the same does not mean that the actual results of the two rounds are the same, so the verdict must be made over all admissible results, not over one sample.  
Y5 compares only whether the signals sent to the input port are the same before and after the operation, and Y6 only whether the initial running states of the two rounds are obtained by the same protocol; neither involves which kind the host belongs to, and both apply alike to the two candidates. For a host satisfying C, its Set acts on the configuration m, does not change the signals sent to the input port, and is not undone by the initialization protocol, so neither Y5 nor Y6 excludes it.

## Section Nine　Distinguishing Validity

### 9.1 Theorem Two (validity)

For any Y in 𝒴^adm:  
　if 𝒮 satisfies C (τ′ ∈ 𝒯), then Read(Y) = 1;  
　if 𝒮 satisfies N, then Read(Y) = 0.

Proof.

The side of C. By Y4, the operation reruns all programs in 𝒫 that read records and act on m, that is, Set (C2), whose input has now been changed to the record of τ′; Set acts on m before delivery. By W2, Env and Π are unchanged. By Y6, the test runs start from the initial running state obtained by the initialization protocol. By C3, P thereafter robustly accepts τ′: all admissible result families after the operation satisfy Acc\_τ′, Acc\*\_τ′ = 1. Hence Read(Y) = 1.

The side of N. By N2, there is no program in 𝒫 acting on m; by W1, there is no program outside the declared boundary either that takes expression records as input and sets k\_P. By Y4, apart from rewriting records and rerunning the programs in 𝒫, the operation does not touch m. Rewriting the records does not by itself change the composition and arrangement in m that determine k\_P, because by N2 the records are not in that part. Hence m is unchanged. By Y5, the signals sent to the input port of P are unchanged as well. By Y6, the initial running state s₀ is also the same. By K0 and W2, the set of admissible results of each delivery is unchanged, 𝓜(m, s₀, u) being the same before and after the operation; where the results are random, the distribution is also unchanged. Hence 𝓜′(D\_test) = 𝓜(D\_test). By Y3, every result family in 𝓜(D\_test) satisfies Acc\_τ, so every result family in 𝓜′(D\_test) satisfies Acc\_τ, Acc\*\_τ = 1. Hence Read(Y) = 0.

What both sides derive is acceptance over all admissible result families, not a prediction of the result of a particular run; since the result family obtained by an actual run belongs to the set of admissible result families, it too is necessarily accepted by the corresponding acceptance protocol. This completes the proof.

### 9.2 Theorem Three (instance)

Take W\_C and W\_N of 7.1, with C₀ = 1 mF, R₀ = 250 Ω, T = 1 s; τ = τ\_int, τ′ = τ\_prop; D\_test is any finite sequence of constant-current deliveries I(t) ≡ iⱼ, each satisfying 0.2 mA ≤ \|iⱼ\| ≤ 1 mA, and the proof below holds for every such choice; the acceptance protocol Acc\_τ of a task τ is: on each delivery, the difference between the output and the function value of τ does not exceed the tolerance of 0.05 V; errors follow the bounded model of 7.1.

On each such delivery, τ\_int gives i·T/C₀ = 1000·i (volts, with i in amperes) and τ\_prop gives R₀·i = 250·i, the difference between the two being 750·\|i\| ≥ 0.15 V (i taking each iⱼ). By the bounded-error model, the set of admissible outputs of the host on one delivery is an interval around the value of the function it realizes: every admissible output differs from this function value by no more than \|V\|·10⁻³ + 0.005 ≤ 0.001 + 0.005 = 0.006 V.

Checking the working domain. Y1: the two tasks share the delivery and reading-scheme type "current in, voltage out," with different extensions. Y2: on each delivery the difference between the two functions is at least 0.15 V, greater than twice the tolerance, 0.1 V. Y3: before the operation both realize τ\_int, every admissible output differing from τ\_int by no more than 0.006 V \< 0.05 V, so all admissible result families satisfy Acc\_τint, and robust Y3 holds. Y4, W1, W2: by the declarations of 7.1. K0: the sets of admissible results of both are determined by configuration, initial running state and delivery according to the bounded-error model of 7.1, and are non-empty for each delivery (the error model gives admissible outputs for each delivery); after delivery there is only direct reading by the voltmeter, with no proxy evaluation outside the declared boundary. Y5: the deliveries of both are connected directly to the input port, and the operation does not change the delivery. Y6: before each delivery both obtain the initial running state by the same protocol ι (7.1) — in the integration configuration the capacitor is discharged to 0 V; after W\_C switches to the proportion configuration, ι has no effect on the resistor, and the compared runs still obtain their initial states by the same protocol. τ\_prop ∈ 𝒯 (the condition of readability on the side of W\_C). Hence both observations lie within 𝒴^adm.

Acceptance and rejection are both made over all admissible results, independently of the particular value the error takes in a given run: for a host realizing some function, every admissible output differs from this function by no more than 0.006 V, so all admissible result families are accepted by the acceptance protocol of this function; every admissible output differs from the other function by at least 0.15 − 0.006 = 0.144 V \> 0.05 V, so all admissible result families are rejected by the acceptance protocol of the other function. This upper bound on error is the certificate of robust acceptance.

By Theorem Two:

　　Read(Y; W\_C) = 1,　Read(Y; W\_N) = 0

This completes the proof.

### 9.3 Theorem Four (distinction)

By Theorem Two, for any common specification q, the read-out on the domain of F\_C is always 1, and on the domain of F\_N always 0:

　　Out\_C(q) ⊆ {1},　Out\_N(q) ⊆ {0}

Take the specification of 9.2, q₀ = (τ\_int, τ\_prop, Π, Env, ι, D\_test, Acc\_τint, Acc\_τprop), where ι is the initialization protocol of 7.1. By Theorem Three, W\_C and W\_N are qualified instances of the two sides under q₀, and both sets of read-outs are non-empty. Hence

　　Out\_C(q₀) = {1},　Out\_N(q₀) = {0},　Out\_C(q₀) ∩ Out\_N(q₀) = ∅

Moreover, under one and the same specification q₀, "this host satisfies C" and "this host satisfies N" predict disjoint read-outs, and one resetting observation suffices to choose between them. This completes the proof.

9.4 Counter-checks: the output is indeed caused by the route from expression to host rules

(a)　On W\_C, only the record is rewritten and Set is not rerun: m is unchanged, all admissible result families still satisfy Acc\_τint, and Read = 0. So the 1 on the side of W\_C comes from the route of Set, not from a constant inherent in the apparatus.

(b)　Add to W\_N a program that "selects and connects the feedback element according to the expression": after resetting, all admissible result families satisfy Acc\_τprop, Read = 1. Now 𝒮 satisfies C (for 𝒯) and does not satisfy N — the same kind of capacitor, connected according to an expression, has become an organized computing element. So the 0 on the side of W\_N comes from the absence of this route.

9.5 Corollary One (the same medium can belong to both sides)

One and the same kind of medium can satisfy C in one host system and N in another; which it satisfies is decided by whether there is a route from expression to host rules in the host system.

Proof. By 9.4 (b): the same kind of capacitor satisfies N in W\_N, and satisfies C after a setting program is added. This completes the proof.

Item 6 of the initial stipulation (the brain is a computing P only when exercising its capacity to compute), the example of device drift of Chapter 7 (two classifications of the same device under two declarations, Counterexample One of the Breach Argument) and the implementation-declaration condition of C₂ in Chapter 8 all agree with this corollary: the boundary lies in the organization of the host system, not in the material.

### 9.6 Corollary Two (actual read-out)

For any Y in 𝒴^adm, if W3 holds:  
　if 𝒮 satisfies C (τ′ ∈ 𝒯), then Read\_obs(Y) = 1;  
　if 𝒮 satisfies N, then Read\_obs(Y) = 0.

Proof. The side of C: by Theorem Two, Read(Y) = 1, that is, all admissible result families after the operation satisfy Acc\_τ′ (C3). By W3, the actual result family μ′ belongs to the set of admissible result families, so Acc\_τ′(μ′) = 1. By Y2, μ′ does not also satisfy Acc\_τ, so Read\_obs(Y) = 1. The side of N: by Theorem Two, Read(Y) = 0, that is, all admissible result families after the operation satisfy Acc\_τ (Y3 and K0). By W3, Acc\_τ(μ′) = 1; by Y2, Acc\_τ′(μ′) = 0, so Read\_obs(Y) = 0. This completes the proof.

So this chapter has two results with different duties: Theorems Two to Four prove mathematically that the sets of read-outs of the two candidates under a common specification are disjoint; Corollary Two proves that, for instances whose model conforms to the real thing (W3), one actual intervention suffices to read out this difference correctly. Corollary Two does not show that one actual observation can certify the robust qualification of an unknown host: W3 is a premise, not a conclusion.

In the instance of 9.2, admissible outputs differ from the function values by no more than 0.006 V, the two functions differ by at least 0.15 V, and the tolerance is 0.05 V; as long as W3 holds, the actual output falls within one of the mutually exclusive domains of acceptance.

## Section Ten　Conclusions That Cannot Be Derived from This Chapter

10.1 Rank cannot be derived from the distinction. Read = 1 shows only that the host rules are set by expressions.

10.2 The upper layers cannot be derived from the host layer. C concerns only the host rules k\_P. The dynamics layer and the object layer on a computing host are driven by dynamics; rule computation takes place in the host layer of generative computation, which is item 16 of the initial stipulation of *The General Form of Computational Layering*, borne within the working domain of that form (σ bearing Φ(∅)) by its Corollary One; this form sets up no separate premise.

10.3 That a host cannot bear mathematical tasks cannot be derived from N. W\_N bears the integration task, with robust acceptance verdict 1 (Theorem One (i)).

10.4 C cannot be derived from "can be described mathematically" (Theorem One (v)).

10.5 C cannot be derived from "intentionally set up" (Theorem One (vi)).

10.6 N cannot be derived from "continuous," nor C from "discrete" (Theorem One (iii)).

10.7 It cannot be derived from this chapter that "every observation that can separate the two must intervene on expression records." Theorem One holds only for 𝒴₀.

10.8 Whether computation holds on a given event cannot be derived from Read. Read characterizes the origin of the Trace-Response relation in a host system, not the truth value of Compₙ of *The General Form of Computation* on a given event; the instance criteria of event-level Dₙ belong to the determination problem, to be taken up by further work on *The Determination Form of Computation*, and are not yet established within the group (the verdicts of v1.0 of that form do not contain Dₙ).

10.9 Whether a dynamics layer can be generated cannot be derived from membership of a candidate, nor the rank of the qualifications of dynamics and objects. The two kinds of host are two sources of dynamics (initial stipulation, items 3 and 5), and the Sedimentation-Field need not depend on computation to be realized (initial stipulation, item 2); the qualifications of the dynamics layer and the object layer are obtained by their own conditions of holding (*The General Form of Computational Layering*, *The Dissolution Form of Cross-Layer Misplacement in Computation*).

10.10 Robust qualification cannot be derived from one or finitely many actual observations. The agreement of Read\_obs with Read presupposes W3 (Corollary Two); that finitely many readings are the same does not show that the sets of admissible results of two hosts are the same.

## Section Eleven　Minimality

A Distinction Form does not prove that its constituent items cannot be deleted. The minimality here is the minimality of the distinguishing observation: to prove Out\_C(q) ∩ Out\_N(q) = ∅, one common specification, one τ′ and one qualified instance on each side suffice. There is no need to exhaust 𝒯, to give a verdict on every host, or to make a reverse construction.

## Section Twelve　Conditions, Status and What Is Unanswered

### 12.1 Conditions

New H: none.

Common antecedent: K0 (3.3), taken alike for both candidates, as a declared condition, not derived from bearing; it is used on the side of N in Theorem Two (deriving from K0 and W2 that the sets of admissible results are unchanged), in the single-valuedness of 8.3 and in Corollary Two.

Conditions of the working domain: W1 (completeness of declaration), W2 (freezing of operating conditions); condition of connection: W3 (the model conforms to the real thing), used only for Corollary Two; the observation conditions Y3 (robust baseline acceptance), Y5 (freezing of delivery) and Y6 (sameness of the initial running state) belong to 𝒴^adm together with Y1, Y2 and Y4.

Inheritance: whether the reading scheme of τ′ in Y1 is legitimate depends, in cases such as encodings and scales, on Computational Bearing General Form-H2, inherited under conditional closure. The reading schemes of the witness pair of 7.1 are direct readings of voltage, and do not involve such cases. Computational Bearing General Form-H1 and H3 are involved only when state types and external proxy evaluation are identified on concrete hosts (instance application): if the clause "no external proxy evaluation after delivery" in K0 must be identified on a concrete host by a formal criterion, it carries Computational Bearing General Form-H3; where it is given directly by the declaration in the declared model, it is a convention of the model and is not carried; when applied to actual hosts, it is a condition of connection together with W3 (the non-carrying of Lemma One of *The Determination Form of Computation* extends only to failed items in which a clause is directly negated by the declaration, and is not the ground here).

### 12.2 Status

Theoretical status: Theorems Two to Four hold under K0, W1, W2 and the conditions of 𝒴^adm (including Y3, Y5 and Y6); Theorem One and Corollary One are given directly by the witness pair of 7.1 and 9.4 (b); Corollary Two requires W3 in addition; the verdicts are made over all admissible result families. The instance of Theorem Three is proved by the upper bound on error of 9.2: all admissible outputs fall within or outside the corresponding tolerance of acceptance, which is the certificate of robust acceptance and robust rejection, independent of the runs of the appendix.  
Model-level instance: the model of Appendix A implements W\_C, W\_N and the operation of 8.2, the error being taken as a bounded model and the deliveries as nearly constant random currents. The read-out has two levels: one makes its determination over all admissible outputs from the noiseless output of the model plus or minus the noise bound, corresponding to the theoretical read-out Read of 8.3; the other makes its determination from the output of the present actual run, corresponding to the actual read-out Read\_obs. In the model, the output of each run is taken from the admissible interval, so W3 holds by construction, and the W3 check of the appendix only demonstrates the chain of reasoning of Corollary Two, verifying no real thing. The results of the two levels agree: Read(W\_C) = 1, Read(W\_N) = 0, the other task being robustly rejected on both sides; 9.4 (a) gives 0 and (b) gives 1; 8.8 (a) and (b) give 0; 8.8 (d) reads out 1 with the Y5 check false, 8.8 (e) reads out ⊥ with the Y6 check false, and in 8.8 (f) robust Y3 does not hold and the read-out after the operation is ⊥, these three observations all lying outside 𝒴^adm; the parameter family (integration with gain 2), as a control, likewise gives 1 and 0; the read-outs for seeds 1 to 20 are all the same. These results concern only the samples run, and are a demonstration of the definitions of the two candidates, not a physical experiment. The appendix's determination over admissible outputs covers only the 40 deliveries taken; the replays with seeds 1 to 20 are a finite spot check. Neither stands in for the universal determination over all admissible results and all deliveries of D\_test in 9.2.  
Physical instance: not carried out.

### 12.3 Unanswered

(1) Giving a verdict of "computing" or "non-computing" on an arbitrary host.  
(2) Mixed hosts: part of the Trace-Response relation set by expressions, part not.  
(3) Hosts that only select parameters according to expressions and reproduce some phenomenon with the same kind of matter (for example, a tank whose fluid viscosity is selected according to an equation): they satisfy C for the corresponding parameter family; their relation to the reproduction with the same kind of matter and environment spoken of in item 7 of the initial stipulation is not answered. If such reproduction should belong to the non-computing side, then C1 must require the members of 𝒯 to differ in operator. The witness pair of Theorems One to Four differs in operator, and is not affected by the answer to this question.  
(4) The methods of verifying W1 and W3; in particular, for an unknown host, how to obtain by actual observation a certificate that it conforms to its declared model.  
(5) Whether the statement "every observation that can separate the two must intervene on expression records" is true or false.  
(6) Instances of a dynamics layer on non-computing hosts. The theorems of this chapter do not take it as premise.  
(7) Hard-wired devices that can realize only one task and must be manufactured anew to be reset: they may satisfy the definition of C, depending on whether their declared system contains the setting entry required by C2; whether or not they satisfy it, 𝒯 contains only one member, and the resetting observation of this chapter does not cover them.  
(8) Whether all computing hosts spoken of in the initial stipulation satisfy C, and all non-computing hosts satisfy N. This chapter distinguishes only the two candidates C and N; whether the two can fully represent the two kinds of host of the initial stipulation has not been proved.

Of these, (1), (2) and (4) are the research needed to rise to determination, to be taken up by further work on *The Determination Form of Computation*, and are not yet established within the group (1.3, Section Fourteen).

## Section Thirteen　Ways of Breach

Breaching Theorem Two: there is an observation in 𝒴^adm in which 𝒮 satisfies C, τ′ ∈ 𝒯, and Read ≠ 1; or 𝒮 satisfies N and Read ≠ 0.

Breaching Theorem One: some observation in 𝒴₀ gives different outputs on W\_C and W\_N.

Breaching closure: the definitions of the two candidates use the name "computing host," use the other candidate, or use qualifications of the dynamics layer or the object layer.

Breaching "the observation does not presuppose membership": give a host system with a complete declared boundary (W1 holding) on which the operation I can be executed only if it is defined according to the system's membership.

Breaching Corollary Two: there is an observation in 𝒴^adm in which W3 holds and 𝒮 satisfies C while Read\_obs ≠ 1, or satisfies N while Read\_obs ≠ 0.

Not constituting a breach: disagreement of output caused by K0, W1, W2, W3, Y3, Y5 or Y6 not holding; disagreement of output obtained by substituting the results of finitely many actual runs for determination over admissible results; some computing host not satisfying C, or some non-computing host not satisfying N (belonging to 12.3 (7)(8)); hosts outside C and N giving ⊥ or any value; the membership a host obtains under the definitions of this chapter disagreeing with intuition (belonging to the kind of 12.3 (3)); whether there is a dynamics layer on non-computing hosts (12.3 (6)).

# distinguish_ref.py —— 计算与非计算宿载区分式：重设观测的参考实现（模型层定义演示，不是物理实验）
# 误差模型有界：漏电按每步 1e-5 衰减，读出噪声在 ±0.005 V 内均匀取值。
# 计算一侧按表达式记录改换反馈元件（积分 ↔ 比例），任务族在算子上不同；参数族（增益 2 的积分）作对照。
# 另含两项反检查、四项申报检查（含随记录改变交付信号的转换程序）、Y5 交付冻结核对，
# 以及运行初态核对（Y6）：初始化协议 ι 在每次交付开始时把反馈网络中接入的储能元件放电到 v0；
# 积分配置中是电容，比例配置中反馈元件是电阻，没有承担产出的储能状态，ι 对它不起作用，程序不使用 v0。
# 读出分两层：Read_robust 对申报模型的全部许可结果判定（无噪声输出 ± NOISE 的区间），对应正文的理论读出 Read；
# Read_obs 对本次实际运行的产出判定，对应正文的实际读出；W3 核对实际产出是否落在申报的许可结果集合之内。
# 另含随机二值器件的检查（8.8 己）：许可结果集合为 {0, 1}，稳健 Y3 不成立。
import random, json
C0, R0 = 1e-3, 250.0          # 法、欧
T, dt = 1.0, 0.01
EPS = 0.05                    # 接受容差（伏）
NOISE = 0.005                 # 读出噪声界（伏）

def deliveries(rng, n=40):    # D_test：只取 |(1/C0)∫I dt| ≥ 0.2 V 的交付（Y2 所需）
    out = []
    while len(out) < n:
        base = rng.uniform(-1e-3, 1e-3)
        I = [base + rng.uniform(-2e-4, 2e-4) for _ in range(int(T/dt))]
        if abs(sum(I)*dt/C0) >= 0.2: out.append(I)
    return out

TASKS = {   # 表达式记录 → 任务：读法共用"电流入、电压出"
    'int':  lambda I: sum(I)*dt/C0,          # τ_int：V(T) = (1/C0)∫I dt
    'prop': lambda I: R0*I[-1],              # τ_prop：V(T) = R0·I(T)
    'int2': lambda I: 2*sum(I)*dt/C0,        # 参数族对照：增益 2 的积分
}

class Host:
    def __init__(self, name, procs, rng, tx=None):
        self.name, self.procs, self.rng, self.tx = name, procs, rng, tx
        self.records = {'expr': 'int'}       # 边界内的表达式记录
        self.element = ('C', C0)             # 介质配置：反馈元件（裸电容即自身）
        self.v0 = 0.0                        # 运行初态：积分配置中每次交付开始时的电容电压（初始化协议 ι）
    def delivered(self, I):                  # 送到 P 输入端口的实际信号
        return self.tx(self, I) if self.tx else I
    def run(self, I):                        # 一次实际运行：无噪声输出加有界噪声
        return self.det(I) + self.rng.uniform(-NOISE, NOISE)
    def det(self, I):                        # 介质自身的迹应：电容积分或电阻比例（含漏电），不含噪声
        J = self.delivered(I)
        kind, val = self.element
        if kind == 'C':
            v = self.v0                      # ι 取得的运行初态
            for i in J:
                v += i*dt/val; v *= (1 - 1e-5)
        else:
            v = val*J[-1]                    # 比例配置：没有承担产出的储能状态，不用 v0
        return v
    def intervene(self, new_expr, rerun=True):
        for k in self.records: self.records[k] = new_expr   # 改写边界内全部表达式记录
        if rerun:
            for p in self.procs: p(self)                     # 重跑读这些记录的全部申报程序

def set_by_expr(h):            # 设定入口：按表达式记录选择并接入反馈元件
    e = h.records['expr']

def random_binary():            # 8.8 己：满足 N 的随机器件，每次运行独立输出 0 或 1
    M = {0, 1}                              # 许可结果集合，操作前后相同；τ 接受 0，τ′ 接受 1

def run_all(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    D = deliveries(rng)
    def acc_robust(task, host):              # 稳健接受：许可结果区间 [det − NOISE, det + NOISE] 全部在容差内
        return all(abs(host.det(I) - TASKS[task](I)) + NOISE <= EPS for I in D)
    def rej_robust(task, host):              # 稳健拒绝：许可结果区间全部在容差外
        return all(abs(host.det(I) - TASKS[task](I)) - NOISE > EPS for I in D)
    def O(host, t, t2, rerun=True, v0_after=None):
        before = [host.delivered(I) for I in D]
        s0_before = host.v0
        if not acc_robust(t, host): return {'Read': 'not-admissible'}
        host.intervene(t2, rerun)
        if v0_after is not None: host.v0 = v0_after                  # 违反 Y6 的对照：第二轮从另一初态开始

    h = WN(); h.procs = [heat_only]; res['adv1_N_with_nonsetting_proc'] = O(h, 'int', 'prop')

    res['adv4_N_with_different_initial_charge'] = O(WN(), 'int', 'prop', v0_after=0.3)
