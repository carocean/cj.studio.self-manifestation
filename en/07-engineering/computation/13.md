# The Distinction Form of Intervention in Generative Computation

## Section One　The Single Task and the Form-Kind

### 1.1 The single task

In a stretch of computation, there are two dynamical duties within the segment: that historical conditions are formed within the run segment, driven by execution and read in by what follows; and that historical conditions within the segment do not change with actual passage. Can these two be separated by one legitimate delivery intervention? What can this intervention separate, and what can it not?

What is to be proved is not that the two duties are defined in different words, but that:

there exists a common observation specification q such that a qualified system satisfying formation within the segment, connected to it, reads out 1, and a qualified system satisfying no formation within the segment, connected to it, reads out 0:

　　Out\_G(q) ∩ Out\_R(q) = ∅

At the same time, the boundary of resolution of this distinguishing observation is given: the read-out of delivery intervention is determined only by the arguments that the Continuation kernel reads, so it cannot determine in general the whole of the seven-item qualification of *The General Form of Generative Computation*; for five structural items among them, this chapter constructs, item by item, pairs of systems that are equivalent step by step and give the same read-out, while that item holds in one and fails in the other. The boundary of resolution belongs to the scope of application of the distinguishing observation (1.3), and is not a second main task alongside the formula above.

### 1.2 What is distinguished, and why

The initial stipulation says that in dynamical transformation history becomes dynamics (initial stipulation, item 1); that generative computation writes the rules in the host, by which the host can automatically generate the dynamics layer, the host giving an initial network of the Sedimentation-Field, which the signal flow thereafter keeps changing in history (initial stipulation, item 2). *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation* proves, within the working domain 𝒲₇, under the group of conditions C₁, C₂, C₄, C₈ and the run-level condition C\_run(σ): whenever a run segment actually contains the witness configuration Γ(σ) — including one passage at a branch position, a subsequent actual re-entry, the trace left being retained until reading-in, and the read-in set not otherwise altered — history cannot fail to become a condition of subsequent transformation within this stretch of running (Theorem One-B); under the rule class of deterministic Continuation 𝔗\_det together with Fgt\*, RT₁(o), NL(x) and C\_pre, there are two legitimate histories, differing only in the first n exogenous experiences, that reroute at re-entry (Theorem Six). Both carry Computational Execution General Form-H3.

These are conclusions on the side of generation. They do not answer: seen from outside, how is one to know that what follows in a system is really undertaken by historical conditions formed within the segment, and not by a table, a schedule or frozen initial values given in advance? Under ordinary deliveries, the two kinds of system can give exactly the same output.

*The General Form of Generative Computation* has already given two restrictions. Corollary One (independence of source): under one and the same qualified field specification, whether the values of the dynamical conditions at the head of the segment are formed by actual Continuation, moved in from another instance, written in by hand or given by initialization does not change the truth value of Sed. So what is to be distinguished is not where history comes from. Corollary Two: there are pairs of processes with identical step-by-step Continuation in which some one of the seven items holds in one member and fails in the other; such items are Hold, ¬Intr, Carry, Sed and Multi. So these five of the seven items cannot be determined by looking only at output.

This chapter works within these two restrictions: the object distinguished is the dynamical duty within the segment — whether historical conditions are updated within the run segment, driven by execution and read in by what follows (W and Rd of *The General Form of Computational Layering*, and the conclusion of within-segment generation of Theorem One-B of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation*); at the same time, the boundary of resolution of this distinction is given.

### 1.3 Form-kind

The Distinction Form, an auxiliary form, standing as a text of its own (Canon Chapter 1 v2.2, Part Four, Chapter Twelve). The purpose of the Distinction Form is to prove that an observation exists which separates two candidate structures; its entry conditions are that the definitions of the two candidates are already separately closed and that the forward mappings of both candidates are already given (the forward mappings of this chapter are written under the common observation specification, and the two sets of read-outs are given by them, see 7.6); its standard composition is declaration of the candidate pair, construction of the distinguishing observation, proof of distinguishing validity, distinction theorem, interfaces; its completion conditions are that the distinguishing observation is explicitly written and its proof of validity completed. In addition, this chapter gives in Section Ten the boundary of resolution of this observation, which is part of the scope of application of the distinguishing observation. The boundary of resolution does not by itself bear the completion conditions: the completion conditions of this chapter remain that the distinguishing observation has been written and its proof of validity completed; Theorems Four and Five and Corollary Two of Section Ten are boundary theorems stating this boundary, playing a role akin to the misjudgement bounds of Determination Forms and the bounds on the extension of observation sets of Indistinguishability Forms, and their breaches are breaches of the boundary statements (Section Thirteen). Theorem Four holds for all observations in 𝒴^adm; the observation set of Theorem Five and Corollary Two is the delivery-intervention family of this chapter, that is, the specifications in 𝒴^adm that use the delivery protocol frozen by this chapter and take 𝓗\_n(x) as their prefix family, and on this family they hold for all specifications; the three of them state bounds on the scope of application of this distinguishing observation, and do not by themselves bear the completion conditions. Observations outside the delivery-intervention family, conclusions of indistinguishability on general observation sets, and bounds on the extension of observation sets belong to *The Indistinguishability Form of the Effect Level* of the original plan, and are not yet established within the group (Section Fourteen).

The Distinction Form answers only whether there is an observation that can separate the two; determining for an arbitrary system whether it is generative computation is the task of a Determination Form.

### 1.4 Results

Below is a summary of the results of this chapter; the formal statements and proofs are in Sections Nine and Ten.

Lemma One (bridging): under deterministic Continuation, if all the arguments of the Continuation kernel are the same on two histories, the direction taken at the test is the same; for a system with no formation within the segment, two prefix histories differing only in the edges passed give the same arguments.

Theorems One to Three: within 𝒲₇, taking as intervention the two legitimate exogenous prefixes "n times via the first outgoing edge" and "n times via the second outgoing edge," compare the direction taken at the same subsequent test delivery. A system with no formation within the segment reads out 0 for every n; a system with formation within the segment, under the premises of Theorem Six of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation* (citing Theorem Six, carrying Computational Execution General Form-H3, 12.1), has an n, computable from the declaration before observation, for which it reads out 1. On the instance with three structural positions, the values of n that reroute are exactly those with n ≥ 4; under that specification Out\_G = {1} and Out\_R = {0}, the two being disjoint.

Theorem Four: a read-out of 1 implies that the arguments of the Continuation kernel differ on the two histories; the converse does not hold. When the read-out is 0, the arguments may not have changed, or may have changed without yet rerouting.

Theorem Five: on an instance realized with the field operators of *The Construction Form of the Minimal Sedimentation Field*, for each of the five items — clauses (i) and (iii) of the qualification of sedimentation, no intrusion, host holding and one and the same run — there is a pair of systems that, on the delivery protocol and prefix family frozen by this chapter, under the same state at the head of the segment, the same exogenous control and the same deterministic Continuation rule, read in the same at every step and give the same read-out, while that item holds in one and fails in the other. Each item is determined by the step partition delimited by executions in *The General Form of Generative Computation* (one pulse being one step) and the declared run, carrying the conditions listed in 12.1; the item of one and the same run carries in addition Computational Execution General Form-H1 and H2.

In one sentence: within the candidates G and R and the working domain (𝒲₇, Z1–Z4, W1′), switch n legitimate exogenous experiences in the prefix to the other outgoing edge (n computed from the declaration) and see whether the test reroutes: what reroutes belongs to formation within the segment that is read in, and what does not reroute belongs to no formation within the segment (the side of G carrying Computational Execution General Form-H3, 12.1); a read-out of 0 does not show that the arguments of Continuation have not changed. On the delivery protocol and prefix family frozen by this chapter, this read-out cannot see by whom the update is computed, where it is stored, whether it is in one and the same run, or whether the carrier has field qualification (the determination of each item carrying the conditions listed in 12.1).

### 2.2 Initial stipulation

This form takes the following stipulations about computation as premises: they are the initial stipulation adopted by this form, are not proved in this form, and do not become proved theorems by being cited. Item numbers are marked when cited:

　Initial stipulation, item 1: in dynamical transformation, history becomes dynamics; applying external intervention to dynamics from outside history is another case, namely intrusion.  
　Initial stipulation, item 2: generative computation writes the rules in the host, and by these rules the host can automatically generate the dynamics layer; the kinds of dynamics include back-erosion, rerouting, shallowing and so on. The host rules need only judge the count, within a time window, of signal flow passing through some sedimentary structure (such as a certain Groove-Line and Terminal Hollow): when the count grows, passage becomes easier; when it shrinks, harder; the host gives an initial network of the Sedimentation-Field, which the signal flow thereafter keeps changing in history.  
　Initial stipulation, item 3: directly changing channels or rewriting weights from outside history, not through the signal flow, is intrusion.  
　Initial stipulation, item 4: in a computing host, dynamics occurs when the signal flow passes and a computational transformation is really carried out through the rules; host rules are the Trace-Response relation between signal and host medium, and the dynamical conditions are the current conditions at each occurrence of Trace-Response that produces a transformation.  
　Initial stipulation, item 5: generative computation contains rule computation; rule computation takes place in the host layer of generative computation.

2.3 Two things read from the initial stipulation

First, generation takes place in the course of the signal flow's passage. Item 4 of the initial stipulation says that the dynamical conditions are the current conditions at each occurrence of Trace-Response that produces a transformation, and item 2 says that the signal flow keeps changing the field in history. So what must be looked at lies within the run segment: when the signal passes, traces are updated, and later Continuation reads the updated traces.

Second, intervention must go through the signal flow. Item 3 of the initial stipulation defines directly changing channels or rewriting weights from outside history, not through the signal flow, as intrusion. So the intervention used for distinction can change only deliveries, and cannot write the field state directly.

### 2.4 Working domain

A single host; the medium is a finite simple directed graph; signals enter in pulses; conclusions are stated within the declared model (𝒲₇ of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation*). Continuous vector media, multiple hosts, autonomous Continuation without exogenous experience, the formation of the object layer (law, Principle, Probability, Intention), and determining for an arbitrary system whether it is generative computation, all lie outside the working domain.

## Section Three　Notation and the Common Antecedent

### 3.1 The host system

　　𝒮 = (P, 𝔾, τ\_Φ, D₀, ι, c, Prot, 𝓡)

P: the host and its declared boundary. 𝔾 = (Ξ, E): the medium, a finite simple directed graph. τ\_Φ: the one-step task, one pulse being one execution of it. D: the field state, containing the edge trace m\_e of each edge and the position trace n\_v of each position, readings taking non-negative rational values, the zero trace written o. D₀: the declared field state at the head of the segment. ι: the initialization protocol, setting D to D₀ and the pulse index to zero. When this chapter speaks of the same system "running twice" (7.3, Z1, Z2), it means two passes of the observation process, each beginning with ι; in one pass there is usually only one run, while one pass of the relay system contains two runs (10.3). c = (SED, Mask, Avail): the exogenous control configuration — the sedimentation switch, the reading mask, availability. Prot: the delivery and field protocol. The delivery protocol issues the initiation of one pulse each time a delivery arrives, at the same time giving the current position through the entry; it works this way in every run of P, regardless of whether that run began with ι. The field protocol may schedule, at the junctions between two pulses, the permitted protocol operation IdleStep — shallowing only, acting on D, not a one-step transformation; whether it is scheduled follows the declaration of implementation, and this chapter schedules it only in the two systems of the third pair of Theorem Five. When operations at junctions are scheduled, the declared timing of deliveries makes each delivery arrive after the operation at the junction has been completed; the delivery protocol issues the initiation on arrival. The only protocol in Prot that starts a run is ι; there is no protocol of recovery, migration or relay that makes a run continue. 𝓡: the read-in declaration — which quantities the Continuation kernel reads at each position, by what equations these quantities are updated within the segment, by whom they are evaluated, and where they are kept.

The branch position x is a source entry, A(x) = (e₁, e₂) being its two available outgoing edges and their declared order, e\_i = (x, y\_i).

3.2 All the arguments of the Continuation kernel

In one Continuation at x, the Continuation kernel reads: the value of the read-in carrier B(x) (the quantities the score reads), the current non-field state (the current position), Mask and Avail in the exogenous control, the present delivery u and A(x) with its declared order. Together these are written Arg. The sedimentation switch SED decides only whether the field is updated; it does not enter the Continuation kernel, and is not in Arg.

### 3.3 Deterministic Continuation

Continuation takes the available outgoing edge of greatest score, ties being broken by taking the first in the declared order of A(x) (TR5′ of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation*). Under deterministic Continuation, the direction taken at x is a function of Arg.

### 3.4 The common antecedent K0

The field update and Continuation of τ\_Φ in one pulse are given only by D, c, the delivery and the declaration of τ\_Φ; the protocol operations scheduled at junctions are given only by the declaration of Prot; ι is executed at the start of each pass of observation, and does not write D within the run segment.

Grounds: item 4 of the initial stipulation (host rules are the Trace-Response relation between signal and medium); the order within one pulse in 3.3 of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation* (first shallowing, then Continuation, then sedimentation); C₃ of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation*, permitting preloading, moving in and writing in initial values as the field state at the head of the segment. K0 holds alike for both candidates.

## Section Four　Candidate One: Formation within the Segment

### 4.1 Definition

　　G(𝒮; σ) ≔ G1 ∧ G2 ∧ G3

G1 (rule class)　τ\_Φ ∈ 𝔗\_det; shallowing Idle and passage Pass are executed by P (C₂ of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation*).  
G2 (the read-in carrier is the sedimentary carrier)　B(x) consists of the edge traces of A(x) in D and their head-position traces; the Continuation kernel reads their current values each time it continues at x.  
G3 (switched on within the segment)　SED = 1 in σ; B(x) changes within σ only through Idle and Pass within each pulse, and there is no protocol operation at the junctions acting on B(x) (stronger than C₇(σ) of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation*).

### 4.2 Reading

When the signal passes, the traces of the edge passed and of its two end positions deepen by Pass, while the other traces become shallower by Idle; the next time Continuation takes place at x, what the Continuation kernel reads is these updated traces. This is precisely the realization at x of W (sedimentary update within the segment driven by execution) and Rd (what is sedimented is read in by subsequent execution) of *The General Form of Computational Layering*. G does not require that history be formed in this instance: the definition of G does not concern the source of D₀, and D₀ may be a new field or preloaded, moved-in or written-in initial values (C₃ of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation* permits preloading, moving in and writing in initial values; Corollary One of *The General Form of Generative Computation* gives a conclusion in the same direction under one and the same qualified field specification: the source of the values at the head of the segment does not change the truth value of Sed). Nor does G require the other items of the seven: systems with external custody between steps or with relay can still satisfy G (Theorem Five).

## Section Five　Candidate Two: No Formation within the Segment

### 5.1 Definition

　　R(𝒮; σ) ≔ R1 ∧ R2

R1 (deterministic Continuation)　Continuation at x is a deterministic function of Arg.  
R2 (the arguments do not vary with what is passed)　For each item of Arg, the update equation of its sequence of values within σ does not take as argument the edges actually passed in σ (including the edges given by exogenous experience); it may depend on D₀, ι, the declared evolution of the exogenous control, the pulse index and the timing of deliveries. "Argument" here is understood by dependency closure: if some item of Arg depends on some intermediate quantity, the update equation of that intermediate quantity may not take the edges actually passed as argument either, and so on through all the declared quantities.

### 5.2 Instances

Each of the following is determined by the declaration, not depending on any run:  
　Frozen running: SED = 0, D is constantly D₀ within the segment, and the Continuation kernel reads D;  
　Shadow copy: SED = 1, the Continuation kernel reads the values at the head of the segment, and Idle and Pass write to another carrier;  
　Schedule: the Continuation kernel reads quantities given by pulse index;  
　Route table: the Continuation kernel reads, as read-in values, table entries addressed by pulse index, the score and the solver following the common specification;  
　Zeroed read-in: the B(x) read in by the Continuation kernel is declared constantly to take the zero-trace value, the score and the solver following the common specification (the masking being implemented by the read-in declaration, not through Mask);  
　Rule constant: the values of D are written in the rule configuration of τ\_Φ, SED = 0.

### 5.3 Reading

R is not "non-generative computation." Frozen running is in R, yet it is of the same type as the standard positive case GS2 of *The General Form of Generative Computation* (inference in frozen mode in the same system after training), and is not excluded by the general formula of that form. What R characterizes is only that actual passage within the run segment does not enter the later reading-in at x.

## Section Six　The Two Candidates Are Each Closed

### 6.1 What each reads

G reads the rule class of τ\_Φ, the carrier declaration of B(x) and the protocol operations within σ. R reads whether Continuation is deterministic, and the table of arguments of the update equations of the items of Arg together with its dependency closure. Both can be checked item by item against the declaration.

### 6.2 Each has a determinate truth value

On an 𝒮 whose declaration has been frozen, the three clauses of G and the two clauses of R all have determinate truth values.

### 6.3 What each does not use

Neither candidate uses the other, uses the verdict "generative computation," uses the verdict on any of the seven items, or uses any read-out of delivery intervention.

### 6.4 Hence the candidates are closed

By 6.1 to 6.3, the definitions of the two candidates are already separately closed.

6.5 Mutual exclusion in definition is not observable separation

G3 requires that B(x) change within σ with Pass, and Pass acts on the edges actually passed; R2 excludes updates taking what is actually passed as argument. The two are mutually exclusive in definition. Whether they can be separated by observation requires a separate proof.

### 6.6 Incomplete coverage

Systems in which B(x) varies with what is passed but whose rules do not belong to 𝔗\_det (for example, overwriting on passage, or updating by error), and systems updated by a mixture of passage and a schedule, lie outside both candidates, and the theorems of this chapter make no assertion about them. Systems with an additional protocol operation at junctions acting on B(x) (such as the two systems of the third pair of Theorem Five) also lie outside both candidates.

## Section Seven　The Delivery-Intervention Observation

7.1 The prefix family and the test delivery

𝓗\_n(x): prefixes consisting of n exogenous experiences delivered to x, with the same timing. Take

　　h⁰ = n times (x, p, e₁),　h¹ = n times (x, p, e₂),　u\_test = (x, p)

An exogenous experience is a legitimate delivery in which the delivery itself gives the edge of that hop (C₅ of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation*); the intervention is replacing the prefix h⁰ by h¹.

### 7.2 The common observation specification

　　q = (𝔾, the declared order of A(x), x, D₀, ι, Mask, Avail, Idle, Pass, S, the Continuation solver, n, h⁰, h¹, u\_test, the read-out prescription)

q freezes the medium, the field-update rules Idle and Pass, the score function and solver, the prefixes, the test delivery, the reading scheme, the comparable initial state and Mask and Avail in the exogenous control. q does not contain the host's own declaration of implementation: the sedimentation switch SED, the read-in declaration 𝓡 (which quantities the Continuation kernel actually reads), the evaluator and the place of keeping, the run identity, and the class of carrier on which the values of D lie. Of these, SED is declared by each candidate: G contains SED = 1, the frozen-running and rule-constant instances contain SED = 0, and the other instances of R contain SED = 1. Connecting a host system to q gives an observation package Y = (𝒮, q).

### 7.3 Operation and read-out

The same system is run twice, each time first reset to D₀ by ι, once going h⁰ + u\_test and once h¹ + u\_test. Write Route\_𝒮(h) for the actual outgoing edge of x at u\_test:

　　Read(Y) = 1　　if Route\_𝒮(h⁰) ≠ Route\_𝒮(h¹)  
　　Read(Y) = 0　　if Route\_𝒮(h⁰) = Route\_𝒮(h¹)

Under deterministic Continuation there is no randomness, and the theoretical read-out given by the declared model is the same as the read-out given by actual runs. For actual hosts, their agreement presupposes that the real thing conforms to the declared model (W3′ of 7.5).

### 7.4 The admissible working domain 𝒴^adm

Z1　In the two runs of the same system, τ\_Φ, 𝔾, c (including the SED declared by that system) and Prot are the same; Mask and Avail take the values given by q, keeping both outgoing edges on A(x) readable and available.  
Z2　Both runs start from the same D₀ by the same frozen ι. ι acts before the run segment, and does not write D within the segment.  
Z3　h⁰ and h¹ belong to the declared family of legitimate deliveries; the intervention only replaces deliveries, and does not write D outside delivery (initial stipulation, item 3).  
Z4　u\_test is the same.  
W1′ (completeness of declaration)　The carriers, update equations, evaluators and places of keeping of the items of Arg are all within the declaration.

### 7.5 The condition of connection

W3′ (the model conforms to the real thing)　For actual hosts, the direction taken in each actual run is the same as that given by the declared model. W3′ is used only to apply the read-out of this chapter to actual hosts, and is not a condition of Theorems One to Five; like the question, in *The Determination Form of Computation*, of whether the declared model agrees with the actual host, it belongs to the conditions of the domain of determination, and cannot be proved by finitely many runs.

7.6 Forward mappings and sets of read-outs

　　F\_G: defined on {Y ∈ 𝒴^adm : 𝒮 satisfies G}, taking the value Read(Y).  
　　F\_R: defined on {Y ∈ 𝒴^adm : 𝒮 satisfies R}, taking the value Read(Y).

Fixing q, the sets of read-outs of the two candidates are

　　Out\_G(q) = { F\_G(𝒮, q) : (𝒮, q) in the domain of F\_G }  
　　Out\_R(q) = { F\_R(𝒮, q) : (𝒮, q) in the domain of F\_R }

G and R are mutually exclusive, and one and the same 𝒮 cannot belong to both. What is compared is the sets of read-outs of the two sides under one and the same frozen specification q; whether a set of read-outs is non-empty is not assumed from the way it is written, but given by qualified instances (9.3, 9.4).

### 7.7 The observation can be executed

All that needs to be done is: set the initial values by ι, deliver according to the prefix, make one test delivery, and record the direction taken at x. All of these can be done in the declared environment of implementation; they read no unobservable quantity and do not read the names of the candidates.

7.8 The observation does not presuppose membership

The operation uses one and the same definition for every system: the same prefixes, the same test delivery, the same read-out. The definition contains no cases divided according to candidate. Membership appears only in the proof of Theorem One.

### 7.9 Several cases

　(a)　The D₀ of the two runs differ: frozen running can also read out 1. This violates Z2, and is not within 𝒴^adm.  
　(b)　Mask or Avail is changed between the two runs: R can also read out 1. This violates Z1.  
　(c)　Traces are written directly into D, not through delivery: this violates Z3 (intrusion under item 3 of the initial stipulation).  
　(d)　The Continuation kernel additionally reads a register that records the edges passed in exogenous experience: R2 does not hold, and the system is not within R.  
　(e)　A read-in carrier is omitted from the declaration: the system may be wrongly assigned to G or R. This violates W1′.  
　(f)　The rules have random Continuation and the two runs sample independently: even under frozen running, the directions of the two runs may differ. The read-outs above presuppose deterministic Continuation; read-outs under random Continuation are listed as unanswered, and must be stated separately under a shared random source or by read-out at the level of kernels (12.3 (4)).

## Section Eight　The Bridging Lemma

### 8.1 Lemma One (bridging)

Suppose deterministic Continuation, and Y ∈ 𝒴^adm.  
(1) If Arg is the same at u\_test after h⁰ and after h¹, then Route\_𝒮(h⁰) = Route\_𝒮(h¹).  
(2) If 𝒮 satisfies R, then Arg is the same at u\_test after h⁰ and after h¹, and hence Read(Y) = 0.  
(3) If 𝒮 satisfies G, and the premises of Theorem Six of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation* hold on the declaration of 𝒮, then the set N\_sw(𝒮) ≔ {n : Route\_𝒮(h⁰) ≠ Route\_𝒮(h¹)} is non-empty.  
(4) If a pair G, R additionally satisfies baseline equivalence Route\_G(h⁰) = Route\_R(h⁰), then for n ∈ N\_sw(G):

　　Route\_G(h¹) ≠ Route\_G(h⁰) = Route\_R(h⁰) = Route\_R(h¹)

Proof.  
(1) Under deterministic Continuation, the scores are given by the read-in values of B(x) and Mask, the available outgoing edges by Avail and A(x), the order for ties by the declared order of A(x), and the position at which Continuation takes place by the current position and the delivery. All the arguments being the same, the direction is the same.  
(2) h⁰ and h¹ have the same timing and the same pulse indices, differing only in the edges given by exogenous experience. By R2, for every item of Arg and every intermediate quantity in its dependency closure, the update equation does not take the edges passed as argument; by Z1, Z2 and Z4, the two evolve from the same D₀, the same exogenous control, the same timing of deliveries and the same u\_test. Hence Arg is the same; by (1), Read(Y) = 0.  
(3) h⁰ and h¹ are precisely the two legitimate histories of Theorem Six of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation*: "deliver to x the exogenous experience (x, p, (x, y₁)) n times, then deliver (x, p) to x" and "replace the first n times by (x, p, (x, y₂))." Theorem Six asserts that there is an n for which the actual outgoing edges of x at the last delivery differ.  
(4) By (3), Route\_G(h¹) ≠ Route\_G(h⁰); by baseline equivalence, Route\_G(h⁰) = Route\_R(h⁰); by (2), Route\_R(h⁰) = Route\_R(h¹). This completes the proof.

### 8.2 Two kinds of distinction

Read is a self-comparison of two runs of the same system, and Theorems One to Three use only it. (4) concerns a pair of systems that take different directions on the same intervention history h¹, which requires baseline equivalence in addition; it shows that the case "equivalent ordinarily, separated after intervention" does exist (9.5). The two kinds of distinction are stated separately, and do not stand in for each other.

### 8.3 Fixing n

(3) asserts only that N\_sw(𝒮) is non-empty. To fix the n of q before observation, use the following procedure: the input is the whole declaration of 𝒮 (the medium and order, Idle, Pass, the score function and tie rule, D₀, ι, c, the prefix family, u\_test, the read-out prescription), containing no result of observation; for n = 1, 2, … compute on the declared model the directions of h⁰ + u\_test and h¹ + u\_test, and take as n\* the first at which they differ. The premises of Theorem Six are read item by item from the declaration (TR1–TR4 and TR5′ from the specification of the rules, Fgt\* from the form of Idle, RT₁(o) computed on the zero trace, NL(x) from the medium, C\_pre from the declaration and permissions); when the premises hold N\_sw is non-empty, and the procedure stops in finitely many steps. First take a digest of the declaration, then compute n\*, recording both together, and only then carry out the observation runs: n is decided by the declaration, not chosen after seeing the read-out. The n of q need only belong to N\_sw(𝒮); N\_sw is not required to be monotone in n.

## Section Ten　The Boundary of Resolution

10.1 Theorem Four (the read-out depends only on the arguments)

For observations in 𝒴^adm, Read(Y) is determined only by the Continuation rule and the two values of Arg at u\_test after h⁰ and after h¹: if two systems have the same Arg at each of these two places, their read-outs are the same. Hence Read = 1 implies that Arg differs on the two histories; the converse does not hold.

Proof. The first sentence follows by applying Lemma One (1) to each of the two systems. When Read = 1 the directions at the two places differ, and by the contrapositive of Lemma One (1), Arg differs. An instance for which the converse fails: for 𝒮₁ of Theorem Two with n = 1, 2, 3, after h¹ m\_{e₂} = 6n/25 \> 0 = m\_{e₂} after h⁰, so Arg differs, while e₁ is taken at both places, and Read = 0. This completes the proof.

So: a read-out of 1 witnesses that a difference in the arguments of Continuation has already produced observable rerouting; a read-out of 0 may mean either that the arguments have not changed or that they have changed without yet rerouting, and a read-out of 0 does not determine whether the arguments have changed.

10.3 Model-level determination of five of the seven items

(A) Composition

The systems of Instance Two are constructed from mutually independent components. The host P and its field-state carrier (the persistent field state of the field), the current-position register, the shadow carrier, the rule-configuration carrier (read-only after construction, taking part in no update), and the delivery protocol of P are within the declared boundary; the evaluator Q, the external storage and the initiator are outside the boundary. Each run is an independent run object, recording by whom it was initiated, and executes no further step after it ends. The structural differences of the five pairs are given by the actual operations of these components, not by names:  
　Rule constant: what the Continuation kernel actually reads is the rule-configuration carrier, not the field-state carrier;  
　Shadow copy: the Continuation kernel reads the field-state carrier, the Idle and Pass required by the switched-on mode are actually written into the shadow carrier, and the field-state carrier does not change within the segment;  
　External evaluation at junctions: the former is switched-on running in which Prot schedules in addition one IdleStep at each junction, executed by P; the latter has the same scheduling, while at each IdleStep Q receives a copy of the field values, computes the new values by the same shallowing law, and writes them back into the field-state carrier;  
　External storage: at each junction, the field-state carrier is moved out of P, held by the external storage, and handed back to P unchanged;  
　Relay: the earlier run ends after the read-out of the last pulse of the prefix; the initiator separately starts another run on the same host, not through ι, the state remaining in place on the carriers of P, and the test pulse belongs to the later run. The initiator only issues the initiation of the run, and neither reads nor writes D or the carried-over components.

One pass of observation of the relay system contains two runs (3.1). The initiator does not write D and does not replace deliveries, and Z1–Z4, checked against the observation process, still hold.

(B) The segment, step partition and run taken for determination

σ runs from the initiation of the first pulse to the read-out of the test pulse, the final segment being empty. By 3.1 and 𝒲₇, one pulse is one execution of τ\_Φ. The seven items are determined by the step partition π delimited by executions in Part Two of the Appendix (Formalization) of *The General Form of Generative Computation*: s\_k is the initiation of the k-th pulse issued by the delivery protocol, and e\_k is the latching of the exit position of that pulse. Within one pulse there are in turn: Idle when SED = 1, one hop of Continuation at x, Pass when SED = 1, the pulse index increasing by one, and the latching of the exit position. All of these are within the k-th step.

Within the junction J\_k = (e\_k, s\_{k+1}) (k = 1, …, n) there are only the following operations:  
　the two systems of the pair with external evaluation at junctions: the scheduled IdleStep;  
　the external-storage system: moving the field state out and handing it back;  
　the relay system: within J\_n, the ending of the earlier run and the initiation of the later run.  
The junctions of the other implementations are empty. These operations all take place after the read-out of one execution and before the initiation of the next execution, and are not within any execution. The arrival of the (k+1)-th delivery is s\_{k+1}, and at the same time gives the current position through the entry.

So π covers σ, with no operation left unassigned. Each s\_k is issued by the declared protocol, not a moment marked by an observer. Each e\_k is the event of the result of that step being latched in the output carrier; D′ is written by Pass on the object written by Pass, and is already there at latching. So π is legitimate and individuable. This partition is given by the intervals of execution and by the order declared in 3.1. 3.2 of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation* permits declaring one pulse, or each hop within it, as one step; in Instance Two one pulse is exactly one hop, and the two declarations give the same partition.

State carriers and output carriers are declared by component. On the side of D, In\_k is the object read by the Continuation kernel, and Out\_k is the object written by Pass (the same as In\_k, unwritten, when SED = 0); on the side of x, they are where the current-position register and the pulse index lie. Classification of components: D is all edge traces and position traces; the current position is declared at junctions as an input slot, given by the (k+1)-th delivery at s\_{k+1}; the pulse index is a carried-over component; c is an exogenous control quantity; the score and the current effective relation are instantaneous quantities. The run identity of each system is given by the run object.

(C) Grounds for determining each item

By Part Three of the Appendix (Formalization) of *The General Form of Generative Computation*, relative to the σ, π and run of (B), each item is determined on the ground of the actual operations above:  
　Step: each step is initiated by the delivery protocol, and P completes within that pulse Idle (when SED = 1), reading-in, Continuation, Pass (when SED = 1) and latching; within the step no link outside the boundary evaluates the result (x′, D′) of the present step; all the operations of one step belong to one and the same run. The result (x′, D′) of each step is computed by P within that pulse: x′ is obtained by Continuation (or by the edge given by delivery), and D′ is what P obtains by applying Idle and Pass to D. Q takes no part in evaluation within any step, and ¬Sub holds; the initiation, operation and read-out of each step are completed by P in one and the same run. This is precisely the case given in Section Six of *The General Form of Generative Computation* — "at every junction, the shallowing scheduled by the protocol is evaluated on P's behalf by a machine outside the declared boundary," while "Step holds" — and is also that form's Corollary Two (2). That form classes the case where "an external machine computes the D′ of the present step and writes it back" under ¬Sub of Step, which is different from this.  
　The other items hold:  
　　Carry — Q only takes a copy and keeps nothing in custody; D and the carried-over components are held by P throughout the junction;  
　　Sed(iii) — IdleStep is a permitted protocol operation listed by that form (shallowing only, acting on D), the value Q writes back is the same as that given by the shallowing law, every change is a protocol operation, and the updates required by the declared modes all fall on the field-state carrier;  
　　Sed(i) — D lies on the persistent field state of the field;  
　　Hold — Out\_k and In\_{k+1} are the same field-state carrier and the same register, so (a) holds; the D at s\_{k+1} is what the D written out at e\_k becomes through the IdleStep at the junction, so (b) holds; the pulse index is not rewritten at junctions and the current position is obtained by delivery, so (c) holds;  
　　Multi — the whole segment belongs to one run.  
The items of the former hold likewise, the IdleStep at junctions being executed by P.

(4) The pair for Carry. The moving out and handing back by external storage take place after e\_k and before s\_{k+1}, within J\_k.  
　Within J\_k, D is not held by P, so Carry fails.  
　The external storage only stores and does not compute, and does not evaluate D, so ¬Intr holds.  
　The field-state carrier is handed back unchanged, Out\_k and In\_{k+1} are connected by storage-only or transfer-only passage, and the value on handing back is the same as on moving out, so Hold holds.  
　There is no external link within steps, so Step holds. The other items are as in switched-on running.

(5) The pair for Multi.  
　How the earlier run ends. After the latching e\_n of the exit position of the n-th pulse of the prefix, the earlier run r₁ ends, and thereafter executes no further step.  
　How the later run is initiated. Within J\_n, the initiator outside the boundary starts the run r₂ on the same host. It is issued neither by the dynamics of r₁ nor by the declared protocol: the only protocol in Prot that starts a run is ι, and r₂ does not go through ι. The initiation s\_{n+1} of the test pulse is issued by the delivery protocol in r₂.  
　How the state is carried over. D, the current-position register and the pulse index all remain in place on the carriers of P, and the first pulse of r₂ reads and writes on the original carriers, without copying. Out\_n and In\_{n+1} are the same place; the D and pulse index at s\_{n+1} are just the values written out at e\_n.  
　The other items hold:  
　　Carry, Hold(a)(b)(c) — by the preceding clause;  
　　¬Intr — the initiator does not evaluate D or the carried-over components;  
　　both clauses of Sed — the changes of D are all protocol operations;  
　　Step — the operations of each step belong to one and the same run (steps 1 to n to r₁, step n+1 to r₂), and each s\_k is issued by the delivery protocol.  
　Multi fails. The first n steps of σ belong to r₁ and step n+1 to r₂, so σ does not as a whole belong to one run.  
　Why this is not legitimate recovery or live migration. *The General Form of Computational Execution* prescribes that live migration of a virtual machine, or recovery from a checkpoint on another machine, is one and the same run on one and the same instance when the source machine, the target machine and the transmission channel are all within the declared boundary and the transmission does not evaluate by proxy. *The General Form of Generative Computation* lists continuing training after recovery from a checkpoint on the same instance as a continuation of the same run (GS8), and lists "relay (not declared as a recovery protocol)" as Multi failing. In this pair there is neither migration nor transmission. As a physical process on the host, an in-place restart on the same host without transmission does not differ from recovery within the boundary; the distinction between the two is given by whether it is declared as a recovery protocol. The Prot of this pair has no recovery protocol, and the initiation of r₂ is issued by the initiator outside the boundary, not by Prot or by the dynamics of r₁, so it is a relay "not declared as a recovery protocol."  
　Conditions. The general criteria of this distinction belong to Computational Execution General Form-H1 (continuation by the same instance across an enabling gap, as distinguished from a reset redo after interruption, including checkpoint recovery) and H2 (the criterion of one and the same run, including the distinction between checkpoint recovery and rerunning), both pending verification; *The General Form of Generative Computation* also prescribes that "the distinction between relay and recovery is determined by Computational Execution General Form-H2." So "the latter fails only on Multi" in this pair carries Computational Execution General Form-H1 and H2. If the criteria of these two conditions recognize such a relay as a continuation of the original run, the witness of this pair lapses accordingly.

(6) For the execution records and checks of each item on Instance Two, see Appendix A. This completes the proof.

10.5 Corollary Two (the delivery-intervention read-out does not determine the five items)

For X ∈ {Sed(i), Sed(iii), ¬Intr, Carry, Multi}, there is no function that takes the read-out of delivery intervention as its argument and determines X on 𝒴^adm: the corresponding pair of Theorem Five has the same read-out, while X holds in one and fails in the other. X takes values by the segment, step partition and run of 10.3 (B), carrying the conditions listed in 12.1; when X is Multi, it carries in addition Computational Execution General Form-H1 and H2.

Proof. If there were such a function f, f would take the same value on the two systems of that pair while the truth values of X differ, a contradiction. This completes the proof.

### 10.6 Gov and the seven items as a whole

The former of each pair of Theorem Five holds on the items determined in 10.3 (by the segment, step partition and run of 10.3 (B), carrying the conditions listed in 12.1); Sed(ii) holds because P realizes the sedimentation mode (C₂ of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation*). Gov is not determined by this chapter: by the derivation of Theorem Eight of *The Necessity Form of Generative Computation*, it holds when the comparison states differ only in D, ε\_role is smaller than the total variation of the two distributions (under deterministic Continuation the total variation is 1 when the directions differ), and there is an AdmVar coverage certificate; the instances of this chapter give no AdmVar coverage certificate. Hence whether the former of each pair of Theorem Five satisfies the seven items as a whole is undetermined (lacking the AdmVar coverage certificate for Gov), and so it cannot be inferred either that it is generative computation (the sufficient direction of GenComp is unanswered in *The General Form of Generative Computation*). If the former of some pair obtains in addition a certificate for Gov, then by the same argument as Corollary Two, the delivery-intervention read-out cannot separate systems satisfying the seven items from those not satisfying them either.

10.7 Summary of the boundary of resolution

A delivery-intervention read-out of 1 shows that on these two histories the quantities read by the Continuation kernel differ, and that this difference has already produced rerouting; within the candidates G and R, it separates formation within the segment from no formation within the segment (Theorems One to Three; the side of G carrying Computational Execution General Form-H3, 12.1). It does not determine whether the quantities read by the Continuation kernel have changed (Theorem Four), nor by whom the update is evaluated, where it is kept, whether it is in one and the same run, whether the writes fall on the state carrier, or whether the values lie in the persistent field state of the field (Theorem Five, Corollary Two; the determination of each item carrying the conditions listed in 12.1, the sameness of run carrying in addition Computational Execution General Form-H1 and H2).

11.6 The qualifications of the dynamics layer and the object layer cannot be derived from this chapter, nor that law, Principle, Probability or Intention holds in the host (*The General Form of Computational Layering*, *The Dissolution Form of Cross-Layer Misplacement in Computation*).

## Section Thirteen　Ways of Breach

Breaching Theorem One: there is an observation in 𝒴^adm in which 𝒮 satisfies R while Read = 1; or 𝒮 satisfies G and the premises of Theorem Six while Read = 0 for every n.

Breaching Theorem Two: point out that some premise in the declaration of Instance One does not hold, or that N\_sw(𝒮₁) ≠ {n ≥ 4}.

Breaching Theorem Five: point out that the two systems of some pair have different Arg on some prefix at some step; or that the step partition of 10.3 (B) is not legitimate and individuable; or that, on that partition and the declared run, the determination of the items listed disagrees with the definitions of *The General Form of Generative Computation*.

Breaching closure: the definitions of the two candidates use the other candidate, use the verdicts on the seven items or on generative computation, or use the read-out of delivery intervention.

Breaching "the observation does not presuppose membership": give a system with a complete declaration (W1′ holding) on which the operation can be executed only if it is defined according to the system's membership.

Not constituting a breach: disagreement of read-out caused by Z1–Z4, W1′ or W3′ not holding; any read-out given by systems outside the candidates; read-outs of random Continuation under independent sampling; some system satisfying the seven items reading out 0, or some system not satisfying the seven items reading out 1 (this is precisely the content of Theorem Five); the non-existence of a uniform specification (12.3 (3)).

Theorems Two and Three of Section Nine, Instance Two and Theorem Five of Section Ten, and the checks of 9.6 are run on the model below (the cases of 7.9 are analytic illustrations, not run); it demonstrates the definitions of the two candidates and the paired witnesses, and is not a physical experiment.
