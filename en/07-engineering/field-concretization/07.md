# The Realization Form of Field Concretization

1\. Declaration of Form Kind  
This paper is a Realization Form (an auxiliary form of establishment proof). Its sole task is this: for the Sedimentation-Field targets that *The Reachability Form of Field Concretization* has proved reachable, to give a realization scheme under an explicitly declared real constraint set, verify the constraints item by item, give realization records and realization theorems, and declare the parts not realized.  
The conclusions of this paper fall into four groups.  
　1\. Algorithmic-level realization: χ₀ and Instance A of the Adjudication Form take programs as hosts. The host's state variables are exactly the Structural Positions, its process functions are exactly the mechanism processes, and its entry functions are exactly the signal entries. Theorem 1 gives two simulation relations: the exact host equals the original model beat by beat; the binary64 host equals the declared rounded operation graph beat by beat. Realization Record 1 gives execution records for 7 finite inputs and implements the protocols SEP, MOD, LOC, IMP and OUT-f on the program.  
　2\. Preservation of qualification under finite precision: after the single numeric entry (Definition 4), if the interval backend can decide every comparison of the qualification decision tree, then the binary64 realization and the exact model take the same branches and give the same verdict (Theorem 2); interval safe evaluation gives only the correct verdict or “numerically unknown”, and never a wrong verdict (Theorem 3).  
　3\. Number-system boundaries: equality points do not satisfy a positive margin (Proposition 1); the eventual outcome of binary64 proportional decay differs with the operation graph and the subnormal policy (Lemma 1); a complete F model can flip its qualification as a result, while a single atom flip need not flip the qualification (Proposition 2).  
　4\. Candidate part: the requirements and certification traceability matrix for the material layer (Section 9), substitution and control operations, and the status of candidate hosts (Section 10). This part is not a premise of the realization theorems and does not constitute a material realization certificate.  
Strength position: in the strength order “compatibility ≺ construction ≺ reachability ≺ realization” of the Canon Chapters, Part Two, Chapter Five, this paper takes realization.  
The realization in this paper is limited to the algorithmic level: finite executions with programs as hosts, completed under the declared platform, number systems and budget. It is not a realization by a material host, nor is it qualification certification of any biological or physical system.  
Theoretical increment: this paper answers a question that none of the upstream papers takes on—under what real constraints, and on what evidence, a reachable target is realized and not merely simulated. The answer has four parts:  
　(A) object identity at the algorithmic level and the two simulation relations;  
　(B) execution records and protocol implementation under the real constraint set;  
　(C) conditions under which the complete upstream three-valued qualification decision tree is preserved under finite precision, and the safe-evaluation contract;  
　(D) number-system boundaries: equality points, operation graphs, subnormal policies.  
Interval inclusion, exact predicates and rounding analysis are existing methods in themselves. The increment of this paper lies in bringing in the upstream decision tree atom by atom, isolating the non-numeric atoms, and giving the concrete conditions under which Sedimentation-Field qualification is preserved, and fails, under these number systems.

2.3  Three Kinds of Evidence Side by Side  
Realization evidence is of three kinds. The three stand side by side and do not form a progression:  
　(1) Algorithmic-level realization: the program itself is the host, and what is realized is an algorithmic-level object. This is the object of Sections 4–8 of this paper.  
　(2) Computational simulation of a material process: the program simulates some material system. It is a realization of an algorithmic-level object, not a realization of that material system. The literature cited in Section 10 is of this kind and serves only for comparison.  
　(3) Material-host realization: a material system bears the Structural Positions, the processes and the entries. No records of this kind exist at present.  
The three kinds cannot substitute for one another. That kind (1) holds does not imply kind (3), and the results of kind (2) do not extend to the simulated organization.

4\. Realization Scheme  
Definition 2 (host program). A host program is a state machine (Host in Appendix A.2), given by a position-name table, a process table and an entry-pulse table:  
　each process registers a label, a carrier (inside B or out-of-boundary), an action position, a numeric law (constant or linear), read positions, a coefficient k, a bias b and a run condition (depending on the letter of the current beat);  
　each entry pulse registers a label, an action position, an amplitude and whether it is out-of-boundary.  
Each beat beat(u) executes, in order:  
　read the pre-beat state;  
　register as effective the entry pulses of the current beat's letter that have a \> ε;  
　for each process that runs in the current beat, compute the component c from the pre-beat state by its numeric law, and register it as effective if \|c\| \> ε;  
　add the effective components one by one to the pre-beat value in order of occurrence (pulses first, then processes);  
　a negative post-state leaves the domain of definition.  
The execution log records, beat by beat, the pre-beat state and the post-beat state, and, for every pulse and process that actually occurs, its label, carrier, action position, read positions, numeric law, component and whether it is effective.  
The program stores no verdicts. The execution log is transcribed into an upstream F run specification, and the verdicts are computed from it by the upstream evaluator.  
Object identity (code element → model element):  
　χ₀ (Chi0Host in Appendix A.2):  
　　state\["x"\] → Structural Position x (Sedimentation-Degree δ);  
　　entry letters u1, u5 → out-of-boundary signal pulse α·u (u = 1, 5), the same as the F-layer encoding chi0\_gf of Appendix A.6 of *The Reachability Form of Field Concretization*;  
　　procs\["wb"\] → read-out write-back inside B, linear law β·δ, reads x, runs every beat;  
　　procs\["dec"\] → shallowing inside B, linear law −λ·δ, reads x, runs every beat;  
　　readout() → activity read-out a\_t = δ.  
　　The cap δ\_max is not encoded. The program asserts at every beat that the post-state is ≤ δ\_max, so it runs only in the region where the cap is not hit; there the original model and the encoding agree beat by beat.  
　Instance A (LinUnitHost in Appendix A.2), with the same encoding as inst in Appendix A.6 of *The Reachability Form of Field Concretization*:  
　　state\["w"\] → Structural Position w;  
　　pulses\["init"\] → out-of-boundary initialization pulse +1 at the entry beat;  
　　procs\["fwd"\] → pure read inside B, linear law, coefficient 0, bias 0, reads w, runs every beat;  
　　procs\["opt"\] → optimizer, linear law Δw = (2 − w)/4 (k = −1/4, b = 1/2), runs only under letter a; its carrier is declared as χ\_ext (out-of-boundary) or χ\_in (inside B);  
　　readout\_fwd() → OUT-f: the read-out of fwd equals w.  
　　The host does not enforce 𝓗\_A; legitimate histories are guaranteed by the external input regime.  
Implementation of the protocols:  
　beat(u, knock=…) → MOD: withdraw the specified process or pulse in the current beat;  
　beat(u, loc=…) → LOC: before the current beat, set only the specified Structural Position.  
Number systems: the same host runs in the exact or the binary64 number system (Num in Appendix A.2). The parameters are declared rationals; a binary64 run takes their nearest doubles.

5\. Simulation Relations  
Definition 3 (declared rounded operation graph). When the host runs in binary64, its operation graph is as follows:  
　parameters and initial state take the nearest doubles to the declared rational values;  
　a linear-law component is fl(b + fl(k · s)), where s is the sum of the read positions, accumulated step by step with fl in the order of the read positions;  
　the effective components are added one by one to the pre-beat value in order of occurrence: fl(fl(pre + t₁) + t₂)…;  
　the effectiveness threshold and the out-of-domain comparison are performed directly on the double values.  
The binary64 backend of the evaluator feval in Appendix A.1 performs the same operations in the same order.  
Theorem 1 (simulation relations).  
　(a) Exact simulation. When the host runs in the exact number system, within the declared legitimate domain (χ₀ does not hit the cap; the histories of Instance A belong to 𝓗\_A), the pre-beat state, the process components, the effectiveness registrations and the post-beat state of the program at beat t are equal, item by item, to those of the rational recursion of 4.2 of *The Mathematical Form of Field Concretization* on the same event sequence.  
　(b) Rounded-operation-graph simulation. When the host runs in binary64, its states are equal to those of the operation graph of Definition 3, beat by beat and bit by bit.  
　(b) does not claim that the binary64 states equal the exact states of the original model. Whether the binary64 realization preserves the qualification of the original model is settled by the sufficient condition of Theorem 2, or by item-by-item comparison of the concrete atoms and verdicts.  
Proof.  
　(a) The beat steps of Definition 2 are exactly steps two to five of 4.2 and 5.1: read the pre-beat state; register entry pulses by a \> ε; compute each component from the pre-beat state by its numeric law and register it by \|c\| \> ε; add the effective terms to update; a negative post-state leaves the domain of definition. In the listed region the cap of χ₀ is not triggered (the program asserts this at every beat). In the exact number system the operations involve no rounding, and addition does not depend on order. Induction on the number of beats gives the result.  
　(b) The host code corresponds item by item to Definition 3: Num.c takes the nearest double; value first accumulates the read positions with fl, then computes fl(k·s), then fl(b + ·); beat applies pulses first and processes after, adding them one by one to the pre-beat value. Induction on the number of beats gives the result. ∎  
Witness (Appendix B.1): for the χ₀ history (u1, rest, rest), the exact host gives x = 8/25; the binary64 host gives x = 0.32000000000000006, which is not equal to 8/25 under the upstream decimal reading either. The two relations must be signed off separately.

7\. Preservation of Qualification under Finite Precision

### 7.1  Atoms of the Decision Tree

The qualification decision tree of the upstream evaluator field\_final.run consists of the following atoms.  
　State-dependent numeric comparisons:  
　　effective pulse a \> ε;  
　　effective component \|c\| \> ε;  
　　pure-read condition of a linear law \|c\| ≤ ε;  
　　bilinear partner reading ≠ 0 (exactly nonzero, not replaced by ε);  
　　available position s(i) \> ε;  
　　process formation c \> ε and s(tgt) ≤ ε;  
　　leaving the domain of definition s′ \< 0;  
　　window-end retention s(i) \> ε.  
　Parameter check: coefficient k ≠ 0, decided exactly on the declared rational parameters, not through the run-time number system.  
　Non-numeric atoms: certified\_effect, certified\_read, path-edge certification, Ident, HistEv, formation order (beat indices). They are passed through unchanged and are not altered by numeric margins.  
The atoms are combined by strong Kleene rules into TraceSet, Part, HDyn and Q. The general evaluator feval of Appendix A.1 rewrites the upstream decision tree according to this table of atoms and hands the numeric operations and comparisons to a number-system backend.  
Definition 4 (single numeric entry and effective specification). The reading policy of the upstream field\_final.exact is: a float is taken as the rational given by its decimal representation repr (0.1 is read as 1/10); integers and Fractions are kept as they are.  
After the upstream validity check, feval.normalize normalizes all declared numbers (initial state, ε, pulse amplitudes, process coefficients and biases) into rationals once, by the same policy; the resulting specification is called the effective specification.  
The exact, binary64 and interval backends all operate only on the effective specification; binary64 and interval then convert from the rational values. Normalization is idempotent.  
Hence the exact backend and the upstream are the same effective model, and exact fallback returns to the upstream model.  
If floats were read in by their binary expansion values, the model would not be the same as upstream: in the single-process model with k = 0.1, b = 0.2, ε = 0.3, the component under the decimal reading is exactly equal to the threshold, and the upstream gives 0; read in by binary expansion values, the component exceeds the threshold and the verdict is 1. Appendix B.2 (3) checks this: after normalization both give 0, the same as their Fraction form.  
Verification (Appendix B.2 (1)(3)): the exact backend and the upstream field\_final.run give the same Q, model by model, on 6000 random models (294 of them leaving the domain of definition), and also on 3000 mixed-numeric-entry models, in which each number is changed to a float declaration with probability 1/2 (122 of them leaving the domain of definition).  
This is a finite comparison and covers only the numeric domain of its generator; sameness of meaning over the whole domain is given by the construction of Definition 4.  
Definition 5 (interval backend and safe evaluation). An interval \[lo, hi\] encloses the exact value with finite double endpoints:  
　parameters and initial state are converted from a rational r: let f be the nearest double to r; if f = r, take the single point \[f, f\]; otherwise take f and the neighbouring float of f on the side of r as the two endpoints, so that the interval encloses both r and f;  
　addition takes the sum of the endpoints, multiplication takes the minimum and maximum of the four endpoint products, and each is then moved outward to the neighbouring float (math.nextafter); additions and multiplications with exact zero are not moved outward;  
　comparison: an answer is given when the interval satisfies the comparison as a whole or fails it as a whole; otherwise it cannot be decided;  
　when conversion of a declared value overflows, or a non-finite value appears after an endpoint operation or an outward move, it cannot be decided.  
For a valid declaration, the safe evaluation safe\_run has only three outcomes:  
　(1) decided: every comparison is decided by the intervals and every endpoint is finite; Q is output;  
　(2) ⊥ (numerically unknown): some comparison cannot be decided, or the values cannot be represented with finite endpoints;  
　(3) error: an invalid declaration rejected by the upstream check, or leaving the domain of definition. Invalidity and numerical unrepresentability are reported separately.  
Theorem 2 (preservation theorem). Let a finite deterministic F specification be normalized by Definition 4 into an effective specification. The binary64 realization means the binary64 backend of Appendix A.1: parameters and initial state take the nearest doubles; a constant law takes b, a linear law takes fl(b + fl(k·s)) (s being the fl accumulation of the read positions in order), a bilinear law takes fl(b + fl(fl(k·s₁)·s₂)), and the effective components are added one by one to the pre-beat value in order of occurrence; for linear laws this is the operation graph of Definition 3. If the interval backend can decide every comparison, then:  
　(a) the Q of the interval backend equals the exact Q;  
　(b) in the binary64 realization above, every branch of the decision tree is the same as in the exact realization, so Q is the same; moreover, that realization does not overflow.  
Proof.  
　Inclusion invariant: at every beat, the exact value of every state and every intermediate quantity lies in its interval. The conversion of parameters and initial state encloses both the rational value and its nearest double (Definition 5). Between the exact result r of an endpoint operation and its round-to-nearest value fl(r) there is no other float, so the neighbouring float outward from fl(r) lies beyond r; this holds equally at binade boundaries and in the subnormal range. After this outward move, the sum of the endpoints for addition and the extremes of the four endpoint products for multiplication enclose all possible values of the exact result; additions and multiplications with exact zero give exact results and need no outward move.  
　Inclusion of binary64 values: the same intermediate quantity of the binary64 realization also lies in the same interval. Parameters and initial state are enclosed by Definition 5; when the operands lie in their intervals, the exact result lies between the exact extremes of the endpoint operations; rounding is monotone, so the binary64 result lies between the rounded endpoint values, inside the interval after the outward move. The endpoints are finite, so the binary64 values are finite and do not overflow.  
　Same branches: a comparison that the intervals can decide gives the same answer for every value in the interval, so the exact value and the binary64 value take the same branch. The parameter check is decided exactly on the rational parameters and the non-numeric atoms are passed through unchanged; both are the same in the two realizations.  
　Induction on the beats and on the levels of the decision tree, with strong Kleene combination, gives the same Q. ∎  
Scope: finite deterministic F specifications, finite windows, the three backends described and default round-to-nearest. General stochastic Hist is not covered, and no tolerance is introduced to change the upstream thresholds.  
Verification (Appendix B.2 (2)(3)):  
　Of 6000 random models, the interval decides 3515, all the same as exact; on the models that the interval decides completely, the binary64 verdicts are all the same as exact. For each of the 4 models whose binary64 and exact verdicts differ, the interval returns ⊥.  
　Of 3000 mixed-numeric-entry models, the interval decides 1751, all correctly, and the binary64 verdicts are all the same as exact.  
Theorem 3 (safe evaluation). Under the contract of Definition 5, safe evaluation returns only a verdict equal to the exact Q, or ⊥; it never gives a wrong verdict. On ⊥, falling back to the exact number system yields the exact verdict, at the cost of the resources of the exact realization.  
Proof. Outcome (1) is given by Theorem 2 (a). Endpoints are checked for finiteness after conversion, after operations and after outward moves, so no non-finite endpoint occurs in outcome (1). Outcome (2) gives no verdict. ∎  
Verification (Appendix B.2 (4)): initial state 10^400 (conversion overflow), initial state 2·MAX (conversion overflow) and initial state MAX plus pulse +1 (outward-move overflow) all return ⊥; exact fallback for 10^400 gives Q = 0; overflow in the outward move of an endpoint addition and overflow in an endpoint multiplication both yield “cannot be decided”.  
Conservativeness: interval arithmetic does not account for dependency. With cancelling processes, such as +β·δ and −λ·δ of χ₀, the envelope width grows by a factor of about 1 + β + λ per beat, while the centre is only multiplied by 1 + β − λ; the relative width thus grows by a factor of about (1 + β + λ)/(1 + β − λ) = 7/4 per beat, and on long windows the interval returns ⊥ (Realization Record 1 (6)). This is a safe unknown, not a negative determination. Safety is not decidability; exact fallback does not come with a uniform resource budget.

8\. Number-System Boundaries  
Proposition 1 (equality points). ε = 3/10, a single-process linear law, read position x = 1, coefficient 1/10, bias 2/10: the component is exactly 3/10, not greater than the threshold, and so ineffective; binary64 computes 0.2 + 0.1 as 0.30000000000000004, which is effective. Exact Q = 0, binary64 Q = 1, and the interval returns ⊥ (Appendix B.2 (5)).

Lemma 1 (eventual outcome of binary64 proportional decay, depending on the operation graph and the subnormal policy). The coefficient a ∈ (0, 1) of the multiplication graph and the coefficient k ∈ (−1, 0) of the addition graph are each stored floating-point parameters, declared separately; in general fl(a − 1) need not equal a − 1, and the two graphs are compared only when the listed a and k satisfy k = a − 1 exactly. Write N = 2^(−1022) (the smallest positive normal number) and q = 2^(−1074) (the subnormal spacing); default round-to-nearest-even.  
　(a) Multiplication graph x ← fl(a·x), subnormals retained; in the subnormal range the state is m·q, i.e. m ↦ round(a·m): for a ≤ 1/2 it reaches 0 in finitely many steps; for a \> 1/2 it never becomes 0 and stops at a positive integer fixed point.  
　(b) Addition graph x ← fl(x + fl(k·x)) (the operation graph of linear shallowing in the F layer), subnormals retained, i.e. m ↦ m + round(k·m): for k \< −1/2 it reaches 0 in finitely many steps; for k ≥ −1/2 it stops at a positive integer fixed point.  
　(c) The two graphs differ at a = 1/2 (k = −1/2): the multiplication graph goes to zero, while the addition graph stops at q.  
　(d) Strict descent in the normal range: for the multiplication graph with 0 \< a ≤ 1 − 2^(−52), fl(a·x) \< x for every normal x; for the addition graph with −1 \< k ≤ −2^(−52), 0 ≤ fl(x + fl(k·x)) \< x for every normal x. Hence, within these coefficient ranges, the classification of (a)(b) holds for every positive starting point.  
　(e) Flush-to-zero mode (round first, then flush: each operation first rounds to nearest even and then sets a resulting subnormal value to 0), from a normal starting point, with coefficients in the ranges of (d): the multiplication graph goes to zero in finitely many steps; the set of normal fixed points of the addition graph is exactly  
　　S\_k = {normal float x : \|RN(k·x)\| \< N} = {normal float x : \|k\|·x \< N − q/2},  
　　and after finitely many steps the orbit either goes to zero or stops at a normal value in S\_k; both occur, depending on the starting point.  
Proof.  
　(a) In the subnormal range, the round-to-nearest value of a·m·q is round(a·m)·q. For a \> 1/2, a·m \> 1/2, so the rounded value is at least 1; also a \< 1, so round(a·m) ≤ m; the orbit does not increase and stops at a positive integer fixed point. For a ≤ 1/2: m = 1 gives round(a) = 0 (a = 1/2 is a tie, and rounding to even gives 0); for m ≥ 2, round(a·m) ≤ round(m/2) ≤ m − 1, a strict descent, so the orbit reaches 0 in finitely many steps.  
　(b) fl(k·m·q) = round(k·m)·q, and adding it to m·q is exact, so m′ = m + round(k·m). For k \> −1, round(k·m) ≥ −m, so m′ ≥ 0. For k \< −1/2: m = 1 gives round(k) = −1, i.e. the orbit reaches 0; for m ≥ 2, m′ ≤ m + k·m + 1/2 \< m, a strict descent. For k ≥ −1/2: for m = 1 the decrement is round(k) = −0, so m′ = 1; for m ≥ 2, m′ ≥ m(1 + k) − 1/2 ≥ 1/2, so m′ ≥ 1; also m′ ≤ m, so the orbit stops at a positive integer fixed point.  
　(c) By (a)(b).  
　(d) Let g be the spacing between x and the float immediately below it. If x is not a power of 2, g \< 2^(−52)·x; if x = 2^e (e \> −1022), g = 2^(−53)·x; if x = N, g = q = 2^(−52)·x. Hence always g ≤ 2^(−52)·x, and x − g is a float.  
　　Multiplication graph: a·x ≤ x − 2^(−52)·x ≤ x − g, and rounding is monotone, so fl(a·x) ≤ x − g \< x.  
　　Addition graph: \|k·x\| ≥ 2^(−52)·x ≥ g, g is a float, and rounding is monotone and symmetric, so c = fl(k·x) ≤ −g, x + c ≤ x − g, and fl(x + c) ≤ x − g \< x. Also k·x \> −x, so c ≥ fl(−x) = −x and x + c ≥ 0.  
　　There are finitely many normal numbers, so from any normal starting point the orbit enters the subnormal range or 0 in finitely many steps, and from then on follows (a)(b). The two cases in which the orbit cannot pass directly from the normal range to 0: for the multiplication graph with a \> 1/2, a·x \> x/2 ≥ N/2, and rounding is monotone, so fl(a·x) ≥ fl(N/2) = N/2 \> 0; for the addition graph with k ≥ −1/2, k·x ≥ −x/2, so c ≥ fl(−x/2) ≥ −x/2 − q/2 (for x ≥ 2N, x/2 is a normal number and exact; for N ≤ x \< 2N, x/2 falls in the subnormal range and the rounding error does not exceed q/2), hence x + c ≥ x/2 − q/2 ≥ N/2 − q/2 \> q/2, and fl(x + c) ≥ q \> 0.  
　(e) Multiplication graph: by (d) the orbit strictly descends, and the first result that falls into the subnormal range is set to 0.  
　　Addition graph, the equivalence: the float immediately below N is N − q, whose significand is odd, and the midpoint of the two is N − q/2. Hence RN(v) \< N if and only if v \< N − q/2; v = N − q/2 is a tie, which rounds to the even value N, and so does not belong to S\_k.  
　　Points in S\_k all stall: RN(k·x) is subnormal or zero and is set to 0, and x + 0 = x.  
　　Points outside S\_k never stall: \|RN(k·x)\| ≥ N, so the component is a normal number and is not set to 0; also \|k\|·x ≥ 2^(−52)·x ≥ g, so by the argument of (d) fl(x + c) ≤ x − g \< x, and flushing to zero can only turn it into 0, which is still less than x.  
　　Hence outside S\_k the orbit strictly descends, and after finitely many steps it either falls into the subnormal range and is set to 0, or enters S\_k and stalls. ∎  
The coefficient conditions cannot be dropped: the multiplication graph with a = 1 − 2^(−53) (the only float in the interval (1 − 2^(−52), 1)) leaves N unchanged in one step, because a·N is exactly a tie and rounding to even returns N; the addition graph with k = −2^(−60) leaves 1.0 unchanged in one step and stays in the normal range.

A.1  feval.py  General number-system F-layer evaluator: single numeric entry, three backends (exact / binary64 / interval) and safe evaluation (Definitions 3, 4 and 5; Theorems 2 and 3)

~~~python
"""The Realization Form of Field Concretization: general number-system F-layer evaluator.
Rewrites, item by item, the decision tree of the upstream evaluator field_final.run in Appendix A.1 of The Mathematical Form of Field Concretization; numeric operations and comparisons are handed to a number-system backend:
  Exact    : exact rational arithmetic (same meaning as upstream);
  Float64  : IEEE 754 binary64, default round-to-nearest (Python float); parameters are first rounded to the nearest double;
  Interval : interval arithmetic whose double endpoints enclose the exact value; after each operation each endpoint moves outward by one ulp (math.nextafter);
             raises Undecided when a comparison cannot be decided by the intervals, a declared value lies outside the finite range, or an endpoint (including after the outward move) is non-finite. Safe evaluation returns only the verdict the exact value gives, or ⊥.
Every state-dependent comparison in the decision tree goes through the backend: the effective-change threshold, exact nonzero (bilinear partner reading), the pure-read condition, available positions,
formation registration, leaving the domain of definition, and window-end retention. The check that the coefficient k ≠ 0 is decided exactly on the declared rational parameter (parameters are not run state).
Certification constants (cert_read, path edges, Ident, HistEv) are not numbers: they are passed through unchanged and are not altered by numeric margins."""
import math
from dataclasses import replace
from fractions import Fraction
from collections import defaultdict
import field_final as up

def normalize(x):
    """Single numeric entry: following the policy of upstream field_final.exact (a float is read as the rational given by its decimal representation repr; ints and Fractions are kept as they are),
    normalize all declared numbers (initial state, threshold, pulse amplitudes, process coefficients and biases) into one rational effective specification.
    Every backend (exact, binary64, interval) operates only on this specification; the binary64 and interval values are then converted from the rational values."""
    E = up.exact
    return replace(x, initial=tuple(E(v) for v in x.initial), epsilon=E(x.epsilon),
                   pulses=tuple(replace(p, amount=E(p.amount)) for p in x.pulses),
                   processes=tuple(replace(m, coefficient=E(m.coefficient), bias=E(m.bias)) for m in x.processes))

class Undecided(Exception):
    pass

class Exact:
    name = 'exact'
    conv = staticmethod(lambda x: Fraction(x))
    add = staticmethod(lambda a, b: a + b)
    mul = staticmethod(lambda a, b: a * b)
    neg = staticmethod(lambda a: -a)
    gt = staticmethod(lambda a, b: a > b)        # a > b
    nonzero = staticmethod(lambda a: a != 0)
    absv = staticmethod(abs)

class Float64:
    name = 'binary64'
    conv = staticmethod(lambda x: float(Fraction(x)))
    add = staticmethod(lambda a, b: a + b)
    mul = staticmethod(lambda a, b: a * b)
    neg = staticmethod(lambda a: -a)
    gt = staticmethod(lambda a, b: a > b)
    nonzero = staticmethod(lambda a: a != 0)
    absv = staticmethod(abs)

def _fin(x):
    if not math.isfinite(x):                    # endpoint overflow or NaN: safely return unknown, give no verdict
        raise Undecided('interval endpoint non-finite (the value cannot be represented with finite binary64 endpoints)')
    return x

def _down(x):                                   # checked before and after the outward move: the operation result and the moved result must both be finite
    return _fin(math.nextafter(_fin(x), -math.inf))

def _up(x):
    return _fin(math.nextafter(_fin(x), math.inf))

class Interval:
    """Interval (lo, hi), lo ≤ exact value ≤ hi. The round-to-nearest error is at most half an ulp, so moving outward by one ulp after each operation suffices to enclose the exact value."""
    name = 'interval'
    @staticmethod
    def conv(x):
        x = Fraction(x)
        try:
            f = float(x)
        except OverflowError:                   # a legitimate exact declared value lies outside the finite binary64 range: the value is not representable; return unknown
            raise Undecided('declared value outside the finite binary64 range')
        _fin(f)
        lo = f if Fraction(f) <= x else _down(f)
        hi = f if Fraction(f) >= x else _up(f)
        return (lo, hi)
    @staticmethod
    def add(a, b):
        if a == (0.0, 0.0):            # adding an exact zero is exact; no outward move needed
            return b
        if b == (0.0, 0.0):
            return a
        return (_down(a[0] + b[0]), _up(a[1] + b[1]))
    @staticmethod
    def mul(a, b):
        if a == (0.0, 0.0) or b == (0.0, 0.0):   # multiplying by an exact zero gives an exact zero
            return (0.0, 0.0)
        ps = [a[0] * b[0], a[0] * b[1], a[1] * b[0], a[1] * b[1]]
        return (_down(min(ps)), _up(max(ps)))
    @staticmethod
    def neg(a):
        return (-a[1], -a[0])
    @staticmethod
    def gt(a, b):
        if a[0] > b[1]:
            return True
        if a[1] <= b[0]:
            return False
        raise Undecided('comparison a > b cannot be decided by the intervals')
    @staticmethod
    def nonzero(a):
        if a[0] > 0 or a[1] < 0:
            return True
        if a[0] == 0 and a[1] == 0:
            return False
        raise Undecided('nonzero check cannot be decided by the intervals')
    @staticmethod
    def absv(a):
        if a[0] >= 0:
            return a
        if a[1] <= 0:
            return (-a[1], -a[0])
        return (0.0, max(-a[0], a[1]))

def run(x, B=Exact, trace=None):
    """Same decision tree as field_final.run; B is the number-system backend. trace (a list) can collect the beat-by-beat atoms, for checking realization records."""
    up.check(x)                     # invalid declaration: raises ValueError, not swallowed as unknown
    x = normalize(x)                # single numeric entry (same effective model as upstream)
    eps = B.conv(x.epsilon)
    zero = B.conv(0)
    state = {s: B.conv(v) for s, v in zip(x.sites, x.initial)}
    formation = defaultdict(list)
    signal_marks = defaultdict(list)
    part, hdyn = [], []
    active_T = set(x.declared_T)
    for t in range(x.window[0] - 1, x.window[-1] + 1):
        before = dict(state)
        contributions = defaultdict(list)
        for p in x.pulses:
            if p.step != t:
                continue
            a = B.conv(p.amount)
            if p.certified is True and B.gt(a, eps):
                contributions[p.target].append((p.name, a))
                formation[p.target].append((p.name, t, p.external))
                if p.external:
                    signal_marks[p.target].append((p.name, t))
                if trace is not None:
                    trace.append(('pulse', t, p.name, 'effective'))
        for m in x.processes:
            if m.step != t:
                continue
            k, b = Fraction(m.coefficient), Fraction(m.bias)
            kB, bB = B.conv(k), B.conv(b)
            if m.law == 'constant':
                value = bB
            elif m.law == 'linear':
                acc = zero
                for s in m.reads:
                    acc = B.add(acc, before[s])
                value = B.add(bB, B.mul(kB, acc))
            else:
                value = B.add(bB, B.mul(B.mul(kB, before[m.reads[0]]), before[m.reads[1]]))
            eff = B.gt(B.absv(value), eps)
            is_action = up.tri_and(eff, m.certified_effect)
            support = ()
            if m.law == 'linear' and (k != 0 or not eff):
                support = m.reads
            elif m.law == 'bilinear' and k != 0:
                support = tuple(s for i, s in enumerate(m.reads) if B.nonzero(before[m.reads[1 - i]]))
            available = [s for s in support if s in active_T and B.gt(before[s], eps)
                         and any(a[1] < t for a in formation[s])]
            is_cond = up.tri_and(bool(available), m.certified_read)
            action_support = ()
            if m.law == 'linear' and k != 0:
                action_support = m.reads
            elif m.law == 'bilinear' and k != 0:
                action_support = tuple(s for i, s in enumerate(m.reads) if B.nonzero(before[m.reads[1 - i]]))
            action_available = [s for s in action_support if s in active_T and B.gt(before[s], eps)
                                and any(a[1] < t for a in formation[s])]
            action_nodes = [f'state:{s}:{t}' for s in action_available]
            action_route = up.path_status(x.links, action_nodes, m.name, x.closed_graph) if action_nodes else False
            is_hist = up.tri_and(bool(action_available), m.certified_read, action_route)
            if is_action is True:
                contributions[m.target].append((m.name, value))
                if B.gt(value, eps) and not B.gt(before[m.target], eps):
                    formation[m.target].append((m.name, t, not m.carrier_inside))
                if not m.carrier_inside:
                    signal_marks[m.target].append((m.name, t))
            if t in x.window:
                if m.carrier_inside:
                    part.append(is_cond)
                hdyn.append(up.tri_and(m.target in active_T, is_action, is_hist))
            if trace is not None:
                trace.append(('proc', t, m.name, bool(is_action), is_cond, is_hist))
        for s in x.sites:
            new = before[s]
            for _, v in contributions[s]:
                new = B.add(new, v)
            if B.gt(zero, new):
                raise ValueError('negative trace depth')
            state[s] = new
        if trace is not None:
            trace.append(('post', t, dict(state)))
    ts = x.declared_T
    eligible = all(B.gt(state[s], eps) and formation[s] for s in ts)
    sources = {o for site in ts for o, _ in signal_marks[site]}
    trace_v = up.tri_and(bool(sources), eligible, x.identity, x.trace_history)
    pw, hw = up.tri_or(part), up.tri_or(hdyn)
    return {'trace': trace_v, 'part': pw, 'hdyn': hw, 'Q': up.tri_and(trace_v, pw, hw), 'state': state}

def safe_run(x):
    """Safe evaluation: interval backend.
    When it returns decided=True, every comparison was decided by the intervals and every endpoint is finite, and Q equals the exact value;
    when a comparison cannot be decided, a declared value lies outside the finite binary64 range, or an endpoint operation or outward move overflows, it returns Q = '⊥' (numerically unknown), and an exact fallback is possible;
    an invalid declaration (rejected by upstream check) and leaving the domain of definition raise ValueError as usual and are not swallowed as unknown."""
    try:
        r = run(x, Interval)
    except Undecided as e:
        return {'Q': '⊥', 'decided': False, 'why': str(e)}
    assert all(math.isfinite(e) for v in r['state'].values() for e in v)
    return {'Q': r['Q'], 'decided': True}
~~~

~~~python
"""The Realization Form of Field Concretization E1: algorithmic-level host programs.
What is realized is the algorithmic-level object itself (not a simulation of some physical system): the program's state variables are the Structural Positions, its process functions are the mechanism processes,
and its entry functions are the signal entries. Each beat, following 4.2 of The Mathematical Form of Field Concretization, reads the pre-beat state, computes each component, registers by the effectiveness threshold and updates; the execution log records, beat by beat,
the pulses and processes that actually occurred (label, carrier, action target, read positions, numeric law, component, whether effective). The program stores no verdict.
The verdict is computed after the execution log is transcribed into an upstream F run specification: by the upstream evaluator field_final.run (exact number system), or by the
same decision tree in the host's own number system (feval, binary64). Number system: 'exact' (Fraction) or 'binary64' (Python float)."""
from fractions import Fraction
import field_final as up

class Num:
    def __init__(self, kind):
        assert kind in ('exact', 'binary64')
        self.kind, self.ops = kind, 0
    def c(self, x):
        return Fraction(x) if self.kind == 'exact' else float(Fraction(x))
    def add(self, a, b):
        self.ops += 1
        return a + b
    def mul(self, a, b):
        self.ops += 1
        return a * b

class Host:
    """Generic host-program framework: sites, procs (label → (carrier inside B?, action target, law, read positions, k, b, run condition)), entry pulse table."""
    sites = ()
    procs = {}
    pulses = {}          # letter → [(label, action target, amplitude, out-of-boundary?)]
    eps = Fraction(0)
    def __init__(self, kind, init):
        self.N = Num(kind)
        self.state = {s: self.N.c(v) for s, v in zip(self.sites, init)}
        self.log = []
        self.t = 0
    def value(self, pid, pre):
        inside, tgt, law, reads, k, b, cond = self.procs[pid]
        k, b = self.N.c(k), self.N.c(b)
        if law == 'constant':
            return b
        acc = self.N.c(0)
        for s in reads:
            acc = self.N.add(acc, pre[s])
        return self.N.add(b, self.N.mul(k, acc))
    def beat(self, letter, knock=(), loc=None):
        """One beat. knock: labels of the processes or pulses withdrawn in this beat (implementation of MOD); loc: before this beat, set a position to a value (implementation of LOC)."""
        if loc:
            for s, v in loc.items():
                self.state[s] = self.N.c(v)
        pre = dict(self.state)
        eps = self.N.c(self.eps)
        ev = {'t': self.t, 'letter': letter, 'pre': pre, 'pulses': [], 'procs': []}
        terms = {s: [] for s in self.sites}      # effective components of this beat, in order of occurrence (pulses first, then processes)
        for (pid, tgt, amt, ext) in self.pulses.get(letter, []):
            if pid in knock:
                continue
            a = self.N.c(amt)
            eff = a > eps
            ev['pulses'].append((pid, tgt, amt, ext, eff))
            if eff:
                terms[tgt].append(a)
        for pid, (inside, tgt, law, reads, k, b, cond) in self.procs.items():
            if not cond(letter) or pid in knock:
                continue
            c = self.value(pid, pre)
            eff = abs(c) > eps
            ev['procs'].append((pid, inside, tgt, law, reads, k, b, c, eff))
            if eff:
                terms[tgt].append(c)
        for s in self.sites:                     # add term by term to the pre-beat value (same operation graph as 4.2 and feval: ((pre + t1) + t2) + …)
            new = pre[s]
            for v in terms[s]:
                new = self.N.add(new, v)
            self.state[s] = new
            if self.state[s] < 0:
                raise ValueError('negative trace depth')
        ev['post'] = dict(self.state)
        self.log.append(ev)
        self.t += 1
        return ev
    def to_spec(self, w, T, links_cert=True):
        """Transcribe the execution log into an upstream F run specification: entry beat = the last beat in the log before W; W = the last w beats.
        Used only when the formative history is 1 beat (log length w + 1); in that case it has the same meaning as the upstream F run."""
        assert len(self.log) == w + 1
        pulses, procs, links = [], [], []
        for ev in self.log:
            t = ev['t']
            for (pid, tgt, amt, ext, eff) in ev['pulses']:
                pulses.append(up.Pulse(f'{pid}@{t}', t, tgt, Fraction(amt), ext))
            for (pid, inside, tgt, law, reads, k, b, c, eff) in ev['procs']:
                name = f'{pid}@{t}'
                procs.append(up.Process(name, t, tgt, inside, law, tuple(reads), Fraction(k), Fraction(b), True, True))
                if t >= 1:
                    for r in reads:
                        links.append(up.Link(f'state:{r}:{t}', name, links_cert))
        init = tuple(Fraction(self.log[0]['pre'][s]) for s in self.sites)
        return up.Spec(sites=self.sites, initial=init, window=tuple(range(1, w + 1)), pulses=tuple(pulses),
                       processes=tuple(procs), links=tuple(links), epsilon=self.eps, declared_T=tuple(T))
~~~

~~~python
"""The Reachability Form of Field Concretization: alphabet-gated F model, window reading, augmented reachability search and brute-force comparison (exact rational arithmetic, strong Kleene three-valued logic)
~~~
