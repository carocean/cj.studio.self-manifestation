# The Grading Form of Tendency Types

1\. Declaration of Type  
This paper is of the single-axis stratified type among Grading Forms. The criterion axis is a single quantity: the pairwise direction sign of a Trace-Response pair  
　σᵢⱼ(x) = sgn\[(sᵢ − sⱼ)(rᵢ − rⱼ)\], taken as + at a tie (sᵢ = sⱼ) with rᵢ ≠ rⱼ.  
The sign is + (separating) when share and relative growth are in the same order, − (merging) when they are in reverse order, and 0 (neutral) when the relative growths are equal. Hosts are graded by the sign set of σ on the declared domain R, Σ(χ) = {σᵢⱼ(x) : x ∈ R, i ≠ j}.  
　1\. The four grades are defined by the sign set: {0}; contains + and ⊆ {0, +}; contains − and ⊆ {0, −}; contains both + and −. This is the sign support of a single quantity, not a splice of two criteria.  
　2\. The four grades are mutually exclusive, exhaustive, and each nonempty; no total order is set among them. The three names rising, flat and falling are sign labels and do not constitute an order of grades.  
　3\. A tendency type is a property of (host, declared level, family Φ, declared domain B\_dyn, stationary inflow), not of the medium alone; B\_dyn is fixed according to Section 5.  
　4\. What is graded is the tendency direction, that is, whether the pattern of activity shares goes on converging, spreading out, or staying unchanged; whether a host has a tendency at all is not adjudicated by this grading (H3).  
Notation: the net update law is written Ψ (an autonomous ordinary differential equation ẋ = Ψ(x)), to be distinguished from the F layer of *The Mathematical Form of Field Concretization* (a finite separable numerical model with pulses, process components, carriers and sources). The physical boundary of a host is written B\_phys (the B in χ = (P, L, B, W) of the General Form), and the forward-invariant domain of states is written B\_dyn; a route “inside the boundary” means inside B\_phys, not inside B\_dyn. In the slime-mould model, Dᵢ is the conductance (the adaptive variable).  
Theoretical increment and resistance: T1, T3, T4 and T6 are elementary arguments; T5 combines a weighted Chebyshev sum inequality with the Schur–Ostrowski condition. The core of this paper is the transposition theorem (T4, a necessary condition for strict reversal) and monotonicity of concentration (T5); in addition there are the tie lemma and its corollaries (in the mathematical vector-field class with a fixed share map, sign-definite types containing qualified tie points have empty interior, and their totality is nowhere dense), the variability of tendency type under restricted closed coarse-graining (T6 (c)), the domain lemma for the maximal-domain entry conditions together with representative examples of each grade, and the rules for the declared domain. All conclusions carry the conditions H1–H4.

### 2.6  Entry Conditions

　(1) Share map: s is C¹ on an open set containing B\_dyn, with sᵢ \> 0.  
　(2) Lipschitz open domain and precompactness: there is an open set U ⊂ ℝᴺ containing B\_dyn on which Ψ is locally Lipschitz (solutions are unique forward and backward, and the flow depends continuously on initial values); B\_dyn is forward invariant, and every forward orbit starting from B\_dyn is precompact in U (its closure is a compact subset of U). U may contain boundary points of B\_dyn; the topological space for precompactness is U, and limit points of orbits must lie in U.  
　(3) Share-stationary set A₀: the set of points of B\_dyn that lie in ω-limit sets and on whose whole orbit v ≡ 0. R = (B\_dyn \\ A₀) ∩ {sᵢ \> 0 ∀i}, R ≠ ∅, and the part where all shares are positive is forward invariant. Where a share falls to 0 in finite time, the time window is divided into segments and the family is replaced by a smaller one.  
　(4) The Trace-Responses of the family Φ satisfy Tr^rest = 1 (H1), as operationalized evidence; this does not replace the three duties of 2.4.  
　(5) A thresholded read-out (such as κ = max(g − θ, 0)) is not differentiable at the threshold and can drive a share to 0 in finite time; it must be declared in segments, each segment satisfying the conditions of this section separately. The family Φ is fixed within the declared time window.  
　(6) Hosts whose inflow is not stationary, or whose shares are rewritten from outside the boundary, do not enter this grading.  
　(7) Model classes used for Corollary 2′ of T8 additionally require uniform precompactness: the union of the forward orbits starting from each compact subset of B\_dyn is precompact in U.

3\. Fixing the Prior Facts  
The structure graded: at the declared level, the Trace-Responses jointly receive one stationary shared inflow, activity is allocated by shares, the Trace-Responses deepen and shallow with activity, and the shares change accordingly. The activity share s is an operationalized reading of the mode of organization (H2); s is not taken as the mode of organization itself.

4\. Grading Criterion and Definitions of the Grades  
4.1  Tendency Direction of a Trace-Response Pair (any pair i ≠ j)  
　Separating: (sᵢ − sⱼ)(rᵢ − rⱼ) \> 0; or sᵢ = sⱼ with rᵢ ≠ rⱼ (a tie being pulled apart).  
　Merging: (sᵢ − sⱼ)(rᵢ − rⱼ) \< 0.  
　Neutral: rᵢ = rⱼ.

### 4.2  State Tendency Direction

Rising: no merging pair and some separating pair; falling: no separating pair and some merging pair; flat: every pair neutral; mixed: both separating and merging pairs.

### 4.3  Host Tendency Types (on R)

　Rising type: the sign set contains + and not −; nowhere on R is there a merging pair, and there is a separating pair.  
　Flat type: the sign set is {0}; everywhere on R every pair is neutral.  
　Falling type: the sign set contains − and not +; nowhere on R is there a separating pair, and there is a merging pair.  
　Indefinite type: the sign set contains both + and −; R has both separating pairs and merging pairs. This refers only to tendency directions of opposite sign on the declared domain, not to randomness, nor to indeterminacy of the dynamical law.  
4.4  Differences between Grades Are Structural Differences  
The four types are distinguished by the structure in which positive and negative direction signs occur on the declared domain: neither, positive only, negative only, and both, corresponding to flat, rising, falling and indefinite respectively. T3–T5 give the dynamical consequences that each type guarantees: in the flat type the shares are frozen (T3); in the rising type the majorization order does not decrease, and in the falling type it does not increase (T5); a strict reversal of the same pair can occur only in the indefinite type (corollary of T4), though not every indefinite type undergoes a strict reversal. These consequences are one-way conclusions derived from the tendency type and do not on their own form sufficient criteria for it; for example, a majorization order that does not decrease over the whole domain does not imply the rising type. The distinction between rising and falling is a distinction in the sign of σ, not in a degree of correlation.

5\. Rules for the Declared Domain  
Breach attempt: taking different legitimate forward-invariant domains for the same host can yield different tendency types. Example: the slime-mould model of type I with m = 2 and two tubes of unequal length L = (1, 1.05). Taking B\_dyn to be the open positive quadrant gives the indefinite type (6.4(3)); taking B\_dyn = {q₁ \> q₂} (qᵢ = (Dᵢ/Lᵢ)/Σⱼ(Dⱼ/Lⱼ)) gives the rising type: in this domain r₁ − r₂ = (D₁/L₁² − D₂/L₂²)/K² (K = ΣDⱼ/Lⱼ), which is positive because D₁/L₁ \> D₂/L₂ and L₁ \< L₂, so the pair is separating everywhere; moreover r₁ − r₂ \> 0 on the tie line, so this domain is forward invariant.  
Rules:  
　1\. Principal reading: B\_dyn must be declared before typing, and is taken to be the interior X of all admissible states at the declared level (fixed by the state variables and their physical ranges, whether or not the declared history reaches them), e.g. the open positive quadrant for two tubes and the open positive orthant for local families; it must satisfy the Lipschitz open-domain and precompactness conditions of 2.6(2). The entry conditions of the principal reading must be proved separately for each representative example (the domain lemma) and cannot be replaced by forward invariance of some small bounded domain. Throughout this paper every instance is typed by the principal reading.  
　2\. Shrinking B\_dyn to obtain a sign-definite type is a prohibited formulation (E2).  
　3\. Basin-restricted tendency type: whoever, for physical reasons, cares only about one basin of attraction declares a basin-restricted tendency type and at the same time reports the tendency type on the maximal domain; a basin-restricted tendency type may not be promoted to the tendency type of the host.  
　4\. Reachable tendency type: typing on the set of states actually reachable from s₀ under the declared history is only a supplementary report and does not replace the principal reading.  
Under these rules, the tendency type of the example above is the indefinite type.  
Convention on A₀: removing the share-stationary set is a convention. On A₀, v = 0, which adds only 0 to the sign set: for types other than flat, adding or deleting all-neutral points does not change the classification; for an all-neutral model it decides whether R is nonempty and must be declared separately. Only share-stationary points are removed, not closed orbits or limit cycles: hosts whose shares oscillate back and forth stay in R and are judged indefinite. Faces on which a share is 0 are treated as smaller families and typed separately.  
Principal reading and observation: the principal reading types by Ψ on all admissible states of X. In model mode Ψ is declared and typing raises no such problem. In measurement, within an arbitrary model class with no additional certified structural constraints, readings covering only a strict subdomain generally do not determine the principal-reading tendency type. Legitimate settings over the whole domain, with the corresponding share-velocity readings, are an idealized route of sufficient information (local settings in the sense of 𝒴₁+LOC of *The Indistinguishability Form of Field Concretization*), but not the only route: if a model has been certified to belong to some parametric family, the known functional form, parameter certification and growth-rate differences at a few states can fix the type through analytic monotonicity; consistent readings of a continuous law on a dense observable domain can also yield whole-domain information. Having the capacity for settings is not the same as having completed whole-domain certification; finite samples prove only that the observed tendency directions exist (E5).

6\. Theorems and Proofs

### 6.1  T0 (R Is Forward Invariant)

Under the entry conditions of 2.6, R is forward invariant.  
Proof: write Fl\_t for the flow of Ψ. It suffices to show: if x ∈ B\_dyn, t \> 0 and Fl\_t(x) = y ∈ A₀, then x ∈ A₀. Since y ∈ A₀, y ∈ ω(z) for some z ∈ B\_dyn, so there are tₙ → ∞ with Fl\_{tₙ}(z) → y. Ψ is locally Lipschitz on U, y ∈ U, Fl\_{−t}(y) = x is defined, and Fl\_{−t} is continuous on a neighbourhood of y; hence Fl\_{tₙ−t}(z) = Fl\_{−t}(Fl\_{tₙ}(z)) → x, that is, x ∈ ω(z). x and y lie on the same whole orbit, and v ≡ 0 on the whole orbit of y, so the same holds for x, and x ∈ A₀. Hence B\_dyn \\ A₀ is forward invariant; intersecting with the forward-invariant part where all shares are positive, R is forward invariant. ∎  
6.2  T1 (Mutual Exclusivity and Exhaustiveness)  
T1a: the four sign conditions are pairwise disjoint. ∎  
T1b: when R ≠ ∅, Σ(χ) is nonempty and Σ ⊆ {−, 0, +}; according to whether it contains + and whether it contains −, it falls into exactly one of the four. n ≥ 2 and R ≠ ∅ cannot be omitted; R ≠ ∅ ensures that the flat type does not hold vacuously. ∎

### 6.3  Domain Lemma and Witnesses

Domain lemma (maximal-domain entry conditions for share laws of type β = 1)  
Let the state be x ∈ X = (0, ∞)ⁿ, n ≥ 2, with positive weights L₁, …, Lₙ, shares qᵢ(x) = (xᵢ/Lᵢ)/Σⱼ(xⱼ/Lⱼ), and net law Ψᵢ(x) = φᵢ(qᵢ(x)) − λxᵢ (λ \> 0), where each φᵢ: \[0, 1\] → \[0, φ̄\] is continuous and strictly increasing with φᵢ(0) = 0, C¹ on (0, 1\], and φᵢ′ \> 0 on (0, 1). Then:  
　(i) X is forward invariant, and xᵢ(t) ≤ max(xᵢ(0), φ̄/λ); the total S = Σxᵢ satisfies S(t) ≥ min(S(0), m/λ), where m = minᵢ φᵢ(1/n) \> 0. Both bounds are uniform for initial states in compact sets.  
　(ii) If each φᵢ is Lipschitz on \[0, 1\], let U = {x ∈ ℝⁿ : xᵢ \> −η ∀i, Σⱼ max(xⱼ, 0) \> 0} (η \> 0) and define qᵢ with max(xⱼ, 0) in place of xⱼ (so that qᵢ ∈ \[0, 1\]); then Ψ is locally Lipschitz on U and U ⊃ X; the union of the orbits starting from a compact subset K of X lies in {xᵢ ≥ 0, xᵢ ≤ upper bound, S ≥ min(min\_K S, m/λ)}, which is a compact subset of U, so the orbits are uniformly precompact in U (limit points may lie on the boundary of X, but within U).  
　(iii) If each φᵢ satisfies φᵢ(q) ≥ κq^γ near 0 (0 \< γ \< 1, κ \> 0), then the orbits starting from a compact subset K of X uniformly satisfy xᵢ(t) ≥ min(min\_K xᵢ, cᵢ) \> 0; the union of the orbits is precompact in X; take U = X, and Ψ is locally Lipschitz on X.  
　(iv) Under (ii) or (iii), the share map q is C¹ on a neighbourhood of X; the part where all shares are positive is X itself and is forward invariant; A₀ consists of the share-stationary points in X; R ≠ ∅ except for all-neutral models.  
Proof: (i) At xᵢ = 0, Ψᵢ = 0, and Ψᵢ ≥ −λxᵢ gives xᵢ(t) ≥ xᵢ(0)e^{−λt}, so X is forward invariant; Ψᵢ ≤ φ̄ − λxᵢ gives the upper bound; since Σqᵢ = 1, some qᵢ ≥ 1/n, so Ṡ = Σφᵢ(qᵢ) − λS ≥ m − λS, which gives the lower bound. (ii) Near every point of U the denominator Σmax(xⱼ, 0)/Lⱼ has a positive lower bound, so qᵢ is locally Lipschitz and φᵢ ∘ qᵢ is a composition of Lipschitz functions; the compact set described follows from (i). (iii) On \[δ, 1\], φᵢ(q)/q^γ has a positive minimum; taking the smaller of this and the κ near 0 gives a positive lower bound κ\* on \[0, 1\]: φᵢ(q) ≥ κ*q^γ. By (i), S ≤ S̄ and qᵢ ≥ L\_min xᵢ/(Lᵢ S̄), so Ψᵢ ≥ κ′xᵢ^γ − λxᵢ with κ′ = κ*(L\_min/(Lᵢ S̄))^γ; take cᵢ = (κ′/λ)^{1/(1−γ)}: when xᵢ \< cᵢ, Ψᵢ \> 0, so xᵢ does not fall below min(xᵢ(0), cᵢ). (iv) Immediate. ∎  
Application: local families with β = 1 and φ(q) = (Iq)^α: α ≥ 1 falls under (ii), 0 \< α \< 1 under (iii) (γ = α). The slime-mould model of type I, f = q^m (λ = 1): m ≥ 1 under (ii), m \< 1 under (iii). Type II, f = 31q³/(1 + 30q³), falls under (ii); the constructed example f(q) = q^{0.2}e^{2.5q}/e^{2.5} under (iii) (γ = 0.2); unequal lengths L = (1, 1.05) with m = 2 under (ii). The write laws (Iq)^α and 2^{1−α}(Iq)^α of the coarse system of T6 (c) fall under (ii).  
Exclusion: parameters of local families with β ≠ 1 are outside this lemma; for them only the sign formula is issued, not the full set of entry conditions. Example: α = 3, β = 1/2, I = λ = 1, initial state (0.1, 1): αβ \> 1, the share s₁ tends monotonically to 0, and the orbit tends to the boundary point (0, 1); but Ψ₂(h, 1) = (1 + √h)^{−3} − 1 and \|Ψ₂(h, 1) − Ψ₂(0, 1)\|/h ≈ 3/√h → ∞, so there is no locally Lipschitz extension containing (0, 1), and the entry conditions of the principal reading are not satisfied (numerical comparison in Appendix B.5).  
Mechanism witness (model mode, relation layer)  
For every representative example of domain-lemma type, declare:  
　Physical boundary B\_phys: the medium (units or tube network); the Structural Positions x₁, …, xₙ are inside the boundary; T = {1, …, n}; resolution ε = 0, so any nonzero component is effective.  
　Actual formative history: at the entry beat t₀ − 1 an external signal pulse forms each xᵢ from 0 to aᵢ \> 0 (entering Form and Mark); the actual initial state is a ∈ X.  
　Window W = \[t₀, t₁\], t₁ \> t₀; the inflow I is declared as a fixed external supply, and the Trace-Response source is witnessed by signal entry (2.4).  
　Processes (all carriers inside B\_phys): for each i, a write process wᵢ with target xᵢ and law φᵢ(qᵢ(x)), reading every xⱼ; a shallowing process dᵢ with target xᵢ and law −λxᵢ, reading xᵢ. The evidence edges are the canonical edges; POS and REC are declared; Ident and HistEv are taken as 1.  
Check: Act(wᵢ): φᵢ(qᵢ) \> 0 on X. HistCond(wᵢ): ∂(φᵢ ∘ qᵢ)/∂xᵢ = φᵢ′(qᵢ)·qᵢ(1 − qᵢ)/xᵢ ≠ 0 (n ≥ 2 and a positive state give 0 \< qᵢ \< 1); the read position xᵢ exceeds ε and was formed before W, so the available positions are nonempty; the same process wᵢ has both Act and HistCond and its target belongs to T, so HDyn = 1. Part: the conditioning support of wᵢ contains available positions and its carrier is inside B\_phys, so Part = 1. TraceSet: Src—the signal-entry pulses leave Mark on T; Ret—forward invariance of X gives xᵢ(t₁⁺) \> 0 = ε, and Form is nonempty; hence TraceSet = 1. In-boundary: HistCond of wᵢ passes through reads within B\_phys and has no out-of-boundary component; the route lies entirely within B\_phys. This template is a declared model with mechanisms and is not derived from Ψ (2.5(4)); Tr^rest is listed separately, only as a capacity predicate.  
Beat-wise reading: on entry into the R layer, the flow map of duration Δ serves as the one-beat transition, W takes the beats t₀, …, t₁, and the entry beat is t₀ − 1; the one-beat component of each process is its integral over the beat, ∫φᵢ(qᵢ(x(τ)))dτ for the write and ∫−λxᵢ(τ)dτ for shallowing, and their sum is the one-beat increment. This process-wise integration within the beat is a declared decomposition (with ε = 0 it does not affect any of the judgments). On X, φᵢ(qᵢ) \> 0 and λxᵢ \> 0, so every component is nonzero for any Δ \> 0. Ψ is C¹ in X, and the flow is C¹ in the beat-start state; as Δ → 0, on the compact set swept by the actual orbit within the finite window W, the partial derivative of the write component divided by Δ with respect to the beat-start state converges uniformly to ∂(φᵢ ∘ qᵢ)/∂xᵢ, which is continuous on that compact set and bounded away from 0; hence there is a Δ₀ \> 0 such that for Δ ∈ (0, Δ₀) the dependence of the write component on xᵢ is nonzero, and the checks above hold under the beat-wise reading as well. The Tr^rest witness uses the same Δ, and that witness holds for every Δ \> 0. Continuous transitions are not within the solvable domain of the R layer; this paper replaces solving with analytic checks.  
Tr^rest witness (model mode; entry state pulses under the same fixed supply)  
The background supply I = I₀ is the same for every letter, on both sides and in the rest tail, so the net law Ψ used for typing does not change. Signal-entry letter w: at the start of the beat a state pulse P\_w(x) = x + η is applied from outside B\_phys to the declared entry positions, where η ∈ \[0, ∞)ⁿ is a fixed nonzero vector (of the same kind as the signal entry in the formative history); rest is P\_rest(x) = x. With the rest flow Fl\_t (the flow of Ψ), define the one-beat transition Π\_u(x) = Fl\_Δ(P\_u(x)), with Δ \> 0 fixed. P\_u preserves X and the rest flow preserves X (domain lemma (i)), so every finite word is legitimate and 𝓗 is taken to be all finite sequences. Take the common starting point a = s₀ ∈ X (the declared starting point of 3.1(2) of the Adjudication Form); the witness pair ((w), (rest)) differs at only 1 position, so it qualifies for any declared k ≥ 1 and H\_max ≥ 1, with q any finite nonnegative integer and 0 ≤ r \< 1; the model is deterministic with no out-of-boundary component, and clamping is the identity (*The Adjudication Form of the Sedimentation-Field* 3.3). After q rest beats (q is the rest-window parameter of the Adjudication Form and r its resolution threshold, unrelated to the shares qᵢ and the relative growths rᵢ), the states on the two sides are Fl\_{(q+1)Δ}(a + η) and Fl\_{(q+1)Δ}(a). The initial states differ; if the two states were equal, continuing back along both orbits to time 0 by local backward uniqueness would give a + η = a, a contradiction; so the two states differ. Both sides are point distributions, the total variation of the in-boundary joint distributions is 1 \> r, and Tr^rest = 1. Tr^rest compares the joint distributions of all in-boundary states; this witness does not treat the joint difference as a marginal difference in every coordinate; where a witness for each Structural Position is needed, entries and measurements are given item by item according to the declared reading blocks. This construction applies to all representative examples of 6.4 and to the coarse system of T6 (c); it needs no extension of φᵢ with respect to I and does not change the declaration of fixed supply. This is an analytic value in model mode; continuous-state models are not within the supported domain of the adjudication programs in the appendix of *The Adjudication Form of the Sedimentation-Field* (numerical comparison in Appendix B.5).

### 6.4  T2 (Every Grade Is Nonempty)

Each representative example below satisfies all entry conditions of 2.6 (domain lemma) and comes with the mechanism witness and the Tr^rest witness of 6.3.  
(1) Locally exchangeable family (rising, flat, falling)  
Read-out κ = g^β (β \> 0), sᵢ = κ(gᵢ)/Z, Z = Σκ; write ġᵢ = (I sᵢ)^α − λgᵢ. Let c = α − 1/β; then  
　rᵢ − rⱼ = βI^α Z^{−1/β}(sᵢ^c − sⱼ^c).  
Proof: let uᵢ = gᵢ^β; then u̇ᵢ/uᵢ = βġᵢ/gᵢ = β\[I^α sᵢ^α gᵢ^{−1} − λ\]; substituting gᵢ = (sᵢZ)^{1/β} gives u̇ᵢ/uᵢ = βI^αZ^{−1/β}sᵢ^c − βλ; since rᵢ = u̇ᵢ/uᵢ − Σₖsₖu̇ₖ/uₖ, subtracting gives the result. ∎  
The formula holds for all α, β \> 0. For a non-tied pair sᵢ ≠ sⱼ it is separating when αβ \> 1 and merging when αβ \< 1; when αβ = 1 every pair is neutral. For a tied pair sᵢ = sⱼ it is neutral for all these parameters (the occurrence of flat states in a rising-type host is permitted by the definition of host type). Three levels are kept apart below: pointwise pair direction, state tendency direction, and host type over the whole domain. The full entry conditions are issued only for β = 1: α \> 1 rising type, α = 1 flat type, 0 \< α \< 1 falling type; the representatives are α = 1.5, 1 and 0.5. In all three cases R contains nonuniform interior states: when c ≠ 0, A₀ contains only the uniform equilibrium in the interior; when c = 0, A₀ is the surface {Σg = I/λ}, outside which R remains. The mechanism witness follows the template (φ(q) = (Iq)^α).  
(2) Two-tube slime mould with equal lengths (the model of Tero et al. 2007)  
Ḋᵢ = f(Qᵢ) − Dᵢ, where Dᵢ is the conductance, Qᵢ = sᵢ = Dᵢ/S and S = D₁ + D₂. Then r₁ − r₂ = \[h(s₁) − h(s₂)\]/S, with h(q) = f(q)/q.  
Proof: rᵢ = Ḋᵢ/Dᵢ − Ṡ/S, so r₁ − r₂ = f(s₁)/D₁ − f(s₂)/D₂ = \[h(s₁) − h(s₂)\]/S. ∎  
Type I, f = q^m (every m \> 0 lies within the domain lemma): m \> 1 rising type, m = 1 flat type, m \< 1 falling type. This agrees with the report of Tero et al. (2007): for m \> 1 only one tube survives, depending on the initial values; for m = 1 with equal lengths every point of the segment is an equilibrium; for m \< 1 both tubes survive. The mechanism witness follows the template: the adaptation process f(Qᵢ) and the shallowing process −Dᵢ, with carriers inside the tube network and the flow allocation Qᵢ conditioned on the conductances.  
(3) Indefinite type (analytic point values, all within the domain lemma)  
　Type II, f = 31q³/(1 + 30q³), two tubes of equal length, a = 30 (computed in this paper; the two-tube analysis of type II in Tero's original takes L₂ = 1.1): h = 31q²/(1 + 30q³), h(0.55) − h(0.45) = −0.116, h(0.95) − h(0.05) = +0.970: merging when the shares are near one half, separating near the extremes (bistable). At the witness points r₁ ≠ r₂, so v ≠ 0; the points are not in A₀ and lie in R.  
　Constructed example f(q) = q^{0.2}e^{2.5q}/e^{2.5}: h ∝ q^{−0.8}e^{2.5q}; computed with the unnormalized q^{−0.8}e^{2.5q}, h(0.55) − h(0.45) = +0.546 and h(0.95) − h(0.05) = −1.247 (the normalized h is this value times e^{−2.5}, with the same sign): separating near one half, merging near the extremes (balance at an intermediate point).  
　Type I, m = 2, two tubes of unequal length L = (1, 1.05): qᵢ = (Dᵢ/Lᵢ)/Σ. On the tie line {D₁/L₁ = D₂/L₂}, r₁ − r₂ = f(1/2)(1/D₁ − 1/D₂) ≠ 0 (D₁ ≠ D₂), and q₁ − q₂ changes sign across the tie line (transversally), so by T8 the host is of indefinite type. ∎ A strict transposition can occur along a trajectory: from the initial state D = (0.5, 0.54), q₁ starts at 0.493, and in the end tube 1 alone survives (numerical example in Appendix B.5, not a proof; the indefinite verdict rests on T8 alone).  
The mechanism witness of each example follows the template; for the Tr^rest witness see 6.3.  
6.5  T3 (The Flat Type Is Equivalent to Shares Being Constant along Trajectories)  
If all rᵢ are equal to ρ, then 0 = Σvᵢ = Σsᵢrᵢ = ρ, so v ≡ 0; conversely, v ≡ 0 gives r ≡ 0. ∎ (Unfolding of the definitions.)  
6.6  T4 (A Strict Transposition Must Merge before It Separates)  
Let Λ = log(sᵢ/sⱼ), so that Λ′ = rᵢ − rⱼ. If Λ(t\_a) \> 0 \> Λ(t\_b) along a trajectory in R, then there exist t\_a \< t₁ \< t₂ \< t\_b such that the pair is merging at t₁ and separating at t₂.  
Proof: the zero set N₀ = {τ ∈ \[t\_a, t\_b\] : Λ(τ) = 0} is nonempty by the intermediate value theorem and closed by continuity, so t₋ = min N₀ and t₊ = max N₀ are attained, with Λ(t₋) = Λ(t₊) = 0 and t₋ ≤ t₊. On \[t\_a, t₋\] the mean value theorem gives t₁ ∈ (t\_a, t₋) with Λ′(t₁) = −Λ(t\_a)/(t₋ − t\_a) \< 0 and Λ(t₁) \> 0, that is, merging. On \[t₊, t\_b\] it gives t₂ ∈ (t₊, t\_b) with Λ′(t₂) \< 0 and Λ(t₂) \< 0, that is, sᵢ \< sⱼ and rᵢ \< rⱼ: separating. t₁ \< t₋ ≤ t₊ \< t₂. ∎  
Corollaries (issued for strict reversal only):  
　Rising type: when Λ \> 0, Λ′ ≥ 0, so Λ cannot fall to 0 and an existing strict order is preserved; a tie can be pulled apart, in either direction.  
　Falling type: when Λ \> 0, Λ′ ≤ 0; a strict order can merge into a tie at a finite time but cannot cross 0 (otherwise T4 would require separating, and the falling type has no separating pair).  
　Neither type has a strict reversal of the same pair. A dominance transposition means a reversal of the strict ranking; the splitting and merging of ties are counted separately. A strict dominance transposition can occur only in the indefinite type.  
　The weak order (including tie relations) need not be preserved. Smooth examples: ẋ = 1 − x, x ∈ (−1, 1), s₁ = 1/2 + e(x)/10, s₂ = 1 − s₁. Take e(x) = exp(−1/x²) for x \> 0 and 0 otherwise: for x ≤ 0 the pair is tied, and for x \> 0, s₁ \> s₂ and the pair is separating; all states are only rising or flat, so this is the rising type, and the tie is pulled apart as the trajectory goes from x = −1/2 to positive x. Take e(x) = exp(−1/x²) for x \< 0 and 0 otherwise: only falling or flat states occur, and a strict order merges into a tie at a finite time. With B\_dyn = (−1, 1) and U = ℝ, both examples satisfy the mathematical entry conditions 2.6(1)–(3); they only illustrate the limits of order, are not host instances, and carry no mechanism witness or Tr^rest witness; the ω-limit point x = 1 is not in B\_dyn, which does not affect these two transients.

### 6.7  T5 (Monotonicity of Concentration)

Let Γ be defined on a symmetric open convex set (in ℝⁿ) containing the interior of the simplex, and be symmetric, C¹ and Schur-convex. In the rising type, Γ(s) does not decrease along trajectories in R; in the falling type it does not increase.  
Proof: dΓ/dt = Σᵢ ∂ᵢΓ·sᵢrᵢ. For all i, j, (rᵢ − rⱼ)(∂ᵢΓ − ∂ⱼΓ) ≥ 0: when sᵢ \> sⱼ, the absence of merging gives rᵢ ≥ rⱼ and the Schur–Ostrowski condition gives ∂ᵢΓ ≥ ∂ⱼΓ; when sᵢ = sⱼ, symmetry gives ∂ᵢΓ = ∂ⱼΓ. Since Σsᵢrᵢ = Σvᵢ = 0, dΓ/dt = ½Σᵢⱼsᵢsⱼ(rᵢ − rⱼ)(∂ᵢΓ − ∂ⱼΓ) ≥ 0 (Chebyshev's sum inequality with weights s). For the falling type the sign is reversed. ∎  
Majorization order: for any smooth convex function ϑ, Σϑ(sᵢ) is a symmetric C¹ Schur-convex function and does not decrease along rising-type trajectories; a continuous convex ϑ is approximated uniformly by smooth convex functions on the compact subdomain of the simplex occupied by a finite segment of trajectory; by the Hardy–Littlewood–Pólya characterization, s(t₂) majorizes s(t₁) (t₂ \> t₁). The reverse holds for the falling type.

### 6.8  T6 (Invariance and Its Limits)

(a) The tendency type depends only on the share trajectory and is invariant under diffeomorphic re-encoding of the state: the conjugate dynamics and the composed share map preserve Ds·Ψ and its sign.  
(b) State tendency direction is not guaranteed to be preserved under merging of shares: for the rising type of 6.4(1) (α = 1.5, β = 1, c = 1/2), at s = (0.4, 0.3, 0.3), rᵢ − rⱼ ∝ sᵢ^c − sⱼ^c gives r₁ \> r₂ = r₃, which is rising; merge the shares of Trace-Responses 2 and 3: the relative growth of the merged share is r₂₃ = (v₂ + v₃)/(s₂ + s₃) = r₂ \< r₁, while s₁ = 0.4 \< s₂₃ = 0.6, so at the reading (0.4, 0.6) the pair is merging, that is, falling (numerical comparison in Appendix B.2). This is only a conclusion at the reading level and does not pass itself off as closure of coarse dynamics.  
(c) Tendency type can change under restricted closed coarse-graining. Order of construction: the complete fine system, restricted to a forward-invariant subsystem, merged into a closed coarse system. In the exchangeable family, g₂ = g₃ implies ġ₂ = ġ₃ (permutation symmetry), so the subspace {g₂ = g₃} is forward invariant and merging on it is exact: the coarse host has state (g₁, G), G = g₂ + g₃, with s₁ = κ(g₁)/(κ(g₁) + 2κ(G/2)), ġ₁ = (I s₁)^α − λg₁ and Ġ = 2(I(1 − s₁)/2)^α − λG, so the dynamics is determined by the coarse state itself. For β = 1 and α = 1.5, s₁ = g₁/S, s₂₃ = G/S, and r₁ − r₂₃ = (I^α/S)\[s₁^{α−1} − 2^{1−α}s₂₃^{α−1}\], whose sign is the sign of s₁ − s₂₃/2: separating for s₁ \> 1/2, merging for 1/3 \< s₁ \< 1/2, separating for s₁ \< 1/3. Hence the fine host is of rising type and the coarse host of indefinite type (numerical check in Appendix B.3). The conclusion of this clause is: there exist a rising-type fine host and a forward-invariant subsystem of it such that merging the shares of that subsystem forms a closed coarse dynamics of indefinite type; whether the whole three-dimensional fine host has the same closed two-dimensional coarse dynamics lies outside this conclusion. The coarse system satisfies domain lemma (ii); its instance qualification is declared separately by the mechanism-witness template (write laws (Iq)^α and 2^{1−α}(Iq)^α), and its Tr^rest witness is as in 6.3.  
Tendency type is therefore relative to the partition of the family; carrying a host's tendency type over to its coarse-graining requires a separate proof.

### 6.9  T7 (Codimension of the Flat Type)

(a) In the (α, β) parameter plane of 6.4(1), the flat type (as far as the sign formula goes) is the smooth curve αβ = 1, of codimension 1.  
(b) Fix an open set O and a C¹ share map s on O, and suppose Ds(x₀) ≠ 0 at some point x₀ of O. Then in the linear space 𝒱 of C¹ vector fields on O, the constraint subspace 𝒱₀ = {Ψ ∈ 𝒱 : Ds·Ψ = 0 on O} has infinite algebraic codimension.  
Proof of (b): take a vector w with Ds(x₀)w ≠ 0; by continuity of Ds there is a ball B₀ ⊂ O centred at x₀ on which Ds(x)w ≠ 0. Inside B₀ take countably many pairwise disjoint small balls Bₖ, with C¹ compactly supported nonnegative cutoff functions (bump functions) χₖ on them, χₖ positive at some point of Bₖ, and let Ψₖ = χₖw. For any finite set of coefficients cₖ, not all zero, Σcₖ Ψₖ satisfies Ds·(Σcₖ Ψₖ) = cₖχₖ Ds·w ≠ 0 within some Bₖ with cₖ ≠ 0, so it does not belong to 𝒱₀. Hence {Ψₖ} is linearly independent modulo 𝒱₀, and the quotient space is infinite-dimensional. ∎  
Limitation: the antecedent Ds ≠ 0 cannot be omitted. Under a constant share map (Ds ≡ 0), every Ψ gives v = 0, the flat type is the whole class, and the codimension is 0; constant shares do not mean absence of historical dynamics (H2). The object of (b) is a fixed O and s; “identically 0 on an R that varies with Ψ” is not a fixed subspace. “Empty interior” (Corollary 2′ of T8) is not the same as “infinite codimension.”

### 6.10  T8 (Tie Lemma) and Corollaries

T8: let x be an interior point of R with sᵢ(x) = sⱼ(x), such that sᵢ − sⱼ takes both signs in every neighbourhood of x, and rᵢ(x) ≠ rⱼ(x). Then the pair is both separating and merging within a neighbourhood of x, and the host is of indefinite type.  
Proof: s is C¹ and Ψ is continuous, so v = Ds·Ψ and rᵢ − rⱼ are continuous in a neighbourhood of x; hence there is a neighbourhood N ⊂ R of x (x being an interior point of R) in which rᵢ − rⱼ has the same sign as rᵢ(x) − rⱼ(x) and is nonzero. Taking points in N at which sᵢ − sⱼ has the same sign and the opposite sign gives separating and merging. ∎  
Corollary 1: in the rising, falling and flat types, rᵢ = rⱼ must hold at tie points in the interior of R that satisfy “both signs in every neighbourhood.”  
Corollary 2: every tendency type that is sign-definite over the whole domain requires rᵢ = rⱼ at such tie points in the interior of R.  
Corollary 2′ (empty interior; the mathematical vector-field class with a fixed share map)  
　Model class 𝔉: given a declared level, an open state domain X = B\_dyn, a C¹ share map s and an open set U ⊃ X, 𝔉 is the set of autonomous vector fields on U satisfying the mathematical entry conditions of 2.6 (Ψ locally Lipschitz and C¹ near X, X forward invariant, the part where all shares are positive forward invariant, uniform precompactness, R ≠ ∅); its topology is the compactly supported C¹ topology, that is, a neighbourhood base of Ψ is {Ψ + Θ : Θ ∈ C¹\_c(U), ‖Θ‖\_{C¹} \< δ} ∩ 𝔉 (δ \> 0). 𝔉 is a mathematical vector-field class: its members carry no qualification of Tr, TraceSet, carrier decomposition or W; its conclusions do not transfer to subfamilies of realizable mechanism models; transfer to such subfamilies presupposes a separately stated admissible-perturbation assumption and its item-by-item proof.  
　Qualified tie point: x₀ in the interior of R with sᵢ(x₀) = sⱼ(x₀) and D(sᵢ − sⱼ)(x₀) ≠ 0.  
　Proposition: let Ψ ∈ 𝔉 be of sign-definite type, with a qualified tie point x₀ in the interior of R. Then every compactly supported C¹ neighbourhood of Ψ contains a Ψ′ ∈ 𝔉 of indefinite type; and the indefinite type is open in 𝔉. Hence sign-definite types containing qualified tie points have empty interior in 𝔉.  
　Proof (i): by Corollary 1, rᵢ = rⱼ at x₀, that is, D(sᵢ − sⱼ)(x₀)·Ψ(x₀) = 0. Take a closed ball K₀ ∋ x₀ that lies in X, keeps away from the boundary of X and from the set where shares are not all positive, and has D(sᵢ − sⱼ) ≠ 0 throughout; take a C¹ function Θ supported in K₀ with D(sᵢ − sⱼ)(x₀)·Θ(x₀) ≠ 0. Let Ψ′ = Ψ + εΘ with ε ≠ 0 sufficiently small. Check Ψ′ ∈ 𝔉 item by item: local Lipschitz continuity and C¹ are evident; X and the part where all shares are positive are open sets, forward invariant under Ψ, and Ψ′ equals Ψ in a neighbourhood of their boundaries, so a Ψ′-orbit that left them would first have to reach the boundary along a stretch of Ψ-orbit inside that neighbourhood, contradicting forward invariance under Ψ; uniform precompactness: the pieces of a Ψ′-orbit outside K₀ are all Ψ-orbit segments starting from K₀ or from the initial compact set, so their union is contained in the union of K₀ with the Ψ-orbits starting from these two compact sets, which is precompact in U by the uniform precompactness of Ψ. At x₀, v′ᵢ − v′ⱼ = εD(sᵢ − sⱼ)(x₀)·Θ(x₀) ≠ 0, so r′ᵢ(x₀) ≠ r′ⱼ(x₀) and v′(x₀) ≠ 0; by continuity of v′, v′ ≠ 0 in some neighbourhood of x₀, which is disjoint from A₀′; so x₀ is an interior point of R′, R′ ≠ ∅, and x₀ is still a qualified tie point (s is unchanged). By T8, Ψ′ is of indefinite type.  
　Proof (ii): an indefinite-type Ψ has points x₊, x₋ in R carrying σ = + and σ = − (strictly) for some pair; at these points some difference of r is nonzero, so v ≠ 0. At fixed points the differences of r depend continuously on the point values Ψ(x), so a small compactly supported C¹ perturbation Ψ′ ∈ 𝔉 preserves these strict signs and v′(x±) ≠ 0; v′(x±) ≠ 0 puts x± outside A₀′, and x± lie in X with all shares positive, so x± ∈ R′ and Ψ′ is still of indefinite type. ∎  
　Limitation: in structured subclasses such as the exchangeable parametric families, sign-definite types can be open. When a basin-restricted tendency type is declared under Section 5 and R avoids the tie set, the rising type can be robust.  
Corollary 4 (nowhere dense): let S be the set of sign-definite types in 𝔉 containing qualified tie points, and O the set of indefinite types in 𝔉. Then S is nowhere dense in 𝔉.  
Proof: by Corollary 2′, O is open, S ∩ O = ∅, and S ⊆ cl(O). Hence cl(S) ⊆ 𝔉 \\ O (O being open), and also cl(S) ⊆ cl(O), so cl(S) ⊆ cl(O) \\ O = ∂O. The boundary of an open set has no interior points, so int(cl(S)) = ∅. ∎ The proof does not use the Baire theorem; the conclusion does not include density of the indefinite type in the whole of 𝔉; it concerns only the set S and does not involve other sign-definite types without qualified tie points.  
　On the given tendency difference (kept apart from Corollary 2′ and without the same standing as a proof): a given tendency difference (unequal medium parameters, H4) may break the condition of equal growth rates at ties and so produce the indefinite type; whether it does depends on how the difference actually enters the update law. The two-tube slime mould with unequal lengths is an instance in which it does (6.4(3)); this does not constitute a proof that “hosts with terrain are generally of indefinite type.”  
Corollary 3 (the tie rule creates no extra type): the tie rule “sᵢ = sⱼ with rᵢ ≠ rⱼ counts as separating” does not change the typing of a host at interior points of R.  
Proof: let x be an interior point of R with sᵢ(x) = sⱼ(x) and rᵢ(x) ≠ rⱼ(x). Then vᵢ(x) − vⱼ(x) = sᵢ(x)(rᵢ(x) − rⱼ(x)) ≠ 0, that is, D(sᵢ − sⱼ)(x)·Ψ(x) ≠ 0; along the flow line through x, sᵢ − sⱼ changes sign strictly monotonically at x, so it takes both signs in every neighbourhood of x; by T8 a neighbourhood of x already contains strict separating and merging, and the host is of indefinite type, regardless of whether x itself is counted as separating. If rᵢ(x) = rⱼ(x), the rule is not triggered. ∎ The tie rule can matter only on the boundary of R.

7\. Final Statement  
Under H1–H4 and the declaration (host, level, Φ, B\_dyn, stationary inflow), with B\_dyn taken as the maximal domain according to Section 5, the tendency type is fixed by the sign set of the direction sign σ on R; the four grades are mutually exclusive, exhaustive and nonempty (the representative examples of 6.4 satisfy all entry conditions and come with mechanism witnesses):  
　Rising type (only + and 0): existing strict orders are preserved, and ties can be pulled apart; non-decreasing in the majorization order.  
　Flat type (only 0): the activity shares undergo zero transformation, that is, this reading of the mode of organization is unchanged; it does not follow that the mode of organization itself is unchanged (H2).  
　Falling type (only − and 0): strict orders are not reversed and can merge into ties; non-increasing in the majorization order.  
　Indefinite type (both + and −): strict dominance transpositions can occur only here.  
The rising, flat and falling types require equal growth rates at ties (Corollary 2 of T8); in the mathematical vector-field class 𝔉 with a fixed share map, sign-definite types with qualified tie points in the interior of R have empty interior, and their totality is nowhere dense (Corollaries 2′ and 4). A given tendency difference may place a host in the indefinite type, depending on how it enters the update law.

8\. Concretization Instances (instantiation responsibility; they do not bear the validity of the conclusions of Sections 6 and 7; H1–H4 carried)  
　Rising-type candidate: synapse elimination at the neonatal neuromuscular junction, where several axons jointly occupy one junction and after competition only one remains (Turney and Lichtman 2012, PLoS Biology, DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.1001352; the introductory synopsis by Robinson 2012, PLoS Biology, DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.1001354). There is a tension: the original reports back-and-forth flips of territory share, and the outcome cannot be predicted deterministically from relative shares, that is, strict transpositions occur. By the corollary of T4, if the transpositions are a systematic drift, the indefinite type should be assigned; only if they are attributed to fluctuation (E1) or to out-of-boundary rewriting (such as laser ablation) may the rising type be assigned. This must be declared; stationary input, separation of drift and Sedimentation-Field qualification are all uncertified.  
　Flat type: not issued (E5, E7). Synaptic scaling (Turrigiano et al. 1998, Nature, DOI 10.1038/36103, which reports multiplicative scaling) counts only as an approximate observation of the state tendency direction “flat”; activity blockade changes the inflow and so violates stationarity of inflow.  
　Falling-type candidate: the slime-mould model of type I with m \< 1 (a model instance; Tero, Kobayashi, Nakagaki 2007, Journal of Theoretical Biology 244: 553–564, DOI 10.1016/j.jtbi.2006.07.015). A material instance is undetermined.  
　Indefinite-type candidates: the two-tube slime mould with unequal lengths (a model instance; Tero's original gives the model and the outcome—with m = 1 and unequal lengths only the shorter tube survives—and the indefinite verdict is this paper's computation); river avulsion (no primary literature yet; not a certified example).

9\. Bounds of Misjudgment  
　E1  Fluctuation tendency: the object of grading is the drift reading (the expected update law), not the whole stochastic actual process; fluctuation tendency is not counted in tendency direction and must be reported separately.  
　E2  Declared domain: take the maximal admissible domain under Section 5 (principal reading), with precompactness in a Lipschitz open domain U containing X; shrinking B\_dyn to obtain a sign-definite type is a prohibited formulation; basin-restricted and reachable tendency types are only supplementary reports and must be annotated separately.  
　E3  Given tendency difference (terrain): counted in the computation of tendency direction (H4); it may break equal growth rates at ties and produce the indefinite type, depending on how it enters the update law; this is not used to decide whether it is a tendency in the sense of Definition 3.  
　E4  Lateral terms: counted; tendency direction is judged by shares, whatever the source of the tendency.  
　E5  Measurement: finite measurement issues only the existence of tendency directions; it issues neither the flat type nor universal tendency-type claims; typing depends on the model, and its declaration is an instantiation responsibility; whole-domain setting is only one route of evidence gathering, not the only one.  
　E6  Coarseness of the family: tendency type is relative to the declared partition; tendency type can change under closed coarse-graining (T6 (c)).  
　E7  Limits of the rising, flat and falling types: in the parameter plane of 6.4(1), the flat type (as far as the sign formula goes) has codimension 1; for fixed O and s with Ds ≠ 0 the constraint subspace has infinite algebraic codimension, and when Ds ≡ 0 the codimension is 0 (T7); all three types require equal growth rates at ties, and instances must declare where this comes from; in the mathematical vector-field class 𝔉, sign-definite types containing qualified tie points have empty interior, and their totality is nowhere dense (Corollaries 2′ and 4 of T8); neither “empty interior” nor “nowhere dense” means “infinite codimension.”

A.1  shi\_type.py  Numerical sampling type classifier: sampled typing of the locally exchangeable family and the two-tube slime-mould model, analytic point values for type II and the constructed example, and an evidence-strength comparison (6.4, Section 4)

~~~python
"""Tendency-type grading · numerical sampling type classifier (numerical examples for continuous-time models; not a universal adjudication program)
Evidence strength:
  · state_dir computes in floating point with two tolerances: a relative-growth difference |r_i − r_j| ≤ TOL (1e-10) counts as “neutral”; a share difference |s_i − s_j| ≤ 1e-14 counts as a tie; nonzero differences within tolerance are counted as neutral,
    so “flat” means only that no nonzero sign was seen within tolerance, not an exact flat type. Exact flat types and universal classification come only from the analytic proofs (main text 6.4, 6.9).
  · host_type summarizes finite samples: an empty sample returns “undetermined (no samples)”, not the flat type; “no opposite sign seen” is not sign-definiteness over the whole domain.
  · local() samples M points uniformly in [0.05, 5]^n; it does not compute the share-stationary set A₀ (sample points fall into it with probability zero; this is a sampling convention, not a proof).
  · tero() samples on the grid [0.01, 1]² and discards points with |F| < 1e-9; this is a sampling rule, not a computation of A₀ in general, and the grid is no proof of the reachable domain.
State tendency direction (for each pair (i,j), s_i,s_j > 0):
  separating: (s_i−s_j)(r_i−r_j) > 0, or s_i = s_j with r_i ≠ r_j; merging: (s_i−s_j)(r_i−r_j) < 0; neutral: r_i = r_j (numerically |r_i−r_j| ≤ TOL)
  rising = no merging and some separating; falling = no separating and some merging; flat = all neutral; mixed = both separating and merging
Sample tendency types: rising type / falling type / flat type (within tolerance) / indefinite type / undetermined (no samples)"""
import numpy as np, itertools
TOL = 1e-10
def state_dir(s, v):
    r = v / s; div = conv = False
    for i, j in itertools.combinations(range(len(s)), 2):
        dr = r[i] - r[j]
        if abs(dr) <= TOL: continue
        ds = s[i] - s[j]
        if abs(ds) <= 1e-14 or ds * dr > 0: div = True
        else: conv = True
    return 'flat' if not (div or conv) else 'mixed' if (div and conv) else 'rising' if div else 'falling'
def host_type(dirs):
    if not dirs: return 'undetermined (no samples)'
    S = set(dirs) - {'flat'}
    if not S: return 'flat type (within tolerance)'
    if S == {'rising'}: return 'rising type'
    if S == {'falling'}: return 'falling type'
    return 'indefinite type'
# Two-tube slime mould (Tero et al. 2007): D' = f(Q) − D, D the conductance (adaptive variable), Q = (D/L)/Σ(D/L); grid sampling, points with |F| < 1e-9 discarded
def tero(f, N=120, L=(1.0, 1.0)):
    dirs = []
    for D1 in np.linspace(0.01, 1.0, N):
        for D2 in np.linspace(0.01, 1.0, N):
            k = np.array([D1 / L[0], D2 / L[1]]); s = k / k.sum()
            F = np.array([f(s[0]) - D1, f(s[1]) - D2])
            if np.abs(F).max() < 1e-9: continue          # sampling rule: discard near-zero-velocity points (not a computation of A₀ in general)
            dk = F / np.array(L); v = (dk * k.sum() - k * dk.sum()) / k.sum() ** 2
            dirs.append(state_dir(s, v))
    return host_type(dirs)
# Locally exchangeable family: read-out g^β, write (I s)^α, shallowing 0.1 g; n = 3; uniform sampling in [0.05, 5]^n
def local(a, b, n=3, M=3000, seed=1):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed); dirs = []
    for _ in range(M):
        g = rng.uniform(0.05, 5, n); k = g ** b; s = k / k.sum()
        F = s ** a - 0.1 * g
        dk = b * g ** (b - 1) * F; v = (dk * k.sum() - k * dk.sum()) / k.sum() ** 2
        dirs.append(state_dir(s, v))
    return host_type(dirs)
if __name__ == '__main__':
    print('The following is numerical sampled typing (floating point, tolerance 1e-10, finite samples), for illustration only, not a universal conclusion; for the universal classification see the analytic proofs in the main text')
    print('— locally exchangeable family (n=3) —')
    for a, b in [(1.5, 1), (1, 1), (0.5, 1), (1.5, 2 / 3), (2, 0.5), (1, 1.5), (1, 0.7)]:
        print(f'α={a:.2f} β={b:.2f} αβ={a*b:.2f} → {local(a, b)}')
    print('— two-tube slime mould, equal lengths —')
    cases = {'type I m=2': lambda q: q ** 2, 'type I m=1': lambda q: q, 'type I m=0.5': lambda q: q ** 0.5,
             'type II m=3 a=30 (bistable)': lambda q: 31 * q ** 3 / (1 + 30 * q ** 3),
             'constructed f=q^0.2·e^{2.5q} (intermediate balance)': lambda q: q ** 0.2 * np.exp(2.5 * q) / np.exp(2.5)}
    for k, f in cases.items(): print(f'{k:34s} → {tero(f)}')
    print(f'{"type I m=2, unequal lengths L=(1,1.05)":34s} → {tero(lambda q: q ** 2, L=(1, 1.05))}')
    h2 = lambda q: 31 * q ** 2 / (1 + 30 * q ** 3); hc = lambda q: q ** -0.8 * np.exp(2.5 * q)
    print('6.4(3) analytic point values (unnormalized h for the constructed example): type II a=30: h(.55)-h(.45)=%+.3f, h(.95)-h(.05)=%+.3f; constructed example: at s=.55 %+.3f, at s=.95 %+.3f'
          % (h2(.55) - h2(.45), h2(.95) - h2(.05), hc(.55) - hc(.45), hc(.95) - hc(.05)))
    # Tied pair: two non-exchangeable Trace-Responses with velocities ±0.1875 at s=(0.5,0.5)
    print('direction verdict for tie separation:', state_dir(np.array([.5, .5]), np.array([.1875, -.1875])))
    print('— evidence-strength comparison —')
    s, v = np.array([.6, .4]), np.array([1e-12, -1e-12])
    ex = np.sign((s[0] - s[1]) * (v[0] / s[0] - v[1] / s[1]))
    print(f'tiny nonzero velocity v=(1e-12,-1e-12), s=(.6,.4): exact sign {ex:+.0f} (rising), state_dir gives “{state_dir(s, v)}” (counted as neutral within tolerance)')
~~~

A.3  t6\_coarse.py  Closure check of restricted closed coarse-graining and sample tendency type of the coarse system (6.8(c))

~~~python
"""Numerical check for main text 6.8(c) (closure is a floating-point comparison; typing of the coarse host is finite grid sampling; the analytic sign determination is in main text 6.8(c)): exact merging on the exchangeable invariant subspace {g2 = g3} (a closed coarse-grained host)
Fine host: n=3, read-out g^β (β=1), write (I s)^α (α=1.5), shallowing λg — rising type (main text 6.4(1)).
Coarse host: state (g1, G), G = g2 + g3, constraint g2 = g3 = G/2 (exchangeability makes this subspace invariant, so the dynamics closes):
  s1 = κ(g1)/(κ(g1)+2κ(G/2)), s23 = 1 − s1
  ġ1 = (I s1)^α − λ g1, Ġ = 2 (I s23/2)^α − λ G
"""
import numpy as np
from shi_type import state_dir, host_type
a, b, I, lam = 1.5, 1.0, 1.0, 0.1
k = lambda g: g ** b
def coarse_v(g1, G):
    s1 = k(g1) / (k(g1) + 2 * k(G / 2)); s = np.array([s1, 1 - s1])
    F1 = (I * s1) ** a - lam * g1; FG = 2 * (I * (1 - s1) / 2) ** a - lam * G
~~~

A.5  domain\_T2.py  Numerical comparison for the domain lemma, the exclusion example, and the entry-pulse witness under the same fixed supply (6.3, 6.4)

~~~python
"""Maximal-domain entry conditions of the T2 representative examples: numerical comparison (RK4 integration, finitely many initial states; comparison only, the proof is the domain lemma in main text 6.3)
For each representative example, initial states are sampled in the principal reading X = open positive orthant and integrated to t = 60; reported are the upper and lower bounds of the total S, the behaviour of the smallest component,
and, when the smallest component tends to 0, whether F admits a Lipschitz extension near the boundary (estimated by difference quotients). In addition, α=3, β=1/2 serves as the exclusion comparison,
and entry state pulses under the same fixed supply (P_w(x) = x + η, followed by the same rest flow) are used to check that two histories differing by a single pulse have different states after rest."""
import numpy as np
def rk4(F, x, T=60.0, h=1e-2):
    steps = max(1, round(T / h)); h = T / steps          # after rounding the step count, integrate with the actual step size so that the endpoint is exactly T
    xs = [x.copy()]
    for _ in range(steps):
        k1 = F(x); k2 = F(x + h / 2 * k1); k3 = F(x + h / 2 * k2); k4 = F(x + h * k3)
        x = x + h / 6 * (k1 + 2 * k2 + 2 * k3 + k4); xs.append(x.copy())
    return np.array(xs)
def local(alpha, beta, I=1.0, lam=1.0):
    def F(g):
        k = np.maximum(g, 0) ** beta; s = k / k.sum()
        return (I * s) ** alpha - lam * g
    return F
def tero(f, L=(1.0, 1.0)):
    L = np.array(L)
    def F(D):
        k = np.maximum(D, 0) / L; s = k / k.sum()
        return f(s) - D
    return F
inits = [np.array(x) for x in ([0.1, 1.0], [1.0, 0.1], [0.5, 0.52], [3.0, 0.02], [0.02, 3.0])]
cases = [
    ('local family β=1, α=1.5 (rising)', local(1.5, 1.0), 'ext'),
    ('local family β=1, α=1 (flat)', local(1.0, 1.0), 'int'),
    ('local family β=1, α=0.5 (falling)', local(0.5, 1.0), 'int'),
    ('Tero type I m=2 (rising)', tero(lambda q: q ** 2), 'ext'),
    ('Tero type I m=1 (flat)', tero(lambda q: q), 'int'),
    ('Tero type I m=0.5 (falling)', tero(lambda q: q ** 0.5), 'int'),
    ('Tero type II a=30 (indefinite)', tero(lambda q: 31 * q ** 3 / (1 + 30 * q ** 3)), 'ext'),
    ('constructed example q^0.2·e^{2.5q} (indefinite)', tero(lambda q: q ** 0.2 * np.exp(2.5 * q) / np.exp(2.5)), 'int'),
    ('Tero type I m=2, L=(1,1.05) (indefinite)', tero(lambda q: q ** 2, L=(1, 1.05)), 'ext'),
]
print('numerical comparison (RK4, h=0.01, t∈[0,60], 5 initial states); proof: domain lemma in main text 6.3')
for name, F, mode in cases:
~~~
