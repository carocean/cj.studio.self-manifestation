## Section 2  Integrative Statement

### 2.1  Tendency

Tendency is the inclination possessed by the organizational state of the field's current Trace-Response set: it makes subsequent activity occur more easily in some ways and less easily in others.  
Tendency exists before activity. When a ditch has been dug but the water has not yet come, the tendency is already in place; once the water comes, where it runs, where it gathers, and where it is blocked are all guided by this organization. The shape of the ditch bears the tendency; the water actually flowing through is the carrying-out of the tendency.  
In a neural network, connections, synaptic strengths, and the conditions of integration and firing together determine which activity is easily evoked, which paths are easily continued, and which activity is suppressed. Trace-Responses and their organization take part in bearing this inclination. Tendency therefore cannot be attributed to any single weight or bias alone.  
Reading of tendency: take Φ\_𝓘 at level L on input class 𝓘, and read “changing the tendency” as the net tendency difference ΔΦ\_W over the time window W (\[1\] working domain).

2.2  Tendency Raising and Field Induction  
Field Induction means changing the conditions and organization of a field so that the field has an inclination to generate and continue in the required direction.  
Field Induction must have an object, namely a field that already has an organizational state of its Trace-Response set; Field Induction starts from this organizational state. A field does not innately have the current organizational state of its Trace-Response set; the process of acquiring this organizational state is called tendency raising. Tendency raising studies how to pre-build a concretized field; only once there is an initial field can there be Field Induction on it, transforming it in certain directions. The two stand apart as follows:  
　Tendency raising: pre-build a concretized field and acquire the initial organizational state of the Trace-Response set.  
　Field Induction: starting from this state, transform the field in the required direction through tuning and host adjustment.  
Tuning and host adjustment are both adjustment, and where there is nothing to be adjusted there is nothing to adjust; what tendency raising acquires is precisely the first thing to be adjusted. What is pre-built need not be uniform, but it must have tendency, borne by the corresponding structure (\[4\]). The organization acquired by tendency raising is one source of the starting organization Org₀ of Field Induction; a field may also already be running when it undergoes Field Induction (\[4\] Corollary).

2.3  The Two Modes of Action of Field Induction  
Field Induction has two basic modes of action: tuning and host adjustment.  
Tuning: under the structure and structural relations of the current Trace-Response set, tuning uses suppression and disinhibition as its means of transformation to transform the current state dynamically, thereby realizing a dynamic transformation of structure and structural relations on the invariant layer of structure and structural relations, that is, generating a dynamic layer, and so changing the distribution of tendency. What stays invariant is the physical connectivity; what changes is whether pathways are open or closed, and their degree of permeability. Tuning does not directly add or remove synapses; it constitutes, on top of the current state of the Trace-Response set, a dynamic logical structure layer, and this structure is a subset of the current Trace-Response-set structure. In what the Inner Chapters call “carving structure into destiny,” what is carved is the dynamic structure above the physical structure.  
Host adjustment: it transforms the structure and structural relations, field states, transformation mechanisms, and actual execution in the host level (in the sense of the Computation Group Form) of the regulated field, and so changes the distribution of tendency—including the formation and elimination of synapses, the deepening and shallowing of synaptic permeability, changes in the degree of neuronal integration, and so on. Host adjustment is a direct transformation of the organization of the physical layer.

2.4  Distinguishing the Two Modes of Action  
The sole basis of the distinction is the layer on which the actual change to the regulated target falls and the route by which it occurs: one that directly changes the physical layer of the Trace-Response set (the rules, parameters, or organization of the host level) is host adjustment; one that opens and closes existing pathways by suppression and disinhibition and adjusts their degree of permeability, constituting a dynamic logical structure on top of the existing physical links, is tuning. Whether weights change, and whether the change is retained, are not bases of the distinction; who the actor is, and whether it is inside or outside the field, play no part in it either (\[1\] Corollary).  
Suppression and disinhibition not only open and close pathways but also strengthen or shallow them; their action has three layers:  
　L1  Degree of permeability: the strength of current transmission on the dynamic layer;  
　L2  Order of manifestation: within one overlapping history, changing the order in which the flow proceeds along the tendency and what comes out (2.10);  
　L3  Actual depth through channeling: by suppression and disinhibition, tuning makes signals regularly flow into, or avoid, some region, thereby indirectly changing permeability and integration—paths into which the flow is channeled deepen through the field's own sedimentation, and paths seldom traversed shallow; this change belongs to the field's own sedimentation and does not change the classification of tuning. In the G₀ example of \[3\], after gating has channeled the flow, one input raises w(x→h2) on the h2 branch from 1 to 6/5 through sedimentation; in a spiking sedimentation field with baseline decay, applying suppression silences region Q completely, the positive write-back drops to zero while decay continues as usual, and Q shallows; after the suppression is lifted, Q resumes firing and deepens (7.2 U10 (4)).  
Hence changes of permeability can appear under both modes: host adjustment directly changes parameters such as weights; tuning does not directly change parameters, but indirectly changes permeability and integration through channeling. The difference between the two lies in the route.  
Input and sedimentation: what merely enters as a signal is input; the subsequent weight change caused by the field's own rules is sedimentation; neither is host adjustment or tuning. Teaching signals, distillation data, and environmental inflow are, as far as their way of entering is concerned, all merely input; in training, the act that writes weights directly through the loss, backpropagation, and optimizer steps is host adjustment; in a spiking network, weight change caused by STDP is sedimentation (\[2\] Corollary, \[24\] Control Arm).  
Acts from outside the field, and hijacking: “outside” is taken relative to the regulated field—a sleep region is also part of the field, but it adjusts other regions and so lies outside them. An act that comes from outside the regulated field and opens and closes the regulated field's existing pathways by suppression and disinhibition is tuning in its mode of action (\[3\] Argument Flow 3); since its opening and closing are decided from outside the field, it replaces the tuning law of the regulated field on these pathways, and this is hijacking; taking over the intention of the regulated field is also hijacking. What cannot hijack can only be host adjustment: a regulated field that keeps its original control and merely entrusts an outside device with direct writes to its host is of this kind. Before judging, the boundary of the regulated field must first be fixed: a device that executes the regulated field's own opening and closing decisions belongs to the control chain of the regulated field and is not an act from outside the field.  
Host adjustment and tuning have no direct relation: host adjustment is not set up for the sake of tuning; the conscription of tuning comes from the object level, and research on host adjustment cannot replace research on the conscription of tuning at the object level. When one act contains both direct writing and gating, the parts are labeled and assessed separately by their actual landing points and routes (\[2\], \[3\] Interfaces).  
Proved: ordinary dense feedforward networks have no tuning, only host adjustment—tuning is impossible on the closed specification 𝔖\_ffn, the landing points of acts are direct writes and input, and input is not Field Induction (\[8\]); on the principal instance of the memristive crossbar array, a goal-directed direct write satisfying the necessary structure of host adjustment has been constructed, and tuning has been proved impossible within the frozen permitted class (\[25\]); in spiking networks, a tuning circuit conscripted by in-field control activity and acting in both directions, suppression and disinhibition, has been explicitly constructed (\[19\]).

### 2.5  Content and Content Alignment

Content is the object in what the Computation Group Form calls the object level: Laws, and the Principles, probabilities, intentions, and the like that hold in the corresponding structure; a question-answering Law and reasoning are both content. Having content does not require Self-Manifestation, that is, there need be no manifestation within it. Content may not be directly observable from outside, yet it can be measured indirectly: the question-answering Law in training technology cannot be observed directly at the object level, but can be measured through the output and its loss (\[7\] Definition 3.9 and the read-back rules).  
Tuning is the realization on the host, through the dynamic layer, of an intention at the object level—or, if the intention has not yet arisen, of a Law capable of conscripting tuning—converging toward a content goal. Whether the content goal must already be manifest for this to happen is an open question (7.2 U3). The key to research on tuning lies in the Law by which content and host converge into alignment: the convergence relation between the two is necessarily a Law, called the tuning law (2.7). The focus of research on tuning is where control activity comes from and how it is actually conscripted by an intention or a Law at the object level.  
Host adjustment is the process in which a trainer, in order to generate content (such as a question-answering Law or reasoning) at the object level, adjusts the host so as to approach that generation. This process is usually negative-feedback alignment: target content → observe the loss → evaluate the effect of host transformation → carry out the host transformation → new target content …; the observation is borne by the trainer outside the target field domain, and in feedforward networks it is usually realized by the loss, gradients, backpropagation, and the optimizer (\[7\] formalizes this closed loop and reads it back to the necessary structure of host adjustment). This procedure can be fully automated, akin to unsupervised learning, and can be realized by maintenance Law Engineering, because the procedure itself can sediment into a Law (2.7).  
Tuning and host adjustment are both modes of action by which Field Induction grows the required content; by their goal they are together called content alignment.  
Goal and tendency must be kept apart: as regards content alignment, the goal is how one wishes the field to be; tendency is how the field is prone to be at present. Field Induction studies how to bring the two into alignment and studies their general structure for use in tendency raising, iterating in this way without end (2.9).

### 2.6  Alignment Equilibrium

Host adjustment and tuning do not differ in their content goal: both are convergence to equilibrium, together called content-alignment equilibrium, or alignment equilibrium for short; what host adjustment reaches is host-adjustment equilibrium, and what tuning reaches is Tuning-Balance (also called Experience-Balance, a term of the Inner Chapters). The two differ only in content observation and host implementation.  
The difficulty of host adjustment lies in what counts as having aligned with the goal, that is, the stopping condition of alignment; for the whole negative-feedback loop, it is what counts as host-adjustment equilibrium. This Group Form's answer: host-adjustment equilibrium is that the regulated field realizes the target relation on the declared inputs (alignment) and, continuing under the protocol, writes back no more (protocol stop) (\[10\] Definition 2.2); error-driven host adjustment necessarily converges to host-adjustment equilibrium on the first-proof subclass (\[10\]), and its limit, boundary, and out-of-bound behavior have been characterized (\[16\]); the complete criterion for adjudicating host-adjustment equilibrium from observation is J\_bal (\[13\]). Reaching the target (ℓ ≤ ε), a single zero update, the monitoring flag, or the optimizer stopping: none of these is host-adjustment equilibrium (\[13\] Theorem 5).  
Tuning-Balance is the stopping point of tuning: it is reached when Biased-Flow no longer guides tuning; automatic Experience-Balance does not require Intent to be present (Inner Chapter 9).

2.7  Tuning Law, Maintenance Law Engineering, and Hijacking  
Tuning law: the convergence relation between content convergence and host convergence is itself a Law, sedimented in the Tuning-Field. The action of the tuning law spans the Information-Realm and the physical to bring about alignment. The Law, the organization bearing the Law, and the executing activity stand apart from one another. Tuning is used when the field is awake, that is, during normal running. In spiking networks, the control coupling q\_g that is formed and retained in the field and enters the gating chain has acquired Law-candidate qualification, and an action certificate has been established that the Law candidate L\_g actually takes part in gating and transmission (\[19\] Section 6, 7.2 U10 (2)).  
Maintenance Law Engineering: it can take on observation, evaluation, the act, and the observation of consequences, executing host adjustment automatically; it lies outside the field it maintains, and may lie within the whole field. Field Induction Law Engineering that intercepts the law changes of the induced field by its own sedimentation and regulates repeatedly on that basis has been explicitly constructed and has acquired Law-candidate qualification (\[15\]), and it reaches host-adjustment equilibrium in 2 windows under the joint protocol (\[18\]); Law Engineering substitution versus additional training, and weight-space programming versus Law Engineering, both have executable discriminations (\[21\], \[22\]).  
Hijacking: maintenance Law Engineering generally does not act on tuning; if it is used for tuning, that is hijacking—either hijacking its intention or replacing its tuning law. Opening and closing the regulated field's pathways from outside the field according to the outside party's own goals replaces its tuning law on these pathways (2.4). For judging in a run history whether replacement or bypass actually occurs, the working predicates and their identifiability are given in 7.2 U10 (5).  
Sleep and dreams: rewriting the host level while cognitive activity is in progress would throw content into temporary disorder and disturb cognition; having sleep bear host adjustment is the evolutionarily optimal choice. Accordingly, tuning is carried out when there are dreams (hijacking the intention or the tuning law), and host adjustment when there are none. This is a hypothesis to be tested (7.2 U11). The Inner Chapters on dreams and sleep: waking and sleeping are mutually exclusive in the Intent-Field; there is a sleep region in the field, which is itself a maintenance Law Engineering, performing host adjustment and tuning on the field.

### 2.8  What Field Induction Is Not

Environmental inflow with no purpose of acting is not Field Induction but the carrying-out of tendency. The fundamental distinction between the two lies in content alignment: tuning has Tuning-Balance, and host adjustment has the host-adjustment equilibrium shown by the loss, whereas environmental inflow has nothing it is to align with. Environmental inflow can still leave Trace-Responses and change the subsequent tendency.

2.9  Generalized Structure and the Direction of Evolution  
In the course of conscripting the host to align with content, host adjustment necessarily transforms the structure of the Trace-Response set into a specific structure corresponding to the content goal; the structure that tuning transforms lies only on the dynamic layer and need not grow Trace-Response-set structure, but tuning necessarily transforms the structure of the dynamic layer into a specific structure corresponding to the content goal (\[17\] gives proofs on two declared hosts and tasks).  
The goal of Field Induction therefore lies in acquiring generalized structure: for example, the structures of the subfields described in the Inner Chapters—how the Experience-Field produces content structures related to the laws of motion, how the Imagery-Field produces content structures of the form of Impedance in the perception circuit, and so on. Once these generalized structures are mastered, one need not start from content alignment, but can obtain them directly by tendency raising. Field Induction thus has a direction of evolution:  
　content alignment → tendency raising → content alignment again → …  
On a two-input spiking host, the structure formed in alignment has been turned into a tendency-raising procedure for a new field: \[20\] establishes the identity and source of the conjunctive inhibitory Trace-Response structure, \[23\] gives its preservation, failure, and return, and \[26\] turns it into a tendency-raising procedure for a new field—the full branch bears and aligns directly at the entry, the member branch forms membership qualification during continuation, and after the fixed perturbation both branches acquire actual-window Field-Concretization Qualification when the budget is N\_b ≥ 5.

2.10  Overlapping Histories and the Order of Manifestation  
When a field forms, it does not divide into the concrete and the common; concrete and common are readings made after recognition. A field is laminated layer upon layer like sedimentary strata, and domain overlap forms thereby: each lamination produces Edge-Seepage and the nucleus deepens further, while earlier layers are not overwritten (Field Chapters: new Trace-Responses do not overwrite old ones, but are laminated on top of them). In learning, the route that the Line-Bundle reaches by tuning convergence, that is, the one on top, is fixed as the default; thereafter the same signal naturally flows along it, so the later-arrived comes forth first (Inner Chapter 9: “the well-worn Law stands uppermost; the later comes forth first”). To retrieve earlier-arrived content, one can only converge, by tuning, the route of the Line-Bundle back to the original layer, and cannot rely on signal strength—the output of biological neurons is zero-sum, and strength is not the main measure; the earlier the content pursued, the more tuning it costs (Outer Chapter 3: “old recognition may emerge layer by layer”; Inner Chapter 9: “to pursue what came before demands effort in tuning”). The order of manifestation runs from new to old, and the order of acquisition from old to new: what is learned later builds on what was learned earlier.  
Constructed: on a spiking sedimentation field equipped only with generic mechanisms, layers are borne by overlapping histories, new learning lies on top, and the default read-out is the newest layer; under convergence of the tuning law, a restart scan point by point on the declared grid reads out the layers in turn, C, B, A, with the Trace-Responses preserved during read-out, and the first hitting grid point for retrieving A is higher than that for retrieving B (\[27\]).

### 2.11  Research Program

Building on Field Concretization, we study Field Induction in three kinds of concretized field: feedforward networks, spiking networks, and non-neural hosts (such as memristive material networks).  
Feedforward networks represent the implementation of Field Induction in abstract neural networks; with the existing Transformer as the focus, we study the actual effect of their architecture and training on Field Induction, and summarize their methods of Field Induction and existing experience. Results obtained: ordinary dense feedforward networks have no tuning (\[8\]); the formalization, convergence, inversion, and adjudication of the error-driven training closed loop (\[7\], \[10\], \[11\], \[13\], \[16\]); the classification by instance of existing training methods and of attention (7.2 U10 (3)).  
For spiking networks, existing spiking neural networks such as Brian2 are the object; we study the actual effect of their architecture and training on Field Induction, and summarize their methods of Field Induction and existing experience; Brian2 is a simulator, and specific network architectures and training methods are studied separately. Results obtained: constructions of a tuning circuit, Field Induction Law Engineering, the reachability of Law candidates, the tendency raising of generalized structure, and the manifestation of overlapping histories (\[15\], \[19\], \[24\], \[26\], \[27\]).  
For non-neural hosts we study their possible methods of Field Induction, and summarize existing experience as well. Result obtained: the principal instance of the memristive crossbar array (\[25\]).  
This Group Form does not stop at summarizing the experience of existing training techniques; it must also invent and discover, with the emphasis on developing methods of Field Induction for spiking-network concretized fields. The subsequent Self-Manifestation-Field Substrate Group Form will build spiking networks that approximate the implementation of biological neurons, as the substrate for building Self-Manifestation-Fields in the future; Field Induction in spiking networks is therefore the research-and-development focus of this Group Form. This Group Form aims to discover methods more effective than the existing Field Induction methods of feedforward networks, and thereby to supply Field Induction technology to subsequent Group Forms (7.2 U11).

2.12  Separate Adjudication of the Three Levels  
The host level, dynamics level, and object level of the Computation Group Form are adjudicated separately, and a later level is not automatically inferred from an earlier one (*The Master Map of the Computation Group Form* v1.1, Section 7); running an algorithm, writing back weights, and producing good outputs do not automatically acquire object-level qualification. The dynamic logical organization formed by tuning is not the same as the dynamics level. The three columns—model column, prediction column, and reality column—are an axis of evidence registration, separate from each of the three levels (*The Master Map of the Field Concretization Group Form* v1.0, 1.3).
