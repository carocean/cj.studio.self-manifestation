# The General Form of Field Induction

This paper takes up one master proposition:

When an act of Field Induction actually occurs, whether the induced field is neural tissue, an artificial network, or some other concretized field, and whatever mechanism the act passes through and whether it comes from inside or outside the field, what structure must it carry that it cannot lose? This Group Form's initial stipulation on Field Induction is as follows: Field Induction starts from the organization of the field's existing Trace-Response set and changes the conditions and organization of the field, causing the field to have an inclination to generate and continue in the required direction; tendency is the inclination possessed by the organizational state of the field; the modes of action of Field Induction are divided by the layer on which the actual change to the induced target falls: one that directly transforms the physical layer of the Trace-Response set (the rules, parameters, or organization of the host level) is host adjustment, and one that opens and closes existing pathways by suppression and disinhibition, adjusts their degree of permeability, and constructs a dynamic logical structure on top of the existing physical links is tuning; the content to which the required direction points is an object at what the Computation Group Form calls the object level. These are stipulations that this Group Form takes as premises without proof. Within this stipulation, which structures must an act of Field Induction carry? What does each of them bear, and how is each irreplaceable by the others?

Object: an act of Field Induction Ind(ι), ι = (F, Xg, Xa, a, W, L, 𝓘), with an already established act of Field Induction as antecedent. F is the induced field; Xg is the goal bearer and Xa the executor of the act; the two may be the same organization or different ones, and may lie inside or outside F; a is a stretch of actual process of Xa within the time window W; L is the descriptive level and resolution; 𝓘 is the declared read-out input class. Each of the above items stays fixed throughout every round of questioning; changing any one of them changes the instance to which the present Field Induction qualification applies.

Working domain and inheritance boundary: the antecedent is that Ind(ι) is established. The induced field F is a concretized field: its declared mechanism admits, at the rule level, legitimate witnesses of the three duties of Trace-Response, organizational participation, and historical dynamics (the historical action may be borne through retention or out-of-boundary processes), and in some declared actual window it acquires Field-Concretization Qualification (the three items Trace-Response, organizational participation, and historical dynamics of the same Trace-Response set are actually borne); this actual window need not be W; rule-level conditions do not replace actual qualification, and qualification within W is not inferred from qualification in the actual window. The tendency reading takes the function Φ\_𝓘 on level L and input class 𝓘, and the reading covers only the declared 𝓘. Within the working domain of this Form, “changing the tendency” is read as the net tendency difference within W, ΔΦ\_W := Φ\_𝓘(end of W) − Φ\_𝓘(start of W) ≠ 0; maintenance-type cases, in which effective actions cancel and the net tendency difference is zero, are not in the working domain. The inherited items are: this Group Form's initial stipulations on Field Induction, tendency, mode of action, and content; the characterization of “action” and “organizational conditioning” in Section 3 of *The General Form of Field Concretization*, together with its Field-Concretization Qualification read by actual window; and the stipulation in Section 10 of the Computation Group Form that training must have its update, retention, and subsequent reading-in verified separately, and that an immediate change in read-out every time is not a general requirement. The internal structures of tuning, host adjustment, and tendency raising are developed by the later Forms of this Group Form, and their conclusions are not premises of this Form.

We state in advance how far this question is answered. The sub-questions that actually arise and are answered are as follows.  
First: given starting organization, action, and a net tendency difference, is there already Field Induction? No. There must be a goal specification, borne by a goal bearer, that points to the object-level content required for the field (Argument Flow 1); proved.  
Second: given in addition a goal specification, is there already Field Induction? No. The act must actually act on the induced field (Argument Flow 2); proved.  
Third: given in addition the act's action on the field, is there already Field Induction? No. Within W the act must take part in forming the tendency through an actual bearing chain (Argument Flow 3); proved. Bearing requires only that there be one bearing moment within W; it requires neither immediate visibility nor persistence to the end point of W.  
Fourth: given in addition a bearing chain, is there already Field Induction? No. The goal specification must take part in determining the act through an actual route (Argument Flow 4); proved. The route is not restricted to the interior of Xg or Xa.  
Fifth: are any of the following necessary: a negative-feedback closed loop, reading the loss in the present act, iteration, progress in the present act, reaching the goal, stopping adjustment in finite time, an actor outside the field, a change of physical links, a change of weights, a human actor, identifiability of the act relation from the end state of the field, visibility at the moment the act ends, persistence of bearing to the end point of W? None is necessary; proved (Argument Flow 6).  
Sixth: how do established acts of Field Induction branch by implementation layer and route? One that directly transforms the physical layer is host adjustment; one that constructs a dynamic logical structure by suppression and disinhibition is tuning; the actor's location plays no part in the branching. Whether Field Induction acts outside these two exist, and how they are classified, is unanswered (Argument Flow 7).  
Overall conclusion: an act of Field Induction necessarily has six duty groups on the same instance: starting organization; the goal specification and what it points to (the two atomic predicates GoalRep ∧ Target combined into one group); the net tendency difference within W; the act's action on the field; the bearing chain within W; and goal condition. The six duty groups cannot be replaced by the weaker conditions tested; whether they suffice to identify Field Induction (the converse) is unproved.

In this paper we set out, through persistent questioning, to find where the proof must go, and to derive the General Form of Field Induction.

Our goal is as follows:

Starting from the initial stipulation and the working domain, unfold item by item the arguments and relations implicit in the four terms “starting from existing organization,” “changing the conditions and organization of the field,” “causing the field to have an inclination,” and “in the required direction”; prove that each item is necessary for any established act of Field Induction; prove that they cannot be replaced by the weaker conditions tested; exclude unnecessary features that are often mistaken for necessary ones; and give the branching read-back of modes of action and state the strength of what this Form proves.

We begin with the result.

What follows is the abbreviated form of the results of this paper. The proofs are in the Argument Flow, the Proof of Minimal Structure, and “Master Formula and Master Theorem”; the formal definitions are in the Appendix.

　　Ind(ι) ⇒ ∃T, g \[ Org₀(F, T) ∧ GoalRep(Xg, g) ∧ Target(g, F) ∧ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 ∧ Eff(a, F) ∧ Bear(a, Φ\_𝓘; W) ∧ GoalCond(a, g) \]  
　　Bear(a, Φ\_𝓘; W) :⇔ ∃τ ∈ W · BearAt(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ)

Here Org₀(F, T) says that at the start of W, F already has the Trace-Response set T and its organization; GoalRep(Xg, g) ∧ Target(g, F) says that within W, Xg bears a goal specification g, and g points to object-level content required for F; Eff(a, F) says that within W, a actually applies effective action to the conditions or organization of F; BearAt(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ) says that the change left on F by the effective action of a takes part, through an actual material route, in determining that part of the organization or conditions bearing Φ\_𝓘 at τ on which Φ\_𝓘 depends; GoalCond(a, g) says that g takes part, through an actual material route, in determining at least one of the occurrence, timing, site of action, direction, or strength of a. The seven atomic predicates take the same F, Xg, Xa, a, W, 𝓘 as arguments and are combined by duty into six groups. This is the necessary direction; the converse is unproved.

In One Sentence

Within the declared working domain of net tendency difference, an established act of Field Induction must have six duty groups on the same instance: starting organization, goal specification, net tendency difference, effective act, bearing chain, and goal condition. Whether the six duty groups suffice to identify Field Induction is not yet proved.

Abstract

This Group Form's initial stipulation on Field Induction has already said that Field Induction starts from the existing organization of the field, changes the conditions and organization of the field, and causes the field to have an inclination to generate and continue in the required direction, and that its modes of action divide by implementation layer into host adjustment and tuning. This stipulation gives the definition of Field Induction, but does not itself unfold which arguments each of its terms carries or what relations the terms require of one another. This paper starts from an established act of Field Induction and asks about these.

Starting organization, action on the conditions or organization of the field, and a net tendency difference within W can be read directly from the definition. But a field with only environmental inflow has all three: inputs deepen pathways by the field's own write rules and the tendency changes, yet nothing induces anything in any direction. “The required direction” must have a bearer: in any act of Field Induction, some bearer must hold within W a goal specification pointing to the content required for the field. Given a goal specification, the act can still land elsewhere while the tendency of the field is changed by inflow; the subject of “changing the conditions and organization of the field” is the act in this Field Induction, and it must act on the induced field. An act acting on the field can still change only a quantity that never enters the reading and is read by no successor mechanism, while the tendency is still changed by inflow; “causing the field to have an inclination” requires that the change caused by the act take part in producing this inclination: there must be a bearing chain—at some moment within W, the change left by the act takes part, through an actual route, in determining the organization that bears the tendency. The bearing chain requires neither visibility at the moment the act ends nor persistence to the end point. With act and bearing both present, the goal can still be a mere bystander: a schedule written before the goal appeared, and not reading the goal when executed, happens to push the field toward the goal; “in the required direction” requires that what requires take part in determining the act: there must be goal condition.

A negative-feedback closed loop, reading the loss in the present act, iteration, progress in the present act, reaching the goal, stopping adjustment in finite time, an actor outside the field, a change of physical links, a change of weights, a human actor, identifiability of the act relation from the end state of the field, visibility at the moment the act ends, persistence of bearing to the end point: for each of these there are acts of Field Induction, established under the initial stipulation, that lack it, so none is a necessary structure.

Proved: any established act of Field Induction has the six duty groups on the same instance, and none of them can be replaced by the weaker conditions tested. Established acts of Field Induction branch by implementation layer and route into host adjustment and tuning, and the actor's location plays no part in the branching. Unanswered: the converse; maintenance-type cases; the classification of Field Induction acts that are neither direct writes nor suppression and disinhibition; the interface between a goal-directed direct value write and intrusion discrimination; whether Xg must have object-level qualification; uniqueness and global minimality.

Preliminary Distinctions

What follows only fixes objects and terms; it bears no part of the necessity of the structural items.

Tendency and its reading. Tendency is the inclination possessed by the organizational state of the field (including the patency and permeability of pathways). At level L and on input class 𝓘 this Form reads it as a function Φ\_𝓘 of the organizational state; Φ\_𝓘 is a reading, not the tendency itself, and covers only the declared 𝓘. “The part on which Φ\_𝓘 depends” is taken as structural dependence: Φ\_𝓘, as a function of the organizational state, is not constant over the possible values of that part; Φ\_𝓘 is not required to change along the actual change.

Act. The act a is a stretch of actual process of Xa within W. An action that enters F only as a signal and is processed by F's own basic rules is called input; a physical-layer change produced by F's own basic rules is called sedimentation. Neither input nor sedimentation is a mode of action of the act; an act may take part in bearing the tendency through input and sedimentation.

Goal specification, reading target, and content qualification. The goal specification g is a description, held by Xg, of a piece of content to be generated (such as an input–output relation). A reading target is an observable derived from g (such as a loss, or the firing rate of some unit); it is an engineering indicator and does not become object-level content by being chosen as the indicator, and its correspondence with g must be declared. Content qualification concerns whether F already has the content that g points to, and is judged separately by criteria such as those of the Law Group Form. g may point to content not yet established in F. The three do not substitute for one another.

Action and conditioning. We adopt the characterization in Section 3 of *The General Form of Field Concretization*: action is actually applied by material means and may cancel; when a legitimate component decomposition exists, the components are the exact criterion; indivisible joint action need not have a unique decomposition; a process may be conditioned on past states through an actual material route and through historical continuity, and is not restricted to direct reading at the same moment.

Host adjustment and tuning. As to the actual change to the induced target: one that directly transforms the physical layer of the Trace-Response set—the rules, parameters, or organization of the host level, such as adding or removing links, changing weights, changing permeability, or changing integration parameters—is host adjustment; one that opens and closes existing pathways by suppression and disinhibition and adjusts their degree of permeability, constructing on top of the existing physical links a dynamic logical structure that is a subset of the current Trace-Response-set structure, is tuning. Tuning makes signals regularly flow into or avoid some region; the deepening and shallowing that then occur by the field's own basic rules belong to sedimentation and do not change the classification. The two cannot be told apart by whether weights changed or whether the change is retained; who the actor is, and whether it is inside or outside the field, play no part in this distinction. Hijacking (taking over the intention or replacing the tuning law) is a different concept and plays no part in the classification into host adjustment and tuning.

Counterfactual comparison. Comparison in which a is removed and the remaining processes are kept is a method of certifying bearing, not the content of bearing; that the result is unchanged after removal does not by itself prove non-participation.

The scalar host H₀. The constructed examples of the Argument Flow all fall on the same declared host, whose mechanism and qualification are as follows (state variables and rules added in individual examples are declared where they occur). State: weight w, threshold θ, learning condition e; reading Φ = w − θ (input class 𝓘 = {x = 1}). F's own rules: when a deepening input s arrives, w ← w + e·s·w; when a copy input c arrives, w ← c. The act may rewrite w, θ, e directly (direct write). Qualification window W\_q (preceding the W of every example): starting point (w, θ, e) = (10/11, 0, 1/10); within W\_q a deepening input s = 1 arrives once, and w changes from 10/11 to 1.  
Lemma 0  H₀ acquires Field-Concretization Qualification on W\_q.  
Proof: Trace-Response—the input s leaves a change on w (10/11 → 1); the change persists beyond W\_q, and its source is the passage of a signal. Organizational participation—this write reads in the current w and e, and the reading Φ reads in the current w and θ. Historical dynamics—the increment e·s·w = 1/11 of this actual write within W\_q is determined by the w already left, and acts again on the same w. All three items are actually borne on the same Trace-Response set {w} within W\_q. ∎  
The W of every example lies after W\_q, with starting point (w, θ, e) = (1, 0, 1/10) unless otherwise declared; which inputs are sent in within W is stated for each example. Lemma 0 gives qualification on W\_q and does not imply qualification within the W of any example; the working domain of this Form, too, requires only the former.

Argument Flow

1

What is known so far is the initial stipulation of Field Induction, the instance declaration, and the working domain. On these alone, is it already clear which structures an act of Field Induction carries at minimum?

It is not. Three items can be read directly from the initial stipulation: at the start of W, F already has the Trace-Response set T and its organization (“starting from existing organization”), written Org₀(F, T); within W some process actually applies effective action to the conditions or organization of F (“changing the conditions and organization of the field”); within W the net tendency difference ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 (“causing the field to have an inclination,” read as a net difference under the working domain). But the stipulation does not unfold what bears “the required direction,” or what the relation is between the action and the change of tendency.

Are these three items enough on their own? Consider a field with only environmental inflow: F is H₀; within W an environmental deepening input s = 1 arrives ten times, w increases from 1 to (11/10)¹⁰ ≈ 2.594, and Φ increases with it. Starting organization is present, action is present (the inflow changes the organization through F's own rules), and the net tendency difference is present. But by the protocol the inflow has no object of alignment: no person and no mechanism sent it in so that F would generate some content. This judgment rests on the protocol's declaration of “no goal”; it is not read off the reading. The initial stipulation distinguishes “changing the tendency” from “inducing in the required direction”; this example has only the former. The three items are not enough.

What is missing is “the required direction.”

Lemma 1 (Goal specification)  In any Ind(ι), there exists g such that GoalRep(Xg, g) ∧ Target(g, F).  
Proof: The initial stipulation says that Field Induction causes the field to have an inclination in the required direction, and that the required direction points to object-level content. “Required” is a relational predicate: there is something that requires and something that is required. In ι, what requires is the declared Xg; what is required is a piece of object-level content to be generated, and it must be borne by some description, for otherwise “the required direction” has no referent within W, which is incompatible with the actually holding relation “causing the field to have an inclination in the required direction”: what actually occurs within W is Field Induction, not the after-the-fact naming of some change. Hence within W there is a description g borne by Xg, and g points to object-level content required for F. ∎  
In the example above all three items are present while no bearer holds a goal specification; hence the structure of Lemma 1 cannot be replaced by the three items. g is a description of content to be generated; the reading target derived from it and the qualification judgment of whether F already has the content g points to are both kept separate from g; see Preliminary Distinctions.

2

We now have Org₀, action, net tendency difference, and goal specification. Is Field Induction already closed?

No. Suppose Xa actually carries out the act a according to g, but a falls on another field F′ and never reaches F, while the environmental inflow of Argument Flow 1 continues on F as before. Org₀ is present, there is action on F (the inflow is its source), the net tendency difference is present, and the goal specification is present. But the party bearing g does nothing to F, and the tendency change of F has nothing to do with it. The “action within W” of the previous round did not specify whose action it was.

Lemma 2 (The act's action on the field)  In any Ind(ι), Eff(a, F).  
Proof: In the initial stipulation, the subject of “changing the conditions and organization of the field” is the Field Induction itself; in ι, the actual process of the Field Induction is a. If within W a applies no effective action to the conditions or organization of F, then no change on F issues from this act of Field Induction, and “Field Induction changes the conditions and organization of the field” has no actual bearer. ∎  
In the example above Org₀, action, net tendency difference, and goal specification are all present while Eff fails; hence Eff cannot be replaced by what was obtained before.

3

We now have Org₀, goal specification, net tendency difference, and Eff(a, F). Is Field Induction already closed?

No. Let F be H₀ with an added state variable u: u does not enter Φ, and none of F's rules reads u. a reaches F but only changes u from 0 to 1; meanwhile the environmental inflow of Argument Flow 1 changes w as before, and ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0. The first four items are all present, but there is no actual connection between the action of a and the formation of the tendency.

What must be added is the relation between a and the formation of the tendency. The initial stipulation says that Field Induction is “causing the field to have … an inclination”: “causing” is a causative relation, and the change of tendency must have a part that issues from the act.

Lemma 3 (Bearing chain)  In any Ind(ι), there exists τ ∈ W such that the change left on the conditions or organization of F by the effective action of a takes part, through an actual material route, in determining that part of the organization or conditions bearing Φ\_𝓘 at τ on which Φ\_𝓘 depends.  
Proof: If no moment within W satisfies this relation, then throughout W none of the changes left by a takes part in the organization or conditions bearing the tendency; every change of tendency within W is borne by processes other than a, and the “causing” in “Field Induction causes the field to have an inclination” has no actual bearer. ∎  
How strong should this relation be taken to be? Lemma 3 asserts only that one such moment exists within W, and nothing more. Two examples show that stronger requirements would exclude legitimate acts of Field Induction. First (learning condition): F is H₀ with starting point e = 1/10; a changes e to 11/10 according to the goal and does not change w directly; then a deepening input s = 1 arrives once, F's own rule writes w ← w + e·s·w, and w changes from 1 to 2.1; with a removed and this input kept, w changes from 1 to 1.1. At the moment a ends, Φ has not changed; if the change made by a were required to enter the function Φ directly, a would be excluded. Yet a changed the conditions, and F's successor write carried this change into the tendency; Lemma 3 holds with τ taken as the moment the write is completed. Second (overwrite): F is H₀; at t₁, a, following the goal g: w\* = 2, reads w and updates it from 1 to 2 by w ← w − ½·2(w − 2); at t₂ a copy input c = 3 arrives, and F's own rule writes w ← 3, without reading w₁ and without being determined by a or g. Φ goes 1 → 2 → 3; with a removed and the copy input kept, it goes 1 → 1 → 3; the net difference over W is 2. If a's bearing were required to persist to the end point of W, a would be excluded; yet a did bear the tendency at t₁, and Lemma 3 holds with τ = t₁. The earlier example that changes only u has no moment in the whole of W that satisfies the relation, and is excluded.

We write

　　BearAt(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ): the relation of Lemma 3 holds at moment τ;  
　　Bear(a, Φ\_𝓘; W) :⇔ ∃τ ∈ W · BearAt(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ).

This relation lies on a chain of actual processes, not on an instantaneous state function; it requires only participation, not sole determination, nor does it require making a difference to Φ\_𝓘; it presupposes no shares of the tendency difference, no additivity, and no unique decomposition; W is fixed as originally declared and is not narrowed after the fact because of later overwriting.

Three readings must be kept apart here: the acted-on component entering the function Φ directly (direct component dependence); Φ not being constant along the actual joint change; and bearing dependence through history. Bear takes the third, which contains the first as a special case (chain length zero) and does not require the second. Mathematical illustration: the acted-on components x and y both enter Φ = x + y + z, while the joint change (1, 1, 1) → (2, 0, 1) keeps Φ at 3 throughout—the acted-on components enter Φ, yet Φ does not change along the actual change.

Nor does Bear absorb the net tendency difference. Let F be H₀; within W, a adds 1 to w while another act simultaneously subtracts 1 from w: Eff and Bear both hold, and the net difference is zero. The three propositions “a bore the tendency within W,” “a's bearing persists to the end point of W,” and “the net tendency difference between the start and end of W is nonzero” do not substitute for one another; in the overwrite example the first and third hold and the second does not.

4

We now have Org₀, goal specification, net tendency difference, Eff, and Bear. Is Field Induction already closed?

No. Let F be H₀, and let Xa = Xg hold the goal specification g: w\* = 2, with the loss (w − 2)² as reading target. Xa executes a schedule written before g appeared and not reading g when executed; the schedule directly rewrites w from 1 to 1.9, Bear holds, ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0, and the loss happens to fall from 1 to 0.01. The first five items are all present: the goal is held, the act acts on the field and bears the tendency, and the result is even close to the goal. But g plays no role whatever in a; had g been something else, a would have been executed just the same. “The schedule was written before the goal appeared and does not read the goal when executed” is a historical stipulation in the construction protocol, not a fact read off the fall of the loss.

Lemma 4 (Goal condition)  In any Ind(ι), g takes part, through an actual material route, in determining at least one of the occurrence, timing, site of action, direction, or strength of a.  
Proof: The initial stipulation says that Field Induction causes the field to have an inclination “in the required direction”; this direction is the direction required by what requires it, and only when what requires it leads toward it is there “induction.” If g takes part in determining no aspect of a, then the occurrence and form of a have nothing to do with g; even if the action of a on F happens to point toward g, it merely coexists with g and is not led in the required direction by what requires it; “in the required direction” has no actual bearer in a. ∎  
In the example above Org₀, goal specification, net tendency difference, Eff, and Bear are all present while goal condition fails; hence goal condition cannot be replaced by what was obtained before.

Must the route lie inside Xg or Xa? No. Lemma 4 requires only an actual material route; if g is issued by Xg, returns through an out-of-boundary relay Z, and the act is then carried out by Xa, Lemma 4 still holds.

5

We now have Org₀, GoalRep ∧ Target, ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0, Eff, Bear, and GoalCond. Is Field Induction already closed?

Check the initial stipulation term by term: “starting from existing organization”—Org₀; “changing the conditions and organization of the field”—Eff (Lemma 2); “causing the field to have … an inclination”—ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 and Bear (Lemma 3); “in the required direction”—goal specification (Lemma 1) and goal condition (Lemma 4). Every term of the stipulation now has a proved necessary bearer. Forcing stops.

Only the necessary direction is checked here. Whether the presence of all six duty groups suffices for Field Induction to be established is unproved; see “Unanswered.”

6

We now have the six duty groups. Which other features that are often held to be necessary must an act of Field Induction carry? We test them one by one. All the following examples fall on H₀ (starting point (1, 0, 1/10)). Each example is directly an act of Field Induction under the initial stipulation: starting from the existing organization of H₀, an act led by a goal specification changes the conditions or organization of F, so that the tendency of F changes. They are acts of Field Induction, not obtained by inferring backward from the six duty groups (which are only necessary conditions); the six duty groups can be read off one by one in each example, consistently with the master theorem. Which inputs are sent in within W is stated for each example.

A negative-feedback closed loop, reading the loss in the present act, and iteration are not necessary. No input within W. Following the goal specification y = 3x, Xa makes a single direct write w := 3, reading no loss this time, with no closed loop and no iteration: the written value is determined by the goal specification (goal condition); the direct write acts on F (Eff); Φ goes from 1 to 3, and the bearing chain holds directly at the moment of writing (Bear); ΔΦ\_W = 2.

Progress in the present act and reaching the goal are not necessary. No input within W. Goal w\* = 2, loss (w − 2)²; from the goal, by gradient computation, Xa carries out one step w ← w − 1.5·2(w − 2); w changes from 1 to 4, and the loss rises from 1 to 4. The goal takes part, through the gradient, in determining the act; the act acts on F, and Φ changes; on this occasion it moves away from the goal and does not reach it.

Stopping adjustment in finite time is not necessary. No input within W. Goal w\* = 2, loss (w − 2)², gradient act with step size 1, w\_{n+1} = w\_n − 2(w\_n − 2) = 4 − w\_n, with no stopping rule. From w\_{n+2} = w\_n and w₀ = 1 we get w\_{2k} = 1, w\_{2k+1} = 3: the goal takes part in determining every step, and every step changes the host; the loss stays at 1, the goal is never reached, and adjustment never stops. Under the net-difference reading of the working domain, only windows of a single step or an odd number of steps are taken; windows of an even number of steps have zero net difference and do not serve as positive examples within the domain of this Form.

An actor outside the field is not necessary. No input within W. A single direct write w := 3 is executed according to the goal by a maintenance mechanism inside F; the values are as in the first example, and all six duty groups hold.

A change of physical links and a change of weights are not necessary. No input within W. Following the goal, Xa directly writes the threshold θ: 0 → −1; links and weights are both unchanged, and Φ goes from 1 to 2.

A human actor is not necessary. In the two gradient-act examples, what holds the goal specification and executes the update within W is a computing device and a program; the person who set the goal takes no part within W.

Identifiability of the act relation from the end state of the field is not necessary. Compare two histories; the declared state of F is the whole of (w, θ, e), with no additional record of any kind; the controller is outside F. History A is the overwrite example of Argument Flow 3: at t₁, a writes w from 1 to 2 following the goal g: w\* = 2, and at t₂ the copy input c = 3 writes w ← 3. Under the initial stipulation, History A is an act of Field Induction: starting from existing organization, a, led by the goal specification, rewrites w, so that the tendency changes; the six duty groups can be read off one by one (Org₀; g is borne by Xg; ΔΦ\_W = 2; a acts on F; bearing at τ = t₁; the amount a writes is determined by g). History B has no a, only the same copy input. The end states of both histories are (3, 0, 1/10), and every component of the declared state is the same, yet the former contains an act of Field Induction and the latter does not. Hence in this example the act relation cannot be identified from the end state of F; identification requires additional history or records outside the field.

Visibility of the act's effect at the moment the act ends is not necessary (the learning-condition example of Argument Flow 3). Persistence of the act's bearing to the end point of W is not necessary (the overwrite example of Argument Flow 3).

Each example above is an act of Field Induction lacking the corresponding feature; the missing feature is therefore not a necessary structure.

7

We now have the six duty groups. How is an established act of Field Induction read back by mode of action?

Antecedent: ι is already established as an act of Field Induction (not merely inferred from Eff), and a regulated field B′ ⊆ F is declared. By the classification in Preliminary Distinctions, we look only at the layer on which the actual change of a to B′ falls and the route it takes: a direct transformation of the physical layer of B′ is host adjustment; opening and closing the existing pathways of B′ by suppression and disinhibition and adjusting their degree of permeability, constructing a dynamic logical structure on top of the existing physical links, is tuning; what enters B′ only as a signal is input, and a physical-layer change occurring thereby, or through flow guided by tuning, is sedimentation; neither of these two is a mode of action of the act, and neither changes the classification above. Who the actor is, whether it is inside or outside B′, and whether it hijacks play no part in this branching. A single act may contain both a direct-write part and a gating part, each part being labeled with its mode of implementation; this is a label of the mode of action, not an event qualification: whether some part independently stands as an act of host adjustment or of tuning is determined separately by *The General Form of Host Adjustment* and *The General Form of Tuning*.

This is a branching read-out of the initial stipulation; the layer on which a change falls must be judged by mechanism, not by variable name. The fixed schedule of Argument Flow 4 and the goalless inflow of Argument Flow 1 do not satisfy the antecedent; they are not Field Induction and fall into neither branch. Whether there are Field Induction acts that are neither direct writes nor suppression and disinhibition, and how they are to be classified, is unanswered.

Proof of Minimal Structure

Load-bearing classification. Org₀ is inherited from the initial stipulation (“starting from existing organization”); ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 is inherited from the initial stipulation and read as a net difference under the working domain; GoalRep ∧ Target is proved by Lemma 1, with the phrase “object-level content” inherited; Eff is proved by Lemma 2; Bear is proved by Lemma 3, with the readings of “action” and “conditions” for each link of the chain inherited from *The General Form of Field Concretization*; GoalCond is proved by Lemma 4. Each of the four lemmas is drawn out by the gap in the corresponding round of the Argument Flow: a countermodel shows that what was obtained before is insufficient, and the lemma proves, from the corresponding term of the initial stipulation, that the item is necessary for any established act of Field Induction. This Form has no H conditions: Bear introduces no additional assumptions of decomposability, transitivity, or direct action; the working-domain stipulations (the net-difference reading, the declaration of 𝓘, and the Field-Concretization Qualification of the induced field by actual window) are not H, but downstream citations must keep them.

Irreplaceability. The following are witnesses that deletion candidates are insufficient, not breaches of the master theorem:  
(i) Org₀ ∧ action ∧ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 ⇏ GoalRep ∧ Target (the environmental inflow of Argument Flow 1, relative to the no-goal protocol);  
(ii) Org₀ ∧ GoalRep ∧ Target ∧ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 ⇏ Eff(a, F) (Argument Flow 2, the act landing elsewhere);  
(iii) adding Eff ⇏ Bear (Argument Flow 3, the act changing only the unread u);  
(iv) adding Bear ⇏ GoalCond (Argument Flow 4, the fixed schedule);  
(v) Eff ∧ Bear ⇏ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 (Argument Flow 3, simultaneous cancellation).  
Each countermodel keeps everything obtained before and lacks only the item under test. Bear already takes the weakest strength asserted by Lemma 3, the existence of one bearing moment within W; the learning-condition example and the overwrite example show that the stronger immediate reading and end-point reading would exclude legitimate acts of Field Induction.

Non-mergeability. Goal specification and goal condition cannot substitute for each other: the fixed schedule has the former without the latter. The net tendency difference and the bearing chain cannot substitute for each other: the cancellation example has the latter without the former, and the example changing only u has the former without the latter. Eff and Bear cannot substitute for each other: the example changing only u has the former without the latter.

Exclusion of proposed additions. For each item listed in Argument Flow 6 there are acts of Field Induction, established under the initial stipulation, that lack it; none enters N.

Scope. Minimality is limited to this: the listed weaker candidates cannot replace the duties unfolded. Uniqueness, global minimality, and uniqueness of realization are unproved.

Master Formula and Master Theorem

Master formula (necessary direction):

　　Ind(ι) ⇒ ∃T, g \[ Org₀(F, T) ∧ GoalRep(Xg, g) ∧ Target(g, F) ∧ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 ∧ Eff(a, F) ∧ Bear(a, Φ\_𝓘; W) ∧ GoalCond(a, g) \]

Read-back. ι = (F, Xg, Xa, a, W, L, 𝓘) is the declared instance, and F is a concretized field within the working domain; T is the Trace-Response set of F at the start of W; g is the goal specification. Org₀(F, T): at the start of W, F already has T and its organization. GoalRep(Xg, g): Xg bears g within W. Target(g, F): g points to object-level content required for F; g is kept separate from the reading target and from content qualification. ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0: at level L and on input class 𝓘, the readings at the start and end of W differ. Eff(a, F): within W, a actually applies effective action to the conditions or organization of F. Bear(a, Φ\_𝓘; W): there exists a moment τ within W such that the change left by a takes part, through an actual material route, in determining that part of the organization or conditions bearing Φ\_𝓘 at τ on which Φ\_𝓘 structurally depends. GoalCond(a, g): g takes part, through an actual material route, in determining one of the occurrence, timing, site, direction, or strength of a. The seven atomic predicates share the same F, Xg, Xa, a, W, 𝓘; GoalRep and Target combine into one duty group, giving six groups in all; the tuple is not a causal chain, and the order of proof is not a temporal order.

Master Theorem  Within the working domain, for any Ind(ι), the items on the right-hand side of the master formula hold together.

Proof: Org₀ is given by the initial stipulation “starting from existing organization”; ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 is given by the initial stipulation “causing the field to have an inclination” together with the net-difference reading of the working domain; GoalRep ∧ Target by Lemma 1, Eff by Lemma 2, Bear by Lemma 3, GoalCond by Lemma 4. All four lemmas are proved for an arbitrary established Ind(ι) and do not depend on the countermodels given; the countermodels show only that each item cannot be replaced by what was obtained before. The items take the same arguments of the same instance ι, so the conjunction holds. ∎

Strength. The master theorem states the necessary direction. Whether the presence of all six duty groups implies Ind(ι) is unproved; they do not serve as an identification criterion for Field Induction.

Corollary (Branching of modes of action)  Given that Ind(ι) is established and a regulated field B′ ⊆ F is declared, within the actual change of a to B′, the part that directly transforms the physical layer is implemented in the mode of host adjustment, and the part that constructs a dynamic logical structure by suppression and disinhibition is implemented in the mode of tuning; the actor's location, its identity, and whether it hijacks play no part in the branching (Argument Flow 7). This branching gives labels of the mode of action and does not confer on any part an independent event qualification as host adjustment or tuning.

Breach Modes

With the object domain, working domain, and entry conditions stated by this Form all kept, exhibit an act of Field Induction for which some item on the right-hand side of the master formula fails; or prove that some item can be deleted, or weakened to a tested candidate, while Field Induction still holds; or show that the proof of one of the four lemmas fails. Exhibiting only another physical realization of the same duty is not a breach; a counterexample that falls only in maintenance-type cases, in cases where the induced field lacks Field-Concretization Qualification, or in cases that do not meet the entry conditions is a boundary case, not a breach.

Interfaces

Input: this Group Form's initial stipulations on Field Induction, tendency, mode of action, and content; the characterization of action and organizational conditioning in Section 3 of *The General Form of Field Concretization*, and Field-Concretization Qualification read by actual window; Section 10 of the Computation Group Form.

Output: the six necessary duty groups of an act of Field Induction and their working-domain readings; the branching read-back of modes of action. Later Forms may use these up to the strength of the necessary direction and may not use them as an identification criterion for Field Induction.

— To *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction* and *The Necessity Form of Field-Induction Action*: the net tendency difference is a result to be guaranteed, not an antecedent; Bear supplies the form of the condition that the act, at some moment within W, takes part in forming the tendency through an actual bearing chain, including indirect historical routes through retention and successor mechanisms, and excluding end-point persistence.

The core antecedents of this Form are not supplied by downstream Forms that take the conclusions of this Form as premises.

Appendix: Formalization

Notation. F: the induced field; B′ ⊆ F: the declared regulated field; T: the Trace-Response set of F; W = \[t\_s, t\_e\]: the declared time window; L: level and resolution; 𝓘: the declared read-out input class; Φ\_𝓘: the reading function, organizational state → ℝ; a: a stretch of actual process of Xa within W; g: the goal specification.

Definition 1 (Structural dependence)  Let Φ\_𝓘 = f(c, r), where c is some part of the organization or conditions and r is the rest. Φ\_𝓘 is said to depend on c if there exist two possible values c₁, c₂ of c and one and the same r such that f(c₁, r) ≠ f(c₂, r).

Definition 2 (Bearing moment)  BearAt(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ): τ ∈ W, and τ is no earlier than the start of the action of a; there exists a chain of actual processes a → ζ₁ → … → ζ\_k (k ≥ 0), each link of which is an actual action or an actual conditioning relation in the sense of *The General Form of Field Concretization*, starting from the change a leaves on F and ending at that part of the organization or conditions bearing Φ\_𝓘 at τ on which Φ\_𝓘 depends.

Definition 3  Bear(a, Φ\_𝓘; W) :⇔ ∃τ ∈ W · BearAt(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ).
