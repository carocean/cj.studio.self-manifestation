# The General Form of Host Adjustment

This paper takes up one master proposition:

When a host adjustment actually occurs, whether the regulated field is neural tissue, an artificial network, or some other concretized field, whether the actor is a training program, a maintenance mechanism, or a device outside the field, and whether what it changes is connections, weights, thresholds, or learning conditions, what structure must it carry that it cannot lose? This Group Form's initial stipulation on host adjustment is as follows. Host adjustment is one mode of action of Field Induction, and the actual change it makes to the regulated target is a direct transformation of the physical layer of the Trace-Response set: a direct alteration of the rules, parameters, or organization of the host level, such as adding or removing connections, changing weights, changing the degree of permeability, or changing integration parameters. An action that merely enters as a signal and is processed by the field's own basic rules is input; a subsequent change in the physical layer caused by the field's own rules is sedimentation; neither is host adjustment. Who the actor is, and whether it is inside or outside the field, plays no part in this distinction. This Group Form takes these stipulations as premises and does not prove them. *The General Form of Field Induction* has proved that an act of Field Induction necessarily has six duty groups on the same instance. What else must a host adjustment carry beyond these six groups?

Object: a host adjustment Retune(κ), κ = (B′, Xg, Xa, a, W, L, 𝓘), with an already established host adjustment as antecedent. B′ is the regulated field, that is, the induced field of this act of Field Induction (in instances of *The General Form of Field Induction*, take F := B′); Xg, Xa, a, W, L, 𝓘 are as in *The General Form of Field Induction*; Φ\_𝓘 reads the tendency of B′. B′ may be one block of a larger system; that larger system fills no argument of this Form. Xa may lie inside or outside B′. Each of the above items stays fixed throughout every round of questioning.

Working domain and inheritance boundary: the antecedent is that Retune(κ) is established. B′ must satisfy on its own the working domain of *The General Form of Field Induction*: B′ is a concretized field (its declared mechanism admits, at the rule level, legitimate witnesses of the three duties of Trace-Response, organizational participation, and historical dynamics (the historical action may be borne via retention or out-of-boundary processes), and it acquires Field-Concretization Qualification in some declared actual window, which need not be W); the reading is Φ\_𝓘 on L and 𝓘, and “changing the tendency” is read as a nonzero net tendency difference within W, ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0. That B′ is a subdomain of some concretized field does not thereby give it qualification. Host attribution must be declared: every host parameter (connection, weight, permeability, integration parameter, learning condition, rule) belongs to exactly one field block; the input channels of B′ are written into its field specification; the side to which each cross-boundary connection belongs is fixed in advance and not adjusted afterwards. The inherited items are: the master theorem of *The General Form of Field Induction* v1.0 (six necessary duty groups, necessary direction) and its branching read-back of modes of action; this Group Form's initial stipulation on host adjustment; the delimitation of action and condition in Section 3 of *The General Form of Field Concretization*; the provisions of the Computation Group Form that the host level and the dynamics level are separate and that writing weights in training is a host transformation. The internal structures of tuning and tendency raising are developed by the other Forms of this Group Form; their conclusions are not premises of this Form.

We state in advance how far this question is answered. The sub-questions that actually arise and are answered are as follows.  
First, once a host adjustment has the six duty groups of Field Induction, is host adjustment fully unfolded? No: a direct transformation of the physical layer of B′ is required (Argument Flow 1). Proved.  
Second, once there is also a direct transformation, is it fully unfolded? No: the bearing of the tendency must pass through this direct transformation (Argument Flow 2). Proved. The bearing chain is constrained only at its starting point; the route after it may leave the boundary and return, and may pass through the field's own sedimentation.  
Third, how is an influence transmitted through another subfield read relative to B′? It is reported separately by actual landing point and route, not classified by source: if an act directly writes only another subfield, its influence on B′ enters only through declared input channels and is processed by B′'s own basic rules, and B′ has no direct-write landing point, then relative to B′ the act is not a host adjustment; the cross-boundary part is reported as input, and within it the physical write-back formed by B′'s own rules is reported as sedimentation (Argument Flow 3). Proved. Cases in which the output of another subfield gates B′ or drives a direct write to B′ are judged separately along the actual chain.  
Fourth, are any of the following necessary: the actor being outside the regulated field; the goal, content evaluation, stop control, and raw data being outside the regulated field; a negative-feedback closed loop, reading the loss in the present act, iteration; changing rules; directly writing tendency-bearing parameters; changing weights; progress in the present act, reaching the goal, stopping adjustment in finite time? None is necessary. Proved (Argument Flow 5). Hijacking plays no part in the classification of host adjustment. Engineering permission: classification does not grant permission, and the two are judged separately; whether host adjustment entails permission is unanswered.  
Overall conclusion: a host adjustment necessarily has the six duty groups of Field Induction plus two items: a direct transformation of the physical layer of the regulated field (Rewrite), and a bearing chain that starts from this direct transformation (Bear\_R). Neither can be replaced by the six duty groups, and the latter cannot be replaced by the former; the converse is unproved.

In this paper we set out, through persistent questioning, to find where the proof must go, and to derive the General Form of Host Adjustment.

Our goal is as follows:

Starting from the conclusions of *The General Form of Field Induction* and the initial stipulation on host adjustment, unfold the mode stipulation “directly transforming the physical layer” into the duties that host adjustment necessarily carries; prove that every established host adjustment requires them; prove that the six duty groups of Field Induction cannot replace them; treat compound events transmitted through another subfield; exclude features often mistaken as necessary for host adjustment; and state the strength of what this Form proves.

We begin with the result.

What follows is the abbreviated form of the results of this paper. The proofs are in the Argument Flow, the Proof of Minimal Structure, and “Master Formula and Master Theorem”; the formal definitions are in the Appendix.

　　Retune(κ) ⇒ the six duty groups of Ind(κ) (F := B′) ∧ Rewrite(a, B′) ∧ Bear\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘; W)  
　　Bear\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘; W) :⇔ ∃τ ∈ W · BearAt\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ)

Here Rewrite(a, B′) says that at least part of a directly transforms the physical layer of B′ (the rules, parameters, or organization of the host level); BearAt\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ) says that a has some actual direct-write part on B′ such that, starting from the change this same part leaves on B′, it takes part, through an actual material route, in determining that part of the organization or conditions bearing Φ\_𝓘 at τ on which Φ\_𝓘 depends. The six duty groups and these two items take the same B′, Xg, Xa, a, W, 𝓘 as arguments. Bear\_R implies Rewrite and Bear, while Rewrite does not imply Bear\_R: the two are progressive duties, “there is a direct write” and “that direct write bears the tendency.” This is the necessary direction; the converse is unproved.

In One Sentence

Within the declared working domain of net tendency difference, an established host adjustment necessarily has the six duty groups of Field Induction and necessarily has a direct transformation of the physical layer of the regulated field, and the bearing of the tendency starts from this direct transformation. Whether these suffice to identify host adjustment has not been proved.

Abstract

*The General Form of Field Induction* has shown that an established act of Field Induction necessarily has six duty groups on the same instance (starting organization, goal specification, net tendency difference, the act's action on the field, bearing chain, goal condition) and branches by implementation layer into host adjustment and tuning. Host adjustment, as one mode of Field Induction, carries all six groups; but the six groups do not restrict the route: an act may merely send signals to be processed by the field's own rules, or it may directly rewrite the field's host. Starting from an established host adjustment, this paper asks what the mode stipulation forces beyond the six groups.

A process that sends teaching signals according to a goal, with the field's own write rule deepening the weight, can have all six duty groups, yet it is input and sedimentation, not host adjustment; hence a direct transformation of the physical layer of the regulated field is required. Even with a direct transformation, the transformation may land on a parameter that is never read, while the change in tendency is borne entirely by signals sent at the same time and by sedimentation; host adjustment causes the field to have an inclination by way of direct transformation, so the bearing must start from this direct transformation. After the starting point of the bearing, the route is unrestricted: directly rewriting a learning condition, after which a relay outside the field leads the field's own rules to rewrite the weight, is equally host adjustment.

Influences transmitted through another subfield are reported separately by actual landing point and route, not classified by source: directly writing only the parameters of another subfield, when the influence enters the regulated field only through declared input channels and is processed by the regulated field's own rules, and the regulated field has no direct-write landing point, is not host adjustment relative to the regulated field; the cross-boundary part is input, and the physical write-back formed by the regulated field's own rules is sedimentation. The actor outside the field; the goal, evaluation, stop control, and raw data outside the field; a negative-feedback closed loop, reading the loss, iteration; changing rules; directly writing tendency-bearing parameters; changing weights; progress, reaching the goal, stopping adjustment: for each of these there are host adjustments, established by the initial stipulation, that lack it, so none is a necessary structure.

Proved: an established host adjustment has, on the same instance, the six duty groups, Rewrite, and Bear\_R; the six groups cannot replace Rewrite, and the six groups together with Rewrite cannot replace Bear\_R. Unanswered: the converse; whether directly clamping the current running state counts as a direct transformation of the physical layer; physical cases in which direct write and input are hard to tell apart; the intrusion interface; whether host adjustment entails engineering permission; uniqueness and global minimality.

Preliminary Distinctions

What follows only fixes objects and terms; it bears no part of the necessity of the structural items.

Direct transformation and input. A change that is executed by the mechanism of the actor itself (or of a device it drives) and alters the rules, parameters, or organization of the host level of B′ is called a direct write; a signal that enters B′ and is processed by B′'s own basic rules is called input; a change in the physical layer caused by B′'s own basic rules (the write-back rules in the declared mechanism) is called sedimentation. The distinction depends on who executes the change and on which layer it lands, not on variable names, nor on where the actor is located: a write that an actor inside B′ executes directly according to the goal specification is likewise a direct write, and is not renamed sedimentation because of its location. One act may contain both direct-write and input parts, which are marked part by part. Direct write is a label for the mode of implementation, not an event qualification: a direct-write part is not a host adjustment merely because it is a direct write; whether some part is established as a host adjustment is assessed separately under this Form and the initial stipulation.

Bearing. Bearing is read as in *The General Form of Field Induction*: participation suffices, and sole determination is not required; the route may be direct, or may pass through retention, successor mechanisms, out-of-boundary processes and round trips, or indecomposable joint processes; it suffices that one bearing moment exists within W, without any requirement of immediate visibility or of persistence to the end; counterfactual comparison is a method of certification, not the content of bearing.

Hijacking and engineering permission. Hijacking means taking over the intention or replacing the tuning law; it is a different concept and plays no part in the classification of host adjustment. Engineering permission concerns whether an act is permitted under a given engineering regime; classifying an act as host adjustment does not grant permission, and the two are judged separately.

The host H₀ and B′. The constructed examples in the Argument Flow all take the scalar host H₀ of *The General Form of Field Induction* as the regulated field B′: state (w, θ, e), reading Φ = w − θ (𝓘 = {x = 1}), own rules the deepening input w ← w + e·s·w and the copy input w ← c; H₀ acquires Field-Concretization Qualification on the qualification window W\_q (*The General Form of Field Induction*, Lemma 0). In each example W lies after W\_q and the starting point is (w, θ, e) = (1, 0, 1/10), unless stated otherwise; which inputs are sent within W, and which additional state variables and external components there are, is stated in place in each example. External components do not belong to B′; host attribution is declared as above.

Argument Flow

1

What we currently know is the master theorem of *The General Form of Field Induction* (F := B′), the initial stipulation on host adjustment, the instance declaration, and the working domain. On these alone, is it already clear which structures a host adjustment carries at minimum?

It is not. A host adjustment is an act of Field Induction, so all six duty groups are present: B′ has a starting organization; Xg bears the goal specification; the net tendency difference within W is nonzero; a acts on B′; a bears the tendency at some moment within W; the goal takes part in determining a. But the six duty groups do not restrict the route.

Consider a process that sends teaching signals according to a goal: B′ = H₀; Xa, guided by the goal specification w\* = 1.21, selects and sends the deepening input s = 1 twice; B′'s own rule writes w ← w + e·s·w, w goes from 1 to 1.1 and then to 1.21, and Φ increases accordingly. The six duty groups can be read off one by one: the starting organization is present; the goal specification is borne by Xg; the net tendency difference is nonzero; a acts on B′ by sending signals; the signals a sends bear Φ via B′'s own rule; how many times to send is determined by the goal. But every change within B′ in this process is executed by B′'s own rules: it is input and the sedimentation that follows. By the initial stipulation, this is not host adjustment. The six duty groups are not enough.

Lemma 1 (Direct transformation)  In any Retune(κ), at least part of a directly transforms the physical layer of B′: it is executed by the mechanism of the actor itself (or of a device it drives) and alters the rules, parameters, or organization of the host level of B′. We write Rewrite(a, B′).  
Proof: Take any established Retune(κ). By the initial stipulation on host adjustment, the actual change this action makes to B′ includes a direct transformation of the physical layer; this actual change is executed by some part of a, and that part is a direct write to B′, so Rewrite(a, B′) holds. If a had no such part, the instance would not satisfy the mode condition of the initial stipulation, contradicting Retune(κ). ∎  
Actions that merely enter as signals and are processed by B′'s own rules (together with the sedimentation that follows), and actions that open and close existing pathways by suppression and disinhibition, are examples that fail this mode condition; we do not claim here that they exhaust all non-direct-write actions.

2

We now have the six duty groups and Rewrite. Is host adjustment closed?

No. Let B′ be H₀ plus one state variable u: u does not enter Φ, and no rule of B′ reads u. a consists of two parts: a direct write changing u from 0 to 5; and, at the same time, sending the deepening input s = 1 once, so that B′'s own rule writes w from 1 to 1.1. Both parts are determined by the goal. The six duty groups hold (the bearing chain runs through the sent signal and sedimentation); Rewrite also holds (u is directly written). But remove the direct-write part, and Φ still goes from 1 to 1.1; remove the sending part, and Φ does not change. The basis is not this comparison alone: u does not enter Φ and no rule reads it, so the change made by the direct write does not take part in bearing the tendency anywhere in W. The change in tendency is borne entirely by input and sedimentation.

Lemma 2 (Bearing that starts from the direct transformation)  In any Retune(κ), there exist τ ∈ W and some actual direct-write part a\_R of a on B′ such that, starting from the change this same a\_R leaves on B′, it takes part, through an actual material route, in determining that part of the organization or conditions bearing Φ\_𝓘 at τ on which Φ\_𝓘 depends. We write BearAt\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ), and Bear\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘; W) :⇔ ∃τ ∈ W · BearAt\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ).  
Proof: Host adjustment is Field Induction carried out by way of direct transformation of the physical layer; in host adjustment, the bearing chain of *The General Form of Field Induction* (“causing the field to have an inclination”) must be borne in this way. Suppose the change that each direct-write part of a leaves on B′ does not take part in bearing the tendency anywhere in W. Then a bears the tendency entirely through its non-direct-write parts; in this act of Field Induction, “causing the field to have an inclination” is neither borne by the direct transformation nor achieved by way of direct transformation, contradicting Retune(κ). ∎  
One a may contain several direct-write segments: it suffices that one of them satisfies Lemma 2, and not every direct-write segment need bear; nor may a bearing chain be pieced together from different segments or different events.  
Lemma 2 constrains only the starting point of the bearing chain, not the route after it. An example: B′ = H₀; outside B′ there is a relay Z, which reads the learning condition e of B′ and, if e \> 1/2, sends the deepening input s = 1 to B′ once, and otherwise sends nothing; Z does not read the intention and does not change patency. According to the goal, a directly writes e from 1/10 to 11/10; Z reads e and sends s = 1 to B′; B′'s own rule writes w ← w + e·s·w, and w goes from 1 to 2.1. Remove a: e stays at 1/10, Z sends nothing, and w remains 1. The bearing chain a →(direct write) e →(Z reads and sends) s →(B′'s own rule) w → Φ leaves B′ and returns, passing on the way through the sedimentation of B′. Lemma 2 holds with τ taken as the moment the write to w is completed; if the bearing chain were required to stay within B′, this host adjustment would be excluded. In the previous example, the direct write to u satisfies Lemma 2 at no moment of W and is excluded.

Lemma 2 is a specialization of Bear in *The General Form of Field Induction*: the starting point is restricted to the change left on B′ by some actual direct-write part, and the rest of the reading is unchanged; no in-boundary, immediacy, or end-point requirement is added.

3

We now have the six duty groups, Rewrite, and Bear\_R. If an act influences B′ through another subfield, how is it read relative to B′?

Let B′ = H₀, and let there be another subfield B″ with parameter v; at every beat, the rule of B″ sends v into the input channel of B′ as the deepening input s; the connection B″→B′ is declared as belonging to B″. According to the goal, a directly writes v from 0 to 1; B″ sends s = 1 into B′; B′'s own rule writes w from 1 to 1.1. Separate reporting: the event a is a direct write to the physical layer of B″, with its landing point in B″; the output of B″ is input relative to B′; the change of w within B′ is executed by B′'s own rule and is sedimentation; the historical connection a → v → s → w → Φ is recorded as it stands. Relative to B′, a involves no direct transformation, so the conclusion of Lemma 1, Rewrite(a, B′), fails, and a is not a host adjustment of B′. The basis is which layer the change lands on and who executes it, not labels such as “normal operation” or “intermediary.” Whether a is a host adjustment relative to B″ is assessed separately under a separate declaration for B″ (which requires the Field-Concretization Qualification of B″, a reading, and the six duty groups). If the declaration assigns the connection B″→B′ to B′, and what a directly writes is exactly this connection, then a has a direct-write landing point in B′ and the classification changes accordingly; this is decided by the prior declaration of host attribution. The conclusion of this example depends on three conditions: the influence enters B′ through a declared input channel; the physical write-back within B′ is executed by B′'s own basic rules; a has no direct-write landing point in B′. That the source is the output of another subfield does not by itself decide whether, at B′, it is input, gating, or a driven direct write: if the output of B″ opens and closes existing pathways of B′ through a suppression channel, what happens at the B′ end is a gating change, not a sedimentary write-back; if it drives a device that directly writes physical parameters of B′, the actual chain must be checked for whether B′ has a direct-write landing point. This example does not decide these two cases.

The current running state of B′ changes as B′'s rules process input; the executor is B′'s rules, so this does not constitute a direct write. Whether directly clamping the current running state, bypassing B′'s rules, counts as a direct transformation of the physical layer: see “Unanswered.”

4

We now have the six duty groups, Rewrite, and Bear\_R. Is host adjustment closed?

Check the initial stipulation term by term: host adjustment is Field Induction — the six duty groups (inherited); “a direct transformation of the physical layer of the Trace-Response set” — Rewrite (Lemma 1); the direct transformation “thereby” causes the field to have an inclination — Bear\_R (Lemma 2) and the net tendency difference. The initial stipulation states explicitly that the actor's location plays no part in the distinction, so no location item is introduced. Forcing stops.

5

We now have the six duty groups, Rewrite, and Bear\_R. Which other features are often taken to be necessary for host adjustment? We test them one by one. In each example below, B′ is H₀ (starting point (1, 0, 1/10)) and there is no input within W, unless stated otherwise. By the initial stipulation, each example is directly a host adjustment: the act is led by the goal specification, directly transforms the physical layer of B′, and changes the tendency of B′. The examples are not obtained by reasoning backward from the necessary conditions; the six duty groups, Rewrite, and Bear\_R can be read off in each of them one by one.

The actor being outside the regulated field: not necessary. A maintenance mechanism inside B′ directly writes w := 3 according to the goal specification y = 3x, and Φ goes from 1 to 3. The maintenance mechanism is neither of the two basic rules of H₀, nor does it process input; it executes the write directly according to the goal, so this is a direct write.

The goal bearer, content evaluation, stop control, and raw data being outside the regulated field: not necessary. Besides the state of H₀, B′ contains an internal controller: a goal register g\_B′ = 3, an evaluation quantity d = g\_B′ − w, a stop flag (set when d = 0), and the raw data w that it reads; all four are inside B′, and host attribution assigns them to B′. Outside B′ there is an actuator E: E receives only instructions issued by B′ (address w, increment d) and, following an instruction, directly writes w ← w + d; it reads no other state of B′, sets no patency, and replaces none of the rules of B′. B′ issues (w, 2); E writes w from 1 to 3; d becomes 0; the stop flag is set. The executor is outside, writes directly, and bears the tendency; the goal, evaluation, stop, and raw data are all inside.

A negative-feedback closed loop, reading the loss in the present act, iteration: not necessary. According to the goal specification y = 3x, Xa directly writes w := 3 once, reading no loss, with no closed loop and no iteration, and Φ goes from 1 to 3.

Changing rules: not necessary. The previous example writes only the parameter w; B′'s own two rules are unchanged.

Directly writing a tendency-bearing parameter: not necessary. In the relay example of Argument Flow 2, what a directly writes is the learning condition e; the tendency-bearing w is changed by B′'s own rule. The difference from Lemma 1 is this: a direct transformation of the physical layer is necessary, but the item transformed need not be a parameter on which Φ directly depends.

Changing weights: not necessary. According to the goal, Xa directly writes the threshold θ: 0 → −1; the weight is unchanged, and Φ goes from 1 to 2.

Progress in the present act and reaching the goal: not necessary. Goal w\* = 2, loss (w − 2)²; from the goal, via gradient computation, Xa directly writes one step w ← w − 1.5·2(w − 2); w goes from 1 to 4, and the loss rises from 1 to 4.

Stopping adjustment in finite time: not necessary. Goal w\* = 2, gradient direct writes with step size 1, w\_{n+1} = 4 − w\_n, with no stopping rule: w\_{2k} = 1, w\_{2k+1} = 3; the loss is constantly 1, the goal is never reached, and adjustment never stops. Under the net-difference reading, only single-step or odd-step windows are taken.

Each example above is a host adjustment that lacks the corresponding feature; the missing feature is therefore not a necessary structure of host adjustment.

Hijacking plays no part in the classification of host adjustment: whether hijacking and direct write occur together is reported separately by event. Engineering permission: classification does not grant permission, and the two are judged separately. No valid instance of “a host adjustment holds while engineering permission does not,” with a proof of its classification, has yet been given; whether host adjustment entails permission is unanswered, and “permission not certified” is not to be taken for “proved not permitted.”

Proof of Minimal Structure

Load-bearing classification. The six duty groups are inherited from the master theorem of *The General Form of Field Induction* v1.0 (taking F := B′; the condition for citing it is that B′ on its own satisfies that Form's working domain, which the working-domain declaration of this Form guarantees); Rewrite is proved by Lemma 1; Bear\_R is proved by Lemma 2, with the reading of bearing inherited from *The General Form of Field Induction*. Each lemma is drawn out by the gap in the corresponding round of the Argument Flow: a countermodel shows that what was obtained so far is insufficient, and the lemma proves, from the initial stipulation on host adjustment, that the item is necessary for every established host adjustment. This Form has no H conditions: the concretization qualification of B′ and host attribution are working-domain declarations, not antecedents awaiting proof, but downstream citations must retain them.

Irreplaceability. The following are witnesses that deletion candidates are insufficient, not breaches of the master theorem:  
(i) six duty groups ⇏ Rewrite (Argument Flow 1, sending teaching signals according to a goal);  
(ii) six duty groups ∧ Rewrite ⇏ Bear\_R (Argument Flow 2, the direct write lands on the unread u, and the tendency is borne by the signal sent at the same time and by sedimentation).  
Both countermodels preserve everything obtained before and lack only the item under test.

Implication and non-equivalence. Bear\_R implies Rewrite (the bearing starts from some actual direct-write part) and Bear; Rewrite does not imply Bear\_R, nor does Bear imply Bear\_R (both shown in Argument Flow 2). Logically, Rewrite can be absorbed into Bear\_R, but the two duties differ: Rewrite expresses “there is a direct write,” and Bear\_R expresses “that direct write bears the tendency.” Keeping both is a progressive unfolding, not a conjunction of mutually non-implying independent items.

Exclusion of proposed additions. For each item listed in Argument Flow 5 there is a host adjustment, established by the initial stipulation, that lacks it; none enters the master formula.

Scope. Minimality is limited to this: the listed weaker candidates cannot replace the duties unfolded. Uniqueness and global minimality are unproved.

Master Formula and Master Theorem

Master formula (necessary direction):

　　Retune(κ) ⇒ ∃T, g \[ Org₀(B′, T) ∧ GoalRep(Xg, g) ∧ Target(g, B′) ∧ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 ∧ Eff(a, B′) ∧ Bear(a, Φ\_𝓘; W) ∧ GoalCond(a, g) ∧ Rewrite(a, B′) ∧ Bear\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘; W) \]

Read-back. κ = (B′, Xg, Xa, a, W, L, 𝓘); B′ is a concretized field that on its own satisfies the working domain of *The General Form of Field Induction*, and host attribution has been declared. The first seven atomic predicates are read back as in *The General Form of Field Induction* and combine into the six duty groups. Rewrite(a, B′): at least part of a is executed by the mechanism of the actor itself (or of a device it drives) and alters the rules, parameters, or organization of the host level of B′. Bear\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘; W): there exist a moment τ within W and some actual direct-write part a\_R of a on B′ such that, starting from the change this same a\_R leaves on B′, it takes part, through an actual material route, in determining that part of the organization or conditions bearing Φ\_𝓘 at τ on which Φ\_𝓘 structurally depends; the route after that is unrestricted. All items share the same B′, Xg, Xa, a, W, 𝓘. Bear\_R implies Rewrite and Bear.

Master Theorem  Within the working domain, for any Retune(κ), all items on the right-hand side of the master formula hold simultaneously.

Proof: Host adjustment is Field Induction, so the master theorem of *The General Form of Field Induction* v1.0 (F := B′, with B′ satisfying its working domain) gives the six duty groups; Lemma 1 gives Rewrite; Lemma 2 gives Bear\_R. All items take the same arguments of the same instance κ, so the conjunction holds. ∎

Strength. The master theorem states the necessary direction. Whether the right-hand side implies Retune(κ) is unproved; the items do not serve as criteria for identifying host adjustment.

Corollary (Separate reporting of events through another subfield)  If an act directly writes only another subfield B″, its influence on B′ enters only through declared input channels and is processed by B′'s own basic rules, and a has no landing point of direct physical-layer transformation in B′, then relative to B′ it is not a host adjustment; the cross-boundary part is reported as input, and within it the physical write-back formed by B′'s own rules is reported as sedimentation (Argument Flow 3).  
Proof: a has no direct-write landing point in B′, so Rewrite(a, B′) fails, and by Lemma 1 the act is not a host adjustment relative to B′; the rest of the separate reporting follows the delimitations in the Preliminary Distinctions. ∎

Breach Modes

With the object domain, working domain, and declaration of host attribution stated by this Form all kept, exhibit a host adjustment in which Rewrite or Bear\_R fails; or prove that one of the two can be deleted, or weakened to a tested candidate, while host adjustment still holds; or show that the proof of Lemma 1 or Lemma 2 fails. Exhibiting only another physical realization of the same duty is not a breach. If the B′ of a counterexample lacks Field-Concretization Qualification, or its host attribution was not declared in advance or was altered afterwards, the case is a boundary case, not a breach.

Appendix: Formalization

Notation. B′: the regulated field; B″: another subfield; Z: a relay outside B′; E: an actuator outside B′; the state of H₀ is (w, θ, e), with reading Φ = w − θ; own rules: deepening input w ← w + e·s·w, copy input w ← c.

Definition 1 (Direct write)  A part a\_R of an act a is called a direct write to B′ if the change a\_R causes is executed by the mechanism of the actor itself (or of a device it drives) and lands on rules, parameters, or organization of a host level declared as belonging to B′. Rewrite(a, B′) :⇔ a contains a nonempty direct-write part.

Definition 2  BearAt\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ) if and only if τ ∈ W and there exists an actual direct-write part a\_R of a on B′ (in the sense of Definition 1) such that τ is no earlier than the start of a\_R and there exists an actual process chain a\_R → ζ₁ → … → ζ\_k (k ≥ 0), each link of which is an actual action or an actual conditioning relation in the sense of *The General Form of Field Concretization*, starting from the change this same a\_R leaves on B′ and ending at that part of the organization or conditions bearing Φ\_𝓘 at τ on which Φ\_𝓘 depends. Bear\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘; W) :⇔ ∃τ ∈ W · BearAt\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘, τ). By definition, Bear\_R ⇒ Rewrite and Bear\_R ⇒ Bear.
