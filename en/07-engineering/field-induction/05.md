# The General Form of Field Construction by Self-Manifestation-Field Induction

This paper takes up one master proposition:

When a field construction has genuinely taken place—whether what is built is a structure on neural tissue, on an artificial network, or on some other concretized field, whichever of tendency-raising, host-adjustment, and tuning events the construction process consists of, and whether the target structure grows through the field’s own continuation, is given directly by a single write, or is acquired directly from a pre-built organization—what structure must it carry that it cannot lose? Further, when the field construction is controllable—when the presence or absence of the declared structure varies with the goal—what more does it carry?

This Group Form's initial stipulation on field construction is as follows: field construction is the process of laying out generative conditions through tendency raising and Field Induction so that the declared target structure forms on the field. A field-construction process consists of tendency-raising events and Field Induction events; input that enters only as signals, and the field’s own operation and sedimentation, are not events of the field-construction process, though they may occur during field construction. Whether a target structure has formed is decided by its object criterion. The controllable field construction treated in this paper is field construction in which the presence or absence of the declared structure varies with the goal, relative to the declared family of alternative goals; it is one particular reading of “controllable”, not a definition of all controllability. Tendency raising, Field Induction, host adjustment, tuning, tendency, and mode of action are read according to the initial stipulations of the first four papers of this Group Form. These are stipulations that this Group Form takes as premises without proof.

Objects. Instance κ = (H, M, 𝒯, Crit, X, G\_𝒯, Proc, 𝒫, 𝔊, W). H is the host, M its declared mechanism, and F the field constructed on H; W is the time window; the input and environment within W are fixed in the declaration. 𝒯 is a nonempty class of target structures, each τ ∈ 𝒯 carrying an object criterion Crit\_τ. Time is ordered by phase: 0⁻ is the entry of W; 0⁺ is after the events of 𝒫 at the entry have completed and before the field’s own first operation; k is after the kth beat of operation; 0⁻ \< 0⁺ \< 1 \< 2 \< …, and the state at a given phase has only one Crit value. That τ “forms” within W means that Crit\_τ does not hold at the entry 0⁻ and holds from some later phase on. G\_𝒯 is the goal specification for 𝒯, held by the declared bearer X. 𝒫 is the field-construction process, consisting of tendency-raising events and Field Induction events. Proc is the declared field-construction procedure, which yields the process from the goal specification, 𝒫 = Proc(G\_𝒯); 𝔊 is the declared family of alternative goals, containing G\_𝒯. Each of the above items stays fixed throughout every round of questioning.

Object 1 (field construction) Make(κ): X holds G\_𝒯; every τ ∈ 𝒯 forms on H within W; and this formation is actually borne by 𝒫.  
Object 2 (controllable field construction) Make\_C(κ) :⇔ Make(κ) ∧ GoalResp(κ), where GoalResp(κ): for every τ ∈ 𝒯 there is G′ ∈ 𝔊 such that τ does not form when the field is constructed from the same entry state (the complete state on H at the entry of W, including the case where nothing is installed), with the same M, input, environment, random source, and W, and only the procedure replaced by Proc(G′). If goal-dependent tendency raising directly determines the initial organization, the comparison likewise starts from the same uninstalled entry state, and the difference in organization is counted among the goal-varying aspects, not as an uncontrolled background difference.  
GoalResp is a fact of comparison between the results of two field constructions: it looks only at results and says nothing about which part of the process acts. It holds relative to the goal family 𝔊 and the criterion Crit: adding or removing alternative goals can change its truth value; if control shows only in the strength, position, or timing of the declared structure varying with the goal, while the structure itself appears under every goal in 𝔊, the case does not belong to Make\_C. Make includes chance growth; Make\_C is a subclass of Make.

Working domain and inheritance boundary: the antecedent is that Make(κ) or Make\_C(κ) already holds. Single host, single field: every event of 𝒫 takes the same field F on H as its induced field or its tendency-raised field, and the time window of every Field Induction event lies within W; the mechanism of F, the host boundary, the input, the environment, and W are declared in advance. Field Induction events in 𝒫 are read under the working domain of *The General Form of Field Induction*, and F must acquire Field-Concretization Qualification on some declared actual window; tendency-raising events are read under *The General Form of Tendency Raising*. The declared mechanism is deterministic (or compared under a fixed seed), so that the two comparisons of GoalResp can be rechecked. The inherited items are: this Group Form’s initial stipulations on field construction, tendency raising, Field Induction, tendency, and mode of action; from *The General Form of Field Induction* v1.0, the six duty groups, the bearing reading (participation suffices, sole determination is not required; “the part depended on” is taken as structural dependence; counterfactual comparison is a method of certification, not the content of bearing) and the branching of modes of action; Rewrite and Bear\_R from *The General Form of Host Adjustment* v1.0; Gate and Bear\_G from *The General Form of Tuning* v1.0; Pot, MechC, Init, and Acq from *The General Form of Tendency Raising* v1.0; the three duties of *The General Form of Field Concretization* v1.0.

We state in advance how far this question is answered. The sub-questions that actually arise and are answered are as follows.  
First, whether each event of 𝒫 having the necessary structure of its class already amounts to field construction — no; some event in 𝒫 must actually take part in determining the formation of each target structure (Argument Flow 1); proved.  
Second, given formation bearing as well, what else field construction necessarily carries — 𝒫 is nonempty; within W there is organization on H bearing a tendency (general tendency); and the declared mechanism permits, at the rule level, legitimate witnesses of the three duties of Trace-Response, organizational participation, and historical dynamics (MechC). The capacities of Continuation Accumulation and Restructuring borne by native write-back by the field itself do not follow from this; they hold when the declared mechanism includes native write-back, and their general necessity is open. The relation between goal and formation is not among these: chance growth is also field construction (Argument Flow 2); proved.  
Third, what else controllable field construction necessarily carries — the goal-varying aspects of the process must take part in determining the formation of each target structure (Argument Flow 3); proved. The goal being held, the events being determined by the goal, and the events bearing the formation: none of the three suffices.  
Fourth, under what conditions each of the seven items of the original Engineering Chapter 1 (Differential Condition, Biased-Flow, Continuation Accumulation, tendency, tuning, Restructuring, Field Induction) is necessary — general tendency is necessary for field construction in general; mechanism-level Continuation Accumulation and Restructuring Capacity hold when the declared mechanism includes native write-back by the field itself, and their general necessity is open; Continuation Accumulation taking part in the first formation of the target structure, tuning, Field Induction events, tendency-raising events, and Biased-Flow (on the declared comparison unit) are in general not necessary, and are conditionally necessary under the corresponding target criteria or sub-routes; the Differential Condition, broad Biased-Flow, the whole-window reading of Continuation Accumulation, and tuning under dynamic allocation are open (Argument Flow 5).  
Fifth, the items of the original chapter’s limit formula and the place of training are rewritten under this paper’s current reading (Argument Flows 6 and 7).  
Overall conclusion: a field construction necessarily has the necessary structure of each event, formation bearing D1, a nonempty process, general tendency, and the rule-level mechanism condition MechC; a controllable field construction additionally and necessarily has goal bearing D2. Whether these suffice to establish field construction or controllable field construction (the converse) is unproved.

In this paper we set out, through persistent questioning, to find where the proof must go, and to derive the General Form of Field Construction by Self-Manifestation-Field Induction.

Our goal is as follows:

Starting from the conclusions of the first four papers of this Group Form on single events and from the initial stipulation on field construction, to unfold the necessary cross-event duties of field construction and of controllable field construction; to prove that every instance that holds requires them; to prove that the weaker conditions tested cannot replace them; and on this basis to reread the seven items of the original Engineering Chapter 1, the limit formula, and training, marking the strength of what this Form proves.

We begin with the result.

What follows is the abbreviated form of the results of this paper. The proofs are in the Argument Flow, the Proof of Minimal Structure, and “Master Formula and Master Theorem”; the formal definitions are in the Appendix.

Here Ev(𝒫) says that every event of 𝒫 has the necessary structure of its class; FormBear(𝒫, τ) (D1) says that 𝒫 contains an event whose induced change takes part, within W and through an actual route, in determining the formation of τ; S(H; W) says that within W there is organization on H bearing a tendency; MechC is read per *The General Form of Tendency Raising*: it permits legitimate witnesses of the three duties, historical action may be borne by an out-of-boundary process, and native write-back by the field itself cannot be read out of it; GoalForm (D2) says that the goal-determined aspects of the process take part, within W and through an actual route, in determining the formation of τ. This is the necessary direction; the converse is unproved.

Within the declared working domain, a field construction necessarily has the necessary structure of each event, the actual participation of the construction process in the formation of each target structure, organization bearing a tendency, and a mechanism that at the rule level permits witnesses of the three duties; a controllable field construction further necessarily has the participation of the goal-varying aspects of the process in the formation of the target structures. Whether these suffice to establish field construction or controllable field construction has not yet been proved.

Abstract

The four papers on Field Induction, host adjustment, tuning, and tendency raising give the necessary structure of single events. Field construction composes several such events into a process so that target structures form on the field. This paper asks: beyond the structure of single events, what else does field construction necessarily carry, and what more does controllable field construction carry?

Every event in a process can qualify and the target structure can form, while the formation has nothing to do with these events: the field would have grown it anyway, and the process changed only an unrelated branch. Hence formation bearing D1 is required. With D1, field construction can still be a matter of chance: the process performs only one pseudorandom tendency raising unrelated to the goal, and the target structure happens to grow. Chance growth is also field construction, so field construction as such contains no relation between goal and formation; but it is not controllable field construction, since changing the goal leaves the result unchanged. Controllable field construction requires that the presence or absence of the declared structure vary with the goal; from this one can prove that the goal-varying aspects of the process must take part in the formation of the target structure (D2). The goal being held, the events being determined by the goal, and the events bearing the formation: none of the three yields D2, since the process can, according to the goal, change only a marker unrelated to the target structure.

The original Engineering Chapter 1 organized the duties of field construction by seven items: Differential Condition, Biased-Flow, Continuation Accumulation, tendency, tuning, Restructuring, and Field Induction. Reread under this paper: general tendency is necessary for field construction in general; Continuation Accumulation and Restructuring Capacity borne by native write-back by the field itself hold when the declared mechanism includes native write-back, and their general necessity is open; the target structure can be given by a single direct write before any beat of the field’s own operation, so neither Continuation Accumulation taking part in first formation, nor tuning, nor Restructuring taking part in formation is necessary; tendency raising can pre-build directly according to the goal, so Field Induction events are not necessary; the field can pre-exist, so tendency-raising events are not necessary; a goal whose two branches are symmetric and always equal on the declared comparison unit can also be produced controllably, so Biased-Flow is not necessary on that unit. They are conditionally necessary under the corresponding target criteria or sub-routes. The original sentence “Tuning itself does not alter the Potential distribution” is corrected: tuning changes patency by suppression and disinhibition, that is, it changes the tendency. Training that writes weights directly is host adjustment, one realization of Field Induction events in field construction, not a realization of Continuation Accumulation.

Proved: the necessary structure of Make and Make\_C and the irreplaceability of the listed candidates; the current status of the seven items; the current readings of the items of the limit formula and of training. Open: the converse; the general necessity of Continuation Accumulation and Restructuring Capacity without the native write-back condition; whether tendency must be maintained by sustained accumulation; the general non-necessity of the Differential Condition and of broad Biased-Flow; Continuation Accumulation under targets of the field’s own continuation, and in the whole-window reading; reachability-level Continuation Accumulation and Restructuring from the actual starting point; tuning under dynamic allocation; broader control at the level of strength, position, and timing; incomplete pre-writability in open domains; nondeterministic mechanisms; the read-out map; global minimality.

Preliminary Distinctions

What follows only fixes objects and terms; it bears no part of the necessity of the structural items.

Events and non-events. The events of 𝒫 are tendency-raising events and Field Induction events; the mode of action of a Field Induction event is read per the branches of *The General Form of Field Induction*, implemented by host adjustment or by tuning; whether there is any Field Induction act other than these two is open. Input within W and the field’s own operation and sedimentation may occur, and may take part in the formation of the target structure, but are not counted as events of 𝒫. Whether an event holds as tendency raising or as Field Induction is decided by the corresponding General Form and initial stipulations; it does not acquire that qualification by appearing in a field construction. The witnesses of this paper use only host-adjustment direct writes and tendency-raising constructions, and do not invoke the event qualification of tuning.

Tendency and general tendency. Tendency is the inclination possessed by the organizational state of the field. General tendency S(H; W): within W there is organization on H bearing a tendency. Historically formed tendency S\_hist: the part of the inclination within W that is formed or changed by the field’s own continuation; no unique or linear decomposition is presupposed. Definition 3 of the original Engineering Chapter 1, “the tendency structure formed by historical continuation and acting upon subsequent continuation”, is read as a description of S\_hist, not as a definition of general tendency.

Stratifying Continuation Accumulation and Restructuring. This paper reads in layers Definition 2 of the original chapter, “the property by which a single continuation can alter subsequent continuation tendency” (Continuation Accumulation A), and Definition 5, “the capacity of structure, under historical participation, to alter Potential distribution, Competition Domains, and continuation direction” (Restructuring R):  
Native write-back condition C\_native: the declared mechanism includes write-back executed by the field’s own basic rules—when a signal passes, the field’s own rules leave a retained change at a Structural Position, and the organization already left takes part in determining the write-back by the field’s own rules, which acts on the organization of the same Trace-Response set. It is stronger than MechC of *The General Form of Tendency Raising*: MechC requires only that the three duties have legitimate witnesses; historical action may be borne by out-of-boundary processes or by retention, and need not be executed by the field’s own rules.  
Mechanism-level A\_cap: the declared mechanism permits the field’s own continuation to leave retained changes at Structural Positions, and permits changes already left to take part, through the field’s own rules, in determining subsequent processes that act on the organization of the same Trace-Response set.  
Mechanism-level R\_phys: after tendency raising is complete, within the declared valid domain of change, the declared mechanism permits the field’s own sedimentation to change the physical organization; the acquisition by tendency raising itself does not count. The capacity reading of R\_hist is the same as that of A\_cap. Under C\_native, the capacity readings of A\_cap, R\_phys, and R\_hist hold (definitionally).  
Reachability level: starting from the actually acquired starting organization, in legitimate operation after acquisition, the above changes necessarily occur or can necessarily occur. That a rule can write back in some states does not mean it will write back from every actual starting point.  
First-formation reading A\_i\_form (arguments: the same τ and its first-formation time t\_τ): A1\_form, the field’s own continuation takes part in the first formation of τ; A2\_form, changes left within W by the field’s own continuation before t\_τ take part in that formation; A3\_form, sedimentation before t\_τ actually occurs and bears that formation; A4\_form, that change acts, before t\_τ, on the tendency reading on which τ depends.  
Whole-window reading: A2\_win, changes left by the field’s own continuation in an earlier part of W take part in a later part; A4\_win, that change acts on the tendency reading.  
Read-out level: under the declared tendency reading, the tendency distribution can be changed by the field’s own continuation; the read-out map must be fixed.

The current reading of tuning. Definition 4 of the original chapter (“Tuning … to determine: which Biased Flows remain active at present”), the Seventh Round’s “Tuning itself does not alter the Potential distribution”, and the tuning operator of Appendix 1 are read under *The General Form of Tuning*: tuning opens and closes existing pathways and adjusts their degree of permeability by suppression and disinhibition, constituting a dynamic logical structure on top of the existing physical connections; tendency includes the patency of pathways and their degree of permeability, so tuning changes the tendency distribution. The sentence “Tuning itself does not alter the Potential distribution” is withdrawn.

Segmenting Field Induction and tendency raising. In the Note to Definition 6 of the original chapter, Field Induction includes initial layout such as “designing input structures, boundary conditions, and initial fluctuation distributions”. Under this Group Form, acquiring the initial organization is tendency raising, and Field Induction on existing organization during operation branches by implementation level into host adjustment and tuning (whether there is anything beyond these two is open); input structure is input, and boundary conditions are assigned, according to where they actually fall, to the host declaration or to the corresponding event. The G of the original chapter is read in this paper as “the Field Induction events in 𝒫”; tendency-raising events are listed separately.

Argument Flow

1

What is currently known is the necessary structure of single events from the first four papers, the initial stipulation on field construction, the instance declaration and the working domain. On these alone, is it already clear which structures a field construction minimally carries?

It is not. The structure of single events says that each event qualifies on its own; it does not say what bears the formation of the target structure.

Consider an example. Host Z; the field has organization before W: every weight is 1 except w(x→h1) = 1/2; this organization is not acquired by 𝒫. X holds G\_h2. 𝒫 contains only one host adjustment b′, which, according to another goal specification g′ (lowering the z-side reading), directly writes w(h3→z) := 1/5, taking P3 from 1 to 1/5; relative to its own instance, b′ satisfies the necessary structure of host adjustment. At the entry of W, P1 = 1/2 and P2 = 1, and τ\_h2 does not hold; the field runs on its own, and from beat 4 on τ\_h2 holds. Without b′, τ\_h2 likewise holds at beat 4. The ground is not this comparison alone: neither the fluxes nor the write-backs of the two y-side branches read in the z-side connections, and w(h3→z), which b′ changed, takes part in no y-side reading or write-back throughout W. Every event qualifies and the target structure forms, yet no event in 𝒫 takes part in the formation; this is not field construction.

Lemma 1 (Formation bearing)  In any Make(κ), for every τ ∈ 𝒯 there is an event e in 𝒫 whose induced change takes part, within W and through an actual route, in determining the formation of τ. We write this FormBear(𝒫, τ), or D1 for short.  
Proof: The third clause of Make says that the formation of every τ is actually borne by 𝒫. Under the bearing reading of *The General Form of Field Induction*, to bear is to take part in determining through an actual route; 𝒫 consists of events, so bearing must fall on the change induced by some event in it. If for some τ no event’s change takes part in determining its formation, then the formation of that τ is not borne by 𝒫, contradicting Make(κ). ∎  
D1 requires only participation, not sole determination; “it still forms after the event is deleted” does not by itself exclude participation, and the exclusion in the example above rests on path separation.

2

We have the necessary structure of each event and D1. What else does field construction necessarily carry?

By D1, 𝒯 being nonempty implies that 𝒫 is nonempty. Every event in 𝒫 carries a piece of organization bearing a tendency: a tendency-raising event has Pot, and a Field Induction event has Org₀ and ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0; hence within W there is organization on H bearing a tendency.

Lemma 2 (General tendency and mechanism condition)  In any Make(κ), 𝒫 ≠ ∅, S(H; W) holds, and MechC(F, M) holds.  
Proof: 𝒯 is nonempty, and by Lemma 1 each τ has an event bearing its formation, so 𝒫 ≠ ∅. Take any e ∈ 𝒫. If e is a tendency-raising event, then by the master theorem of *The General Form of Tendency Raising* the organization acquired by e satisfies Pot and bears the tendency of F, and MechC(F, M) holds; if e is a Field Induction event, then by the master theorem of *The General Form of Field Induction* F already has organization at the start of W and there is a net tendency difference within W, so within W there is organization on H bearing a tendency; moreover, under the working domain of *The General Form of Field Induction*, F acquires Field-Concretization Qualification on some actual window, and by *The General Form of Field Concretization* and *The Mathematical Form of Field Concretization* the qualification must have a mechanism witness, so the declared mechanism permits legitimate witnesses of the three duties, that is, MechC(F, M). ∎  
MechC does not yield A\_cap or R\_phys borne by native write-back by the field itself: its item (1) is a possible mechanism by which signals leave traces and need not be executed by the field’s own rules; its item (3) is a process in which the organization already left takes part in determining action on the same Trace-Response set, and it can be borne by an out-of-boundary process. For example, the field contains a process that reads the organization already left, while an out-of-boundary device reads the field’s Trace-Responses, retains them, and later changes the field’s organization according to that record: all three duties can have witnesses, yet the field itself has no native write-back rule. A\_cap and R\_phys hold under C\_native; without this condition their general necessity is open.  
Even under C\_native, only the mechanism level is signed. The reachability level is open: in an example off host Z, w ← min(1, (11/10)·w), starting from w = 1, w no longer changes; the rule can write back, yet from this starting point it writes back no more.

Ask again: does field construction also require the goal to take part in formation? Consider an example. Host Z; at the entry of W no organization is installed on H; X holds G\_h2; 𝒫 contains only one tendency raising: an initialization process writes all connections from a fixed sequence of pseudorandom numbers, w = (3/5, 4/5, 1, 6/5, 7/10, 9/10) (in the order x→h1, h1→y, x→h2, h2→y, x→h3, h3→z), and the procedure does not read the goal. P1 = 12/25 and P2 = 6/5, and τ\_h2 does not hold; from beat 3 on τ\_h2 holds. This tendency raising bears the formation of τ\_h2 (D1), X holds the goal, and every clause of Make holds: this is a field construction by chance growth. For contrast, if the tendency raising writes all 1s, P1 and P2 remain equal throughout and τ\_h2 does not form in the whole window. The definition of field construction states only “the goal is held” and “formation is borne by 𝒫”, not any relation between goal and formation; this example satisfies every clause, so no goal–formation relation can be forced out any further for Make. Forcing stops for Make.

3

We have the necessary structure of Make. Has controllable field construction been unfolded?

No. In the previous example the procedure does not read the goal: constructing by G\_h1 yields the same as constructing by G\_h2, τ\_h2 still forms at beat 3, GoalResp fails, and it is not a controllable field construction. The necessary structure of Make does not yield GoalResp.

Candidate: for every τ there is an event bearing the formation of τ, and G\_𝒯 takes part, through an actual route, in determining some aspect of it. Consider an example: tendency raising writes, according to the goal, only a marker value on the h3 branch—w(x→h3) := 1/3 under G\_h2, w(x→h3) := 9/10 under G\_h1—and writes the other connections with the pseudorandom numbers of the previous example. This tendency raising bears the formation of τ\_h2, and one of its aspects is determined by the goal, so the candidate holds; but the h3 branch lies on the path of no y-side reading or write-back, and after the goal is changed τ\_h2 still forms at beat 3, so GoalResp fails. The candidate does not yield GoalResp either.

Lemma 3 (Goal bearing)  In any Make\_C(κ), for every τ ∈ 𝒯, the goal-determined aspects A\_G of the process 𝒫—the aspects that vary with the goal according to the procedure: position, direction, strength, timing, or certain parts of the organization acquired by tendency raising—take part, within W and through an actual route, in determining the formation of τ. We write this GoalForm(Proc, 𝒫, τ), or D2 for short.  
Proof: By GoalResp, take for τ a G′ ∈ 𝔊 such that τ does not form when the field is constructed by Proc(G′). Under the comparison protocol of GoalResp, the two constructions start from the same entry state with the same H, M, input, environment, random source, and W, and only the procedure is replaced, Proc(G\_𝒯) by Proc(G′); by the procedure, the two differ only on A\_G. The mechanism is deterministic (working domain), so whether τ forms, as a function of the values of A\_G, is not constant: the formation of τ structurally depends on A\_G. Under the bearing reading of *The General Form of Field Induction*, “the part depended on” is taken as structural dependence, and counterfactual comparison is a method of certification; hence the values of A\_G in the actual field construction take part, through the events and routes in which they actually lie, in determining the formation of τ. ∎  
The proof uses only the direction “different results under variation of the goal ⇒ dependence”, not “unchanged ⇒ non-participation”. D2 can be borne jointly by several aspects and does not require a single event to bear it alone. D2 implies D1: the event in which A\_G lies bears the formation. D2 is not a renaming of GoalResp: GoalResp is a comparison of the results of two field constructions, whereas D2 is the bearing relation, in the actual field construction, between the goal-determined aspects and the formation.

Positive example. Host Z; at the entry 0⁻ nothing is installed on H; a tendency raising pre-builds all 1s, followed by a host adjustment that, according to the goal, directly writes the non-target branch, the two completing in turn at 0⁺: under G\_h2, w(x→h1) := 3/10; under G\_h1, w(x→h2) := 3/10. In the G\_h2 branch τ\_h2 holds from beat 4 on; in the G\_h1 branch τ\_h2 does not hold in the whole window; GoalResp holds, and this is a controllable field construction. A\_G is the directly written branch; it bears the formation of τ\_h2, and D2 holds.

4

We have the necessary structure of Make and D2 of Make\_C. Is the account closed?

The original chapter’s seven-item “conditional list of roles” is accordingly restated as the organization of duties under the route of growth by continuation—controllable field construction whose target criterion includes directionality and the requirement “formed by the field’s own continuation”, and which lays out generative conditions by Field Induction events. Master Formula and Master Theorem

Master formula (necessary direction):

　　Make(κ) ⇒ Ev(𝒫) ∧ ∀τ ∈ 𝒯 · FormBear(𝒫, τ) ∧ 𝒫 ≠ ∅ ∧ S(H; W) ∧ MechC(F, M)  
　　Make\_C(κ) ⇒ Ev(𝒫) ∧ ∀τ ∈ 𝒯 · FormBear(𝒫, τ) ∧ 𝒫 ≠ ∅ ∧ S(H; W) ∧ MechC(F, M) ∧ ∀τ ∈ 𝒯 · GoalForm(Proc, 𝒫, τ)

Read-back. κ is the declared instance, and the field F on H is the common induced field or tendency-raised field of all events. Ev(𝒫): every tendency-raising event in 𝒫 has the four duties of *The General Form of Tendency Raising*, and every Field Induction event has the six duty groups of *The General Form of Field Induction*, those that already hold as host adjustment or as tuning additionally having the duties of the corresponding General Form. FormBear(𝒫, τ): 𝒫 contains an event whose induced change takes part, within W and through an actual route, in determining the formation of τ (Crit\_τ does not hold at the entry 0⁻ and holds from some later phase on). S(H; W): within W there is organization on H bearing a tendency. MechC(F, M): read per *The General Form of Tendency Raising*, the complete declared mechanism of F permits, at the rule level, legitimate witnesses of the three duties of Trace-Response, organizational participation, and historical dynamics; historical action may be borne by out-of-boundary processes or by retention. Neither A\_cap and R\_phys borne by native write-back by the field itself nor the reachability level can be read out of it. GoalForm(Proc, 𝒫, τ): the aspects A\_G that vary with the goal according to the procedure take part, within W and through an actual route, in determining the formation of τ; it is relative to the declared goal family and criterion. All items share the arguments of the same instance. D2 implies D1.

Master Theorem  Within the working domain, for any Make(κ), all items on the right-hand side of the first formula hold simultaneously; for any Make\_C(κ), all items on the right-hand side of the second formula hold simultaneously.

Proof: Ev(𝒫) is obtained for the events of 𝒫 one by one from the master theorems of the first four papers; D1 from Lemma 1; 𝒫 ≠ ∅, S(H; W) and MechC from Lemma 2; for Make\_C, D2 additionally from Lemma 3. All items take the same arguments of the same instance, so the conjunction holds. ∎

Strength. The master theorem states the necessary direction. Whether the right-hand sides imply Make or Make\_C is unproved; they are not criteria for establishing field construction or controllable field construction. In particular, D2 does not suffice to affirm Make\_C: the goal-determined aspects take part in the formation, yet under an alternative goal the structure may still form (redundant route).

Corollary (Two uses in adjudication)  Necessary-condition check: whatever fails D2 is not a controllable field construction; checking only that the goal is held or that events are determined by the goal is not enough to pass this check; “it still forms after deletion” does not by itself establish non-participation. Affirmative adjudication: one must check Make and GoalResp (comparing alternative goals under the declared goal family and criterion), or have some other proved sufficient certification; having checked D2 is not enough to affirm a controllable field construction.  
Proof: The first sentence is the contrapositive of the second formula of the master theorem; the second follows from the paragraph on strength. ∎

Breach Modes

With the object domain, working domain, and declarations stated by this Form all kept, exhibit a field construction for which some item on the right-hand side of the first formula fails, or a controllable field construction for which some item on the right-hand side of the second formula fails; or prove that some item can be deleted, or weakened to a tested candidate, while the corresponding object still holds; or show that the proof of one of the three lemmas fails. Exhibiting only another physical realization of the same duty is not a breach; a counterexample whose mechanism is nondeterministic, whose goal family or criterion was not declared in advance or was altered afterward, or whose host lacks Field-Concretization Qualification is a boundary case, not a breach.
