# The Mathematical Form of Error-Driven Host Adjustment

## Section 1  Purpose

### 1.1  Structural Sources

*The General Form of Host Adjustment* v1.0 gives the necessary structure of a host adjustment:

　　Retune(κ) ⇒ the six duty groups (F := B′) ∧ Rewrite(a, B′) ∧ Bear\_R(a, Φ\_𝓘; W)

Here the six duty groups come from *The General Form of Field Induction* v1.0: the starting organization Org₀; the goal specification GoalRep and what it points to, Target; the net tendency difference within W; the act acting on the field, Eff; bearing, Bear; and the goal condition, GoalCond. *The General Form of Field Induction* also stipulates that goal specification, reading target and content qualification are kept apart; that bearing requires only participation, one bearing moment within W sufficing; and that “the part depended on” is taken as structural dependence (Appendix Definition 1: with one and the same rest r fixed, there exist legitimate c₁, c₂ that give different readings). Argument Flow 7 of *The General Form of Field Construction by Self-Manifestation-Field Induction* v1.0 stipulates that an optimization act that writes weights directly is host adjustment, one realization of Field Induction events in field construction, not a realization of Continuation Accumulation. Section 10 of the Computation Group Form stipulates that the host level and the dynamics level are separate, and that training must have its update, retention and subsequent reading-in verified separately.

These conclusions are stated in natural language. On a concrete training loop—forward computation, loss, backpropagation, optimizer, parameter write-back—where each of them falls, and when it holds, calls for a formal system that can be computed item by item.

### 1.2  The Object of Formalization

We denote by S\_EDR the structure that the papers above establish for error-driven host adjustment. This Form establishes a formal system for it:

　　S\_EDR ⟼ M\_EDR

The Form kind is Mathematical Form: a principal form, first edition. Entry conditions: the load-bearing structure of S\_EDR has been established by *The General Form of Host Adjustment*, *The General Form of Field Induction* and *The General Form of Field Construction by Self-Manifestation-Field Induction*; the Field-Concretization Qualification, mechanism and host attribution of the regulated field B′ are inherited from these Forms and are not conferred by any formal value of M\_EDR. The engineering quantities of error driving (loss, gradient, optimizer state) enter the definition sequence as model specifications of the implementation class or as purely formal auxiliary quantities; each is read back to an established structural item, and none serves as a new structural item.

### 1.3  Declared Scope

The implementation class 𝒞\_EDR of the first edition of this Form:  
\- the regulated field is a feedforward network on a finite computation graph, with node operations taken from {in, mul, sq, add}, and the forward pass is a polynomial in the parameters and the input;  
\- the loss is the squared loss;  
\- differentiation is backpropagation expanded by the chain rule;  
\- the optimizer is the momentum method (momentum coefficient β ∈ \[0, 1), β = 0 being plain gradient descent; the learning rate η is a nonnegative rational, and η = 0 lies in the domain);  
\- the data form a finite dataset, sampling follows a declared deterministic sampling schedule, and the mini-batch size is 1;  
\- comparisons are made under a fixed sampling schedule.  
𝒞\_EDR does not contain other losses, adaptive optimizers, batch averaging, distributional comparison under random sampling, non-polynomial activations, or self-supervised and reinforcement learning; formal systems on these implementation classes have not been established.

## Section 2  Core Theses

Thesis 1 (Separate reporting of a training step)  In a training step, the input entering the regulated field and the forward computation the field performs by its own rules are the running of input and field, not the direct-write part of the act in this case; the computations of the label, the loss, backpropagation and the optimizer lie outside the regulated field and do not fall on it; only parameter write-back is a direct transformation of the physical layer of the regulated field. Classification goes by the layer on which, and the route by which, the act is actually implemented on the regulated field, not by who the actor is or whether it is inside or outside the field. The separate reporting of mechanisms does not exclude input from being part of an act; it stipulates only that input is not direct write.

Thesis 2 (Preserving mechanism witnesses)  The trajectory does not suffice to decide bearing: one write-back and another can give the same end state, a write-back that is overwritten leaves no trace at the end point, and a variable that is merely read in, without affecting the written value, has nothing to do with bearing. M\_EDR therefore records, for every atomic event, its execution route, its reads and writes, its actual change and its sources; bearing and the goal condition are evaluated three-valuedly on these witnesses.

Thesis 3 (Read-back has a direction)  A formal value 1 of M\_EDR reads back, under the bridging conditions listed in Section 7, as the corresponding necessary item holding; a formal value 0 reads back as failure only when reverse coverage has been proved for that item, and otherwise only the formal value is reported. The conjunction of the necessary items does not imply that a host adjustment has been established, and a fall in the loss does not imply that the content the goal points to already has qualification.

## Section 3  Basic Objects and the Definition Sequence

The definitions are given in order of dependence; each ends with a note of the structural item it corresponds to (correspondence table in Section 7).

Definition 3.1 (Declared model)  𝔐 = (V, λ, Dom, Φ, deg\_Φ, Ports, F\_M, X\_ext, fid). V is the set of state variables; λ(v) ∈ {θ, r, ext}: θ is the physical layer of the regulated field (parameters), r the ordinary running state of the regulated field (inputs, node values), and ext the variables outside the regulated field (the states of the actor and of the goal bearer). Dom(v) is the value domain: a finite value domain, or a predicate value domain together with a finite ordered substitution sequence. Φ is the reading, with its reading level L and the input class 𝓘 it represents declared; Φ reads only the θ layer; it is a mathematical read-out, injects no signal into the field, and is not an event. deg\_Φ(v) is an upper bound on the polynomial degree of Φ in v, a nonnegative integer or undeclared. Ports are the act ports: direct-write ports (writable θ variables) and input ports. F\_M is the component table of the regulated field's own basic rules (forward components). X\_ext is the table of out-of-boundary process components, each of which writes only the ext layer; it is not part of F\_M. fid is the registered identifier of the regulated field, fixed when the model is built. \[Corresponds to: the regulated field B′ and its working-domain declaration\]

Definition 3.2 (Computation graph)  𝒢 = (P, N, out). P is the set of parameters (the leaves of the θ layer); N is the set of nodes in topological order, each node n having an operation op(n) ∈ {in, mul, sq, add} and an argument sequence args(n): in reads the input x; mul(a, b) = a·b; sq(a) = a²; add(a, b) = a + b; the same variable may occur in both argument positions of a node. out is the output node. For each variable v, Ch(v) is the set of distinct child nodes having v among their arguments; each child node is counted once. \[Corresponds to: the organization and installation of the regulated field\]

Definition 3.3 (Events and contract)  An event is ε = (id, kind, reads, writes, fn, deg, part, src), where kind ∈ {In, Run, Rw}: In is input entry, Run is running (a forward component of the field itself, or an out-of-boundary process component), and Rw is direct write; src specifies the execution route—Run must reference a component in F\_M or X\_ext, while In and Rw must pass through the corresponding port. id is taken from the event-identifier domain 𝔈 (Definition 3.7) and is unique within a run; writes may contain one or more variables, and an event whose writes contain only one is called a single-output event; deg is the polynomial degree of fn in each read-in variable and may be omitted. fn is evaluated on the pre-write state. Contract: the read-in variables are declared and have values; the actual output keys of fn equal writes; the layer actually written is compatible with kind (In and Run write r or ext, Rw writes θ); the written values lie in the value domain; the write set does not go beyond the referenced component or port. If any of these fails, the run is domain-invalid. \[Corresponds to: the separate reporting of act, input and the running of the field\]

Definition 3.4 (Phase)  Each atomic event occupies one subphase (k, i), where k is the training step and i the order within the step; the entry is (0, 0). Phases increase strictly in lexicographic order. The snapshot σ\_τ of subphase τ is the full set of state values after the event at τ has written; the entry snapshot is the initial values; each snapshot carries the source map anc\_τ of the same moment. \[Corresponds to: W and the moments within it\]

Definition 3.5 (Actual change)  If the value event ε writes to variable x differs from the pre-write value, ε is said to actually change x. \[Corresponds to: “the change left by the act”\]

Definition 3.6 (Three-valued dependence)  For a function f, an argument assignment args and a variable v: replace v by substitute values from its value domain while keeping the other variables at their values in args; if f changes, dep(f, args, v) = 1; if the check is complete and f does not change, dep = 0; otherwise dep = ⊥. Completeness has only two cases: v has a finite value domain that has been fully enumerated; or f has a declared degree upper bound d in v, and d mutually distinct legitimate substitute values, all different from the current value, have been checked (substitute values are first normalized as exact rationals, those that are not legitimate or that equal the current value are removed, and duplicates are removed preserving order, before counting).  
This is local dependence on a fixed actual background: the rest is fixed at its actual assignment at that moment, rather than ranging over all legitimate rests. dep = 1 exhibits a difference on one actual background, and the actual background is one of the legitimate backgrounds, so it is a witness of structural dependence in the sense of Appendix Definition 1 of *The General Form of Field Induction*; dep = 0 says only that there is no difference on this background, does not exclude a difference on another legitimate background, and is not a proof that structural dependence is absent. \[Corresponds to: “the part depended on” taken as structural dependence—one-directional; see 7.1\]

Definition 3.7 (Sources)  The source domain 𝔖 = 𝔈 ⊎ 𝔄 is the disjoint union of the event-identifier domain 𝔈 and the holding-anchor identifier domain 𝔄; sources are matched by type and identity of identifier, and sources of different types are unequal in every case. When identifiers are implemented as strings, holding-anchor identifiers carry a reserved prefix and event identifiers may not carry it; otherwise the run is domain-invalid. Each variable x carries a source map anc(x): a finite subset of 𝔖 → {determined, undetermined}.  
(a) When event ε actually changes x, anc(x) = {ε: determined}, merged with the following: for each read-in variable u with dep = 1, anc(u); for those with dep = ⊥, the entries of anc(u), each merged in as “undetermined”; those with dep = 0 are not merged. Determined takes precedence over undetermined. Dependence and sources are both taken from the pre-write snapshot.  
(b) When a direct-write event writes to x a value equal to the pre-write value, no source is generated and anc(x) is unchanged.  
(c) When an In or Run event writes to x a value equal to the pre-write value, but one determined anew by this event's reads: the event is not recorded as a source, and anc(x) is reassigned to the result of merging from the read-in variables as in (a) (excluding the event itself); writes to the goal variable g (by whatever route) are the exception and follow (b).  
For a multi-output event, the source of each output variable is computed separately by (a)–(c); what (a) and (c) merge in are the sources the read-in variables had before this event (the pre-write snapshot), not the sources just generated by sibling outputs of this event; once the sources of all outputs have been computed, they are committed together. The source of one output is not merged into another output.  
\[Corresponds to: the act taking part through an actual route; the bearing chain\]

Definition 3.8 (Goal declaration, target-pointing registry and goal sources)  
(a) The target-pointing registry 𝒯 is a table: TargetDecl(g) = (regulated field φ′, relation template). The relation template of the first example of this Form is the input–output relation y = g·x of B′ on the input class 𝓘.  
(b) Goal declaration GoalDecl = (role Xg, goal variable g, regulated field φ).  
(c) Run entry: a training run with GoalDecl requires that 𝒯 contain TargetDecl(g) and that φ = φ′ = fid; if the registration is missing or any of these differ, the run is domain-invalid.  
(d) Goal-source registration is of two kinds. The holding anchor at entry: Xg already holds g at entry, and the anchor's identifier belongs to 𝔄; its role and variable must agree with GoalDecl, and g must have been declared at the ext layer and be available at entry, otherwise the run is domain-invalid; a holding anchor is not an event and not a physical change. The in-window goal-write event: its src is the goal-setting component, its part is Xg, its write set is exactly {g}, and it actually changes g; its identifier belongs to 𝔈. A legitimate run may have no goal-source registration at all.  
\[Corresponds to: GoalRep(Xg, g), Target(g, B′)\]

Definition 3.9 (Training quantities)  g: goal specification; y*: supervisory material (label); ℓ: current error reading; ε: reading target, declared as the threshold ℓ ≤ ε; δ\_v: backward error (adjoint); q\_p: gradient; v\_p: momentum (optimizer state, ext layer); stop: monitor flag. The five—goal specification, supervisory material, current error reading, reading target and object-level content qualification—are not interchangeable. \[Corresponds to: the distinction drawn in *The General Form of Field Induction\* among goal specification, reading target and content qualification\]

Definition 3.10 (Formal predicates)  Fix an act a (marked by part), a time window W and a subset of event kinds K ⊆ {In, Run, Rw}. The events of a are the events whose part is a and whose phase lies within W; a bearing moment τ ranges over the snapshot phases within W; events before W appear only through their sources in the source map. In the examples of Section 7, W is the whole run.  
\- Rewrite^f(a): a contains an Rw event that actually changes a θ variable.  
\- BearAt\_K(a, τ): three-valued—1 if there is a v with dep(Φ, σ\_τ, v) = 1 such that anc\_τ(v) contains an event of a with kind ∈ K and an actual change, marked determined; 0 if, for every v at τ, either dep = 0 or anc\_τ(v) contains no such event; ⊥ otherwise.  
\- Bear\_K(a; W): strong Kleene disjunction over τ ∈ W: 1 if some τ gives 1; 0 if every τ gives 0; ⊥ otherwise. Bear^f = Bear\_{In, Run, Rw}, Bear\_R^f = Bear\_{Rw}.  
\- GoalCond^f(a) (within a legitimate run): ⊥ if no goal source is registered; 1 if, after the write of some Rw event of a that actually changes θ, the source of the written variable contains a registered goal source (matched by type and identifier) marked determined; ⊥ if it contains such sources only as undetermined; 0 otherwise.  
\- Eff^f(a): some event of a actually changes the θ layer of the regulated field.  
\- Org₀^f: at entry the θ-layer variables are installed and nonempty, and the computation graph is declared.  
\- ΔΦ^f\_W: Φ(σ\_{W end}) − Φ(σ\_{W entry}), a quantity; what enters the three-valued table is the predicate ΔΦ^f\_W ≠ 0.  
\- Target^f(g) (a registry query, not a run): ⊥ if 𝒯 contains no TargetDecl(g); 1 if it does and its regulated field is fid; 0 if it does and its regulated field differs from fid.  
\[Corresponds to: Section 7\]

Relations and their properties (domain and direction declared item by item):  
\- ≺\_𝕋 (between subphases): a strict total order—irreflexive, transitive, connected.  
\- Versions: each actual change of a parameter produces a new version, the entry value being the initial version. Pred(ν, ν′): ν is the immediate predecessor version of ν′ (same parameter, ν earlier). As the correspondence ν′ ↦ ν it is a function defined on non-initial versions (each non-initial version has exactly one predecessor, and the initial version has none); it is irreflexive and in general not transitive (when a parameter has three or more versions, Pred(ν₁, ν₂) ∧ Pred(ν₂, ν₃) holds but Pred(ν₁, ν₃) does not).  
\- ≺\_ver (the transitive closure of Pred, ν earlier): a strict partial order—irreflexive, transitive, antisymmetric; a total order on the versions of one parameter, with versions of different parameters incomparable.  
\- Src(e, e′) (e′ is an event with an actual change, and e belongs to the post-write source of some variable that e′ actually changed): reflexive on events with actual changes (e′ lies in its own write source).  
\- AncEv⁺(e, e′) :⇔ Src(e, e′) ∧ e ≠ e′ (between events): irreflexive; time-precedent (the phase of e is earlier than that of e′, since sources come only from events already executed); antisymmetric (by precedence). Transitivity is guaranteed only when the intermediate event is single-output; see Proposition 5.4. For general multi-output events the event projection does not guarantee transitivity (7.3 gives an example). Determinacy: under the conditions of Proposition 5.4, determined composed with determined is determined; a composition involving undetermined is undetermined.  
\- A holding anchor is not an event and does not enter AncEv⁺; the relation between anchors and variables is recorded separately as HoldAnc (a heterogeneous relation; reflexivity and symmetry are not discussed).  
\- Same part (between events): an equivalence relation. Rewrite^f, Bear\_R^f and GoalCond^f are all bound to part.

## Section 4  Equations

For 𝒢 and step k, let x\_k be the input of this step.

(Sampling)  idx\_k = π(k, s), where π is the declared deterministic sampling schedule and s the seed; x\_k = D\[idx\_k\], where D is the finite dataset.  
(Forward)  In topological order, val(n) = op(n)(val(args(n))), in nodes taking x\_k; y\_k = val(out).  
(Label)  y\*\_k = g·x\_k.  
(Observation)  ℓ\_k = (y\_k − y\*\_k)²/2.  
(Adjoint)  δ\_out = y\_k − y\*\_k; for the other nodes and the parameters, in reverse topological order,

　　δ\_v = Σ\_{c ∈ Ch(v)} δ\_c · ∂c/∂v,

where ∂mul(a, b)/∂a = b, ∂sq(a)/∂a = 2a, ∂add(a, b)/∂a = 1, and when the same variable occurs in both argument positions of a node, the derivative is summed over all its occurrences (∂mul(w, w)/∂w = 2w, ∂add(w, w)/∂w = 2). If Ch(v) is empty, δ\_v = 0.  
(Gradient)  q\_p = δ\_p, p ∈ P.  
(Momentum)  v\_p ← β·v\_p + q\_p.  
(Write-back)  p ← p − η·v\_p.  
(Monitoring)  stop = 1 if ℓ\_k ≤ ε, and 0 otherwise.  
(Reading)  Φ(θ) = the output value of 𝒢 at the read-out probe x = 1. The read-out probe represents the input class 𝓘: Φ is an independent mathematical read-out at x = 1; it does not pass through training sampling and produces no run events; the probe value may coincide with a training sample. deg\_Φ(p) is computed recursively over the graph: 0 for in; 1 for the parameter p and 0 for other parameters; mul adds the degrees of its two arguments, sq doubles the degree, add takes the maximum. Degrees of forward events in their read-in variables: for mul(a, b), 2 when a = b and otherwise 1 each; 2 for sq; 1 for add (also 1 when the argument is repeated).

Bridging condition (declared): the chosen loss ℓ(y, y*) = (y − y*)²/2 determines the starting point of the adjoint, δ\_out = y − y\*; the gradients are computed from forward values and the adjoint rules and do not read the scalar ℓ; the scalar ℓ enters only through monitoring. This is only the declaration for this implementation class; if an implementation schedules the learning rate or stops training by ℓ, the corresponding read events must be registered.  
Momentum and zero gradient: the parameter change is −η·(β·v\_old + q). When q = 0 the parameter change is −η·β·v\_old: if η ≠ 0, the parameter is unchanged if and only if β·v\_old = 0; if η = 0, the parameter is unchanged.

## Section 5  Running and Solving

### 5.1  Run Order

The subphases of step k are, in order: sampling (out-of-boundary), input (In), forward (F\_M, one event per node in topological order), label, observation, adjoint (out-of-boundary, one event per variable in reverse topological order), momentum (out-of-boundary, one event per parameter), write-back (Rw, one event per parameter), monitoring (out-of-boundary). If the goal is set within the window, its write event lies at (0, 1). Every event generated in this order is a single-output event.

5.2  The Finite Executable Domain and the Trusted Premises

Finite executable domain: finitely many variables, nodes, parameters, data and steps; values are rational; the fn of every event is a rational polynomial (monitoring has finitely many branches). Trusted premises: every function is total on its value domain; the declared degree upper bounds are true; the declarations of kind and src agree with the actual execution route; every actual landing point of the act a on the regulated field within W has been registered as an event (declaration completeness). These four are not certified by the solver; when they fail, the conclusions of the evaluation fail with them.

### 5.3  Three Kinds of Result

(1) Domain-invalid: the model, a declaration or an event violates the contracts of Definitions 3.1, 3.3, 3.7 and 3.8—including an event identifier of the same type as a holding-anchor identifier, a run with GoalDecl that lacks TargetDecl or whose three regulated-field entries disagree, and a holding anchor not bound to GoalDecl.  
(2) Undetermined ⊥ (within a legitimate run): a dependence check is incomplete; or no goal source is registered in the run, in which case GoalCond^f = ⊥.  
(3) Certified 1 or 0.  
The registry query Target^f(g) is not a run and has its own three values: ⊥ if unregistered, 1 if the regulated field agrees, 0 if it disagrees.

Theorem 5.1 (Finite termination)  Within the finite executable domain, for any declared model and any finite number of steps, the run, the source updates and the evaluation of the formal predicates of Section 3 terminate in finitely many steps, and for each predicate return one of {1, 0, ⊥} or domain-invalid.  
Proof: There are finitely many events; the execution of each event, its contract check and the dependence check for each read-in variable involve only finitely many substitute values; merging source maps is a finite set operation; the predicates enumerate over finitely many snapshots and finitely many variables. ∎  
The theorem holds equally for multi-output events; it certifies neither the truth of the registrations nor the Field-Concretization Qualification of the regulated field.

Theorem 5.2 (Adjoint correctness)  For any node or parameter v of 𝒢, the adjoint δ\_v of Section 4 equals the partial derivative of ℓ with respect to v (at the forward values of this step, the other nodes being regarded as functions of v).  
Proof: By induction in reverse topological order. δ\_out = ∂ℓ/∂y follows directly from the squared loss. Suppose δ\_c = ∂ℓ/∂c holds for every child node c of v. ℓ is affected by v only through the nodes in Ch(v), so by the multivariate chain rule ∂ℓ/∂v = Σ\_{c ∈ Ch(v)} (∂ℓ/∂c)(∂c/∂v); each child node appears in this sum once, and ∂c/∂v is the full partial derivative of c with respect to v, already summed over all positions at which v occurs in c. When Ch(v) is empty, ℓ does not pass through v and the partial derivative is 0, in agreement with δ\_v = 0. Hence δ\_v = ∂ℓ/∂v. ∎  
The theorem does not guarantee that the loss decreases monotonically.

Theorem 5.3 (Reassignment changes only sources)  Definition 3.7(c) changes no state value and no reading trajectory; it differs from Definition 3.7(b) only in the sources at same-value writes of In and Run.  
Proof: (c) acts only when the written value equals the pre-write value, and it changes only anc(x), not the value of x; event functions do not read sources, and Φ reads only the θ layer and not sources; by induction over phases, the state values and Φ of every snapshot are unchanged. ∎  
Hence Bear^f and GoalCond^f, whose definitions depend on sources, may, where (c) takes effect, take values different from those under a rule without reassignment; read-back in this Form always follows Definition 3.7. Actual change and Rewrite^f are unaffected by (c). Why reassignment is necessary (for the witness semantics of this Form): when the goal value is 0, the label is always 0, the same as its initial value; without reassignment, the goal's action on subsequent computation would be cut off here.

Proposition 5.4 (Event transitivity under the single-output condition)  In a legitimate run, if e′ is a single-output event, then AncEv⁺(e, e′) ∧ AncEv⁺(e′, e″) ⇒ AncEv⁺(e, e″); if the first two are both determined, so is the third. In the run of §5.1 every event is single-output, so AncEv⁺ is a strict partial order on it.  
Proof: Let x′ be the unique output of e′, A′ = anc(x′) its source after the write, and e ∈ A′. By Definition 3.7, e′ is generated as a source only in A′; every later source set is either kept by (b) or obtained by (a) or (c) by merging in, as whole sets, the source sets of some read-in variables (with dep = 1 each entry keeps its status, with dep = ⊥ each entry becomes undetermined). By induction over phases we obtain two invariants. First, every source set that contains e′ contains all elements of A′. Second, in every source set in which e′ is determined, the elements determined in A′ are also determined—for e′ being determined must come from some source set, merged in with dep = 1, in which e′ was already determined; by the induction hypothesis the determined elements of A′ are still determined in that set, and in merging, determined takes precedence. From AncEv⁺(e′, e″), the source of some output of e″ contains e′, so by the first invariant it also contains e, and e ≠ e″ (e precedes e′, and e′ precedes e″); when both are determined, by the second invariant e is determined in it. Single output serves to make A′ unique: e′ is generated in only one source set. ∎  
If e′ has several outputs, e′ is generated separately in the source set of each output, and the other elements of these sets may differ; when e lies in the source of one output and e″ reads only another output, e need not be in the source of e″ (7.3 gives an example).

## Section 6  Coupling

6.1  With *The General Form of Host Adjustment*  The formal predicates of M\_EDR are formal images, on 𝒞\_EDR, of the structural items of *The General Form of Host Adjustment*; for the correspondence, direction and strength see Section 7.

6.2  With the Computation Group Form  Correspondences used in this Form: forward events correspond to what the Computation Group Form calls actual events—a node is written under a driving pathway, and one forward pass is one run; parameter write-back is a transformation of the host level, separate from the dynamics level. Correspondences not established in this Form: the mappings of reading legitimacy, of delivery, and of the full-family quantifier of bearing.

6.3  With *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction* v1.0  The declared model, event contract, phase, actual change, three-valued dependence, source map and bearing evaluation are a restricted form of the witness language of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*: event kinds are limited to In, Run and Rw, there is no dynamic layer and no write-back by the field itself, and the connection set of the computation graph is fixed and contains no disconnection. Their definitions and theorems are self-contained within Sections 3 to 5. The table of out-of-boundary process components X\_ext (Definition 3.1) and the source reassignment for same-value writes of In and Run (Definition 3.7(c)) are, in *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*, its X\_ext of Definition 3.1 and its Definition 3.6(c) respectively, with identical provisions; GoalCond^f is the reading of its P6\_Π with Π = {Rw} × {θ}; the time window is taken as in its Definition 3.9. The computation graph (Definition 3.2) and the training quantities (Definition 3.9) are structures proper to 𝒞\_EDR.

## Section 7  Read-Back Rules

### 7.1  Correspondence Table

Bridging conditions (required for affirmative read-back, declared item by item):  
B1  Actual-event correspondence—every formal event corresponds to one actual atomic event, and kind and src agree with the actual execution route (trusted premise of 5.2);  
B2  Reading—Φ is the formal image of the reading Φ\_𝓘 at the declared reading level L on the input class 𝓘;  
B3  Same act and same time window—what part marks is exactly a, and W is the declared window;  
B4  Source chain—merges with dep = 1 correspond to the transmission of actual action, and the source chain corresponds to the route through which the declared actual action passes.

### 7.2  Read-Back Rules

Rule 1  A formal value 1 is read back affirmatively under the bridging conditions listed in the correspondence table. A formal value 0 means that the formal predicate is false within the declared domain. Only when it has been proved that the structural item holding necessarily makes the formal value other than 0 (reverse coverage) may a formal value 0 exclude that structural item; in this Form only the two items marked “excludable” in the correspondence table have this guarantee. Only if the excluded item is a necessary item of host adjustment and the working-domain premises are satisfied may the corresponding host-adjustment candidate be excluded. A 0 without a guarantee of reverse coverage is reported only as a formal value and supports no upstream negation. ⊥ means undetermined and is not read as failure. Domain invalidity is listed separately and is not a formal value.  
Rule 2  Formal value 1 on every necessary item is not read as the establishment of a host adjustment; Retune is not identified by the formal values of this Form.  
Rule 3  A fall in the loss, or reaching the reading target, is read neither as the content the goal points to having qualification nor as GoalCond holding; GoalCond is read only from the goal sources and source map of Section 3.  
Rule 4  When the written-back value equals the pre-write value, the direct write generates no new source and existing sources are kept. If act a has no direct write with an actual change, then Rewrite^f(a) = 0; this does not delete sources produced earlier or by other mechanisms. For the momentum method, whether there is an actual rewrite is judged by the write-back amount η·v\_p: with zero gradient, η and β·v\_old must also be taken into account (Section 4).

### 7.3  Read-Back Examples

In each example below the reading is taken at the read-out probe x = 1 (Section 4), separately from the training data D; unless stated otherwise, D = {0 ↦ 1, 1 ↦ 2}, π(k, 0) = k mod 2, Xg holds g = 2 at entry, β = 0, the initial momentum is 0, and ε = 1/2.

Two-layer linear network: P = {w1, w2}, h = w1·x, y = w2·h; η = 1/8; at entry w1 = 1, w2 = 1/2. Step 1: x = 2, h = 2, y = 1, y\* = 4, ℓ = 9/2, δ\_y = −3, δ\_h = w2·δ\_y = −3/2, q1 = δ\_h·x = −3, q2 = δ\_y·h = −6; write-back w1 = 11/8, w2 = 5/4; Φ goes from 1/2 to 55/32. Read-back: Rewrite^f = 1; Bear\_R^f = 1, witnessed by the subphase of the step-1 write-back of w1 and the variable w1; GoalCond^f = 1, witnessed by the same write-back event, which hits the holding anchor; the scalar ℓ is not in the source of q1. From step 2 on, stop = 1 (ℓ = 81/2048).  
Three-parameter network: data, sampling, g, β, ε and zero initial momentum as above. h = w1·x, s = h², y = w2·s + w3·x; at entry w1 = 1, w2 = w3 = 1/2; η = 1/32. Step 1: x = 2, h = 2, s = 4, y = 3, y\* = 4, ℓ = 1/2, δ\_y = −1, δ\_s = w2·δ\_y = −1/2, δ\_h = δ\_s·2h = −2, q\_{w1} = δ\_h·x = −4, q\_{w2} = δ\_y·s = −4, q\_{w3} = δ\_y·x = −2; write-back w1 = 9/8, w2 = 5/8, w3 = 9/16. Reading degrees deg\_Φ: 2 for w1, 1 for w2 and w3. Bear\_R^f = 1, GoalCond^f = 1.  
Zero update: the two-layer network, at entry w1 = 1, w2 = 2, β = 0, initial momentum 0; take step 1. On D, y = y\*, all δ are 0, and the written-back values equal the pre-write values: Rewrite^f = 0, Bear\_R^f = 0, GoalCond^f = 0. By the correspondence table, Rewrite^f = 0 can exclude Rewrite(a, B′) for this step; Bear\_R^f = 0 and GoalCond^f = 0 are reported only as formal values.  
Zero gradient with momentum: same network and entry, β = 1/2, at entry v\_{w1} = 2, v\_{w2} = 0, η = 1/8, step 1. q1 = q2 = 0, v\_{w1} = 1, and w1 is written from 1 to 7/8: Rewrite^f = 1. Zero gradient does not imply no write-back. Same entry with η = 0: v\_{w1} is still updated to 1, w1 stays at 1, and Rewrite^f = 0.  
A parameter written but never read: add u ∈ θ, with u = 1 at entry, entering neither the forward pass nor Φ (deg\_Φ(u) = 0). The main update is executed by another act b; act a only decays u: u ← (9/10)·u. Rewrite^f(a) = 1, Bear\_R^f(a) = 0, Bear\_R^f(b) = 1. This is of the same type as the “idle direct write” of Argument Flow 2 of *The General Form of Host Adjustment*. Here the 0 comes from deg\_Φ(u) = 0: Φ does not read u on any background.  
A 0 on a local background: Φ = w1·w2 + w3, at entry (w1, w2, w3) = (1, 0, 0); act a writes w1 to 2, and another act b writes w3 to 1. Throughout the window w2 = 0, ΔΦ^f\_W = 1, and Bear\_R^f(a) = 0. But on another legitimate background, w2 = 1, w3 = 0, the readings for w1 = 1 and 2 are 1 and 2 respectively: structural dependence exists. This 0 is reported only as a formal value; it implies neither that the reading has no structural dependence on a nor that there is no upstream bearing chain. This example shows only the direction of read-back; it does not certify that any object in it has Field-Concretization Qualification or host-adjustment qualification.  
Repeated positions: y = mul(w, w), add(w, w), sq(w), with w = 1, x = 2, g = 2: δ\_w is −6, −4, −6 respectively, agreeing with (y − y\*)·∂y/∂w.  
Multi-output event: e1 directly writes w1 := 2 (w1 = 1 at entry); e2, through an out-of-boundary component, writes d\_{w1} := w1 and d\_{w2} := 1 at once; e3 reads only d\_{w2} and writes v\_{w2} := 2·d\_{w2}. AncEv⁺(e1, e2) (via d\_{w1}) and AncEv⁺(e2, e3) (via d\_{w2}), yet e1 is not in the source of e3. The intermediate event e2 is multi-output, so the condition of Proposition 5.4 is not met. No such case occurs in the run of §5.1.  
Same-value output in a multi-output event: the two-layer network, at entry w1 = 1, w2 = 2, d\_{w1} = 0, y\* = 0, and Xg holds g = 2 at entry. One out-of-boundary event writes, from the pre-write state, d\_{w1} := g and y\* := d\_{w1} at once, giving d\_{w1} = 2 (an actual change) and y\* = 0 (equal to its pre-write value, reassigned by 3.7(c)). y\* reads the pre-write d\_{w1}, whose source is empty, so the source of y\* after reassignment is empty: it contains neither this event nor the goal source. A subsequent direct write w1 := y\* changes w1 from 1 to 0, and GoalCond^f = 0: the goal is transmitted only through the new value of d\_{w1}, and what y\* reads is not this new value.  
Goal sources and registration: with g = 0 at entry and g := 2 set within the window, and with g = 2 held at entry, GoalCond^f is 1 in both cases and the parameter trajectories are identical. A run in which all three regulated-field entries agree but no goal source is registered has GoalCond^f = ⊥. A run with GoalDecl that lacks TargetDecl, or whose regulated field disagrees with GoalDecl or fid, is domain-invalid; a holding anchor attached to a parameter makes the run domain-invalid; naming an event with a holding-anchor identifier makes the run domain-invalid.
