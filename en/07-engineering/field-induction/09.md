# The Necessity Form of Field-Induction Action

## Section 1  Purpose

### 1.1  Question

*The Mathematical Form of Field Induction* v1.0 gives the formal system M\_Ind for acts on a declared host, with two objects of reading: the net tendency difference ΔΦ\_W and bearing Bear. In general neither implies the other: a direct write can leave a change, with the act bearing the reading as determined, while the reading at the end of the window equals that at its start; and the reading can change while having nothing to do with the act. Hence the question: under what conditions on an act within a window does a nonzero net tendency difference necessarily arise within the window, with the act's bearing of the reading in that window holding as well?

### 1.2  Propositional Form

　　C ⇒ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 ∧ Bear\_K(a; W) = 1

a is the act, W the time window, and K the set of kinds of the action events of a. The condition group C reads only events and snapshots within the window and structural properties of the reading declared in advance; neither ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0, nor Bear, nor “the tendency has changed” or any fact equivalent to it enters C. Structural properties of the reading (monotone direction, lower bound on the slope, upper bound on the remainder) are stated of the reading function itself, not of the reading results of this run.

### 1.3  Strength

ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 is P3: the difference between the readings at the end of W and at the entry of W is nonzero. Bear\_K(a; W) = 1 is P5: at some snapshot within W, the dependence of the reading on some variable is 1, and the source of that variable contains, as determined, an event of a within W whose kind belongs to K and which makes an actual change. Their conjunction is not attribution: it asserts neither that the net difference at the end of the window is caused by a, nor that the net difference changes once the events of a are removed (Corollary 5).  
The conclusion is a sufficient condition, holding on the declared host of M\_Ind under the H group of Section 6. It gives no necessary condition: when C fails, the conclusion may hold or may fail. The conclusion goes no further than the formal values of M\_Ind and their read-back under 7.1 of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*; whether a is established as a host adjustment or a tuning is checked separately under *The General Form of Host Adjustment* and *The General Form of Tuning*.

## Section 2  Core Proposition

Let an event e of a write a variable v within W: when v ∈ θ, e is a direct write (kind = Rw); when v ∈ m, e is a gating event (kind = Gt). v₀ is the value of v in the entry snapshot of W, v₁ the value written by e, and v\_end the value of v in the end snapshot of W; u denotes the variables of the reading's read set other than v, with u₀ and u\_end understood likewise. The background box B\_u is the box formed by the components of u, each ranging between its values in u₀ and u\_end; B\_u carries only the background that the proofs invoke, namely u₀, u\_end and the component-wise monotone paths used in Section 5, and makes no claim to contain the intermediate states within the window.

C1  Strict displacement (reading structure)  For u ∈ B\_u, Φ is strictly monotone in v, with declared direction sgn; and the declared substitution sequence of v contains a legitimate value different from v\_end. If deg\_Φ(v) is declared, then deg\_Φ(v) ≥ 1.  
C2  Actual displacement  e actually changes v (Definition 3.4), and v₁ ≠ v₀.  
C3  Isolation or controlled cancellation (one of three forms)  
　C3a Isolation: u\_end = u₀.  
　C3b Remainder bound: declare a lower bound m \> 0 on the slope in the direction of v and an upper bound L on the background remainder: for u ∈ B\_u and legitimate values v′, v″ of v, \|Φ(v′, u) − Φ(v″, u)\| ≥ m·\|v′ − v″\|; for u′, u″ ∈ B\_u, \|Φ(v\_end, u′) − Φ(v\_end, u″)\| ≤ L·‖u′ − u″‖₁. Moreover, m·\|v\_end − v₀\| \> L·‖u\_end − u₀‖₁.  
　C3c Same direction: over the ranges involved, Φ is monotone in the same direction in every variable of the read set (uniformly nondecreasing for sgn = +1, uniformly nonincreasing for sgn = −1, and strictly so in v), and the change of each component of u has the same sign as v\_end − v₀ or is 0.  
C4  Preservation or monotone successors (one of two forms)  
　C4a Preservation: after e, no event within W actually changes v.  
　C4b Monotone successors: every event within W after e that actually changes v is a field-rule event (Sed or Run); for each such event, at its pre-write snapshot, the output v has dependence 1 on v; after each write, \|v − v₀\| does not decrease and v − v₀ does not change sign.

C1 is a structural property declared in advance; C2–C4 read only events and snapshots; C3b and C3c additionally invoke constants and monotone directions declared in advance. None of them reads ΔΦ or Bear.

The structural evidence for C1 is of two kinds.  
(1) Affine certificate. Declare Φ affine in v, Φ(v, u) = A(u)·v + B(u), and verify that the total coefficient A(u) is nonzero and of constant sign on B\_u; sgn = sgn A. When v\_end ≠ v₀, A(u) can be obtained from the normalized endpoint difference (Φ(v\_end, u) − Φ(v₀, u))/(v\_end − v₀). The affine declaration itself falls under H1: checking at finitely many points of the substitution sequence whether the values lie on A·v + B is a consistency check, which can only detect inconsistency and cannot prove that Φ is affine, unless the valid domain of v is finite and has been exhausted, or an upper bound d on the degree has been declared in addition and collinearity has been verified at d + 1 distinct legitimate points. Example: f(m) = m + 32m(m − 1/2)(m − 1) takes the same values as m at m = 0, 1/2, 1, yet f(3/8) = 21/16 \> f(1/2) = 1/2, so it decreases between 3/8 and 1/2. A proof of affineness by a degree bound and collinear points concerns a fixed u; for it to hold uniformly over the background box, separate evidence for each u is needed. Checking A at the vertices of the box represents the whole box only when A is multi-affine in the background.  
(2) Non-affine readings. Give separate evidence of strict monotonicity. When a derivative serves as evidence, then for any two legitimate values to be compared, Φ must be continuously differentiable, with a derivative strictly of one sign, on the whole continuous interval joining them (or on an extension of Φ to that interval), uniformly for u ∈ B\_u; checking the sign of the derivative only at legitimate points is not enough: with valid domain {0, 1} and Φ(v) = v(v − 1)(2v − 1), the derivative is 1 at both points and the function value is 0 at both. On a finite or discrete valid domain, verify the strict order relation among the legitimate values directly. Nor can a two-point endpoint difference take its place: Φ = (m − 1/2)², m is written from 1/2 to 1, the substitution sequence is {0}, and the endpoint difference is 1/4, yet at the end-state substitute value 0, Φ equals the end value, both being 1/4.  
When a variable takes part in the reading along several paths, the paths can cancel one another: Φ = p\_a·m + p\_b·m with p\_a = 2, p\_b = −2; the coefficient of each path is nonzero, yet the total coefficient is 0. Hence a nonzero product of the remaining factors of a single path does not suffice to verify C1; the product check on a single path presupposes that v enters linearly and enters only that one path.

## Section 4  Dynamics

The evolution within W is given by the event-by-event equations of M\_Ind: In, Rw, Gt and Acq events through ports, events of the components of the field's own basic rules in F\_M (Sed, Run), and events of out-of-boundary processes in X\_ext; each event is evaluated on the pre-write state, its sources are computed from the pre-write source snapshot, and all writes are committed together. The condition group adds no dynamical term; C4b places only two requirements on the components of F\_M that write v: dependence 1 on v at the pre-write snapshot, and neither moving v back toward v₀ nor carrying it across v₀.

## Section 5  Theorem Group

Theorem 1 (Necessity of action under isolation)  C1 ∧ C2 ∧ C3a ∧ C4 ⇒ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 ∧ Bear\_K(a; W) = 1, K = {kind(e)}.  
Proof: (i) v\_end ≠ v₀: under C4a, v\_end = v₁, which differs from v₀ by C2; under C4b, \|v\_end − v₀\| ≥ \|v₁ − v₀\| \> 0. By C3a, u\_end = u₀. Hence ΔΦ\_W = Φ(v\_end, u₀) − Φ(v₀, u₀), and by the strict monotonicity of C1 (u₀ ∈ B\_u), ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0.  
(ii) Take τ to be the end of W. By Definition 3.5, the test of dep(Φ, σ\_τ, v) submits legitimate substitute values following the declared substitution sequence, and the first legitimate value v′ different from v\_end is necessarily among those tested (C1; when deg\_Φ(v) is declared, deg\_Φ(v) ≥ 1). By strict monotonicity (u\_end ∈ B\_u), Φ(v′, u\_end) ≠ Φ(v\_end, u\_end), so dep(Φ, σ\_τ, v) = 1. Source: under C4a, e is the most recent actual change of v within W; Definition 3.6(a) gives anc(v) ∋ e, marked determined, and since no event actually changes v thereafter, (b) keeps it; under C4b, every field-rule event after e that actually changes v has dependence 1 on v at its pre-write snapshot, so by 3.6(a) the pre-write anc(v) (which contains e as determined) is merged as a whole into the new source, determined taking precedence over undetermined; hence at the end of W, anc\_τ(v) still contains e as determined. e is an event of a within W, with kind ∈ K, that makes an actual change. Hence BearAt\_K(a, τ) = 1 and Bear\_K(a; W) = 1. ∎  
The proof uses only the condition group and the definitions; it takes neither the value of ΔΦ nor that of P5 as a premise.  
Example 1 (C3a ∧ C4a, direct write): Φ = w − th; a writes w from 1 to 3, th is unchanged, and the substitution sequence contains a value different from 3: ΔΦ\_W = 2, Bear\_R = 1.  
Example 2 (C3a ∧ C4b): the same reading; a writes w from 1 to 2; afterwards an input sets s = 1, and the field-rule sedimentation component w ← w + e·s·w (e = 1/10, with dependence 1 on w) writes w to 11/5: ΔΦ\_W = 6/5, Bear\_R = 1. Negative direction: a writes w to −1/2, and the same component writes w to −11/20; \|w − 1\| increases and w does not cross 1: ΔΦ\_W = −31/20, Bear\_R = 1.  
Example 3 (substitute values): Φ = w, the valid domain of w is the set of all rationals, the substitution sequence is {0}, and a writes w from 0 to 1: ΔΦ\_W = 1, Bear\_R = 1. If the substitution sequence is empty and all else is unchanged, then C1 fails, and ΔΦ\_W = 1 while Bear\_R = ⊥ (Section 6).

Theorem 2 (Necessity of action under a remainder bound)  C1 ∧ C2 ∧ C3b ∧ C4 ⇒ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 ∧ Bear\_K(a; W) = 1.  
Proof: ΔΦ\_W = \[Φ(v\_end, u\_end) − Φ(v\_end, u₀)\] + \[Φ(v\_end, u₀) − Φ(v₀, u₀)\]. The absolute value of the second term is at least m·\|v\_end − v₀\|, and that of the first term is at most L·‖u\_end − u₀‖₁; by C3b the former is strictly greater than the latter, so the sum is not 0 (v\_end ≠ v₀ as in Theorem 1(i)). Bearing as in Theorem 1(ii). ∎  
Example 4: Φ = 2w + th, m = 2, L = 1; a writes w from 0 to 1, and another act writes th from 0 to −3/2: 2·1 \> 1·3/2, ΔΦ\_W = 1/2, Bear\_R(a) = 1.

Theorem 3 (Necessity of action under same direction)  C1 ∧ C2 ∧ C3c ∧ C4 ⇒ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 ∧ Bear\_K(a; W) = 1, and the sign of ΔΦ\_W is s := sgn·sgn(v\_end − v₀).  
Proof: Φ(v\_end, u₀) − Φ(v₀, u₀) is strictly nonzero by C1, with sign s. Along the component-wise monotone path in B\_u from u₀ to u\_end, C3c gives each component a change with the same sign as v\_end − v₀, or 0; Φ is monotone in the same direction (direction sgn) in every variable, so each segment contributes to Φ with sign s or 0: when v\_end − v₀ \< 0 the components are likewise nonincreasing, and the contribution has direction −sgn, which agrees with s. The two terms have the same sign and the former is strict, so the sum is strictly nonzero, with sign s. Bearing as in Theorem 1(ii). ∎  
Example 5: Φ = w·u, with w and u taking positive values; a writes w from 1 to 2, and another act writes u from 1 to 3: ΔΦ\_W = 5, Bear\_R(a) = 1.

Theorem 4 (Unification of direct write and gating)  Theorems 1–3 hold both for v ∈ θ (e is Rw, and the conclusion is Bear\_R = 1) and for v ∈ m (e is Gt, and the conclusion is Bear\_G = 1). For gating, the structural evidence for C1 follows Section 2: declare the reading affine in the gating quantity m, Φ = A(u)·m + B(u), and verify that the total coefficient A(u) is nonzero and of constant sign on B\_u; or declare that m enters linearly and enters only one path, and verify that the product of the remaining factors of that path is nonzero and of constant sign on B\_u; in the non-affine case, give separate evidence of strict monotonicity.  
Proof: The proofs of Theorems 1–3 do not depend on the layer in which v lies; they use only C1–C4 and Definitions 3.4–3.6. ∎  
Example 6 (gating): Φ = w\_1a·w\_1b·m₁ + w\_2a·w\_2b·m₂, m₂ ∈ {0, 1}, with m₂ = 0, w\_2a = 1, w\_2b = 2 at entry; a writes m₂ to 1 by gating, all else unchanged: A = w\_2a·w\_2b = 2 \> 0, ΔΦ\_W = 2, Bear\_G = 1.  
Note: the condition group gives a sufficient structure for ΔΦ and for bearing; whether a is established as a host adjustment or a tuning is checked separately under *The General Form of Host Adjustment* and *The General Form of Tuning*.

Corollary 5 (Bearing and net difference kept apart)  In each of the following examples the valid domains of v and w are the rationals, and the substitution sequences are as noted for each example.  
— Bear does not imply a net difference:  
　Φ = v² (deg\_Φ(v) = 2), substitution sequence {0, 2}, a writes v from −1 to 1: at the end-state substitute value 0, Φ = 0 ≠ 1, dep = 1, Bear\_R(a) = 1; ΔΦ\_W = 0.  
　Φ = max(0, v), substitution sequence {1}, a writes v from −2 to −1: after the write, at the substitute value 1, Φ = 1 ≠ 0, dep = 1, Bear\_R(a) = 1; ΔΦ\_W = 0.  
　The following three examples all take Φ = w − th, with th kept at 0, substitution sequence {0} for w (different from the current value of w whenever it enters the test), and w = 1 at entry: a writes w to w + 1, and another act then writes it to w − 1 (cancellation); a writes w to 3, and another act then writes it to 1 (overwrite); another act first writes w to 2, and a then writes it to 1 (displace and return). In all three, at the snapshot after the write of a, dep(Φ, w) = 1 and anc(w) contains a as determined, so Bear\_R(a) = 1; ΔΦ\_W = 0.  
— A net difference does not imply Bear: Φ = w − th, deg\_Φ(z) = 0; a writes only the variable z outside the reading, and another act writes w from 1 to 3: ΔΦ\_W = 2 while Bear\_R(a) = 0.  
— Even when the condition group holds, bearing is not counterfactual necessity: Φ = w, valid domain of w {0, 1, 2, 3}, w = 1 at entry; a directly writes w := 2, after which the field rule Sed writes w := 3 (if w ≥ 1) or 0 (otherwise). At the pre-write value w = 2 this rule shows a difference under the legitimate substitute value 0, so dep = 1; the write 2 → 3 keeps the direction and increases the distance from the entry value; C1, C2, C3a and C4b hold, ΔΦ\_W = 2, Bear\_R(a) = 1. Keeping the same field rule and W and removing the direct write of a, w is written directly from 1 to 3, and ΔΦ\_W is still 2.  
Proof: Evaluate each example by P3 and P5. ∎

## Section 6  Boundary Cases and Conditions of Structural Collapse

\| Example \| Failing item \| Actual values \|  
\| Φ = v², substitution sequence {0, 2}, v: −1 → 1 \| C1 (not monotone) \| ΔΦ\_W = 0, Bear = 1 \|  
\| Φ = max(0, v), substitution sequence {1}, v: −2 → −1 \| C1 (not strict) \| ΔΦ\_W = 0, Bear = 1 \|  
\| a writes w + 1, then another act writes w − 1 \| C4 (cancellation) \| ΔΦ\_W = 0, Bear(a) = 1 \|

\| Another act first writes w := 2, then a writes w := 1 (the entry value of W) \| C2 (v₁ = v₀) \| ΔΦ\_W = 0, Bear(a) = 1 \|  
\| Φ = 2w + th, w: 0 → 1, th: 0 → −2 \| C3 (remainder bound not met) \| ΔΦ\_W = 0 \|  
\| The connection carrying the gating variable is not on the reading pathway \| C1 \| ΔΦ\_W = 0, Bear\_G = 0 \|  
\| Φ = w, w: 0 → 1, empty substitution sequence \| C1 (no legitimate substitute value enters the test) \| ΔΦ\_W = 1, Bear\_R = ⊥ \|  
\| Φ = p\_a·m + p\_b·m, p\_a = 2, p\_b = −2 \| C1 (total coefficient 0) \| ΔΦ\_W = 0, Bear\_G = 0 \|  
\| Φ = (m − 1/2)², m: 1/2 → 1, substitution sequence {0} \| C1 (not strictly monotone; the affine consistency check fails) \| ΔΦ\_W = 1/4, Bear\_G = ⊥ \|

Conditions of structural collapse: the reading loses strict monotonicity in v (C1); the value written by the act returns to the value at the start of the window (C2); the changes that other acts make to the read set exceed the bound of C3; successor writes within the window move back, cross v₀, or are executed through a route other than the field rules (C4). When any of these collapses, C ⇒ R is no longer guaranteed; ΔΦ\_W and Bear take their values separately and neither implies the other (see the table above and Corollary 5).

H group (additional conditions and trusted premises):  
H1  The structural properties of the reading declared in C1, C3b and C3c are true, including the affine declaration and the monotone direction (on the same footing as the degree declarations of 5.2 of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*);  
H2  The end-state dependence test and the dependence test of each successor event in C4b both submit legitimate substitute values following the declared substitution sequence (C1 and C4b already list the verifiable part as conditions);  
H3  kind and src match the actual route (*The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*, 5.2);  
H4  Disconnection is not in the syntax (*The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*, 1.3).

## Section 7  Summary Formula

　　C1 ∧ C2 ∧ (C3a ∨ C3b ∨ C3c) ∧ (C4a ∨ C4b) ⇒ ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 ∧ Bear\_K(a; W) = 1, K = {kind(e)}

Under H1–H4. The three forms of C3 are given by Theorems 1, 2 and 3 respectively, each holding under C4a and under C4b; Theorem 4 makes them hold for direct write and gating alike. Read-back: under 7.1 of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*, ΔΦ^f\_W ≠ 0 is read back as equivalent to a nonzero net tendency difference (B2); Bear^f = 1 is read back under B1–B4 as the holding of the bearing structural item. What is read back is the dependence and source witnessed by P5, not a causal attribution of the net difference at the end of the window to the act, nor counterfactual necessity.

## Section 8  Interfaces

Input: M\_Ind of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction* v1.0 (Definitions 3.1–3.6; P3, P5, P7 of Definition 3.9 and its conventions for taking windows; the trusted premises of 5.2, the bridging conditions and read-back rules of 7.1 and 7.2); the boundaries of direct write, gating and class qualification in *The General Form of Field Induction*, *The General Form of Host Adjustment* and *The General Form of Tuning* v1.0.

Output: the condition group C1–C4, Theorems 1–4, Corollary 5, the boundary table and the H group. Downstream use must take these over together with the H group and the read-back conditions of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*.  
— To the Adjudication Forms: the condition group is a sufficient condition for the two formal results ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0 and Bear\_K(a; W) = 1, and together with its bridging conditions it can serve as the affirmative side of adjudication; failure of the condition group does not serve as the negative side; no additional strength of attribution is derived from it. Attributing the net difference at the end of the window, or counterfactual necessity, requires a separately established comparison protocol and proof.  
— To *The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence*: whether each step satisfies the action conditions is decided by the condition group of Section 2; the convergence process admits zero write-backs and fixed points, and a nonzero rewrite at every step is not a general premise.  
— To the Construction Forms: a construction must give the structural evidence for C1 and the arrangement of events for C3 and C4.  
— To action through the sedimentation route: Field Chapter 1, Law 5 and Theorem 17 (“the deeper receives the flow first”) give the order in which paths open along the sedimentation route and can serve as a methodological reference; the condition group for the case where field-rule write-back is the main action has not been established.

Unanswered: the attribution of the net difference at the end of the window to the act, and counterfactual necessity (a separately established comparison protocol is required); guarantees of strict displacement on non-monotone readings (piecewise monotonicity, interval analysis); composition conditions when several events of the same act write the same variable; successors beyond C4b (multi-stage chains of out-of-boundary recording and write-back); the condition group when the sedimentation route is the main action.
