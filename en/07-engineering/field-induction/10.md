# The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence

## Section 1  Purpose

### 1.1  Question

*The Mathematical Form of Error-Driven Host Adjustment* v1.0 gives the formal system M\_EDR of error-driven host adjustment: forward pass, label, loss, adjoint, gradient, momentum, and write-back proceed event by event, and monitoring records only whether the loss has reached the reading target. It answers whether a write-back occurs and who bears it; it does not answer when training counts as done. Training is done only when two independent things hold at once: the output of the regulated field already realizes the target relation, and continuing under the original protocol no longer rewrites the parameters. With the former but not the latter, the parameters are still rewritten (whether a rewrite departs from alignment depends separately on its direction); with the latter but not the former, the rewriting merely stops. Hence the question: under what conditions does an error-driven host adjustment necessarily converge to such a state?

### 1.2  Propositional Form

　　C ⇒ R

C stands for the conditions of the first-proof subclass 𝒦₀ of Section 2; R states: the parameters and the momentum converge, the limit state is a host-adjustment equilibrium (Definition 2.2), and the limit point is uniquely determined by the component of the initial state in the untouched subspace together with the interpolation point of the target.

### 1.3  Strength

The conclusion is a sufficient condition and holds under the implementation class of M\_EDR, the first-proof subclass 𝒦₀, and the H group of Section 6. What is proved is a property of the limit state; whether the original trajectory reaches it in finitely many steps is characterized separately by Theorem 2. Convergence is not read as qualification for the occurrence of host adjustment, and low loss is not read as content qualification; the conclusion does not extend to biological learning or to general nonconvex networks.

## Section 2  Core Proposition

### 2.1  Five Distinctions

For one M\_EDR run, a declared input class 𝓘 (taken as the dataset D covered by the sampling table), and a target relation y = g·x, we distinguish five items:  
(a) External halt: the run is terminated from outside. In M\_EDR, stop is a monitoring flag, and the equations of Section 4 contain no training-stop interlock; (a) has no logical connection with the other items.  
(b) A single zero update: at some step the write-back amount η·v\_p is zero for every parameter.  
(c) Protocol stop: when the run is continued from the state (θ, v, phase) under the declared sampling protocol, the write-back amount is zero at every subsequent step. The state comprises the complete optimizer state (parameters θ and momentum v) and the sampling phase.  
(d) Reaching the target: loss ℓ ≤ ε.  
(e) Alignment: the regulated field realizes the target relation on every input of 𝓘, that is, the output under θ equals g·x. No tolerance is allowed.

### 2.2  Host-Adjustment Equilibrium

Definition 2.2  A state (θ, v, phase) that satisfies both (e) and (c) is called a host-adjustment equilibrium.

Host-adjustment equilibrium is the convergence end point of host adjustment; the convergence end point of tuning is Tuning-Balance (also called Experience-Balance), and the two are collectively called content-alignment equilibrium. The two components of host-adjustment equilibrium are independent requirements. Under alignment the gradient is 0 and the next write-back amount is η·β·v: when η·β·v ≠ 0 a write-back still occurs; when the write-back falls in the untouched subspace, alignment on the declared inputs is preserved. Example: y = (w₁ + w₂)·x, g = 2, θ = (1, 1), v = (1, −1), η = β = 1/2: at the next step θ = (3/4, 5/4); the parameters change, while the output on every declared input is still 2x. From the same entry with β = 0, the parameters remain unchanged at the next step and the momentum returns to zero. With stopping but no alignment (e.g., η = 0), the parameters do not move while the output falls short of the target. Zero momentum is not a necessary item of host-adjustment equilibrium.

### 2.3  The First-Proof Subclass 𝒦₀

K1  Linear in the parameters: y = θ·φ(x), with φ determined by the computation graph;  
K2  β = 0, and the initial momentum is 0;  
K3  The sampling table covers D periodically;  
K4  Realizable target: there exists θ\* such that θ\*·φ(x) = g·x for all x ∈ D;  
K5  Step-size condition: 0 \< η‖φ(x)‖² \< 2 for all x ∈ D;  
K6  φ(x) ≠ 0 for all x ∈ D.

K1, K3, and K5 are the original premises of Theorem 20-2 of *The Discrimination Form of Trace-Response Transformation in Artificial Neural Networks* (Field Concretization Group Form, Chapter 20; 4.4); in K2, β = 0 makes the write-back of M\_EDR correspond to the write rule of 20-2, and zero initial momentum is a chosen condition; K4 is the “consistent targets” of 20-2. K5 already implies K6 (η‖φ(x)‖² \> 0 gives φ(x) ≠ 0); K6 is listed separately to mark its use when alignment is inferred backward from zero write-back.

### 2.4  Core Proposition

　　𝒦₀ ⇒ lim θ\_k = θ∞ exists ∧ θ∞ = P\_{V⊥}θ₀ + θ\*\_V ∧ v\_k → 0 ∧ the state (θ∞, 0, any phase) is a host-adjustment equilibrium

V = span{φ(x) : x ∈ D}; P\_{V⊥}θ₀ is the component of θ₀ in V⊥; θ\*\_V is the unique point in V such that θ·φ(x) = g·x for all x ∈ D.

## Section 4  Dynamics

### 4.1  Equations

At step k, following Section 4 of M\_EDR: sample x\_k, run the forward pass to obtain y\_k, take the label y\*\_k = g·x\_k, compute the adjoint and the gradient q\_k, update the momentum v\_k = β·v\_{k−1} + q\_k, and write back θ\_k = θ\_{k−1} − η·v\_k. Under K1 and K2 these combine into

　　θ\_k = θ\_{k−1} + η·φ(x\_k)·(g·x\_k − θ\_{k−1}·φ(x\_k)),　v\_k = q\_k.

This is exactly the write of Theorem 20-2, Δθ = η·s·(t − θᵀs), with s = φ(x\_k) and t = g·x\_k. Example: y = w₁·x + w₂·x², D = {1, 2}: the first six steps of this recursion agree value by value with the event-by-event run of M\_EDR.

### 4.2  Extension

A finite-window M\_EDR run is extended by the same recursion to k = 1, 2, …. The parameter sequence takes values in ℚⁿ, and limits are taken in its completion ℝⁿ; when β \> 0, they are taken in the product of the parameter and momentum spaces. The extension serves only for limit statements; finite-step statements remain within M\_EDR.

## Section 5  Theorem Group

Proposition 1 (Alignment and zero write-back: forward direction)  For any computation graph: alignment ∧ η·β·v = 0 ⇒ continuing from this state under the protocol, the write-back is zero at every step; θ is preserved, alignment is preserved, and the sampling phase advances according to the protocol. In particular, this holds when β = 0 or v = 0.  
Proof: When θ is aligned, δ\_out = 0 on every sampled input; by the adjoint recursion every δ\_p = 0, so q = 0. The next momentum is β·v and the write-back amount is η·β·v = 0, so θ is preserved and alignment is preserved. Thereafter: when η = 0 the write-back is always 0; when η \> 0, β·v = 0, so the momentum becomes 0, and at every later step q = 0, v = 0, and the write-back is 0. ∎

Proposition 2 (Zero write-back: converse direction)  Assume K1, K2, K3, K6, and η \> 0. The state, continued under the protocol for one complete sampling cycle, has zero write-back at every step ⇒ alignment.  
Proof: Under K2, v = q; zero write-back with η \> 0 gives q = 0 at every step, and the parameters do not change within the cycle. Under K1, q = (θ·φ(x) − g·x)·φ(x), and K6 gives θ·φ(x) = g·x; K3 makes this hold for all x ∈ D. ∎  
Without K1 the converse fails: y = w²·x, w = 0, g = 1, x = 2; the residual is −2 and the gradient 2w·x·δ\_out = 0, so the write-back is zero without alignment.

Proposition 3 (Implication diagram)  (c) ⇒ (b); host-adjustment equilibrium ⇒ (c) and ⇒ (e); (e) ⇒ (d) (ℓ = 0 ≤ ε at that step). The following directions fail:  
\- (c) ⇏ (e): when η = 0 every step has zero write-back, and the parameters stop at an unaligned point.  
\- (b) ⇏ (c): y = w₁·x + w₂·x², g = 1, entry (2, −1), η = 1/32, first sample x = 1: at step 1 the residual is 0 and the write-back is zero; at step 2, x = 2, the residual is −2, and the write-back is nonzero.  
\- (d) ⇏ (e): y = w·x, w = 3/2, g = 2, x = 1, ε = 1/2: ℓ = 1/8 ≤ ε without alignment.  
\- (e) ⇏ (c) (β \> 0): y = w·x, w = g = 2, entry momentum 2, β = 1/2, η = 1/8: already aligned, yet the write-back at step 1 is nonzero.  
\- “Zero write-back when the limit state is continued” ⇏ “zero write-back on the original trajectory from some step on”: y = w·x, x = 1, g = 1, θ₀ = 0, η = 1/2: θ\_k = 1 − 2⁻ᵏ, every step writes back, and the limit 1 is a fixed point.  
Proof: The first three (the positive implications) follow from the definitions and Proposition 1; the counterexamples are evaluated one by one with the equations of Section 4. ∎

Theorem 1 (Convergence to host-adjustment equilibrium)  In 𝒦₀, θ\_k converges to θ∞ (in both the full-rank branch, V = ℝⁿ, and the rank-deficient branch, V a proper subspace), and  
(i) θ∞ is aligned on D;  
(ii) the component of θ∞ in V⊥ equals that component of θ₀, and its component in V is θ\*\_V;  
(iii) v\_k = q\_k → 0; the state (θ∞, 0, any phase) is a host-adjustment equilibrium.  
Proof: By 4.1, the run is exactly the periodic write of Theorem 20-2, whose premises are supplied by K1, K2, K3, and K5. Full-rank branch (V = ℝⁿ): by 20-2(1), the one-cycle map is a contraction and the cycle-section limit is unique; with consistent targets (K4) it is the interpolating solution. Rank-deficient branch (V a proper subspace): by 20-2(2), the V⊥ component is unchanged beat by beat and the component in V converges geometrically; each write changes the parameters only along φ(x) ∈ V, and restricted to V the features span V, so by the reading of 20-2(1) on V the cycle section converges in V to the interpolating solution θ*\_V. In both branches, the interpolation point has residual 0 at every phase and is a fixed point of the write at every phase; the cycle-section limit at each phase is obtained from the limit at the preceding phase by a continuous finite-step map, so the cycle sections at all phases coincide, and the step-by-step trajectory θ\_k itself converges to θ∞ = P\_{V⊥}θ₀ + θ*\_V. (i) and (ii) follow.  
(iii) Under K2, v\_k = q\_k, and the momentum is not identically 0: y = w·x, w₀ = 1, g = 2, x = 1, η = 1 gives v = −1 and w₁ = 2 at the first step. The sampling table has a finite period, θ\_k → θ∞, θ∞ has residual 0 at every phase, and q is continuous in θ; hence q\_k → 0 and v\_k → 0. v = 0 is a legitimate momentum component of the interpolating limit state; by Proposition 1, continuing (θ∞, 0) from any phase gives zero write-back at every step together with alignment, which is host-adjustment equilibrium. When the sampling period exceeds 1, the sampling phase cycles periodically and does not converge, so we state the result phase by phase: at every phase, the cycle-section subsequence converges to (θ∞, 0, that phase). ∎  
Example: y = w₁·x + w₂·x², D = {1, 2}, g = 2, η = 1/16: η‖φ‖² is 1/8 and 5/4; the one-cycle map has trace 49/64 and determinant −7/32, so its spectral radius is below 1; the interpolation point (2, 0) is a fixed point of the cycle map. y = w₁·x + w₂·x (φ(x) = (x, x)), entry (1, −1): w₁ − w₂ stays at 2 and w₁ + w₂ → g; with entry (3, −3), w₁ − w₂ stays at 6—the limit varies with the component of the initial state in V⊥. θ₀ = 0, g = 1, x = 1, η = 1/2: v\_k = −2^{−(k−1)} → 0.

Theorem 2 (Finite-step arrival versus asymptotic convergence)  In 𝒦₀, the original trajectory has zero write-back at every step from some step on if and only if θ\_k is aligned from some step on. In the single-mode case (D contains only one input, φ = φ(x)), θ\_k − θ∞ = (1 − η‖φ‖²)ᵏ·(θ₀ − θ∞)\_V, so arrival in finitely many steps occurs if and only if η‖φ‖² = 1 or (θ₀ − θ∞)\_V = 0.  
Proof: “Aligned ⇒ zero write-back thereafter”: under K2, β = 0; when θ\_k is aligned, the next step has q = 0 and v = q = 0, the write-back is zero, and alignment is preserved; proceed step by step (this is Proposition 1 with β = 0). The momentum v\_k = q\_k left by the step that first reaches alignment may be nonzero and returns to zero only at the next step; parameter arrival, zero write-back thereafter, and the return of the momentum to zero may differ by one step index. By Definition 2.2, the first aligned state (θ\_k, v\_k, phase) already satisfies “continuing without write-back,” and is therefore already a host-adjustment equilibrium. “Zero write-back from some step on ⇒ aligned” follows from Proposition 2, using K1, K3, and K6. The single-mode formula follows by direct computation from the recursion. ∎  
Example: θ₀ = 0, g = 1, x = 1: with η = 1/2, θ\_k = 1 − 2⁻ᵏ never arrives; with η = 1 it arrives in one step, with zero write-back thereafter. y = w·x, x = 1, g = 2, w₀ = 1, η = 1: after step 1, (w, v) = (2, −1); after step 2, (2, 0); step 2 has zero write-back; the state after step 1 is already a host-adjustment equilibrium.

Theorem 3 (Unrealizable target: cycle sections without stopping)  Assume K1, K2, K3, and K5 hold and K4 fails. The cycle sections at each phase converge; the cycle sections at the various phases do not all coincide, and on the sections there is a write-back in every cycle: the adjustment does not stop, and there is no alignment.  
Proof: The full-rank branch follows from 20-2(1) (at its original strength: only cycle-section convergence is claimed). For the rank-deficient branch, first retain the V⊥ component by 20-2(2), then restrict to V: the features span V, so by the one-cycle contraction of the full-rank branch on V the cycle sections converge; the section limit at each phase is obtained by a continuous finite-step map. If the sections at all phases coincided at one point θ̄, then θ̄ would be a common fixed point of the writes at every phase; under K5, η \> 0 and φ(x) ≠ 0, so a fixed point requires the residual at that phase to be 0, hence θ̄ is aligned on D, contradicting the failure of K4. Hence the sections do not all coincide; the write at some phase maps one section point to another, and the write-back is nonzero. Unrealizability means precisely that no θ is aligned on D. ∎  
Example (full rank): y = w·x², label g·x, g = 2, D = {1, 2}, η = 1/16: the section after one step at x = 1 is 17/16, and after one step at x = 2 it is 1; the trajectory approaches the sections, and on the sections every step writes back.  
Example (rank-deficient): y = (w₁ + w₂)·x², label 2x, D = {1, 2}, η = 1/32, entry (1, 0), V = span{(1, 1)}: after the two phases the parameter sum is 17/16 and 1 respectively, the parameter difference stays at 1, and every step writes back.  
The external-target boundary of Theorem 20-2: the same feature with alternating labels 0 and 1 (η = 1/2, entry 2/3) gives the sections {1/3, 2/3}. This is an inconsistent external target in the sense of 20-2, not an M\_EDR label with fixed g, and it likewise falls under “only cycle-section convergence is claimed” in 20-2(1).

## Section 6  Boundary Cases and Conditions of Structural Collapse

\| Case \| Status \| Example or reason \|  
\| Step size out of bounds (K5 fails), nonzero initial error \| Convergence not guaranteed; may diverge or may converge \| y = w·x, D = {1, 2}, g = 2: with η = 3/2 the one-cycle factor is (1 − 3/2)(1 − 6) = 5/2, divergence; with η = 3/4 the mode x = 2 is out of bounds and the one-cycle factor is (1 − 3/4)(1 − 3) = −1/2, convergence. Out of bounds does not imply nonconvergence \|  
\| Initially aligned, K2 holds, η out of bounds \| Stays fixed \| η = 3, w = g = 2: zero write-back at every step \|  
\| Nonzero momentum (K2 fails) \| Convergence conditions not given \| Alignment does not imply protocol stop (Proposition 3) \|  
\| Two-layer linear network (bilinear in the joint parameters) \| Unproved \| Does not satisfy K1; 20-2 is not carried over on the strength of the network's name \|  
\| Nonlinear models, batch averaging \| Outside 𝒦₀ \| Batch averaging is not the write-back rule of M\_EDR \|  
\| Inputs with φ(x) = 0 \| Conditional \| Zero write-back on such an input does not imply alignment (Proposition 2 requires K6) \|  
\| Spiking networks \| Not established \| An equivalent update mechanism has not been written out \|

Conditions of structural collapse: an out-of-bounds step size (K5) lets the one-cycle map lose contraction; an unrealizable target (K4) makes convergence reach only cycle sections (Theorem 3); nonzero momentum (K2) decouples alignment from stopping; nonlinearity in the parameters (K1) keeps zero write-back from implying alignment.

H group:  
H1  The extension follows the same recursion and serves only for limit statements;  
H2  The sampling table covers D periodically (the protocol form of K3);  
H3  The target is realizable (K4);  
H4  Alignment allows no tolerance; a tolerance, if taken, is declared separately, and Theorem 1(iii) is rewritten accordingly.

## Section 7  Summary Formula

　　𝒦₀ ⇒ lim θ\_k = θ∞ = P\_{V⊥}θ₀ + θ\*\_V ∧ v\_k → 0 ∧ (θ∞, 0, any phase) is a host-adjustment equilibrium

That is: continued from any phase, the limit state has zero write-back at every step and is aligned. This is a property of the limit state, not arrival of the original trajectory at some step; when the original trajectory arrives in finitely many steps is characterized by Theorem 2; when the target is unrealizable, only cycle sections are obtained (Theorem 3).

## Section 8  Interfaces

Input: M\_EDR of *The Mathematical Form of Error-Driven Host Adjustment* v1.0 (Definitions 3.1–3.4 and 3.9, the equations of Section 4, the parameter domain of 1.3); Theorem 20-2 of *The Discrimination Form of Trace-Response Transformation in Artificial Neural Networks* (Field Concretization Group Form, Chapter 20; 4.4), carried over under its original premises at its original strength; the structure of host adjustment from *The General Form of Host Adjustment* v1.0. Methodological reference: Theorem 20-7 of the same paper (proof in 4.4; 11.4 is its three-level interface).

Output: the five distinctions and the definition of host-adjustment equilibrium; Propositions 1–3; Theorems 1–3; the boundary table and the H group.  
— To *The Adjudication Form of Host-Adjustment Equilibrium*: delivers the distinctions of 2.1, Definition 2.2, and the implication diagram; the criterion for adjudicating from observations whether host-adjustment equilibrium has been reached is established separately.  
— To *The Observational Inversion Form of Host Adjustment*: the observable part of the converged state is registered as an interface; neither is a premise of the other.  
— To *The Necessity Form of Field-Induction Action*: whether each step satisfies the action conditions is adjudicated by that Form; the convergence process admits zero write-back and fixed points, and nonzero rewriting at every step is not a general premise.  
— To *The Reachability Form of Maintenance Law Engineering*: host-adjustment equilibrium as a reachable goal.

Unanswered: convergence conditions under nonzero momentum; convergence of two-layer linear networks and of general nonconvex models; host-adjustment equilibrium under tolerance-based alignment; equivalent updates and convergence on spiking networks.
