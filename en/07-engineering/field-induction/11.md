# The Observational Inversion Form of Host Adjustment

## Section 1  Observation Sets and Declaration of the Forward Map

### 1.1  Question

An error-driven host adjustment leaves observable traces as it runs: the output and loss at each step, and possibly also gradients, write-back amounts, or parameter coordinates. From these observations, what at most can be known about the host adjustment, and what necessarily cannot? The value of the goal, the initial state of the parameters, the registered source of the goal, the bearing of the act, and object-level content qualification: under which observations is each identifiable, and under which is it necessarily unidentifiable?

### 1.2  Forward Map

The forward map H is M\_EDR of *The Mathematical Form of Error-Driven Host Adjustment* v1.0: the event-by-event equations of Section 4 (sampling, forward pass, label, loss, adjoint, gradient, momentum, write-back, monitoring), the sources of Definition 3.7, the goal declaration and goal-source registration of Definition 3.8, and Rewrite^f, Bear\_R^f, GoalCond^f of Definition 3.10. Forward domain 𝒟:

\- The implementation class is strict 𝒞\_EDR (finite computation graphs, op ∈ {in, mul, sq, add});  
\- Value domains (Definition 3.1): the predicate value domains of the parameters, the goal g, node values, labels, loss, adjoints, gradients, and momentum are all the full set of rationals, that is, the numerical slots are open to every rational candidate and its trajectory; the sampling index and the monitoring flag take finite value domains under the original rules;  
\- β = 0, and the initial momentum is 0;  
\- Data D = {0 ↦ 1, 1 ↦ 2}, sampling table π(k, s) = (k + s) mod 2, seed s known; inputs x ∈ {1, 2}, both nonzero;  
\- The observation window is steps 1–K; the run window additionally contains the entry and the phase (0, 1).

### 1.3  Observation Protocols

The protocols are defined separately and are not merged:  
O1  The output y\_k and loss ℓ\_k at each step (before write-back);  
O2-grad  O1 plus the gradient q\_k at each step (every parameter coordinate);  
O2-upd  O1 plus the write-back amount η·v\_k at each step;  
O3  O1 plus the parameter coordinates at the end of each step; with the entry coordinates included, it is written O3⁰;  
O\_end  Only the parameter coordinates at the end of the window, without O1.  
A combined protocol takes the union of the observation fields. If two runs have the same observations under each component protocol, they have the same observations under the combined protocol.  
Contrast: x = 1, θ = 0, η = 0, with the two runs g = 1 and g = −1: the observations under O1, O2-upd, and O3 are the same; the gradients are −1 and 1, so O2-grad separates them.

## Section 2  Statement of the Inversion Problem

Take a run h as the preimage, H\_O(h) as its observation sequence under protocol O, and Z(h) as the quantity to be identified. For an observed value y, the preimage set H\_O⁻¹(y) consists of the runs in 𝒟 that agree with the remaining known quantities and give y under O. Z is identifiable at y if and only if Z(H\_O⁻¹(y)) is a singleton.

There are two kinds of quantifier, marked cell by cell in the read-out:  
— Per observed value: for a given y, write 1 if Z(H\_O⁻¹(y)) is a singleton and 0 if it has at least two values;  
— Protocol-wide: if it is a singleton for every y under the listed conditions, write “global 1”; if there exists a y for which Z(H\_O⁻¹(y)) has at least two values, write “global 0” and give the witness.  
⊥ means undetermined: neither has a singleton been proved nor have preimages with different values been constructed.

1 and 0 are given only for realizable observed values (nonempty preimage set); an observed value with an empty preimage set is reported separately as out of domain or incompatible with the declaration. The scope of “global” is the family of runs generated by the frozen known configuration (1.4) under the conditions listed in the cell.

Identifiability is proved by a singleton fiber; unidentifiability is proved by a concrete construction of two points with different values in the fiber, and the construction must consist of legitimate runs in 𝒟. Algebraic symmetries (reflection of the goal, exchange and sign change of the parameters) guarantee only that the equations and observations coincide; in the open rational value domains of 𝒟, the entry of a symmetric variant and all its subsequent writes are rational, so the variant is a legitimate run. If a narrower value domain is declared, the fiber is taken as the intersection of the analytic fiber with the legitimate runs, and the point count and read-out are recomputed on the intersection; a singleton conclusion (the identifiable direction) is preserved in the narrower domain, since it requires only that the actual observation come from a legitimate run. Example: y = w·x, x = 1, η = 0, w₀ = 1/2, K = 1, with the valid domain of g declared as {0, 2}: g = 2 gives O1 = (1/2, 9/8); the algebraic reflection gives −1, which is not in the valid domain; the other legitimate value 0 gives a different observation; so this fiber is {2}.

## Section 3  Identifiability Analysis

### 3.1  The Goal Value g

Setting: y = w·x, θ₀ known, g hidden. Under squared loss, \|y\_k − g·x\_k\| = √(2ℓ\_k) at each step, so when x\_k ≠ 0, g ∈ {(y\_k ± √(2ℓ\_k))/x\_k}. The intersection of the step-by-step candidates is a necessary condition; uniqueness additionally requires a check of forward consistency. Example: θ₀ = 1/2, η = 1/8, g = 2, s = 0: at step 1 (x = 2) the candidates are {−1, 2}, at step 2 (x = 1) they are {1/2, 2}, and the intersection is {2}.

Theorem 1 (Identifiability bounds for g)  In 𝒟:  
(a) O2-grad: g is identifiable at any step: q\_k = (θ\_{k−1}·x\_k − g·x\_k)·x\_k, so g = θ\_{k−1} − q\_k/x\_k², with θ\_{k−1} obtained recursively from the known quantities and earlier observations.  
(b) O1, K = 1 or η = 0: the fiber is {g, 2θ₀ − g} (both are legitimate in the open rational value domains of 𝒟). Per observed value: a singleton when ℓ₁ = 0 (i.e., g = θ₀), otherwise two points; protocol-wide 0.  
(c) O1, K ≥ 2 and η \> 0: g is identifiable.  
(d) O2-upd, O3: when η \> 0, g is identifiable at step 1; when η = 0, the write-back amount is always 0 and the coordinates are always θ₀, so the observations coincide with O1 and the fiber is as in (b).  
Proof: (a) Solve directly from the adjoint and gradient equations. (b) When η = 0, θ\_k ≡ θ₀, y\_k = θ₀·x\_k does not depend on g, and ℓ\_k = (θ₀ − g)²·x\_k²/2 depends only on \|θ₀ − g\|, so g and 2θ₀ − g give the same observations at every step; when K = 1, the observation at step 1 likewise depends only on \|θ₀ − g\|. (c) θ₁ = θ₀ − η·(θ₀ − g)·x₁², the coefficient of g is η·x₁² ≠ 0, and y₂ = θ₁·x₂ uniquely determines g. (d) The write-back amount is η·q₁ and the coordinate is θ₁; when η \> 0 each uniquely determines g. ∎  
The registered identity of the goal is separate from the value g: this section identifies only the value; for the registered identity see 3.3.

### 3.2  The Initial Parameter State θ₀

Setting: y = w₂·(w₁·x), g known, θ₀ = (w₁, w₂) hidden. Write P = w₁w₂, S = w₁² + w₂², δ₁ = (P − g)·x₁, c = η·δ₁·x₁. The write-back at step 1 gives θ₁ = (w₁ − c·w₂, w₂ − c·w₁), whose product is P₁ = (1 + c²)·P − c·S.

Theorem 2 (Observational equivalence classes of θ₀)  In 𝒟:  
(a) O1, with K = 1, or η = 0, or δ₁ = 0: the fiber is the product level set {w₁w₂ = P}: a hyperbola when P ≠ 0, the union of the two coordinate axes when P = 0.  
(b) O1, K ≥ 2, η \> 0, δ₁ ≠ 0: the fiber is exactly the orbit {±(w₁, w₂), ±(w₂, w₁)}. Point count: 1 when θ₀ = (0, 0); 2 when w₁ = w₂ ≠ 0 or w₁ = −w₂ ≠ 0; 4 otherwise. The point count can be decided from the observations: P and S have been read out, and w₁ = ±w₂ if and only if S = ±2P.  
(c) O2-grad: a singleton when δ₁ ≠ 0 (q₁ = δ₁·x₁·(w₂, w₁)); as in (a) when δ₁ = 0.  
(d) O2-upd: a singleton when η \> 0 and δ₁ ≠ 0; otherwise as for O1.  
(e) O3: θ₁ = \[\[1, −c\], \[−c, 1\]\]·θ₀, with c known from P = y₁/x₁ in O1. When c² ≠ 1 the map is invertible and the fiber is a singleton (including c = 0, where θ₁ = θ₀ is read directly). When c = −1, θ₁ gives only the sum A = w₁ + w₂; together with P, solve t² − A·t + P = 0, whose discriminant is (w₁ − w₂)²: a double root and a singleton when w₁ = w₂, otherwise two points {(w₁, w₂), (w₂, w₁)}. When c = +1, θ₁ gives only the difference B = w₁ − w₂; from w₂² + B·w₂ − P = 0, whose discriminant is (w₁ + w₂)²: a double root and a singleton when w₁ = −w₂, otherwise two points {(w₁, w₂), (−w₂, −w₁)}. In both cases the subsequent observations are determined by θ₁ and add no information. O3⁰ gives a singleton.  
Proof: (a) When η = 0, θ does not change and y\_k = P·x\_k; when K = 1, the observation at step 1 depends only on P; when δ₁ = 0, q₁ = 0, and with β = 0, θ does not change, δ\_k = 0 thereafter, and the observations depend only on P. (b) Upper bound: y₁ determines P; when c ≠ 0, y₂ determines P₁ and hence S = ((1 + c²)·P − P₁)/c; P and S determine {w₁, w₂} up to exchange and joint sign change (w₁ ± w₂ = ±√(S ± 2P)). Lower bound: the forward pass depends only on P; the write-back w₁ ← w₁ − η·δ·x·w₂, w₂ ← w₂ − η·δ·x·w₁ is symmetric under exchange and equivariant under joint sign change, so all points of the orbit give the same observations at every step; all points of the orbit and their trajectories are rational, hence legitimate in 𝒟. The rational points of the product level set in (a) are legitimate for the same reason. Point counts follow from the fixed points of exchange and sign change. (c)(d) Solve directly from the gradient and from the write-back amount. (e) Follows directly from the linear expression for θ₁ and the quadratic in P. ∎  
Example (g = 2, s = 0, x₁ = 2): with K = 2 and η = 1/8, the fiber of entry (0, 0) is a singleton (the residual is nonzero, c = −1, and P = S = 0 uniquely determines the initial state), that of (1, 1) is {(1, 1), (−1, −1)}, and that of (1, 1/2) is {±(1, 1/2), ±(1/2, 1)}. Under O3, with η = 1/4 and entry (1, 1), c = −1 and the double root gives a singleton; with η = 1/6 and entry (1, 1/2), c = −1 and there are two points {(1, 1/2), (1/2, 1)}. With g = −2, s = 1, x₁ = 1, η = 1, c = +1: entry (1, −1) gives a singleton, and entry (2, −1/2) gives two points {(2, −1/2), (1/2, −2)}.  
Rescaling: (1, 1/2) and (2, 1/4) give the same forward function and the same y at step 1; the gradients at step 1 are (−3, −6) and (−3/2, −12), and y at step 2 is 55/32 and 245/64. Two-layer rescaling preserves the forward function but not the multistep trajectory or the gradients.

### 3.3  Source Structure

Contrast: the same y = w₂·(w₁·x), θ₀ = (1, 1/2), η = 1/8, K = 2, the same goal value 2, and three modes of goal registration:  
　Holding anchor at entry: g = 2 at entry, and Xg holds g;  
　Set within the run window: g = 0 at entry, and a goal-setting event at (0, 1) of the run window writes g := 2, before the observation of the first sample of step 1;  
　Unregistered: g = 2 at entry, with no holding anchor and no setting event.  
All three are legitimate goal entries of M\_EDR (Definition 3.8), and all pass the registration contract of the run entry; the forward domain is not enlarged. GoalCond is computed over the whole run window; the observation window is steps 1–K.

Frozen probe coverage 𝒟\_π: on top of 𝒟, the probe specification and its coverage are also listed as known. On the event chain from g to the parameter write-back (label, loss, adjoint, momentum, write-back, and forward pass), for every read-in variable of every event, the number of legitimate substitute values after normalization per Definition 3.6 (removing illegitimate values and those equal to the current value, deduplicating with order preserved) is no less than the degree the event declares for it, and the declared degree upper bounds are true; variables with finite value domains are fully enumerated. In the open rational value domains of 𝒟, taking the 19 rational points {k/7 : −9 ≤ k ≤ 9} as the substitute sequence, with every event declaring degree at most 2, satisfies this coverage: every read-in variable has at least 18 legitimate substitute values after normalization. That the probe list is visible does not by itself guarantee coverage.

Theorem 3 (Unidentifiability of the formal output of GoalCond)  When the mode of goal registration is hidden and the value of g is known:  
(a) General domain, existence: for O1, O2-grad, O2-upd, O3, and any combination of them, there exists an observed value y\* whose fiber contains runs with formal output of GoalCond equal to 1 and runs with it equal to ⊥; protocol-wide 0.  
(b) 𝒟\_π, per observed value: for every observed value y realized by a run in 𝒟\_π, the fiber contains both the holding-anchor variant and the unregistered variant; the formal output of GoalCond is a determinate value, 1 or 0, for the former and ⊥ for the latter. Hence at every such y the formal output of GoalCond takes at least two values. Output 0 arises in two cases: there is no actual rewrite; or there is an actual rewrite whose sources are not determined to hit the goal registration.  
(c) When the coverage condition is not satisfied, no per-observed-value statement is made, and the holding anchor may also give ⊥. Example 1: y = w·x, the same actual write-back, with the substitute sequence of g set empty. Example 2: keep the same 19-point list, but declare the predicate value domain of g as g ≥ 2 (outside 𝒟); y = w·x, w₀ = 1, g = 2, x = 1, η = 1, and the actual write-back goes to 2; all list points lie below 2, so after normalization the goal read has no legitimate substitute value. In both examples the holding-anchor and unregistered branches have the same numerical observations and GoalCond is ⊥ in both; nor can the goal-setting variant make up for this deficiency through its change of registration. In this case only (a) holds.  
(d) What the theorem states is that the formal output of GoalCond cannot be identified from observations. ⊥ is the formal value “goal condition undetermined,” not a negation; the theorem does not assert that the upstream goal condition holds in one branch and fails in the other.  
Proof: (a) Take the contrast above: from step 1 on, the three runs have the same event-by-event equations (the goal-setting event is at (0, 1), and by step 1 the value of g is the same in all three), and their observation fields coincide one by one under every protocol; GoalCond^f consults the goal-source registration: it is 1 for the holding anchor and for setting within the run window, and ⊥ for the unregistered run. Take y\* to be their common observed value.  
(b) Take any run h in 𝒟\_π and construct the other two registration variants; the observations coincide as in (a). When unregistered, there is no goal-source registration, and GoalCond^f = ⊥. With the holding anchor, by the coverage condition of 𝒟\_π, every dependence test on the event chain has, after normalization, no fewer legitimate substitute values than the declared degree, so by Definition 3.6 every test is complete and returns 1 or 0; the source marks are therefore all determinate, and GoalCond^f is 1 or 0. The monitoring event writes only stop; stop is read by no event and does not enter the sources of the parameters. ∎  
Stated separately: the holding anchor and setting within the run window have different source configurations (an anchor versus an event) and the same GoalCond output (1 in both); different source configurations do not mean different predicate values. The value g is identifiable under the conditions of Theorem 1, while the registered identity is not: identifiability of the value does not imply identifiability of the goal source.

### 3.4  Bearing Bear\_R

Proposition 4 (Pure final state: the η = 1 configuration)  Known configuration 𝒦\_end¹: y = w·x, g = 2, s = 1 (x = 1), η = 1, K = 1, entry w₀ hidden; reading Φ(w) = w, deg\_Φ(w) = 1, and the declared substitute sequence of w is the 19 rational points {k/7 : −9 ≤ k ≤ 9}, so that for any realizable final state w has at least 18 legitimate substitute values after normalization, and the dependence test of the reading actually tests at least one legitimate value different from the current w. Under 𝒦\_end¹, O\_end does not identify Bear\_R^f (global 0).  
Proof: With w₀ = 1, one step writes back to 2, and Bear\_R^f = 1; with w₀ = 2, the state is already aligned, the write-back is zero, and Bear\_R^f = 0. O\_end is 2 in both cases. ∎  
The O1 observations of the two runs differ, so this pair is not a witness for O1 or O3. Read-back: by 7.1 of *The Mathematical Form of Error-Driven Host Adjustment*, Bear\_R^f = 1 is read back, one-directionally, as the structural item of bearing under B1–B4, while 0 is reported only as a formal value; hence on this witness fiber (w = 2 at window end), O\_end cannot certify bearing, nor does it follow that bearing fails.

Proposition 5 (Pure final state: the η = 1/2 configuration)  Known configuration 𝒦\_end^{1/2}: as 𝒦\_end¹ but with η = 1/2. Under 𝒦\_end^{1/2}, O\_end uniquely determines w₀, and Bear\_R^f is identifiable (global 1).  
Proof: w₁ = w₀ − (1/2)(w₀ − 2) = 1 + w₀/2, which is invertible for all rational w₀. Φ(w) = w depends strictly on w, and under the frozen probe the dependence test at any snapshot tests a legitimate value different from the current one, so dep(Φ, w) = 1; the write-back makes an actual change if and only if w₀ ≠ 2. Hence Bear\_R^f = 1 if and only if w₀ ≠ 2, that is, w₁ ≠ 2; when w₁ = 2, Bear\_R^f = 0. Example: the window-end value w₁ = 1 inverts uniquely to w₀ = 0, Bear\_R^f = 1, and bearing can be read back under the bridging conditions. ∎

The reading probe is an antecedent of both propositions: if the other settings are kept and only the substitute sequence of w is set empty (the valid domain is still all rationals), the final states for η = 1 and η = 1/2 are 2 and 3/2 respectively, each with an actual write-back, while Bear\_R^f changes from 1 to ⊥; the same final state therefore cannot uniquely determine formal bearing. The empty probe does not satisfy the antecedent of either configuration; the numerical inversion of the initial state, w₀ = 2(w₁ − 1), is unaffected.

The two propositions belong to two different known configurations, and the global quantifier of each ranges only over the family of runs generated by its own configuration. What can be stated over the broader family of configurations is this: there exist a known configuration and an observed value in it at which O\_end does not identify Bear\_R^f. Other pure-final-state configurations are unproved and recorded as ⊥. The identifiability of Bear\_R^f under full-run observation (O1–O3) is undetermined.

### 3.5  Object-Level Content Qualification

𝒟 contains no qualification function and no map of its legitimate changes: the goal-target registration of M\_EDR declares only the input–output relation of the regulated field (Definition 3.8(a)), and training quantities and content qualification are not interchangeable (Definition 3.9). Hence the observation protocols O1–O3 and O\_end establish no qualification read-back; the identifiability of content qualification is undetermined, and no unidentifiability statement is made. Once a qualification map is established, it is tested by fibers as in Section 2.

## Section 5  Residual Declarations

### 5.1  Inferences Not Permitted

\- Low loss does not imply alignment, still less content qualification: y = w·x, w = 3/2, g = 2, x = 1, ℓ = 1/8 ≤ 1/2 without alignment.  
\- The final state does not imply bearing: O\_end is 2 in both cases, while Bear\_R^f is 1 and 0 (Proposition 4).  
\- Identifiability of the value from gradients does not imply identifiability of the goal source: gradients determine the value g (Theorem 1(a)) but not the registered identity (Theorem 3: same gradients, GoalCond 1 and ⊥).  
\- Statistical correlation does not imply identifiability: identifiability is judged only by fibers, not by correlation between observations and the quantity to be identified.

### 5.2  Residuals

Open items: β \> 0 and nonzero initial momentum; conditions for the identifiability of g under O1 in general parameter-linear models; the fibers of θ₀ on multilayer and nonlinear graphs; the identifiability of Bear\_R under full-run observation; noisy observations; the content-qualification map.

### 5.3  H Group and Breach

H1  The forward domain 𝒟 and the known quantities are declared in advance, item by item;  
H2  β = 0, and the initial momentum is 0;  
H3  x\_k ≠ 0;  
H4  Observations are exact rational values, without noise.  
Breach:  
— For “global 1” and per-observed-value 1: exhibit, at that observed value, preimages legitimate in 𝒟 with the same observation but different values of the quantity to be identified;  
— For statements about exact fibers (e.g., the number of orbit points): exhibit a legitimate preimage outside the fiber, or prove that some listed point is illegitimate or gives a different observation;  
— For per-observed-value 0 and “global 0” (existence statements): failure of the given witness (illegitimate, a different observation, or values not different) requires only a replacement witness; to refute the existence, one must prove that at that observed value (for global 0, at every observed value under that configuration) there is no legitimate preimage with a different value. Finding a legitimate preimage outside the fiber does not breach a proved per-observed-value 0; a single identifiable observed value does not breach global 0.
