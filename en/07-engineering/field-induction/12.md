# The Adjudication Form of Field-Induction Action Modes

## Section 1  Properties and Observation-Set Declaration

### 1.1  Question

Consider a stretch of process that falls on a concretized field. From graded observations, can we adjudicate which of its landing points are direct writes, gating, or input, and which are the field's own running? Does the goal enter its landing points through an actual route? Which facts suffice to exclude its being an act of Field Induction, a host adjustment, or a tuning? To what degree can each of the following be adjudicated: the actor's declared attribution, the order of the writes, whether a write is read in by the field after it is made, and whether gating is controlled by a maintenance process? Where observation cannot see, where must adjudication stop and remain undetermined?

### 1.2  Adjudication Instance

Adjudication instance χ = (F, a, W, W entry phase, RW, Π, attribution table, Φ, 𝓘, 𝔐, V). F is the induced field; a is the act (the part tag of events); W is the time window; the events of a within W are the events whose part is a and whose phase lies within W, and events before W appear only through their sources (the windowing of Definition 3.9 of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction* v1.0); the W-entry snapshot is the snapshot at the W entry phase; RW is the read-in window, W by default; Π is an optional event filter (kind set, layer set); the attribution table declares the attribution of each actor with respect to F; Φ and 𝓘 are the reading and its input class; 𝔐 is the declared model (Definition 3.1); V is the observation access level.  
The forward relation is M\_Ind of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction* v1.0: the event contract (Definition 3.2), phases and snapshots (3.3), actual change (3.4), three-valued dependence (3.5), sources and control edges (3.6), versions and version sources (3.7), goal declarations and registrations (3.8), the formal predicates P1–P7 (3.9); the trusted premises (5.2); read-back (7.1, 7.2).

### 1.3  Observation Set

The observation function obs(r, V) gives only the fields visible at the access level concerned:  
\| Level \| Visible fields \|  
\| V0 \| the values of the θ, m, r layers in the phase-by-phase snapshots; the layer table; the reading Φ; the identifier of the induced field \|  
\| V1 \| V0, plus each event's identifier, phase, kind, src, part, read set, and write set \|  
\| V2 \| V1, plus each event's ctrl, the pre-write current versions of the ctrl variables, the current versions read in, and the dependence value of each output on each read-in; the phase-by-phase source map and version sources (source identifiers coincide with event identifiers and holding-anchor identifiers) \|  
\| V3 \| V2, plus goal declarations, target-pointing registrations, and goal-source registrations \|  
Premises common to all levels: observation covers the whole of W; the rule-component table (F\_M, X\_ext) is declared, with the set of the field's own components in F\_M declared as an explicit membership relation; Φ, 𝓘, and the substitute-value sequences are known. From V1 on, event labels are trusted records (H12-2); trusted labels do not supply values beyond observation. Snapshots do not contain the ext layer: the pre-write and post-write values of out-of-boundary variables lie outside observation, and every verdict that depends on actual change is evaluated only for landing points on the θ, m, r layers. A field's own event is an event whose kind is Sed or Run and whose src belongs to the declared set of the field's own components; it is identified by the declared membership relation, not by the name of the component or of the part, and if the same component is renamed with any legitimate name, the verdict is unchanged.  
In this observation protocol, the sub-phases whose phase has first component 0 form the run-entry segment.

### 1.4  Properties

\| Property \| Definition \|  
\| L2 implementation label \| the kind of each landing point of a within W that makes an actual change on a visible layer (θ, m, r): Rw (direct write), Gt (gating), In (input), Acq (acquisition); same-value writes on visible layers are labeled separately; landing points that write ext are reported as “out-of-boundary landing point, outside observation,” with no judgment of actual change or equal value; the Sed and Run of the field's own events in the same period are listed separately \|  
\| P6 formal value \| GoalCond^f(a; W, Π) (Definition 3.9, P6). If no kind set is supplied, there is no filtering; if no layer set is supplied, the default {θ, m, r} is taken; if it is declared empty, no event is selected \|  
\| Target-pointing registration query \| Target^f(·) (Definitions 3.8, 3.9): a query on the registration table that takes an explicit goal variable and an induced-field identifier; it is not a run \|  
\| L1a structural item of the goal condition \| read back from P6 per Section 7.1 \|  
\| L1b excludable side of event qualification \| within legitimate runs, Field Induction is excluded by two items, starting organization (installation at the W entry) and net tendency difference; an inconsistency in the target-pointing registration is reported separately at the registration layer and does not enter run verdicts \|  
\| L1b′, L1b″ excludable side of host-adjustment and tuning qualification \| the excludable 0 of Rewrite and Gate \|  
\| L3a boundary \| the actor's declared attribution with respect to F \|  
\| L3b order \| whether each actual change of a is an entry acquisition, in transit within the window, or outside the window \|  
\| L3c direct read-in after write \| a version written by a within W is read, within RW and strictly after it, by a field's own event as the current version and enters the output of some actual change with dependence 1 \|  
\| L4 hijacking sign \| a gating event of a within W with an actual change, whose ctrl variable has a pre-write current version generated by an event of a maintenance process \|  
Each property is adjudicated separately; the properties are not combined into one overall verdict, do not vote against one another, and do not fill in for one another. The affirmative direction of event qualification (that a is an act of Field Induction, a host adjustment, or a tuning) is unproved in *The General Form of Field Induction* (the converse of the master formula), and the criteria reach only its excludable side. Intrusion and actual hijacking have no closed definition upstream: L3 adjudicates only recordable facts, and L4 only signs.

## Section 2  Construction of the Criteria

Each criterion is a function of (χ, obs(r, V)); the same observation gives the same verdict. Verdict values are 1, 0, ⊥, or a label set; ⊥ carries a reason: upstream undetermined, or insufficient evidence. An invalid domain (a model, declaration, or event not conforming to the contract) is listed separately and is not a verdict value; when the projection a criterion requests lies outside observation, the criterion reports “undeclared projection,” which is not a verdict value either.

J-L2 (V1): for each event of a within W, compare the before and after snapshots for the variables in its write set that belong to the θ, m, r layers; those whose values change are actual changes and are labeled with the event's kind; when the write set lies entirely in visible layers and nothing changes, the event is labeled an same-value write; variables in the write set that belong to the ext layer are reported as out-of-boundary landing points, with no equal-value inference (when the same event writes both r and ext, only the change in r is affirmed). Actual changes on visible layers by the field's own events in the same period are listed separately.  
J-P6 (V3; at V2 restricted to the holding-anchor subdomain): take the actual changes of a within W that pass the filter Π, and check whether the source map of the written variable after the write contains a registered goal source marked as determined. V3: a determined hit gives 1; hits only on undetermined entries give ⊥ (upstream undetermined); no goal-source registration gives ⊥ (upstream undetermined); otherwise 0. V2: goal sources are recognizable only by holding-anchor identifiers (a holding anchor presupposes a goal declaration); a determined hit on a holding anchor gives 1, otherwise ⊥ (insufficient evidence). When the layer set contains a layer outside {θ, m, r}, report an undeclared projection.  
J-Target (registration table): an unregistered goal variable gives ⊥; otherwise the query gives 1 if the registered induced field is F, and 0 if not. A 0 is an admission exclusion at the registration layer: the domain of the corresponding run with a goal declaration is invalid, and the run does not enter run verdicts.  
J-L1a: J-P6 = 1 ⇒ 1; conditional rule: at V3, with H12-1 declared and no goal-source registration at all ⇒ 0; otherwise ⊥ (P6 = 0 is reported only as a formal value).  
J-L1b (V0): the installed θ in the W-entry snapshot is empty (H12-4) ⇒ 0; Φ(end of W) − Φ(entry of W) = 0 ⇒ 0; otherwise ⊥.  
J-L1b′, J-L1b″ (V1): a has no Rw (respectively Gt) event within W with an actual change ⇒ 0; otherwise ⊥.  
J-L3a (V1): take the declared value in the attribution table.  
J-L3b (V1): an Acq actual change of a lying in the run-entry segment is an entry acquisition; one whose phase lies within W is in transit within the window; the rest are outside the window.  
J-L3c (V2): for each event of a within W that actually changes x on a visible layer, take the unique version it generates; among the field's own events within RW and strictly after it (identified by the F\_M components), find those whose current version read in is that version; if some output with an actual change on a visible layer has dependence 1 on x ⇒ 1; otherwise, if some dependence is undetermined ⇒ ⊥; otherwise ⇒ 0. Writes of a before W do not count as events of a within W and appear only through their sources; L3b reads the records of the entry segment and of outside the window separately.  
J-L4 (V2): if, among the Gt events of a within W with an actual change, one has a ctrl variable whose pre-write current version was generated by an event of a maintenance process ⇒ 1; otherwise 0. Equal-value gating is listed separately and does not enter.

## Section 3  Validity of the Criteria

Theorem 1 (J-L2)  Under V1 and H12-2, the label set given by J-L2 equals the set of kinds of the actual landing points of a within W on the θ, m, r layers, and likewise for the separate listings of same-value writes on visible layers and of the field's own events; landing points on the ext layer are reported as outside observation and fall outside this equality.  
Proof: Actual change on visible layers is defined by the before and after snapshots (3.4); the contract constrains the layer each kind writes (Rw, Sed, Acq write θ; Gt writes m; In and Run write r or ext), and H12-2 guarantees that labels agree with the actual routes and that the declaration is complete; the field's own events are identified by the F\_M components referenced by src (3.2). The pre-write and post-write values on the ext layer lie outside observation: with mt = 0 at entry, the same In event writing mt := 0 or writing mt := 1 has the same V1 observation, while the actual change is empty in one case and {mt} in the other, so no judgment is made. ∎

Theorem 2 (J-P6 and J-L1a)  (i) At V3, J-P6 equals GoalCond^f(a; W, Π) (with the layer set of Π a subset of {θ, m, r} or the default). (ii) At V2, J-P6 = 1 ⇒ GoalCond^f = 1. (iii) Under B1–B4, J-L1a = 1 is read back as the structural item of the goal condition; under H12-1, J-L1a = 0 is read back as the goal condition failing.  
Proof: (i) Evaluate term by term per Definition 3.9, P6; the required fields lie within V3. (ii) A holding anchor can be introduced only by a goal declaration, and its identifier is recognizable in the V2 source map; a hit on it is a hit of P6. (iii) A 1 of P6 is read back per 7.1; H12-1 declares that goal declarations and goal-source registrations are exhaustive and cover the goal sources of the act under adjudication, so no registration means no goal source. ∎

Theorem 3 (Excludable side)  J-L1b = 0 ⇒ a is not an act of Field Induction (within the working domain); J-L1b′ = 0 ⇒ a is not a host adjustment; J-L1b″ = 0 ⇒ a is not a tuning.  
Proof: Starting organization and net tendency difference are necessary items of Field Induction (the master formula of *The General Form of Field Induction*), and their formal value 0 is excludable under H12-4 and B2 (7.1); Rewrite and Gate are necessary items of host adjustment and of tuning respectively (*The General Form of Host Adjustment*, *The General Form of Tuning*), and their 0 is excludable when actual landing points are expressible and the registrations are exhaustive (7.1, H12-2). ∎  
When the target-pointing registration query gives 0, the domain of the corresponding run with a goal declaration is invalid; this is an admission exclusion at the registration layer, not L1b within a run.  
Rewrite and Gate both being 0 does not exclude Field Induction: whether there are Field Induction acts other than these two is unanswered upstream.

Theorem 4 (L3, L4)  Under H12-2 and H12-3: J-L3a equals the declared attribution; J-L3b is valid in both directions; the 1 and the 0 of J-L3c are valid in both directions, and ⊥ arises only from undetermined dependence; J-L4 is valid in both directions (at the sign level).  
Proof: L3b is read off from phase and kind. L3c matches readers by unique version identifier and strict precedence; readers are events whose src references a field's own component of F\_M, and dependence values are taken from Definition 3.5; undetermined dependence is not counted as a negative. L4 traces control versions edge by edge: ctrl is a trusted control edge (3.6(d), 5.2). ∎  
Example: a writes w := 2, and a field's own component (its part recorded as local) then reads this version and writes r as w: L3c = 1; if the same component is renamed with any legitimate name and is still declared a field's own component, L3c is still 1, and L2 still lists it separately; if an out-of-boundary Run component not declared as a field's own component reads w, it is not a field's own event, and L3c = 0.  
A 0 on RW presupposes that observation covers RW. L4 = 0 says only that this sign is absent; it does not imply that there is no hijacking; maintenance participation through value-write chains rather than control edges is not within L4.

## Section 4  Bounds of Misjudgment

Theorem 5 (Ambiguity at insufficient levels)  Each of the following pairs consists of legitimate runs whose observations are equal under the same observation protocol and the same access level while the values of the adjudicated property differ. Common setup for all pairs: the declared model of the induced field F contains θ variables w, u, with m supported on w, an r-layer variable r, and out-of-boundary variables g, mt; the reading is Φ = w·m + u; at entry w = 1, u = 0, m = 1, r = 0, mt = 0; the substitute sequence of each rational-valued variable is {k/4 : −12 ≤ k ≤ 12}; W is the whole run (after the entry segment); the landing points of the act a pass through the direct-write port.  
(a) L2, V0: in one run a directly writes w := 2, in the other a field's own sedimentation component writes w := 2—the phase-by-phase snapshots are identical; L2(a) is {Rw} and empty.  
(b) P6, V1: g = 2 is held by Xg (consistent with the goal declaration); a writes w, with read set {w, g} in both runs, the function being w + 1 in one and w + g − 1 in the other, and the written value 2 in both—the event records are identical; at V3, P6 is 0 and 1.  
(c) P6, V2: at entry g = 2, with no holding anchor; an event G, with the goal-setting component and part Xg, writes g := 3, after which a writes w := g according to g; one run has a goal declaration and registration (G registered as the goal event), the other has no goal declaration—the V2 observations are identical and the V3 ones differ; P6 is 1 and ⊥.  
(d) L3c, V1: after a writes w := 2, a field's own run component writes r, with read set {w} in both runs, the function being w − 1 in one and the constant 1 in the other—the event records are identical; at V2, L3c is 1 and 0.  
(e) L4, V1: after a maintenance process writes mt := 1 through an out-of-boundary component, the gating of part I writes m := 0; one run declares ctrl = {mt}, the other does not—the event records are identical; at V2, L4 is 1 and 0.  
Proof: Compare, pair by pair, the fields given by the observation function: (a) differs only in the event records; (b) and (d) differ only in dependence values and source maps (V2 fields); (c) differs only in goal declarations and registrations (V3 fields); (e) differs only in ctrl and control versions (V2 fields). Each value is obtained from the corresponding criterion and Definitions 3.5, 3.6, 3.9. ∎  
Hence, at the access levels shown, no criterion can give a correct and determinate verdict for every observation on the declared domain; criteria must give ⊥ for these ambiguous observations. The necessity of ⊥ is limited to the corresponding ambiguous observations and adjudication targets; it does not imply that every observation at that level must be ⊥. (c) proves only that the three values of P6 cannot be fully recovered at V2 (1 and ⊥ cannot be separated); it does not prove that, under the same observation, the goal condition is semantically true in one run and false in the other.

Table of bounds of misjudgment (within the adjudication domain):  
\| Property \| V0 \| V1 \| V2 \| V3 \|  
\| L2 \| ⊥ \| valid in both directions \| as V1 \| as V1 \|  
\| P6 \| ⊥ \| ⊥ \| a holding-anchor hit issues 1; otherwise ⊥ \| formal value \|  
\| L1a \| ⊥ \| ⊥ \| holding-anchor hit 1; otherwise ⊥ \| 1 read back; 0 only under H12-1; otherwise ⊥ \|  
\| L1b \| 0 reliable; otherwise ⊥ \| as V0 \| as V0 \| as V0 \|  
\| L1b′, L1b″ \| ⊥ \| 0 reliable; otherwise ⊥ \| as V1 \| as V1 \|  
\| L3a \| ⊥ \| declared value \| as V1 \| as V1 \|  
\| L3b \| ⊥ \| valid in both directions \| as V1 \| as V1 \|  
\| L3c \| ⊥ \| ⊥ \| valid in both directions; undetermined dependence ⊥ \| as V2 \|  
\| L4 \| ⊥ \| ⊥ \| valid in both directions (sign level) \| as V2 \|  
The target-pointing registration query is not among the run verdicts: its 0 makes the domain of the corresponding run with a goal declaration invalid. Undeclared projections (such as a layer set containing ext) and landing points on the ext layer are reported as outside the adjudication domain; no formal value or actual change is issued for them.

## Section 5  Adjudication Theorem

Theorem 6 (Adjudication Theorem)  Within the adjudication domain (H12-2, H12-3, H12-4, bridging conditions B1–B4, observation covering W and RW):  
(i) each criterion is a function of (χ, obs(r, V));  
(ii) at the sufficient level of each property, L2, L3b, L3c (except for undetermined dependence), and L4 are valid in both directions; P6 at V3 equals the formal value; the 1 of L1a is read back, and its 0 is issued only under H12-1; L1b, L1b′, L1b″ issue only the excludable 0;  
(iii) at the insufficient levels listed in Theorem 5, criteria must give ⊥ for the ambiguous observations there;  
(iv) the following implications do not hold: L2 containing Rw does not imply L1b ≠ ⊥; L1b = 0 does not imply that L2 does not contain Rw; P6 = 1 does not imply L1b ≠ ⊥.  
Proof: (i) By the construction of Section 2, each criterion reads only the observation fields of the level it requires; (ii) by Theorems 1–4; (iii) by Theorem 5; (iv) by the examples below. ∎  
Example 1: with g = 2 held, a writes w := w + g − 1 (Φ = w·m + u): L2 = {Rw}, P6 = 1, ΔΦ\_W = 1, L1b = ⊥.  
Example 2: a writes u := 1, and another act then writes u := 0: ΔΦ\_W = 0, L1b = 0, while L2 of a contains Rw.  
Example 3 (gating only): a writes only m := 0, by gating: L1b′ = 0, L1b″ = ⊥.  
Example 4 (across windows): a writes u at beat 1, and the field itself writes r at beat 2; taking W as beat 2, the write of a does not enter L2.  
Example 5 (the read-in window of L3c): at beat 1, a writes w := 2; at beat 2, the field itself first reads this version, and then a writes w ← w + 1. Taking W as beat 2: no one reads in the new version within the window, and L3c = 0. Taking W as beat 1 and RW = W: the reader lies outside the window, and L3c = 0; declaring RW as beats 1–2: L3c = 1.

## Section 6  Interfaces

Input: *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction* v1.0 (Definitions 3.1–3.9, 5.2, 7.1, 7.2); the necessary items of *The General Form of Field Induction*, *The General Form of Host Adjustment*, and *The General Form of Tuning* v1.0, and their requirements “To the Adjudication Forms” (cover the necessary items; for a compound act, first mark each part by actual landing point and route, then check qualification part by part).

Output: the adjudication instance and the observation function; the criteria for each property, their validity in both directions, and their bounds of misjudgment; the Adjudication Theorem.  
— To *The Adjudication Form of Host-Adjustment Equilibrium*: landing points for which L2 contains Rw are its candidate landing points; to enter that Form's adjudication domain, the error-driven protocol, periodic sampling, and observation admission must be checked separately.  
— To the Discrimination Forms: the verdicts of L2 and L3 can serve as inputs to discriminating observations.  
— To *The Construction Form of Tuning in Spiking Networks*: J-L2 provides the verification of gating landing points.  
— To upstream Forms: closed definitions of intrusion and actual hijacking, and the affirmative direction of event qualification, are registered as interfaces.

H12-1  Goal declarations and goal-source registrations are exhaustive and cover the goal sources of the act under adjudication (used only for the negative of L1a).  
H12-2  Trusted premises (*The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*, 5.2): kind, src, and ctrl are true, the declaration is complete, and actual landing points are expressible in this grammar.  
H12-3  The attribution table of actors with respect to F is declared.  
H12-4  The representation of installation is complete.

Unanswered: the affirmative direction of event qualification; indirect read-in through history; maintenance participation through value-write chains; an intrusion criterion; independent verification at V1 when labels are candidates pending verification.
