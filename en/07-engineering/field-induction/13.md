# The Adjudication Form of Host-Adjustment Equilibrium

## Section 1  Properties and Observation-Set Declaration

### 1.1  Question

*The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence* v1.0 defined host-adjustment equilibrium: the regulated field realizes the target relation on the declared inputs (alignment) and, as execution continues under the protocol, makes no further write-back (protocol stop). It proved that under certain conditions the limit state of training is a host-adjustment equilibrium, but it did not answer this: at a given moment of training, can observation adjudicate whether the current state is already a host-adjustment equilibrium? None of zero loss, a single step without write-back, or an optimizer that no longer moves is equivalent to host-adjustment equilibrium; what observations, then, do alignment and stopping each require?

1.2  Forward Relation and the Adjudicated Object

The forward relation is Section 4 of M\_EDR in *The Mathematical Form of Error-Driven Host Adjustment* v1.0 (sampling, forward pass, labels, loss, adjoint, gradient, momentum, write-back, monitoring); the implementation class is the computation graphs valid under M\_EDR. The stop of M\_EDR is only a monitoring flag (Definition 3.9), not a training-stop interlock; an external or separately declared training-stop interlock is not part of the adjudicated object.  
The adjudicated object is frozen item by item: the time point t*; the state (θ*, v\*, phase); the protocol thereafter—the periodic sampling schedule (period P, one period covering the dataset D), the learning rate η, the momentum coefficient β, and the goal value g; every input in D is nonzero. What is frozen is the adjudicated object; what the observer can see is listed separately under each of the protocols below.

### 1.3  Properties

(e) Alignment: on every input of D, the output of θ\* equals g·x.  
(c) Protocol stop: from t\* on, as execution continues under the protocol, the write-back amount at every step is zero.  
Bal (host-adjustment equilibrium): (e) ∧ (c) (*The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence*, Definition 2.2).  
(d) Reaching the target: ℓ ≤ ε at some step, with the declared current-step loss ℓ and reading target ε; it is listed separately and takes no part in the three above.

### 1.4  Observation Set

Passive observation: observation within a window of actual execution under the protocol from t\* on; it does not alter the run and follows the original trajectory, so the parameters at the different steps are in general different. The protocols follow *The Observational Inversion Form of Host Adjustment* v1.0: O1 is the output y\_k and the pre-write-back loss ℓ\_k at each step; O2-upd is O1 plus the write-back amount η·v\_k at each step; O2-grad is O1 plus the gradient q\_k at each step. Two projections are also recorded: the pure write-back projection π\_u (containing only the write-back amount at each step) and the pure gradient projection π\_q (containing only the gradient at each step). The minimal fields and window length each criterion requires are declared item by item (Section 2).  
Known quantities: the sampling schedule and the phase at t\* (and hence each step's input x\_k), η, and β are declared per criterion; whether g is known is also declared item by item.  
Active probe A: on the frozen θ\*, make one mathematical read-out (forward output or residual) on each input of D; it does not write back and does not alter θ, v, or the phase; it is not O1–O2.  
Observations are exact rational values.

## Section 2  Construction of the Criteria

Let P denote the sampling period.  
\| Criterion \| Minimal fields \| Window length \| Known quantities \| Adjudication \|  
\| J\_bal \| ℓ\_k, write-back amounts (O2-upd) \| P \| — \| ℓ\_k = 0 and zero write-back amount at every step in the window ⇒ 1; otherwise ⇒ 0 \|  
\| J\_c \| write-back amounts (π\_u) \| P + 1 \| — \| zero write-back amount at every step in the window ⇒ 1; otherwise ⇒ 0 \|  
\| J\_c′ \| write-back amounts, gradients (π\_u + π\_q) \| P \| η \| a nonzero write-back in the window ⇒ 0; when all are zero, η = 0 ⇒ 1; when η \> 0, zero gradient at step 1 ⇒ 1, otherwise ⇒ 0 \|  
\| J\_c^β0 \| write-back amounts (π\_u) \| P \| β = 0 \| a nonzero write-back in the window ⇒ 0; all zero ⇒ 1 \|  
\| J\_e \| active probe A \| — \| g \| residuals at θ\* on all inputs of D zero ⇒ 1; otherwise ⇒ 0 \|  
\| J\_e′ \| ℓ\_k, write-back amounts (O2-upd) \| P \| — \| if at some step k all write-backs before that step (before the sampled step) are zero while ℓ\_k ≠ 0 ⇒ 0; losses and write-backs all zero in the window ⇒ 1; otherwise ⇒ ⊥ \|  
\| J\_e^lin \| y\_k, ℓ\_k, write-back amounts (O2-upd) \| P \| parameter-linear graph y = θ·φ(x), φ, each step's x\_k \| ℓ₁ ≠ 0 ⇒ 0; when ℓ₁ = 0, take g = y₁/x₁ (if g is known, use it directly); the output of θ\* on the input of step k = y\_k + (Σ\_{j\<k} write-back\_j)·φ(x\_k); all equal to g·x\_k ⇒ 1, otherwise ⇒ 0 \|

## Section 3  Validity of the Criteria

Lemma 1 (Discrimination of stopping)  For computation graphs valid under M\_EDR: (c) ⇔ η = 0 ∨ (β·v\* = 0 ∧ ∀x ∈ D: q(θ\*, x) = 0).  
Proof: ⇐: When η = 0 the write-back is identically zero; otherwise β·v\* = 0 and the gradient at θ\* is zero on every input, so at step 1 v = β·v\* + q = 0, the write-back is zero and θ is unchanged, and by induction it is zero at every step thereafter. ⇒: Suppose η \> 0 and (c). A zero write-back at step 1 gives v₁ = β·v\* + q(θ*, x₁) = 0, with θ unchanged; thereafter a zero write-back at step k gives v\_k = β·0 + q(θ*, x\_k) = 0, so the gradient at θ\* is zero on every input that occurs from step 2 on; periodic coverage extends this to all of D (including x₁), whence q(θ*, x₁) = 0 and β·v* = 0. ∎

Lemma 2 (Equivalent form of equilibrium)  Bal ⇔ (e) ∧ η·β·v\* = 0.  
Proof: (e) makes every residual at θ\* zero, and by the adjoint recursion every gradient is zero; substituting into Lemma 1, (c) ⇔ η = 0 ∨ β·v\* = 0. ∎

Theorem 1 (J\_bal)  For computation graphs valid under M\_EDR, J\_bal = 1 ⇔ Bal.  
Proof: ⇒: All write-backs are zero, so θ stays at θ\* throughout the window; ℓ = 0 at each step means that the residual of θ\* on that step's input is zero, and the window length P covers D, giving (e). The zero residual at step 1 makes q₁ = 0, so the write-back amount is η·(β·v\* + 0) = 0, that is, η·β·v\* = 0; Lemma 2 gives Bal. ⇐: Under Bal the residuals, gradients, and write-backs at every step are all zero. ∎

Theorem 2 (J\_c, J\_c′, J\_c^β0)  For computation graphs valid under M\_EDR: (i) J\_c = 1 ⇔ (c); (ii) when η is known, J\_c′ = 1 ⇔ (c); (iii) when β = 0 is known, J\_c^β0 = 1 ⇔ (c).  
Proof: (i) ⇐ is trivial. ⇒: When η = 0, (c) holds. When η \> 0, a zero write-back at step 1 gives v₁ = 0 with θ unchanged; zero write-backs at steps 2 through P + 1 give zero gradients at θ\* on these P consecutive inputs (which cover D); Lemma 1 gives (c). (ii) When there is a nonzero write-back in the window, (c) fails, and the verdict 0 is correct. When η = 0, (c) holds, and the verdict 1 is correct. When all write-backs in the window are zero and η \> 0, θ stays at θ*, at step 1 v₁ = β·v* + q₁ = 0, and steps 2 through P give zero gradients on the remaining inputs; hence (c) ⇔ q₁ = 0 ⇔ β·v\* = 0 (Lemma 1).  
(iii) When β = 0 is known, J\_c^β0 = 1 ⇔ (c): when there is a nonzero write-back in the window, (c) fails; when all are zero and η = 0, (c) holds; when all are zero and η \> 0, β = 0 gives v = q, so zero write-back means that the gradient is zero on every input of this period, θ stays at θ*, and Lemma 1 (β·v* = 0) gives (c). ∎

Theorem 3 (Alignment criteria)  (i) J\_e = 1 ⇔ (e). (ii) Both the 1 and the 0 of J\_e′ are valid. (iii) On parameter-linear graphs, J\_e^lin = 1 ⇔ (e).  
Proof: (i) By definition. (ii) Before the step that gives 0, all write-backs are zero, so θ is still θ*, and ℓ\_k ≠ 0 means that the residual of θ* on x\_k is nonzero; when the verdict is 1, θ is θ\* throughout the window and the residual on every input is zero. (iii) ℓ₁ ≠ 0 means that the residual of θ\* on x₁ is nonzero, so there is no alignment; when ℓ₁ = 0, y₁ = g·x₁, and x₁ ≠ 0 gives g. The parameter at step k is θ\* − Σ\_{j\<k} write-back\_j, and parameter linearity gives θ\*·φ(x\_k) = y\_k + (Σ\_{j\<k} write-back\_j)·φ(x\_k); the window length P covers D. ∎

Examples (checks):  
\- Zero loss without alignment: y = w₁x + w₂x², g = 0, x cycling through 1, 2, η = 1, β = 1/2, entry θ = (1, −1), v = (−2, 0). The loss is 0 at both steps, and the write-back amounts are (−1, 0), (−1/2, 0); the output of θ\* at x = 2 is −2, so there is no alignment. J\_bal = 0, J\_e′ = ⊥, J\_e^lin = 0.  
\- Reaching the target does not adjudicate alignment: the same graph, θ = (3/2, 0), g = 2, η = 0, ε = 1/2: at the two steps ℓ = 1/8, 1/2, both ≤ ε, yet there is no alignment; J\_bal = 0.  
\- An optimizer that does not move does not adjudicate equilibrium: when η = 0, (c) always holds, and Bal depends on (e).

## Section 4  Bounds of Misjudgment

Theorem 4 (Indistinguishability under the pure write-back projection)  On the general declared domain (η and β not restricted as known quantities), using only the pure write-back projection π\_u with window P, there exist two states whose write-back amounts in the window are the same while the truth values of (c) differ. Hence, under this projection and window length, no criterion can adjudicate (c) completely everywhere on the declared domain; a conservative criterion may give 0 when there is a nonzero write-back, and ⊥ when the write-backs are all zero and the state lies in the same fiber as the witness.  
Proof: y = w₁x + w₂x², g = 0, η = 1, β = 1/2, P = 2 (first x = 1, then x = 2). State one, θ = (−1, 1/2), v = (1, 1): at step 1 the gradient (−1/2, −1/2) cancels β·v, and the write-back is zero; at step 2 the residual at x = 2 is zero, and the write-back is zero; at step 3, back at x = 1, the write-back is nonzero. State two, θ = (0, 0), v = (0, 0): an equilibrium, with write-back identically zero. The pure write-back projections of both within window P are zero. ∎  
This indistinguishability belongs only to π\_u: under the full O2-upd (which includes O1), the first-step (y, ℓ) of the two states are (−1/2, 1/8) and (0, 0), which separate them. Known quantities change the conclusion: when η = 0 is known, (c) always holds; when β = 0 is known, J\_c^β0 is complete on window P (Theorem 2(iii)). Momentum cancellation with single-sample periods (P = 1) is of the same type: y = w·x, x = 1, g = 0, η = 1, β = 1/2, w₀ = 1, v₀ = −2: the write-back is zero in the first period and nonzero in the next.

Bounds of Misjudgment: J\_bal, J\_c, J\_c′, J\_e, and J\_e^lin have zero misjudgment within the declared domain (M\_EDR-valid graphs, exact observation, frozen protocol, periodic sampling covering D). The ⊥ of J\_e′ occurs only for states with a nonzero write-back in the window; whether this ⊥ region is necessary on non-parameter-linear graphs is unproved—the active probe J\_e gives a complete criterion. Outside the domain (sampling that is not periodic or does not cover D, noisy observation, a protocol that changes within the window, a separately declared training-stop interlock), the verdict is ⊥.

## Section 5  Adjudication Theorem

Theorem 5 (Adjudication Theorem)  Within the declared domain:  
(i) J\_bal is valid in both directions for Bal (Theorem 1) and is a direct, complete criterion of host-adjustment equilibrium;  
(ii) J\_c is valid in both directions for (c), as are J\_c′ when η is known and J\_c^β0 when β = 0 is known; on the general declared domain, with the pure write-back projection and window P, there are indistinguishable pairs (Theorems 2, 4);  
(iii) J\_e is valid in both directions for (e), as is the passive J\_e^lin on parameter-linear graphs; the 1 and the 0 of J\_e′ are valid (Theorem 3);  
(iv) the strong Kleene conjunction J\_e ∧ J\_c of the component verdicts is a conservative criterion for Bal: the component verdicts are reliable, and the affirmative and negative verdicts of the conjunction are reliable; joint indistinguishability is not inferred from ⊥ in the two components.  
(v) Reaching the target (d), a single zero update, the monitoring flag, the optimizer stopping: none of these is a criterion for Bal.

## Section 6  Interfaces

Input: *The Mathematical Form of Error-Driven Host Adjustment* v1.0 (Section 4, Definition 3.9); *The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence* v1.0 (the distinctions of 2.1 and Definition 2.2; Propositions 1–3); *The Observational Inversion Form of Host Adjustment* v1.0 (observation protocols O1, O2).

Output: Lemmas 1 and 2; the criteria J\_bal, J\_c, J\_c′, J\_e, J\_e′, J\_e^lin and their validity; the bounds of misjudgment; the Adjudication Theorem.  
— To *The Limit Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence*: J\_bal(t*) = 0 excludes only the adjudicated time t*; to read off that equilibrium is reached nowhere within some finite interval, each state in the interval must be adjudicated, or a separate interval proof is needed. Example: y = w·x, x = 1, g = 0, w = 1, η = 1, β = 0: not in equilibrium at entry (write-back amount 1), and in equilibrium after one update (write-backs and losses zero thereafter).  
— To *The Reachability Form of Maintenance Law Engineering*: when J\_bal serves as an acceptance criterion for reachability, it is read back only within the declared domain and observation coverage.  
— To *The Adjudication Form of Field-Induction Action Modes*: landing points whose implementation label is direct write are only candidates; to enter the adjudication domain of this Form, the M\_EDR protocol, periodic sampling, and observation admission must be checked separately.

Unanswered: whether the ⊥ of passive alignment criteria on non-parameter-linear graphs is necessary; noisy observation and alignment within a tolerance; non-periodic sampling.
