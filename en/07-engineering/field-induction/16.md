# The Limit Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence

## Section 1  Basic Setting

### 1.1  Question

*The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence* v1.0 proves in the first-proof subclass 𝒦₀ that, when the model is linear in the parameters, there is no momentum, the sampling covers D periodically, the target is realizable, and the step size is suitable, error-driven host adjustment converges to host-adjustment equilibrium; that in the single-mode case, arrival in finitely many steps occurs if and only if the product of the step size and the squared feature norm is 1 or the initial error is zero on the adjustable subspace; and that when the target is unrealizable, only cycle sections are obtained. It does not answer what else convergence necessarily exhibits as the number of steps tends to infinity, as the step size and the momentum coefficient tend to the boundary, and when the target is unrealizable—when arrival occurs in finite time, under which parameters convergence occurs, how it degenerates beyond the boundary, and where the limit lies when the target cannot be reached.

1.2  Implementation Class and Frozen Items

Implementation class: the parameter-linear computation graphs in M\_EDR of *The Mathematical Form of Error-Driven Host Adjustment* v1.0, y = θ·φ(x) (K1); a sampling table with period P whose within-period order x\_(1), …, x\_(P) covers the dataset D (K3); φ(x) ≠ 0 (K6). Frozen items: P and the within-period order, the starting phase, the step size η \> 0, the momentum coefficient β ∈ \[0, 1), the initial parameters θ₀, the initial momentum v₀, and the target g.  
Notation: V = span{φ(x) : x ∈ D}; for β = 0, the parameter-error map of step j is A\_j = I − η φ\_j φ\_jᵀ (the identity on V⊥, and it leaves V invariant); prefixes B\_0 = I, B\_k = A\_(k) ⋯ A\_(1); the one-cycle map M = B\_P; H = Σ\_j φ\_j φ\_jᵀ (counted with repetition within the period). When the target is realizable (K4), take θ*\_V ∈ V to be the interpolation point; the error is e = θ − (P\_{V⊥}θ₀ + θ*\_V), and alignment ⇔ P\_V e = 0. Branches: for β = 0 with a realizable target, the homogeneous parameter map above is used; for β = 0 with an unrealizable target, the affine cycle map of the parameters θ ↦ Mθ + c is used (Proposition 5); for β \> 0, the extended state (θ, v) is used, and the one-cycle map is the product of the one-step maps of the phases (a power of a single one-step map only when P = 1); the convergence domain with momentum proved in this Form covers only the single-mode case (Proposition 4).

### 1.3  Limit Directions and Their Order

(A) With all frozen items fixed, the number of steps k → ∞;  
(B) the step size η and the momentum coefficient β tend to the boundary of the convergence domain—as a characterization of how the conclusions of (A) vary with the parameters, not as a joint limit in the parameters and the number of steps;  
(C) for β = 0 with an unrealizable target, first k → ∞ to take the long-time cycle sections, then η → 0⁺; the joint limit is not signed.

### 1.4  Three First Times

k\_e: the first alignment; k\_c: from that time on, continuing under the protocol, the write-back is zero at every step (protocol stop); k\_bal: the first time the state is a host-adjustment equilibrium—adjudicated by Lemma 2 of *The Adjudication Form of Host-Adjustment Equilibrium* v1.0 as “aligned and η·β·v = 0,” where v ranges over all write-back coordinates, including the V⊥ component.

## Section 2  Inevitability

In the parameter-linear implementation class, the gradient changes the parameters only along φ(x), and V⊥ is not acted on by the gradient. In each of the three cases the trajectory is determined by one linear or affine cycle map: for β = 0 with a realizable target, the parameter error is e\_{mP+j} = B\_j M^m e₀; for β = 0 with an unrealizable target, the cycle sections of the parameters are determined by the affine map θ ↦ Mθ + c; for β \> 0, the trajectory is determined by the one-cycle map of the extended state (θ, v) (the product of the one-step maps of the phases), and the momentum on V⊥ also decays by β—in this case the parameters by themselves have no cycle map independent of the initial momentum (example: constant feature, labels (1, 2), η = β = 1/2, θ₀ = 0; with initial momentum 0 and 1 the end-of-cycle parameters are 3/2 and 5/4 respectively). The properties of convergence are therefore determined by the spectrum and Jordan structure of the corresponding map, by the actual excitation of the initial state in each generalized eigendirection, and by the projection of the affine term; this cannot be avoided. On this basis, Sections 3 and 4 give, direction by direction, the properties that are necessarily exhibited.

## Section 3  Core Definitions and Structural Characterization

Definition 3.1  The stable subspace E\_s: the sum of the generalized eigenspaces of M\|\_V with \|λ\| \< 1. The actually excited spectral radius ρ\_ex: the largest \|λ\| among the generalized eigenspaces on which the initial error has a nonzero component.  
Definition 3.2  Convergence parameter domain: the set of (η, β) for which convergence holds from all initial states; convergence condition for a given initial state: the condition for convergence to an aligned point from that initial state. The two are written separately.

Proposition 1 (Stop and equilibrium at the same time)  Assume η \> 0, K1, K3, K6, and any β ∈ \[0, 1). For every state, protocol stop ⇔ host-adjustment equilibrium; hence k\_c = k\_bal, and k\_e ≤ k\_bal, possibly strictly.  
Proof: Lemma 1 of *The Adjudication Form of Host-Adjustment Equilibrium* v1.0: stop ⇔ η = 0 ∨ (β·v = 0 ∧ ∀x ∈ D: q(θ, x) = 0). Here η \> 0; under K1, q(θ, x) = (θ·φ(x) − g·x)·φ(x), which under K6 is zero if and only if the residual is zero; hence stop ⇔ aligned ∧ β·v = 0 ⇔ aligned ∧ η·β·v = 0. ∎  
Example: y = w·x, x = 1, g = 0, η = β = 1/2, (w₀, v₀) = (1, 2): (w, v) runs through (1, 2), (0, 2), (−1/2, 1), (−1/2, 0); alignment occurs at step 1, is left at step 2, and thereafter the state does not become a host-adjustment equilibrium in finitely many steps (Proposition 4(iii)).  
For β = 0 with a realizable target the three coincide: k\_e = k\_c = k\_bal (*The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence*, Theorem 2).

Proposition 2 (Finite arrival without momentum)  Assume β = 0, K1, K3, K4.  
(i) Alignment at step k ⇔ B\_k·e₀,V = 0; k\_e = min{k : B\_k e₀,V = 0}; the phase of first arrival is determined by the prefixes, and the null space of the full-cycle map alone does not suffice to determine it.  
(ii) The null space of the prefix restricted to V, B\_k\|\_V, is nondecreasing in k; the union of all prefix null spaces equals ker (M\|\_V)^d, d = dim V.  
(iii) det(M\|\_V) = Π\_j (1 − η‖φ\_j‖²), so M\|\_V is singular ⇔ η‖φ\_j‖² = 1 for some j. If η‖φ\_j‖² ≠ 1 for all j, finite-step arrival ⇔ already aligned at entry; otherwise the initial errors that arrive in finitely many steps are exactly the subspace ker (M\|\_V)^d, which is a proper subspace if and only if M\|\_V is not nilpotent; when M\|\_V is nilpotent, every initial error vanishes within at most d cycles.  
Proof: (i) e\_k = B\_k e₀. (ii) B\_{k+1} = A\_(k+1)B\_k, so ker B\_k\|\_V ⊆ ker B\_{k+1}\|\_V; within a cycle, ker B\_{mP+j} ⊆ ker B\_{(m+1)P} = ker M^{m+1}; once the null-space chain of the powers stops growing it never grows again, and it grows at most d times. (iii) A\_j\|\_V has eigenvalue 1 − η‖φ\_j‖² in the direction φ\_j and 1 elsewhere, so det(A\_j\|\_V) = 1 − η‖φ\_j‖². ∎  
Example (target g = 0, interpolation point θ\*\_V = 0): φ = e₁, 3e₂ (two inputs, a two-step period). With η = 1/9, step 2 annihilates the e₂ component: θ₀ = (0, 5) arrives at step 2, while θ₀ = (1, 5) never arrives. With η = 1/10 every prefix is invertible, and only states already aligned at entry arrive. φ = (1, 1), (1, −1), η = 1/2: M\|\_V = 0, and every initial state arrives within one cycle.  
“In general, arrival does not occur in finitely many steps” means exactly (iii): the exceptional initial states form a subspace, and this is not stated in terms of probability.

## Section 4  Main Results: Inside and Outside the Boundary, and Degeneration beyond It

Proposition 3 (Convergence boundary without momentum)  Assume β = 0, K1, K3, K4.  
(i) For a given initial state: θ\_k converges to an aligned point ⇔ e₀,V ∈ E\_s.  
(ii) For all initial states: the convergence parameter domain is {η \> 0 : ρ(M\_η\|\_V) \< 1}; K5 of *The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence* is a sufficient interval within it. This domain need not be an interval.  
(iii) Sections and the full sequence are written separately:  
　(a) If the phase-0 section error tends to zero, then every prefix e\_{mP+j} = B\_j e\_{mP} tends to zero, and the full sequence converges to an aligned point.  
　(b) If the phase-0 section converges to e\* ≠ 0 (the initial error has a nonzero semisimple component at λ = 1, and the rest lies in E\_s), the phase-j section converges to B\_j e*; the limits at the various phases cannot all coincide—if B\_j e* = e\* for every j, then φ\_j·e\* = 0 for every j, so e\* ∈ V⊥, contradicting e\* ∈ V, e\* ≠ 0. Hence the sections at each phase converge, the full sequence does not converge, and alignment never occurs.  
　(c) Other unit-modulus modes: if the component at λ = −1 is nonzero, the sections oscillate with period two in that mode; if the component in a semisimple mode with \|λ\| = 1, λ ≠ 1 is nonzero (M is a product of symmetric matrices and may have complex eigenvalues), the sections are bounded but do not converge in that mode; a nontrivial Jordan block on a unit-modulus mode grows polynomially according to its order. Mixed initial states superpose by mode.  
　(d) If a component with \|λ\| \> 1 is excited, the sequence diverges; if it is not excited, it has no effect.  
　(e) Rate: when ρ\_ex \> 0, ‖e\_{mP}‖ ≤ c·(1 + m)^{s−1}·ρ\_ex^m (m ≥ 0), where s is the largest order of the excited Jordan blocks with \|λ\| = ρ\_ex; when ρ\_ex = 0 (only zero eigenvalues excited), e\_{mP} is zero within at most s cycles, where s is the largest order of the excited zero-eigenvalue blocks; when the initial error is zero it stays zero. The worst-case rate over all initial states is c·(1 + m)^{s₀−1}·ρ(M\|\_V)^m when ρ(M\|\_V) \> 0, where s₀ is the largest order of the Jordan blocks with \|λ\| = ρ(M\|\_V); when ρ(M\|\_V) = 0, the nilpotent case applies.  
Proof: e\_{mP} = M^m e₀,V; each item follows from the Jordan decomposition of M\|\_V and e\_{mP+j} = B\_j e\_{mP}. (ii) Convergence from all initial states ⇔ M^m e → 0 for every e ⇔ ρ \< 1. ∎  
Non-interval example: y = w·x, D = {1, 3}, period (1, 3): the one-cycle factor is f(η) = (1 − η)(1 − 9η); \|f\| \< 1 ⇔ η ∈ (0, (5 − √7)/9) ∪ ((5 + √7)/9, 10/9). η = 1/10: f = 9/100, convergence; η = 1/2: f = −7/4, divergence; η = 1: f = 0, arrival at step 1; η = 10/9: f = 1, and within each cycle w alternates between 3 and 17/9—the phase-0 section error stays at 1, the phase-1 section error stays at −1/9, and the full sequence does not converge; η = 6/5: f = 49/25, divergence.  
Unexcited unstable component: φ = e₁, 3e₂, η = 1/2, factor −7/2 in the e₂ direction: θ₀ = (3, 0) converges, θ₀ = (3, 1) diverges. Superposition of modes: φ = e₁, (1/2)e₂, η = 2, factors −1 and 1/2: sections (1, 1), (−1, 1/2), (1, 1/4), ….

Proposition 4 (Single mode with momentum)  Assume D contains only one input (P = 1), s = ‖φ‖² \> 0, η \> 0, β ∈ \[0, 1), K4.  
(i) Let e be the component of the parameter error in the φ direction and v the component of the momentum in the φ direction; the one-step map is N = \[\[1 − ηs, −ηβ\], \[s, β\]\] (v′ = βv + s·e, e′ = e − η·v′). Convergence from all initial states ⇔ 0 \< ηs \< 2(1 + β).  
(ii) Under convergence, e\_k, v\_k → 0; on V⊥, v⊥\_k = β^k v₀⊥, and the limit of θ⊥ is P\_{V⊥}θ₀ − η·β·v₀⊥/(1 − β); the limit state is a host-adjustment equilibrium. When β \> 0 and v₀⊥ ≠ 0, the limit point is no longer P\_{V⊥}θ₀ + θ\*\_V; when β = 0 there is no displacement on V⊥ (v⊥ is zero from the first step).  
(iii) When β \> 0, k\_bal is finite ⇔ the entry is already a host-adjustment equilibrium (e₀ = 0 and v₀ = 0 on all write-back coordinates).  
(iv) When β \> 0, the exceptional initial states for k\_e form a union of countably many lines through the origin (one for each k; they may coincide). When β = 0, by Proposition 2: when ηs = 1 every entry is aligned in one step; when ηs ≠ 1 only states already aligned at entry arrive.  
(v) Boundary and beyond:  
　ηs = 2(1 + β): the characteristic roots are −1 and −β. If the entry has only the −1 mode, the motion is exactly a two-cycle; if it has only the −β mode, it converges; if both modes have nonzero projections, it tends to that two-cycle. A nonzero two-cycle limit arises only when the −1 mode is excited.  
　ηs \> 2(1 + β): when β \> 0, both real roots are negative with product β, one of absolute value greater than 1 and one less than 1; when β = 0 the two roots are 0 and 1 − ηs. If the entry excites the unstable root, the sequence diverges; if the entry lies on the stable eigenvector, it converges.  
　ηs ↓ 0⁺: the two roots tend to 1 and β; convergence slows down but stays within the convergence domain. η = 0 is not in the domain of this Form.  
Proof: (i) The characteristic polynomial is z² − (1 + β − ηs)z + β; apply the Jury conditions. (ii) There is no gradient on V⊥; the cumulative displacement is −η Σ\_{k≥1} β^k v₀⊥. (iii) det N = β ≠ 0, so (e\_k, v\_k) = 0 ⇒ (e₀, v₀) = 0; and v⊥\_k = β^k v₀⊥. (iv) When β \> 0, N is invertible, and for each k the condition is one nontrivial linear equation. (v) On the boundary the polynomial is (z + 1)(z + β); beyond it, the sum of the roots 1 + β − ηs \< 0, their product is β, and the discriminant is positive. ∎  
Example: η = 1, β = 1/2, v₀⊥ = 2: θ⊥ runs through −1, −3/2, −7/4, …, with limit −2, and the state does not become a host-adjustment equilibrium in finitely many steps. Boundary, s = 1, η = 3, β = 1/2: entry (1, −2/3) is exactly a two-cycle; entry (1, −1) runs through (−1/2, 1/2), (1/4, −1/4), …, and converges; entry (1, 0) tends to the two-cycle (±3, ∓2). Beyond the boundary, η = 15/4 (roots −1/4, −2): entry (1, −4/3) converges by the factor −1/4, while entry (1, 0) diverges. β = 0, ηs = 3: entry (1, 0) runs through 1, −2, 4, −8, 16.

Proposition 5 (Unreachability: unrealizable target)  
(i) Within the adjudication domain of *The Adjudication Form of Host-Adjustment Equilibrium*, the state is unaligned at every time, so host-adjustment equilibrium fails at all times, and the equilibrium criterion of that Form adjudicates negatively over the whole run. Only the failure of alignment is used.  
(ii) Assume η \> 0, K1, K3, K6, and any β: protocol stop fails at every time (by Proposition 1). Without K6 this does not hold: y = w·(x + x²), D = {−1, 1}, target y = x, w = 1/2: the residuals at the two points are 1 and 0, and both gradients are 0; with zero entry momentum the adjustment stops yet never aligns; η = 0 also stops it. These two examples lie in an enlarged working domain and do not bear on Theorem 3 of *The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence*, which retains K5.  
(iii) Assume β = 0, K1, K3, K6. When η is sufficiently small (taken within the K5 interval), the one-cycle map is a strict contraction on V and the cycle sections at each phase converge; as η → 0⁺ (taken after k → ∞), the section limits at all phases tend to the same point P\_{V⊥}θ₀ + θ\_LS, where θ\_LS = H⁺·Σ\_j g·x\_j·φ\_j is the least-squares solution over the samples in the period, weighted by their numbers of repetitions.  
Proof: Under K5 each A\_j\|\_V is nonexpansive, and the one-cycle map is a strict contraction on V (Theorem 20-2 of *The Discrimination Form of Trace-Response Transformation in Artificial Neural Networks* (Field Concretization Group Form, Chapter 20), as carried over in Theorem 3 of *The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence*); from M\_η\|\_V = I − ηH\|\_V + O(η²) and the invertibility of H\|\_V, ‖(I − M\_η\|\_V)⁻¹‖ = O(1/η); moreover c\_η = O(η), so the section fixed points are uniformly bounded; the section fixed point satisfies (I − M\_η)θ̄ = c\_η with c\_η = η Σ\_j g x\_j φ\_j + O(η²), and dividing by η and letting η → 0⁺ gives Hθ̄\_V = Σ\_j g x\_j φ\_j; each step changes the parameters by η times a bounded quantity, so the sections at different phases differ by O(η); the V⊥ component does not move. ∎  
Example: constant feature φ ≡ 1, target g·x, g = 1, D = {1, 2}. Period (1, 2): the section fixed point is (3 − η)/(2 − η), which for η = 1/2, 1/10, 1/100, 1/1000 is 5/3, 29/19, 299/199, 2999/1999, tending to 3/2; period (1, 1, 2): for η = 1/10, 1/100, 1/1000 it is 371/271, 39701/29701, 3997001/2997001, tending to 4/3.  
(iv) Affine term and unit root (β = 0): the cycle sections satisfy e\_{m+1} = M e\_m + c. If M\|\_V is semisimple at λ = 1 and the projection of c onto that eigenspace (along the other invariant subspaces of M) is nonzero, the sections accumulate this projection every cycle and drift linearly. In general, check with the augmented matrix \[\[M, c\], \[0, 1\]\]: when the initial state actually excites the generalized direction of a second-order block at its unit root 1, that component grows linearly; the other modes are judged by their own properties.  
Example: φ ≡ 1, periodic labels (1, 2), η = 2: both step factors are −1, M = 1, the section map is w ↦ w + 2, the sections are 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, and within the cycles the values are 0, 2, 2, 0, 4, ….  
Divergence beyond the boundary: the same sampling, η = 3: both step factors are −2, M = 4, the section map is w ↦ 4w, with fixed point 0; for w₀ = 1 the sections are 1, 4, 16, 64, 256, diverging; for w₀ = 0 the sections stay at the fixed point (unaligned).

## Section 5  Uniqueness

The θ\_LS of Proposition 5(iii) is unique in V (H\|\_V is invertible); the limit point of Proposition 4(ii) is uniquely determined by the entry. No other uniqueness is claimed.

## Section 6  Interpretation (Not Part of the Formal Structure)

Convergence is not just a matter of “arriving or not.” For one and the same realizable target, training converges when the step size falls in either of two separate intervals, diverges when it falls between them, and on the boundary may circle the aligned point forever. With momentum, the parameters can arrive in one step and then be carried away by inertia; under the convergence condition, an entry that is not an equilibrium reaches equilibrium only in the limit. When the target is unrealizable, as long as the step size and the features are nonzero, training neither stops nor aligns. Without momentum: under a small step size (K5) the sections tend to a cycle determined by the step size and the sampling table, and approach the weighted least-squares solution as the step size tends to zero; if the linear part of the cycle map has a semisimple unit root 1 and the projection of the affine term onto it is nonzero, that component drifts away cycle after cycle (when the affine map itself is the identity, the sections stay put); when the step size goes beyond the boundary, the sections can diverge.

## Section 7  Final Conclusions and Unanswered Questions

For the parameter-linear implementation class of M\_EDR with periodic sampling:  
　with η \> 0, K6 and periodic coverage, stop and equilibrium occur at the same time (Proposition 1); for β = 0, the initial states that arrive in finitely many steps without momentum form exactly a subspace (Proposition 2); for β = 0, the convergence parameter domain is given by the spectral radius of the one-cycle map and may be disconnected, and convergence of the sections does not guarantee convergence of the full sequence (Proposition 3); in the single-mode case with β ∈ \[0, 1), the convergence interval, the two-cycle on the boundary, and the dependence on excitation beyond the boundary are determined, and for 0 \< β \< 1 an entry that is not an equilibrium does not become one in finitely many steps (Proposition 4); when the target is unrealizable, equilibrium fails throughout, stop fails throughout under η \> 0 and K6, for β = 0 under K5 the sections at small step size tend to the weighted least-squares solution, and for β = 0, if the projection of the affine term onto the semisimple unit root 1 of the linear part of the cycle map is nonzero, that component drifts linearly (Proposition 5).  
This Form does not undertake preservation or reversion.  
Unanswered: the convergence boundary and finite arrival under multi-mode periodic sampling with momentum; whether higher-order Jordan blocks with \|λ\| = 1 are realizable; the case in which the affine term and higher-order unit Jordan blocks coexist; the unreachable sections and the small-step-size limit for β \> 0; two-layer networks, general nonconvex models, spiking networks, and tolerance-based alignment.
