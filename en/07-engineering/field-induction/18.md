# The Reachability Form of Maintenance Law Engineering

## Section 1  Initial State and Declaration of Permitted Operations

### 1.1  Question

*The Construction Form of Field Induction Law Engineering* gives an explicit construction of the Law Engineering G and the induced field F: G reads in F's reading and the goal through the input port, retains by its own sedimentation the slope of F's reading in the direction of the written position, and on that basis rewrites F through the direct-write port. Alignment has already occurred in the construction; the question is this: from a given initial state, through permitted operations, can the host-adjustment equilibrium of the induced field be reached? What is the path, and how many steps does it take? Under which protocols can it not be reached?

### 1.2  Initial State

Take the complete entry table of 2.3 of *The Construction Form of Field Induction Law Engineering*: F's host level w0 = w1 = 1/2; run level x0 = x1 = u = y = 0; out-of-boundary goal g = 2, held by the goal holder and pointing to F; G's host level c = C = 1 (acquired at the starting point); G's run level yG = gG = cmd = obs = 0, dlt = 1. The read-out of the induced field is y := w0 + p·w1, the reading is Φ = w0 + p·w1, and the main host takes p = 2.

### 1.3  Permitted Operations

The permitted operation set 𝒪: read-ins at G's input port; G's own field rules (plan, copy, two sedimentations), including the trigger condition — the read-in of the reading difference, the copy and the two sedimentations are executed only when the command of the current window is cmd ≠ 0 and the reading-difference observation has arrived; G's writes to w1 through F's direct-write port; F's own field rules (membrane drive, sedimentation, read-out) and inputs. 𝒪 contains no write of any kind to w0: G writes only w1, and F's sedimentation also writes only w1.  
Joint protocol: the regulation windows W\_n lie at beats 20, 30, 40, …; the events within a window are, in order — F read-out → G reads in y and g → G plans cmd := (gG − yG)/C → G writes w1 += cmd through the direct-write port → F read-out → (when triggered) G reads in the reading difference obs, records the copy dlt := cmd, sedimentation c := c + (obs − c·dlt)/dlt, sedimentation C := c. F has no input within a window. The joint protocol specifies only what happens within windows; host input between two adjacent windows is specified by the declared path (2.1) or by the continuation protocol (1.4). Beat numbers are calendar labels: W\_n lies at beat 10n + 10; only the beats actually executed in a path or continuation count toward the number of beats, and labels not executed do not count.  
Three kinds of variation are also declared, none of which enlarges 𝒪:  
　(i) Restricted protocol R\_obs: G's reading-difference observation does not arrive, the trigger condition is not satisfied, and C keeps its entry value; R\_obs is a subset of 𝒪.  
　(ii) Host and entry family \[Family\]: host F\_p (p ∈ ℚ), with entries w0, w1, g arbitrary rationals, c = C ≠ 0, and the rest as in 1.2; this is a declared extension of the initial state, used for Theorem 2 and the examples of Section 4.  
　(iii) Initial-state variants: used only for the factor example of Section 3; they do not enter the reachability theorem.  
Steps are counted in regulation windows (W1 being the 1st window); the number of beats is reported separately.

1.4  Continuation Protocols and Host-Adjustment Equilibrium

After the window at which equilibrium is to be signed, execution continues under a frozen continuation protocol: the events within windows and their order are as in 1.3; the goal g is fixed; the family of host inputs between two adjacent windows is frozen as one of the following:  
　K0 (idle gap): no input events between windows; F executes no field-rule beats.  
　Kp (protective input): two beats of input (1, 0) between windows. Beat by beat the membrane drive is u := w0; the sedimentation condition x1 = 1 is not satisfied, so w1 is unchanged and the reading is unchanged.  
　Km (main-run continuation): two beats of input (1, 1) between windows, that is, the inputs executed by the main run of the Law Engineering construction between W1 and W2 and between W2 and W3. On a beat with w0 + w1 ≥ 1, sedimentation increases w1 by 1/8 and the reading by p/8.  
All three lie within 𝒪 and the maintenance-window condition (F has no input within a window).  
Adjudication cross-section: the entry state (the first window after it being W1) or the state at the end of some window (the first window after it being the next window once the inter-window beats have been executed under K). The alignment target set is A = {Φ = g}. For a continuation K, the equilibrium set B\_K consists of the adjudication cross-sections σ with Φ(σ) = g such that, when execution continues from σ under K, in every subsequent window G's command is zero and no act rewrites F. Since cmd = (g − Φ\_in)/C, “cmd = 0 in every subsequent window” is equivalent to “the entry Φ = g in every subsequent window,” so B\_K ⊆ A. B\_K depends on the continuation protocol: different K can give different verdicts for the same state.  
Bridging: Definition 2.2 of *The Necessity Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence* defines host-adjustment equilibrium as “alignment and protocol stop.” On the F–G system, alignment corresponds to Φ = g, and protocol stop corresponds to “when execution continues under the declared continuation, the act side makes zero write-back from then on.” Compared with the original setting of that definition there are three differences: first, the one that writes back is another field G, and F's own sedimentation, though not a write-back, can move Φ, so B\_K uses “the entry Φ = g in every subsequent window” to bear both zero write-back and the preservation of alignment; second, the continuation must be declared explicitly; third, this system has no momentum, so the end of the first window belonging to B\_K is already equilibrium. The criterion J\_bal of *The Adjudication Form of Host-Adjustment Equilibrium* takes computation graphs valid under M\_EDR as its adjudication domain, and the F–G joint system is not among them; this Form takes the in-model criterion J\_K — checking window by window “entry Φ = g, cmd = 0, the act does not change w1,” and proving by induction that this is preserved thereafter.  
Three conclusions are kept apart: (i) first alignment — some adjudication cross-section belongs to A; (ii) host-adjustment equilibrium — some adjudication cross-section belongs to B\_K; (iii) re-correction after perturbation — the end of every window belongs to A again, but not to B\_K.

## Section 2  Path Construction

### 2.1  Main Path

Main path: from the initial state of 1.2, execute W1; after W1, first execute beats 25 and 26 (input (1, 1)); then execute W2; after W2, branch according to the continuation K0, Kp or Km. The beats actually executed are beat 20 (W1), beats 25 and 26, and beat 30 (W2). Window by window:  
　W1: Φ\_in = 1/2 + 2·(1/2) = 3/2, cmd = (2 − 3/2)/1 = 1/2, w1 = 1, Φ = 5/2; triggered, obs = 1, dlt = 1/2, c := 1 + (1 − 1·1/2)/(1/2) = 2, C = 2 — the first effective observation forms the slope.  
　Beats 25 and 26: u = 1/2 + 1 ≥ 1, x1 = 1, and sedimentation makes w1 = 9/8 and then 5/4; Φ = 3.  
　W2: cmd = (2 − 3)/2 = −1/2, w1 = 3/4, Φ = 2; triggered, obs = −1, dlt = −1/2, c := 2 + (−1 − 2·(−1/2))/(−1/2) = 2, C unchanged.  
　After W2, under K0 or Kp: in every window the entry Φ = 2, cmd = 0, the direct write writes the same value, the trigger condition is not satisfied, and C and the host level are unchanged. Under Km: in every window the entry Φ = 5/2, cmd = −1/4, and alignment is restored at the end of the window (Proposition 3 of 4.2).  
Eight continuation windows under K0 and Kp: from the 3rd window on, each window's (Φ\_in, cmd, Φ\_out, C) is constantly (2, 0, 2, 2); within the continuation no host sedimentation changes w1, and G's reading-difference read-in and sedimentation are not executed. If after W2 one first executes beats 35 and 36 and W3 under Km (cmd = −1/4) and then switches to K0 or Kp, the states from the end of the 3rd window on belong to B\_K.  
Control: if beats 25 and 26 are not executed after W1 and continuation proceeds directly under K0, the command of W2 is −1/4 (not −1/2), and the end of W2 is likewise aligned and belongs to B\_K0 — this is the case of the learning branch of 2.2; the window-by-window values of the main path depend on the two beats actually executed between W1 and W2.

### 2.2  Learning Branch

On \[Family\], continuation proceeds under K0 or Kp from the entry on (no drift between windows). Write e₀ = w0 + p·w1 − g. When e₀ ≠ 0, the command of W1, cmd = −e₀/C, is nonzero; within the window only the act changes Φ, and the read-out is linear in the direction of w1, so obs = p·cmd, and the sedimentation gives c := c + (obs − c·dlt)/dlt = obs/dlt = p, independently of the entry value of c; C := p. The error at the end of W1 is e₁ = (1 − p/C\_entry)·e₀; if e₁ ≠ 0, the command of W2, −e₁/p, brings the error to zero, and the sedimentation c := obs/dlt = p leaves c unchanged. The entry factor 1 − p/C\_entry acts only on W1 and does not carry over into later windows.

## Section 3  Bounds on Cost and Step Count

Theorem 1 (Main path)  From the initial state of 1.2 (p = 2), along the main path of 2.1 (W1, beats 25–26, W2), the end of W2 belongs to A for the first time, and the end of W2 ∈ B\_K0 ∩ B\_Kp. First alignment and equilibrium are both attained at the end of the 2nd window, and the beats actually executed are beats 20, 25, 26, and 30.  
Proof: Alignment is given by the window-by-window computation of 2.1; at the ends of the earlier windows Φ = 5/2. Equilibrium: suppose that at the end of some window Φ = 2, C = 2, w0 = 1/2, w1 = 3/4. Under K0 there are no events between windows; under Kp, in both beats u := 1/2 \< 1 and x1 = 0, so sedimentation does not write and w1 is unchanged. At the next window the entry Φ = 2, cmd = 0, the direct write writes the same value, the trigger condition is not satisfied, C is unchanged, and at the end of the window the host level and C are the same as at the end of the previous window. By induction on the number of windows, cmd = 0 and the entry Φ = 2 in every subsequent window. ∎

Theorem 2 (Step bound for the learning branch) \[Family\]  Let p ≠ 0, let the entries w0, w1, g be arbitrary rationals, c = C ≠ 0, and let continuation proceed under K ∈ {K0, Kp} from the entry. Then: when e₀ = 0, the entry cross-section already belongs to B\_K (the first window after it being W1); when e₀ ≠ 0, W1 forms C = p, and the end of W1 (if C\_entry = p) or the end of W2 belongs to B\_K. A and B\_K are both attained within ≤ 2 windows.  
Proof: When e₀ = 0 the entry cross-section belongs to A; the first window after it is W1, cmd = 0 in every window, the trigger condition is not satisfied, and the host part of the state is unchanged, so the entry cross-section belongs to B\_K. When e₀ ≠ 0, the claim follows from 2.2. Preservation follows by the induction of Theorem 1: K0 and Kp do not change w1 between windows, whatever the values of w (the sedimentation condition x1 = 1 of Kp is never satisfied). ∎  
On a rational grid (eight values of p, four values each of w0 and w1, three values of g, four values of C, two branches of K, 3072 cases in all) the result holds case by case: every case belongs to B\_K within ≤ 2 windows, with zero write-back and alignment in the eight windows that follow; the conclusion is borne by the proof.

Step count of the constant-slope branch  Under R\_obs, C is fixed at its entry value, and the error recursion is e\_n = (1 − p/C)·e\_{n−1}. The error converges to zero for every initial error ⇔ \|1 − p/C\| \< 1; when e₀ = 0 it is constantly zero under any factor; when e₀ ≠ 0, a factor of 0 reaches the goal in one window, 0 \< \|1 − p/C\| \< 1 gives only convergence in the limit, with no finite number of windows reaching the goal, a factor of −1 oscillates, a factor of 1 (p = 0) stalls, and \|1 − p/C\| \> 1 diverges. Hence a finite step bound can be given only when the factor is 0. Example (initial-state variant p = 2, C = 3/2, factor −1/3, e₀ = −1/2): e\_n = (−1/3)ⁿ·(−1/2) is never zero and tends to zero, in agreement with the distinction between finite arrival and convergence in the limit in Propositions 1 and 2 of *The Limit Form of Host-Adjustment Convergence*.

## Section 4  The Reachability Theorem and the Boundary of Unreachability

### 4.1  Reachability Theorem

Reachability Theorem  (a) For A: from the initial state of 1.2, along the main path of 2.1 (W1, beats 25–26, W2), A is attained for the first time at the end of the 2nd window; in \[Family\] (p ≠ 0, arbitrary entry, C ≠ 0, K0 or Kp from the entry on), A is attained within ≤ 2 windows.  
(b) For B: from the initial state of 1.2, along the same main path, with K0 or Kp taken after W2, B\_K0 and B\_Kp are attained at the end of the 2nd window; in \[Family\], they are attained within ≤ 2 windows.  
Proof: Theorems 1 and 2. ∎

### 4.2  Unreachability

The reachable set is adjudicated separately against A and against B:  
\| Protocol (entry) \| Reachable set ∩ A \| Reachable set ∩ B \|  
\| Main path, K0 or Kp after W2 (1.2) \| nonempty (from the end of W2 on) \| nonempty (end of W2 ∈ B\_K0 ∩ B\_Kp) \|  
\| Main path (Km between W1 and W2 and thereafter) (1.2) \| nonempty (the end of every window from W2 on) \| empty for B\_Km (Proposition 3) \|  
\| R\_obs + K0 (1.2, p = 2) \| empty (Proposition 1) \| empty \|  
\| Host F\_0, g ≠ w0 \[Family\] \| empty (Proposition 2) \| empty \|  
\| Host F\_0, g = w0 \[Family\] \| nonempty (entry cross-section) \| nonempty (the entry cross-section belongs to B\_K0, B\_Kp) \|

Proposition 1 (The restricted protocol cannot reach A)  Under R\_obs + K0, from the initial state of 1.2, the image of the reading over the reachable set is {3/2, 5/2}, which does not contain g = 2.  
Proof: C ≡ 1, the factor is 1 − 2/1 = −1, e₀ = −1/2, and e\_n = (−1)ⁿ·e₀ ≠ 0; the reading alternates between 3/2 and 5/2. ∎  
Under the same protocol, the host with p = 3 already has reading 2 at the entry of 1.2 (e₀ = 0); although the factor is −2, every window is aligned.

Proposition 2 (The reading does not depend on the written position)  On host F\_0 (p = 0), from any entry, the image of the reading over the reachable set is {w0\_entry}; when g ≠ w0\_entry, A is unreachable.  
Proof: Φ = w0, and 𝒪 does not write w0. ∎  
When g ≠ w0 there are two branches: if the reading-difference observation arrives, the first sedimentation of W1 gives c := obs/dlt = 0, which falls outside the nonzero type of G's host variables; this sedimentation event is not legitimate, and the run is aborted in W1; under R\_obs, the command (g − w0)/C is nonzero in every window while the reading does not change, so adjustment never stops. In both branches unreachability is borne independently by Φ ≡ w0. When g = w0 the entry cross-section is already aligned, the command is zero in every window, the trigger condition is not satisfied, and the entry cross-section belongs to B\_K0 and B\_Kp.

Proposition 3 (B\_Km unreachable, A reachable)  From the initial state of 1.2, with Km both between W1 and W2 and after W2 (that is, the main path followed by Km), the end of every window from W2 on belongs to A, while no reachable state belongs to B\_Km.  
Proof: 𝒪 does not write w0, so w0 ≡ 1/2; alignment means w1 = (2 − 1/2)/2 = 3/4. On the next beat of Km, u = 5/4 ≥ 1 and x1 = 1, so sedimentation necessarily writes; on the second beat u = 11/8, and likewise; at the entry of the next window Φ = 5/2 and cmd = −1/4 ≠ 0, and alignment is restored at the end of the window. Hence no aligned state belongs to B\_Km, and on the permitted cross-sections with w0 = 1/2, B\_Km is empty. ∎  
Distinguishing witness: the state at the end of W2, (w0, w1, C) = (1/2, 3/4, 2), with Φ = 2, belongs to A; under K0 and Kp, cmd = 0 in every subsequent window, so it belongs to B\_K0 and B\_Kp; under Km, cmd = −1/4 in every subsequent window, so it does not belong to B\_Km. The repeated act at W3 in the main run of the Law Engineering construction is exactly this case — a single zero command or a single alignment does not suffice to sign equilibrium.  
Unreachability is adjudicated by the intersection of the reachable set under permitted operations with A and B, not by reading fibers: when w1 can be written with any rational value and p ≠ 0, every rational target has an algebraic preimage, and whether a protocol can reach it requires a path besides. The above are unreachability results for specific restricted protocols and hosts; they do not exhaust the causes of unreachability over the full permitted domain.

## Section 5  Declaration of Strength

\- Path existence is proved, together with convergence and step bounds under K0 and Kp in \[Family\]; Km (for B), R\_obs (for A), and p = 0 with g ≠ w0 (for A) each have an unreachability result; Theorem 2 does not extend beyond the declared family.  
\- Host-adjustment equilibrium on this system takes the bridging definition of 1.4 and the in-model criterion J\_K; J\_bal is not invoked outside its adjudication domain.  
\- The Law qualification of the Law Engineering stays at the Law-candidate tier of *The Construction Form of Field Induction Law Engineering*: formal law formation has not been attained, and SC\_Hold of the sufficient direction of *The Necessity Form of Law* has not been closed.  
\- The event qualifications of host adjustment and tuning are not signed; biological sleep is not signed.  
H18-1  The declaration that the mechanism routes in *The Construction Form of Field Induction Law Engineering* are exhaustive is truthful.  
H18-2  F's read-out is linear in the direction of w1 (as given by the declared model).
