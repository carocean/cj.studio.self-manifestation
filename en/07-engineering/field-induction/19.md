# The Construction Form of Tuning in Spiking Networks

## Section 1  Declaration of the Condition Group

### 1.1  Question

*The Compatibility Form of Tuning Conditions* gives a coexistence model of the antecedents of tuning: in a minimal spiking network, the firing of an in-field interneuron closes a presynaptic gate through an inhibitory channel, and transmission actually reads the gate state. Coexistence is not construction. The question is: on a parameter family of spiking networks, what does a circuit that satisfies the conditions of tuning look like? We require an explicit tuning circuit that is conscripted by in-field control activity and changes the permeability of existing connections by suppression and disinhibition, a construction procedure for it, and item-by-item verification within its range of generality.

### 1.2  Condition Group

For a regulated field N, an act I, a window W, a reading Φ, and a goal specification:  
C1 Regulated field: the declared spiking network; its Field-Concretization Qualification is proved in two parts — the rule-level mechanism witness (P10 of Definition 3.10 of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction* v1.0), and Trace-Response, organizational participation and historical dynamics in some actual window.  
C2 Landing point of tuning: (a) in each tuning window, the act lands on the permeability of existing connections through gating (gating port; the m layer is supported on existing connections), and transmission actually reads m; (b) existence in both directions: suppression (closing a gate) and disinhibition (opening a gate) each have an instance in a separate tuning window.  
C3 Evidence tiers of the in-field conscriptor: (i) in-field control-rule tier — the firing of in-field neurons drives gating through a declared control chain; (ii) Law-candidate tier — requires in-field formation (the source contains input; not acquired at the starting point), retention, and dependence of the values written by the act on the carrying organization; (iii) formal Law tier — the formal criteria and read-back of the Law Group Form on the same carrying organization, through the sufficient direction of *The Necessity Form of Law*; (iv) intention route — requires its upstream qualification and connecting steps. Each tier has its own evidence ceiling.  
C4 Control source and bearing: the chain “goal version → in-field control activity → gating event → actual transmission (reading m)”; when the control output is intervened on with input and goal held fixed, the gating changes accordingly. The proof of the control source is kept separate from the classification of the mode of action (L2 of *The Adjudication Form of Field-Induction Action Modes*).  
C5 Necessary structure: Org₀, GoalRep, Target, ΔΦ, Eff, Bear, GoalCond, Gate and Bear\_G of *The General Form of Tuning* are read back item by item (formal read-back; event qualification is not signed).  
C6 Generality: on the declared parameter family, the candidate domain and the success domain are frozen in advance, the construction holds in the success domain, and the failure branches are given class by class.  
C7 Physical layer: we take the strengthened reading of H14-0 of *The Compatibility Form of Tuning Conditions* — no actual change of θ within a tuning window.

## Section 2  Construction Procedure

### 2.1  Parameter Family 𝒩

Numerical domain: all quantities are rational and computed exactly. Existing connections i = 0, …, n−1 (n ≥ 1 finite): weights w\_i ≥ 0 (host level), presynaptic gates m\_i ∈ {0, 1} (m layer, supported on w\_i), presynaptic inputs zpre\_i; one postsynaptic neuron (membrane drive u, firing z); a gating subset G\_sel; reading Φ = Σ a\_i·m\_i·w\_i (the a\_i are declared read-out coefficients); entry gate state m⁰; goal value g ∈ {0} ∪ 𝒢, with g = 0 the baseline; 𝒢 is a finite set of pairwise distinct nonzero rationals, whose members are processed in ascending order of label. The topology is restricted to this feedforward family (a single postsynaptic neuron, presynaptic gates).  
Goal specification: for each g ∈ 𝒢, either a goal reading Φ*\_g is given, or a goal gate state m^g ∈ {0, 1}^n is given directly with m^g\_i = m⁰\_i (i ∉ G\_sel) — unselected gates are not controlled, and their goal state must equal their entry state; when the gate state is given directly, the induced goal reading is Φ*\_g := Φ(m^g).

### 2.2  Construction Steps

Step 0 (goal gate state and specification check): when Φ*\_g is given, solve Φ(m) = Φ*\_g over {0, 1}^{G\_sel} (unselected gates take their entry state), taking the solution that changes the fewest gates (the lexicographically first among them); if there is no solution, stop without producing a circuit — the goal is unsatisfiable. When m^g is given directly, first check its length, its values and the constraint on unselected gates; if any check fails, report that the goal is unsatisfiable on the selected gate set. Write the suppression set C\_g = {i ∈ G\_sel : m⁰\_i = 1, m^g\_i = 0}, the disinhibition set O\_g = {i ∈ G\_sel : m⁰\_i = 0, m^g\_i = 1}, and the changed-gate set S\_g = C\_g ∪ O\_g.  
Step 1 (control neurons): for each g ∈ 𝒢, set up an in-field neuron K\_g: u\_K := q\_g·ctx\_g + d\_g, z\_K := \[u\_K ≥ 1\]. The context ctx\_g := \[g = that value\] is written by an input-port event that reads the goal; d\_g is the teaching drive (an input).  
Step 2 (tonic inhibitory units): for i ∈ G\_sel, set up T\_i: u\_T := tonic\_i·(1 − Σ\_{g : i∈O\_g} z\_K,g) + Σ\_{g : i∈C\_g} z\_K,g, z\_T := \[u\_T ≥ 1\], tonic\_i = 1 − m⁰\_i.  
Step 3 (gating and transmission): the gating-port event (act I) m\_i := 1 − z\_T,i; membrane drive u\_pre := u/2 + Σ m\_i·w\_i·zpre\_i (transmission actually reads m); firing z := \[u\_pre ≥ 1\], after which u := 0 (z = 1) or u := u\_pre (z = 0).  
Step 4 (in-field formation of the control coupling): Hebbian sedimentation q\_g := min(1, q\_g + ½·ctx\_g·\[u\_K,g ≥ 1\]), with q\_g = 0 at entry.  
Entry: u = z = 0; q\_g = ctx\_g = d\_g = u\_K,g = z\_K,g = 0; u\_T,i = z\_T,i = tonic\_i; m = m⁰.  
Variables and layers: θ = {w\_i, q\_g}; m = {m\_i}; the run level holds u, z, zpre\_i, ctx\_g, d\_g, u\_K, z\_K, u\_T, z\_T; the goal g is out-of-boundary. Field-rule components: control, Hebbian sedimentation, tonic, membrane drive, firing, goal setting (out-of-boundary); ports: gating {m\_i : i ∈ G\_sel}, input {zpre\_i, ctx\_g, d\_g}; the declared mechanism routes are exhaustive.

### 2.3  Run Protocol and Phases

For each g ∈ 𝒢 in turn: teaching window T\_g (a goal-setting event sets g; two beats with d\_g = 1 and all inputs 0), followed by one beat with g = 0; after that, for each g: tuning window W\_g (a goal-setting event sets g; two beats with all inputs 1) and reset window R\_g (g = 0, one beat).  
Within each beat the events are, in order: input, context, control and Hebbian sedimentation for each g (in ascending order of label), the tonic inhibitory unit for each i ∈ G\_sel, each gating, membrane drive, firing; their phases are (k, 1), …, (k, ℓ), with ℓ = 4 + 2\|𝒢\| + 2\|G\_sel\|, and the last event is firing; the goal-setting event has phase (k, 0). Window bounds: T\_g = \[(k, 0), (k+1, ℓ)\], W\_g = \[(k, 1), (k+1, ℓ)\], R\_g = \[(k, 1), (k, ℓ)\], where k is the first beat of the window; the window end is the firing event of the last beat in the window. The goal-setting event (k′, 0) of the next beat comes after every beat in the window and always lies outside it; in a teaching window, the Hebbian event of the j-th goal (counting from 0) at the second beat has phase (k+1, 4 + 2j) ≤ (k+1, ℓ) and always lies inside the window.

### 3.1  Relation to the Coexistence Model

Special case n = 1, w = 3/2, a = 1, m⁰ = 1, closing the gate: Φ goes from 3/2 to 0, each item of C5 is 1, and θ does not change within W — this reproduces the gating conclusion of the coexistence model. Differences: in the coexistence model the firing of the interneuron writes m directly (m := 1 − z\_I), whereas this construction passes through two stages, control neuron and tonic inhibitory unit, so that suppression and disinhibition are given by one and the same procedure; the coexistence model has STDP on w (the LTD term takes m·zpre), whereas in this family w has no sedimentation component; the control map of the coexistence model is preset, whereas the context coupling q\_g of this construction is formed in the field. Candidate conflict points rechecked: K1 — the inhibition lands at the gating port, and transmission actually reads m; K2 — control neurons and tonic inhibitory units are the running of the field, and the landing point of the act is the gating event; K3 — the goal enters m through the value-write chain of input, control neuron and tonic inhibitory unit; K4 — in this family w has no sedimentation component and q\_g writes the same value within W, so H14-2 is not needed; K5 — Bear\_G holds through the support w\_i ≠ 0 and through a\_i ≠ 0; K6 — the teaching windows and the tuning windows belong to the same run and the same field.

## Section 4  Generality

### 4.1  Candidate Domain and Success Domain

Candidate domain: G\_sel ≠ ∅; S\_g ≠ ∅ (the selected gates have a nonzero actual change); ∀i ∈ S\_g: w\_i ≠ 0 (support that can be acted on) and a\_i ≠ 0 (the reading has dependence 1 on that gate, i.e. a\_i·w\_i ≠ 0).  
Success domain: within the candidate domain, the net difference of the joint gate transformation in the same window, ΔΦ = Σ\_{i∈S\_g} a\_i·w\_i·(m^g\_i − m⁰\_i), is ≠ 0, and the goal is satisfiable (Φ(m^g) = Φ\*\_g).

Theorem 1 (Construction within the success domain)  For every instance in the success domain of 𝒩 and every g ∈ 𝒢, the construction procedure of Section 2 is executable, and:  
(i) C1: the rule-level P10 and the actual teaching window T\_g hold;  
(ii) C2(a): in W\_g the gates of S\_g change according to m^g, the other gates do not change, and transmission has dependence 1 on each changed gate;  
(iii) formation, retention and the reset control of C3(ii): q\_g goes from 0 through 1/2 to 1 (the source contains input) and is unchanged thereafter; if q\_g is reset to 0 before tuning, the gates do not change in W\_g;  
(iv) C4: the source of the m\_i written by gating in W\_g runs through z\_T, z\_K and ctx to the goal-setting event; with input and goal held and the output of the control neurons to the tonic inhibitory units blocked, u\_T,i = tonic\_i and the gates do not change;  
(v) C5: Org₀, GoalRep / Target, Eff\_W, Gate\_W, Bear\_W, Bear\_G,W and GoalCond (Π = {gating} × {m}) are 1, ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0, and Φ at the end = Φ\*\_g;  
(vi) C7: no θ makes an actual change within W\_g.  
Proof: Teaching: at entry q\_g = 0; at the first beat u\_K = d\_g = 1, the Hebbian rule reads ctx\_g = 1 and u\_K ≥ 1, and q\_g := 1/2 (the source contains the input event); at the second beat u\_K = 1/2 + 1 — the control event reads in q\_g\|1 and makes an actual change — and q\_g := 1. This is independent of w, a, G\_sel and m⁰, so the Trace-Response version q\_g\|1, the read-in event K\_g (second beat) and the successor version q\_g\|2 are present for every instance and every g, and both lie within T\_g (2.3). Retention: the Hebbian rule q := min(1, q + …) is nondecreasing and writes the same value after saturation. Reset control: with q\_g = 0, in W\_g u\_K,g = 0, z\_K,g = 0, each u\_T,i = tonic\_i, and the gates do not change. Tuning: in W\_g, ctx\_g = 1 and d\_g = 0, so u\_K,g = 1 and z\_K,g = 1; for every other g′, ctx and d are 0, and z\_K,g′ = 0. For i ∈ C\_g: tonic\_i = 0, u\_T = 1, m\_i = 0; for i ∈ O\_g: tonic\_i = 1, u\_T = 0, m\_i = 1; for the remaining i ∈ G\_sel: u\_T = tonic\_i, m\_i = m⁰\_i. Hence ΔΦ\_W = Φ(m^g) − Φ(m⁰) ≠ 0 and Φ at the end = Φ\*\_g. Transmission: for i ∈ S\_g, w\_i ≠ 0 and zpre\_i = 1 in W\_g, so the membrane drive has dependence 1 on m\_i. Intervention: when K→T is blocked, u\_T,i does not read z\_K and equals tonic\_i, so m\_i = m⁰\_i. Org₀: the θ installed at entry is nonempty. GoalRep / Target: the model's goal declaration agrees with the target-pointing register and points to N. Bear\_W: from Bear\_G,W. Bear\_G: for i ∈ S\_g, the dependence of Φ on m\_i is 1 by a complete test on the finite value domain {0, 1}, and the source of m\_i contains the gating event. GoalCond: the source of m\_i runs through z\_T, z\_K and ctx to the goal-setting event, with dependence 1 at each step. θ: w\_i has no sedimentation component, and q\_g is saturated. ∎  
What C3 extends to the whole family by the same rule are formation, retention, dependence and the reset control; the tier ceiling of the Law candidate L\_g and the preset control (C3(i)) are evidenced by the main instance.  
On a rational grid (n = 2, w\_i ∈ {0, 1/2, 1}, a\_i ∈ {−1, 0, 1}, G\_sel ⊆ {0, 1}, m⁰ and m¹ fully enumerated; 2916 cases in all, each a complete run of the construction procedure), the theorem holds case by case: in the 624 cases of the success domain the formal values (Eff\_W, Gate\_W, Bear\_G,W, ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0, GoalCond\_W, goal reached) are all 1; for all instances the rule-level P10 and the actual teaching window are 1; all window bounds pass. Instances with n = 31 (sparse G\_sel = {3, 17, 30}, three goals, including suppression and disinhibition in the same window), with n = 12 (all gates selected for G\_sel, read-out coefficients of mixed sign, four goals) and with \|𝒢\| = 8 also hold window by window and goal by goal.

### 4.2  Failure Branches

\| Class \| Formal values (Eff\_W, Gate\_W, Bear\_G,W, ΔΦ\_W ≠ 0, GoalCond\_W) \| Necessary items that fail \|  
\| Empty gating subset (G\_sel = ∅) \| (0, 0, 0, 0, 0) \| Eff, Gate, Bear\_G, ΔΦ, GoalCond \|

Zero-weight and non-dependence defects are located at the level of the gate: a defective gate itself makes an actual change (Eff and Gate are satisfied by it), and its single-gate Bear\_G is 0; when the same window also has effective gates, the Bear\_G and ΔΦ of the whole case can be satisfied by the effective gates.  
Net-difference cancellation: opposite directions — w = (1, 1), a = (1, 1), m: (1, 0) → (0, 1); both gates actually change, each with reading dependence 1, Bear\_G = 1, yet ΔΦ\_W = 0; same direction — w = (1, 1), a = (1, −1), both gates close, ΔΦ\_W = 0. Single-gate dependence and single-gate bearing cannot replace the net difference within the same window.  
Unsatisfiable goal: w = (1, 1/2), a = (1, 1), m⁰ = (1, 1), Φ\* = 2; the reachable readings are {0, 1/2, 1, 3/2}, Step 0 has no solution, and the construction stops. Control: executing with the gate state for Φ = 1, Eff, Gate, Bear\_G and GoalCond are 1, ΔΦ\_W = −1/2, and the reading does not reach 2 — the unsatisfiability of the goal and the success or failure of the necessary items are independent of each other. When m^g is given directly and the goal state of an unselected gate differs from its entry state, Step 0 reports that the goal is unsatisfiable.

## Section 6  Source of the q\_g Candidate Structure

### 6.1  Object and Source Protocol

Object: χ\_g = (L\_g, I\_g, Rel(L\_g), Γ\_cand), where L\_g is “launch control under goal context g”, with carrying organization R\_g = {q\_g}. Its strength is the Law-candidate tier (C3(ii)); the conclusion of this section concerns the source of the candidate structure, not the source of the first law formation of a formal Law.  
The candidate judgment Γ\_cand is evaluated at each evaluation point t:  
　(a) In-field formation — q\_g ≠ 0, and the source of the current version of q\_g contains an input-port event;  
　(b) Action — starting from the frozen snapshot at t (the host-level w and q kept, the gates and the run level returned to their entry values), with goal setting g and two beats of all inputs 1, some gate state in C\_g ∪ O\_g changes;  
　(c) Dependence — in the same test run but with q\_g := 0, no gate state changes.  
　Judge\_t = (a) ∧ (b) ∧ (c), two-valued and defined at every evaluation point.  
Correspondence of Γ\_cand with C3(ii): formation corresponds to (a); retention means that Judge is 1 pointwise at every evaluation point after the first; dependence corresponds to (b)(c) — C3(ii) tests it on the original run by “resetting q to 0 before tuning”, and (b)(c) carry out the same intervention on the frozen snapshot. The test run starts from a frozen snapshot because the gate state is maintained by control activity and is not retained (C2): reading the gates midway through a tuning window or a reset window reads the control activity of the moment, not the carrying organization. Limits: the test run checks only that the gates of C\_g ∪ O\_g change; that the gate state equals the goal gate state, and the transmission reading, are proved separately on the original run by C2 and C5; the frozen snapshot is a diagnostic protocol, not a continuation of the original run.  
Source protocol Π\_src:  
　X\_0: the entry of 2.2 (q = 0, gates m⁰).  
　Dyn^Π: the field rules of Section 2 — control, Hebbian sedimentation, tonic, gating, membrane drive, firing (including the reset after firing). Goal-setting events belong to the external history, not to Dyn^Π.  
　H\_obs: the same run as the main instance of Section 3 — T1, reset beat, T2, reset beat, W1, R1, W2, R2, 12 beats in all (presynaptic inputs are all 0 in the teaching windows and the two reset beats after them, and all 1 in the tuning windows and R1, R2); replayed under the same historical drive and checked event by event against the run generated by the run protocol of 2.3, the numbering, kind, actual change and written value of all 176 events are identical.  
　History items (deletable): the goal-setting event G\_k of each beat (if any), the context input component ctx\_k, the teaching drive d\_k, and the nonzero presynaptic input zp\_k.  
　Deletion semantics (the unit of deletion is always a whole beat): deleting G\_k means that no goal-setting event occurs at that beat — the goal is a persistent register value and keeps its previous value; deleting ctx\_k sets the whole context group of that beat (the context component of every g) to 0, for that beat only, with the other beats unchanged; deleting d\_k sets the teaching-drive vector of that beat (the component of every g) to 0; deleting zp\_k sets the presynaptic input vector of that beat to all 0.  
　Background intervention (not a history deletion; listed separately): blockade of the output of the control neurons to the tonic inhibitory units throughout; the combination taken by the protocol applies the blockade to the history and to the test runs of (b)(c) alike.  
　Evaluation point set T\_Π: the entry and the end of each beat, 13 points in all; granularity Gran^Π: the history items above.  
All the above items are frozen in advance; the granularity, the deletable items, the judgment and the evaluation point set are not changed afterward.

### 6.2  Exclusion of Default Sources

(i) Given by construction: with q\_g preset to 1 at entry and all history items deleted, (a) fails at every point and Judge is all 0 — action and dependence are present, but this is not in-field formation, and it reaches only the control-rule tier of C3(i).  
(ii) Wiring: under the background intervention (blockade applied to both the history and the test runs), Judge is all 0 for both goals, while the formation writes of q\_g are identical item by item to those of the original run (the final value of q\_g is 1). If only the history is blocked and the test runs are not, the q\_g that is formed still suffices to trigger gating in the test runs, and Judge is acquired at the same points as in the original history (g = 1 at the end of the second beat of T1, g = 2 at the end of the second beat of T2) — the two combinations give different verdicts, which shows precisely that the wiring is necessary on the action side but does not enter formation. Counterfactual necessity is not source bearing (the fourth boundary of *The Source Form of Law*), and it is not counted as a source.  
(iii) Gating activity during formation: the gates also make actual changes in the teaching windows, but they are not on the source chain of the final version of q\_g; the source chain of q\_g contains only goal-setting, input, control-neuron running and Hebbian sedimentation events, and no gating events or m-layer variables.  
(iv) Teaching for the other goal: see 6.4; not load-bearing.

### 6.3  Generative Process

In the same teaching window, goal setting makes the context ctx\_g = 1, and over two beats the context is paired with the teaching drive d\_g. At the first beat u\_K = d\_g = 1, the Hebbian rule reads ctx\_g = 1 and u\_K ≥ 1, and q\_g := 1/2; at the second beat u\_K = q\_g\|1·ctx\_g + d\_g = 3/2, reading in q\_g\|1, and q\_g := 1. When q\_g = 1/2, u\_K = 1/2 \< 1 and (b) fails; both sedimentations are load-bearing.
