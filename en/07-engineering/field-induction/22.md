# The Discrimination Form of Weight-Space Programming

## Section 1  Declaration of the Candidate Pairs

### 1.1  Question

What rewrites the weights of the induced field may be a computational process that computes the write amount by a rule, or it may be Law Engineering—a concretized field that retains the slope of the induced field's reading through its own sedimentation. The two can give the same end state, and even the same per-window writes. On the basis of checkable mechanism and history, and not only the end state, can they be separated? At which level of observation?

### 1.2  Common Protocol

Host F\_p: the induced field of *The Construction Form of Field Induction Law Engineering*, with read-out y := w0 + p·w1, entry w = (1/2, 1/2), and goal g. Order within a window: F read-out → the writer reads in y, g → plan → write w1 through the direct-write port → F read-out → (trigger: the command of this window cmd ≠ 0 and the reading-difference observation arrives) the writer reads in the reading difference and updates. Continuation between windows: K0 (no input). Observation runs to the end of regulation window 2.

### 1.3  Candidates

H\_P⁰ (fixed-parameter rule): computational process R⁰, write amount (g − y)/p̂, with p̂ = 2 fixed; it reads in no observation; when deployed to another host, it carries p̂ = 2.  
H\_P¹ (rule updated by observation): computational process R¹, p̂ with initial value 1; on triggering, p̂ := obs/cmd; write amount (g − y)/p̂; when deployed to another host, it starts afresh from the initial value.  
H\_L (Law Engineering): the G of *The Construction Form of Field Induction Law Engineering*, with host-level c, C at entry value 1; on triggering, sedimentation c := c + (obs − c·dlt)/dlt, C := c; write amount (g − y)/C; when deployed to another host, it starts afresh from the entry values.  
Mechanism declaration: the declared boundary of R is a single computational process, all of whose variables are on the run level—p̂ is a run-level register written by a computation component; the components are plan, copy, update, and out-of-boundary components, and the mechanism routes are declared exhaustively. The c and C of G are on the host level and are written by sedimentation.

### 1.4  Observation Family

Nested: 𝒪\_end ⊂ 𝒪\_beh ⊂ 𝒪\_mech.  
𝒪\_end: (w0, w1, Φ) at the end of window 2.  
𝒪\_beh: the per-window readings of F and the projection of the writes on F (beat, phase, position, value); it does not include the writer's internal state or source labels.  
𝒪\_mech: in addition, the writer's mechanism declaration, the rule-level P10 (*The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*, Definition 3.10), the actual version paths, and the source records.  
First-proof domain: p = 3, g = 3 (entry error −1), no drift, the first effective observation arrives, two windows observed.

## Section 3  Validity of the Distinction

Proposition 1 (Fixed-parameter rule)  In the first-proof domain, the error of H\_P⁰ at the end of window 2 is −1/4, and that of H\_P¹ and H\_L is 0.  
Proof: 2.1. ∎

Proposition 2 (Per-window coupling)  Let the host read-out coefficient p be a nonzero rational; let the p̂ of R¹ and the C of G have the same nonzero entry value; let there be no drift between windows; let the reading-difference observations of the two arms arrive at the same times; and let observation run to a common stopping cross-section. Then under the common protocol, in every window the two have the same command, the same write on F, and the same reading of F, and after triggering p̂ = C = p.  
Proof: By induction on the number of windows. Suppose that at the window entry the states of F are the same and p̂ = C. The commands (g − y)/p̂ = (g − y)/C are the same, and so are the writes and readings that follow. When cmd ≠ 0 and the reading-difference observation arrives, both trigger: obs = p·cmd is the same; R¹ gives p̂ := obs/cmd = p; G gives c := c + (obs − c·cmd)/cmd = obs/cmd = p, C := p. When cmd = 0 or the observation does not arrive, neither updates. p̂ and C are always nonzero rationals, so the division in the plan is defined. ∎  
Stopping boundary: if p = 0, then in the first window in which the command is nonzero and the reading-difference observations of both arms arrive, the updates of the two arms give p̂ := 0 and c := 0, falling outside their respective nonzero types, and the legitimate common prefix ends before that update; without a trigger there is no such stop (e.g., for p = 0, g = 1/2 the entry is already aligned, the commands of both arms are zero, and the estimates stay at 1). Example: p = 0, g = 3 triggers in window 1.  
Corollary: In the first-proof domain, H\_P¹ and H\_L are indistinguishable on 𝒪\_end and 𝒪\_beh. On the nonzero generalization domain of Proposition 2, we additionally executed three windows (one window more than observed in the first-proof domain) with read-out coefficients −1, 1/2, 1, 2, 3, 5/2, goals 0, 2, 3, −1, and w1 entries 0, 1/2, 1; in all seventy-two cases the 𝒪\_beh projections are identical window by window; among them, in the three cases whose entry is already aligned, neither writes at all, and these are listed as undecided. The conclusion holds only for the frozen H\_P¹ and does not extend to arbitrary adaptive programs.

Proposition 3 (Conditional separation at the mechanism level)  Under the mechanism declaration of 1.3: the rule-level P10 of G is 1—the Trace-Response version c (from the first execution of Sedimentation 1) is read in by Sedimentation 2 and actually changes C, along the path c —value-write→ C; the rule-level P10 of R¹ is 0—the mechanism declaration is exhaustive, and no mechanism route writes the host level. If the mechanism of R¹ is undeclared, P10 is ⊥.  
Proof: Replay on the same window protocol according to Definition 3.10 of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*. The events of G in window 1:  
\| Event \| Kind \| Read-in \| Actual change \| Input events in the sources of the post-write version \|  
\| Read in y, read in g \| Input \| y; g \| yG; gG \| each its own \|  
\| Plan \| Run \| gG, yG, C \| cmd \| read in y, read in g \|  
\| Read in reading difference \| Input \| y, yG \| obs \| read in y, read in g, read in reading difference \|  
\| Sedimentation 1 \| Sedimentation \| c, obs, dlt \| c (1 → 3) \| read in y, read in g, read in reading difference \|  
\| Sedimentation 2 \| Sedimentation \| C, c \| C (1 → 3) \| same as above \|  
Trace-Response version: the c written by Sedimentation 1, whose sources contain input events, written by the field's own sedimentation (host level); organizational participation: Sedimentation 2 reads in this version and actually changes C; historical dynamics: the new version of C depends on the version of c, along the path c —value-write→ C. All three items are present, so P10 = 1. The mechanism declaration of R¹ is exhaustive and no mechanism route writes the host level, so there is no Trace-Response version and P10 = 0; when the mechanism is undeclared, P10 is ⊥. ∎  
Actual versions in the first-proof domain: in G the event that writes C is sedimentation, and its sources contain input events; in R¹ the event that writes p̂ is a computation component, and its sources likewise contain input events. The separation lies not in the sources but in the carrying level and P10: the carrying organization of G is a host-level Trace-Response; that of R¹ is a run-level register.  
Undecided: missing logs; if p̂ of R were redeclared as host-level sedimentation, the P10 of the two might be the same, and the mechanism separation would have to be re-examined (equal P10 does not determine that the complete declarations and the version logs are equal); implementations outside the domain. ⊥ does not serve as counterevidence.

## Section 4  Discrimination Theorem

Discrimination Theorem  In the first-proof domain and under the common protocol, the earliest tier of the nested observation family at which each candidate pair is distinguishable is as follows:  
\| Candidate pair \| Earliest distinguishing tier \| Earlier tiers \|  
\| H\_P⁰ vs. H\_L \| 𝒪\_end \| none \|  
\| H\_P⁰ vs. H\_P¹ \| 𝒪\_end \| none \|  
\| H\_P¹ vs. H\_L \| 𝒪\_mech (under the mechanism declaration of 1.3) \| 𝒪\_end, 𝒪\_beh insufficient \|  
Proof: Propositions 1, 2, and 3. ∎  
Beyond the first-proof domain: for the host p = 2, g = 2, H\_P⁰ (p̂ = p) and H\_L both end window 2 at (1/2, 3/4, 2)—on a wider host domain, the end states of a fixed-parameter rule and of Law Engineering can overlap; this does not change the conclusion in the first-proof domain.

## Section 5  Declaration of Strength

\- Only the first-proof domain, the common protocol, and the frozen candidates of the three tiers are signed; the behavioral identity of H\_P¹ and H\_L is signed only for the frozen counterpart.  
\- The separation at the mechanism level is conditional on the mechanism declaration of 1.3 and on evidence access, with read-back strength as in 7.1 and 7.2 of *The Mathematical Form of Field Induction*; ⊥ does not serve as counterevidence.  
\- That Law Engineering is superior to programming is not signed; nothing biological is signed.  
H22-1  The declaration that the mechanism routes of R and G are exhaustive is truthful.  
H22-2  The read-out of F\_p is linear in the w1 direction (given by the declared model).
