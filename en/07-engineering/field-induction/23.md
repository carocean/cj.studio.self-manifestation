# The Stability Form of Generalized Structure

## Section 1  The Object of Stability and the Original Qualification

### 1.1  Question

*The Source Form of Generalized Structure* established the conjunctive inhibitory Trace-Response structure Σ\_mem(τ) and gave its source. Is this structure, once established, preserved under the declared changes? After it fails, can it recover under legitimate dynamics? Is what recovers the same Trace-Response, or is it replaced by another Trace-Response? At which quantifier level does each conclusion hold?

### 1.2  Host and Qualification

The host 𝔐\_I and the working domain D\_mem (gates always open), the Trace-Response qualification and the membership test, the relation template R1–R4 and D, and the object Σ\_mem(τ) = Σ(τ) ∧ mem(u) are all taken from Section 1 of *The Source Form of Generalized Structure*. Write the threshold θ = w\_A + w\_B − 1 and the gap d = u − θ; D is d \> 0.

### 1.3  Initial States and Object Levels

First-proof domain: τ = (1, 1, 0); the initial state σ₀ is the final state of S1 of *The Source Form of Generalized Structure*—w = (1, 1), a = (1/2, 1/2), u = 3/2, κ = 1/2; the qualification interval of u begins with the inhibitory sedimentation of beat 1, and membership by retention holds; θ = 1, d = 1/2.  
Second domain: τ = (1, 0, 0); the initial state is the final state of the history listed in 1.5 of *The Source Form of Generalized Structure* in which all bearing positions are members—w = (1, 1/2), a = (1/2, 1/2), u = 1, κ = 1/2; θ = 1/2, d = 1/2. (0, 1, 0) is its relabeled image (*The Source Form of Generalized Structure*, Proposition 1). The conclusions for the single-positive task are given separately and are not extrapolated from the double-positive task.  
Write u₀ for the u of the initial state. The original qualification is Q = Σ\_mem(τ); Q ∧ alignment is reported separately (Σ\_mem does not contain alignment).  
Object levels: fixed-object level—the formation version of the current qualification interval of u is the same as in σ₀, and Q holds; existence level—Q holds, and a newly formed interval is allowed to bear it.

## Section 2  The Permitted-Change Domain and Recomputation of Qualification

### 2.1  Permitted-Change Domain

Δ1: any finite sequence of A, B, AB, with teaching signal t = 0.  
Δ2: as above, with t ∈ {0, 1}.  
Δ3: one direct write u := c by the actor, c ∈ D\_c = {k/8 : k = 0, 1, …, 24}, applied between two beats (before the input of the next beat), with no further direct write afterward, continuing as in Δ1. A write equal to the current value produces no event and does not change the version.  
Δ4: one direct write κ := κ′ by the actor, κ′ ∈ {0, 1/4, 1/2, 1}, then continuing as in Δ1; the compound Δ43: first Δ4, then Δ3, once each.  
Gating changes are not in the permitted-change domain. Adjudication cross-sections: after each direct write and at the end of each beat.

### 2.2  Recomputation of Qualification

Qualification is recomputed cross-section by cross-section under the rules of 1.2 and 1.3 of *The Source Form of Generalized Structure*: a different-value direct write within the interval produces a new depth version, and the retention clock restarts from that version; returning to baseline terminates the interval; a direct write outside an interval does not open an interval. At the fixed-object level, identity is judged by the formation version.

## Section 3  Levels of Immediate Preservation and the Substitution Relation

### 3.1  Three Boundaries

(i) Depth boundary: after the direct write u := c, D fails ⟺ c ≤ θ. The largest element of the failure set on D\_c is θ (1 for double-positive, 1/2 for single-positive), attained at that point—D is a strict inequality; on the continuous value range the supremum is likewise θ and is attained.  
(ii) Immediate qualification boundary: every c ≠ u₀ (including c \> θ) is a different-value write, which makes mem(u) fail at the cross-section after the direct write and at the end of the first beat after it, so Q fails immediately; with no subsequent write, retention is restored at the end of the second beat. c = u₀ is a same-value write; it produces no event, and Q is simply preserved (in both domains: c = 3/2 for double-positive, c = 1 for single-positive).  
(iii) Boundary after recovery: see Proposition 2 of Section 4.

### 3.2  Substitution

c = 0 returns u to baseline and terminates the original interval; if a newly formed interval then bears the structure, this is only a substitution at the existence level, not a recovery of the original Trace-Response—every recovery after c = 0 is accompanied by a change of the formation version; existence-level recovery is signed only when a new interval has been legitimately formed and satisfies retention. A direct write with 0 \< c and c ≠ u₀ only replaces the depth version within the same interval; it is a turnover of internal witnesses, not a substitution of the object.

## Section 4  Dynamic Trajectories, Observation Window, and Recovery

4.1  Trajectories and the Decidable Domain  
The legitimate successor dynamics is the beat rule of 𝔐\_I; the observation protocol is the adjudication cross-sections of 2.1; all the finite sequences listed are decidable.

### 4.2  Preservation

Lemma (Zero write)  On a state in which the gates are always open, the weights are nonnegative, and a\_A + a\_B ≥ 1 (the initial states of both domains and the states reachable from them under 2.1 all satisfy this: recruitment weights only increase, never decrease), executing a beat with t = 0 from a cross-section with d \> 0 produces no actual write.  
Proof: On an AB beat z\_I = 1 (a\_A + a\_B ≥ 1, and recruitment weights never decrease), and the membrane drive is w\_A + w\_B − u = 1 − d \< 1, so there is no firing and no inhibitory sedimentation. A single input X, if recruited, has membrane drive w\_X − u ≤ w\_A + w\_B − u \< 1 (weights nonnegative) and does not fire; if it is not recruited, there is no inhibitory sedimentation. With t = 0, excitatory and recruitment sedimentation are zero. ∎  
Proposition 1 (Guaranteed stability under Δ1)  From σ₀ (both domains), under any t = 0 sequence, Q ∧ alignment holds at the fixed-object level at every cross-section.  
Proof: At σ₀, d = 1/2 \> 0; by the Lemma, the host state, the interval, and the version all stay unchanged, and the retention clock only increases. ∎  
For all t = 0 sequences of length at most 8 (9840 per domain), this holds at every cross-section.

### 4.3  Recovery after a Depth Perturbation

Proposition 2 (Beat-order recovery condition)  From σ₀, after the direct write u := c (c ≠ u₀), continue with a t = 0 sequence; κ is the plasticity coefficient at that time.  
(a) c \> θ: D is preserved and the original interval continues; Q ∧ alignment at the fixed-object level first recovers at the end of the 2nd beat after the direct write, independently of the input.  
(b) 0 \< c ≤ θ, κ \> 0: let m\* be the smallest positive integer with c + m*·κ \> θ. The fixed-object level recovers if and only if the m*-th AB beat exists in the sequence and at least two further beats are executed after it; the first recovery cross-section is the end of the second beat after that AB beat.  
(c) c = 0, κ \> 0: the original interval terminates; with m\* computed for c = 0, under the same condition as in (b), recovery occurs only at the existence level (a new interval); the fixed-object level does not recover.  
(d) κ = 0 and c ≤ θ: no recovery—for c \> 0 the depth cannot be made up, and for c = 0 no new interval can form.  
Proof: On an AB beat z(AB) = \[d ≤ 0\], so while u ≤ θ each AB beat increases u by κ, and once u \> θ, u no longer changes; A and B beats do not change u: in this branch t = 0, the recruitment weights keep their entry value 1/2, single inputs are not recruited, and there is no inhibitory sedimentation. The beat that carries u past θ is the last actual write, and the two beats after it satisfy retention; before recovery, at every cross-section either D fails or mem fails. When c = 0 the original interval has already terminated, and the first inhibitory sedimentation that changes u thereafter opens a new interval. ∎  
In both domains, with κ′ unwritten or taking 0, 1/4, 1, with c ranging over the values of D\_c other than u₀, and over all t = 0 continuations of length at most 7, the decision by the beat-order condition agrees case by case with actual recovery cross-section by cross-section; when κ′ = 0, the values of c that do not recover are exactly 0 and (0, θ\] ∩ D\_c.

### 4.4  Timing and Continued Verification

The two clocks are kept apart: the total clock running from the first actual failure (the direct-write cross-section) is never reset; the retention clock runs from the last actual depth write and is reset by each new depth write. Before u crosses the threshold there may be several actual writes—for example, c = 1/4 continued with A, AB, B, AB, A, B: each of the two AB beats writes once and resets retention, and recovery occurs at the end of beat 6. The first recovery cross-section is a stopping time determined by the sequence, not a uniform deadline in beats; this Form declares no uniform recovery deadline.  
Continued verification (two beats): at the recovery cross-section d \> 0, so by the Lemma every subsequent t = 0 continuation preserves, cross-section by cross-section, the Q and alignment status of that cross-section. For a finite trajectory with fewer than two beats after recovery, only first recovery is signed; for one that covers at least two t = 0 beats after recovery, sustained recovery is signed; one that resumes teaching after recovery may not invoke this guarantee—for example, if the beat after recovery is A(t = 1), D fails at once.

### 4.5  Teaching Continuation

Proposition 3 (Gap invariant and teaching failure)  From the initial states of both domains (d = 1/2, κ = 1/2), along any t ∈ {0, 1} sequence, d ≤ 1/2 at every cross-section; and d ≤ 0 at the end of every teaching beat, including teaching beats taken at cross-sections that have already failed.  
Proof: The increment of inhibitory sedimentation in each beat is at most κ = 1/2. A beat with t = 0: when d \> 0 there is no write, by the Lemma; when d ≤ 0, u increases by at most 1/2, so d ≤ 1/2. A single-input X beat with t = 1: θ increases by 1/2; if X is recruited and fires, u also increases by 1/2—in that case the single-input drive does not exceed that of AB, so d ≤ 0 already at the start of the beat, and d ≤ 0 at its end; otherwise d decreases by 1/2, and d ≤ 1/2 gives d ≤ 0 at the end of the beat. An AB beat with t = 1: θ increases by 1 and u by at most 1/2, so d ≤ 0 at the end of the beat. ∎  
For all t ∈ {0, 1} sequences of length at most 5 (9330 per domain, 44790 cross-sections): d ≤ 1/2 holds everywhere; D fails at the end of all 22395 teaching beats.  
Whole-trajectory guarantee bound: there is no universal failure bound in a finite number of beats—the t = 0 sequences of the same length are all preserved (Proposition 1).  
Recovery after failure is reported separately for Q and for Q ∧ alignment: among the sequences of length at most 5, those in which Q at the fixed-object level has recovered at the final cross-section number 171 each for double-positive and single-positive; among them, those in which Q ∧ alignment has recovered number 171 for double-positive and 81 for single-positive. In the single-positive domain, w\_A ≥ 1 for the positive input A is guaranteed by the entry value and the monotonicity of excitatory sedimentation, and AB is held down by D; when Q holds, alignment holds if and only if the negative single input B satisfies w\_B \< 1 or a\_B ≥ 1 (when a\_B ≥ 1, B is recruited and, by D and w\_A ≥ 1, is inhibited). This criterion agrees one by one with actual alignment at every cross-section where Q holds. Example: in the single-positive domain, B(t = 1), AB, A, A—Q recovers at the end of beat 4, but w\_B = 1 and a\_B = 3/4, so B is not recruited yet fires: not aligned.  
Irrecoverable: a\_X ≥ 1 for a positive input X makes R2 fail permanently (recruitment weights only increase, never decrease).

### 4.6  Law-Layer Perturbation

A direct write under Δ4 changes neither u nor the interval, so Q is unchanged; it changes only m\* and case (d) of Proposition 2 thereafter. Δ43: when κ′ = 0, c ≤ θ does not recover (c = 0 cannot form a new interval either); a different-value direct write with c \> θ and c ≠ u₀ recovers after two beats of retention, Q and Q ∧ alignment at the fixed-object level both recover, and continued verification under Δ1 applies; a same-value write causes no failure; when κ′ ≠ 0, substitute the actual κ′ and compute by Proposition 2. The single-positive task is computed separately with its own threshold and initial state.

## Section 5  Stability Boundaries and Level Quantifiers

Boundaries: the supremum of the failure set of D is θ and is attained; the immediate qualification failure set is all values of D\_c other than u₀, and c = u₀ preserves; recovery is given by Proposition 2; under Δ2 the earliest possible failure is at absolute beat 1 (teaching at the first beat), the first failure of a given trajectory is at the actual beat number of that trajectory's first teaching beat (teaching may be delayed), and no whole-trajectory guarantee bound exists.  
Quantifiers:  
\- Δ1: at all cross-sections of all legitimate trajectories, the same Trace-Response u bears Q ∧ alignment (∃x∀t form).  
\- Δ3 (κ \> 0): preserved when c = u₀; for different-value writes there exist recovering trajectories; first recovery holds on the family of trajectories satisfying the beat-order condition of Proposition 2, and sustained recovery on those among them that then cover two t = 0 beats; it does not hold over all legitimate trajectories (e.g., an all-A continuation does not recover when c ≤ θ). When c = 0 there is only existence-level recovery: after recovery there is a qualifying Trace-Response at every cross-section of the certified continuation; this is not the fixed-object-level ∃x∀t, nor does it cover the failure segment before recovery.  
\- Δ2: at all teaching beats of all trajectories, D fails at the end of the beat; recovery is reported separately for Q and Q ∧ alignment, with no uniform deadline.  
\- Δ43 (κ′ = 0): c ≤ θ does not recover; c \> θ with c ≠ u₀ recovers after two beats; c = u₀ preserves.

## Section 6  Stability Theorem and Declaration of Strength

Stability Theorem  Within 𝔐\_I, D\_mem, the initial states of the two domains, and the permitted-change domain of 2.1:  
(i) under Δ1 the fixed object has guaranteed stability (Proposition 1);  
(ii) under Δ3 a same-value write preserves; a different-value depth perturbation immediately makes the qualification fail, and recovery follows the beat-order condition of Proposition 2; for c = 0 there is only substitutive recovery at the existence level (when a new interval is legitimately formed and satisfies retention);  
(iii) under Δ2, D fails at the end of every teaching beat (Proposition 3); recovery after failure is reported separately for Q and Q ∧ alignment, the single-positive domain additionally requires the negative single-input criterion, and recovery is impossible when a\_X ≥ 1 for a positive input;  
(iv) persistence after recovery is limited to t = 0 continuations (Zero-Write Lemma), and no uniform recovery deadline is declared;  
(v) Δ4 and Δ43 are as in 4.6.  
Proof: Sections 3 and 4. ∎  
Strength:  
\- Limited to the declared host, the two tasks, the initial states of the two domains, and the permitted-change domain of 2.1; nothing is signed for other hosts, three or more inputs, gating changes, or other initial states.  
\- The case-by-case checks on finite sequences serve only as witnesses; the universal conclusions are borne by the proofs of Propositions 1–3.  
H23-1  The declaration that the host's mechanism routes are exhaustive is truthful; weights are nonnegative.  
H23-2  Retention window of 2 beats; baseline taken as the entry value (as in H20-2 of *The Source Form of Generalized Structure*).
