# The Reachability Form of Host-Adjustment-Law Candidates in Spiking Networks

## Section 1  Declaration of the Initial State and the Permitted Operations

### 1.1  Question

The actor does not directly write the connection a Law needs; it only rewrites one plasticity parameter of a spiking network and supplies teaching input, and the target Trace-Response is formed by the network's own activity-dependent plasticity. We ask: starting from a point that does not yet have the qualification of the target Law, can such host adjustment reach a target Law frozen in advance? By what path, and in how many steps? After the act is removed, does this Law still bear within the network's own running, and can it be re-traversed? Under which restricted protocols is it unreachable?

The target qualification of this Form is the Law-candidate tier: it is judged by the criterion Cand stated explicitly in Section 3. The gap between the criteria required for the formal Law tier and the criterion of this Form is listed in Section 6 and is not filled by this Form.

### 1.2  The Regulated Network N

N is a spiking network at the algorithmic level, with discrete beats and rational values: inputs A, B; an output unit z; synapses syn\_A, syn\_B with host values wA, wB; a law-layer plasticity parameter κ, belonging to the structural component rule\_κ. Each beat runs in order:  
　Input: sA, sB are given by the letter (A = (1, 0), B = (0, 1), AB = (1, 1), rest = (0, 0)); the teaching current I is given from outside the boundary;  
　Integration: vt = λ·v + wA·sA + wB·sB + I;  
　Firing: z = \[vt ≥ θ\]; on firing v := 0, otherwise v := vt;  
　Hebbian sedimentation: wX := min(hi, wX + κ·sX·z), X ∈ {A, B}.  
Parameters λ = 1/2, θ = 1, hi = 2. Levels: wA, wB, κ belong to the host level (θ layer); v, vt, z, sA, sB, I belong to the run level.

### 1.3  Initial State

wA = wB = 1/4, κ = 0, v = 0.

1.4  Permitted Operations and the Three Ledgers

There are only two kinds of external operation: direct write and input. Sedimentation is a dynamic step of N itself, not an external operation. The three are booked separately:  
　(i) Direct transformation: the actor writes κ through the direct-write port (landing on the law-layer plasticity parameter, not on the target Trace-Response wA); the writable values of κ are {0, 1/4, 1/2};  
　(ii) Teaching input: letters and the teaching current are sent in through the input port; the letter is one of the four A, B, AB, rest, and the teaching current I ∈ {0, 1};  
　(iii) Sedimentation: N's own Hebbian writes.  
The permitted operation set 𝒪 consists of (i) and (ii) under the permitted-value table above, together with the dynamic steps of N.  
Directly setting wA or wB, and erasure, are not in 𝒪. The mechanism routes of N (integration, firing, Hebbian, input port, direct-write port) are declared exhaustive.  
The target Trace-Response wA can be changed only by (iii): (i) writes only κ, and (ii) writes only the run level.

1.5  Target Law and Adjudication Cross-Sections

The target Law χ\_L = (L, I, Rel(L), Γ) is frozen in advance:  
　L: if A arrives (with no teaching current), z fires;  
　Domain of validity I = {A};  
　Rel(L): a single duty d, whose antecedent is that A arrives with I = 0, whose leading term is {wA}, whose read-out is z, and which is deterministic;  
　Γ: the criterion Cand of Section 3.  
Declared tendency: Φ := wA; declared tendency change: Φ from 1/4 to at least θ.  
Candidate bearing domain: all 7 nonempty subsets of Existing = {syn\_A, syn\_B, rule\_κ}.  
Adjudication cross-sections: the entry, and the state after each event (direct write, each beat).

## Section 2  Construction of the Path

### 2.1  Main Path (Keep Arm)

In one and the same run, execute in order:  
　Before beat 1: direct write κ := 1/4;  
　Beats 1–3: teaching (A, I = 1);  
　Withdrawal point (before beat 4): from here on, teaching input (I constantly 0) and all direct writes stop; κ keeps 1/4;  
　Continuation after withdrawal, beats 4–11: A, rest, B, A, AB, rest, rest, A.  
Beat by beat: beat 1, vt = 1/4 + 1 ≥ 1, fires, wA := 1/4 + 1/4 = 1/2; beat 2, vt = 1/2 + 1, wA := 3/4; beat 3, vt = 3/4 + 1, wA := 1. After withdrawal: beat 4, vt = 1, fires, wA := 5/4; beat 6 (B), vt = 1/4 \< 1, v = 1/4; beat 7 (A), vt = 1/8 + 5/4, fires, wA := 3/2; beat 8 (AB) fires, wA := 7/4, wB := 1/2; beat 11 (A) fires, wA := 2. Every A beat after withdrawal fires.  
Actual number of steps: 1 direct write, 3 teaching beats.

### 2.2  Retraction Arm

As the main path, except that at the withdrawal point there is an additional direct write κ := 0. This one write belongs to the withdrawal declaration; it is booked separately and is not counted as an act after withdrawal. After withdrawal no beat has a sedimentation write, wA stays at 1, and every A beat fires.

### 2.3  Control Arm

The same teaching, with no direct write; κ keeps its entry value 0. The teaching beats fire (vt = 1/4 + 1), but the sedimentation writes the same value and wA stays at 1/4; after withdrawal, an arriving A does not fire.

### 2.4  The Three Ledgers

\| Arm \| Direct transformation \| Teaching input (I = 1) \| Sedimentation (actual changes) \| Direct setting of w \|  
\| Keep \| κ := 1/4 (before beat 1) \| beats 1–3 \| wA: 1/2, 3/4, 1, 5/4, 3/2, 7/4, 2; wB: 1/2 \| none \|  
\| Retraction \| κ := 1/4; κ := 0 at the withdrawal point \| beats 1–3 \| wA: 1/2, 3/4, 1 \| none \|  
\| Control \| none \| beats 1–3 \| none \| none \|

End points (wA, κ): keep (2, 1/4); retraction (1, 0); control (1/4, 0). The differences between end points serve only as corroboration; the effect of the direct write is borne by the version chain of 2.5.

2.5  The Bearing Chain of the Direct Write

The direct write leaves the version κ\|1 of κ. The three sedimentations of the teaching segment write wA = 1/2, 3/4, 1; each reads in κ\|1, the dependence of the written value on κ is true, and the sources of the versions written all contain κ\|1; the same holds for the sedimentations after withdrawal. The integration of beat 4, after withdrawal, reads in wA\|3 (written by the sedimentation of beat 3), and the firing reads in that beat's integration version, with dependence on it true. Version path:

　κ\|1 —value-write→ wA\|3 —value-write→ vt\|4 → z

The effect of the direct write is thereby connected to the declared tendency change (the three sedimentations taking wA from 1/4 to 1 all have κ\|1 as a source and depend on κ) and to the generation of the target Law (the generation chain of the read-out after withdrawal leads back through wA to κ\|1). The direct write lands on the physical layer of N, and the bearing of the tendency starts from this direct write; the successor route runs through N's own sedimentation. The control arm, with the same teaching and no direct write, does not form the target Trace-Response: pure teaching input plus sedimentation does not constitute this path.

## Section 3  The Law-Candidate Criterion and Adjudication by Cross-Section

### 3.1  The Criterion Cand

K0 (Carrying)  R ⊆ Existing, R ≠ ∅.  
K1 (Sole bearing)  In the context (A arrives, I = 0), with the quantities outside R taking all their valid values, z is constantly 1. Valid domain: v ∈ \[0, θ) (Lemma 0), taking the whole interval; w outside R in \[0, hi\]; κ in the writable set {0, 1/4, 1/2}—κ does not enter the integration read-out, and its value does not affect K1.  
K1′ (Minimality)  R satisfies K1, and no proper subset of R satisfies K1.  
K2 (Non-immediacy)  The components of R all belong to the host level and are retained under freezing (stripping the immediate drive of the run level, v := 0); K1 holds in the frozen context.  
K3 (In-field formation)  The current versions of the bearing components in R were written by sedimentation events, and their sources contain input-port events; K1 does not hold at the entry cross-section.  
Cross-section qualification: Cand(R, t) = K0 ∧ K1′ ∧ K2 ∧ K3.  
Reported in addition after withdrawal:  
K4 (Real continuation)  In the actual running of N after withdrawal (with sedimentation as usual), every A beat fires, and the solution of Cand at each cross-section is unchanged.  
K5 (Re-traversal)  Two protocols are reported separately, both starting from the withdrawal point and the ends of the 8 beats after it, 9 cross-sections in all: (D) diagnostic protocol—from the frozen state at each cross-section, with plasticity frozen, run A, rest, A in turn, and the A beats fire; (C) real-continuation protocol—from each cross-section take the full state (including κ), with sedimentation as usual, execute all 256 sequences in {A, B, AB, rest}⁴ (I = 0), and every A beat fires.

### 3.2  Adjudication of K1

Lemma 0  Under 𝒪, from the initial state of 1.3, v ∈ \[0, θ) at the end of every beat.  
Proof: At the entry v = 0. Suppose v ≥ 0 before a beat; the letter components, the teaching current, and w are all nonnegative (writes to w only add nonnegative amounts and are clipped to \[0, hi\]), so vt = λ·v + wA·sA + wB·sB + I ≥ 0; on firing v := 0, otherwise v := vt \< θ. Induction on the number of beats. ∎  
Exhaustive enumeration over the full permitted-value table from the entry for up to 8 beats (8400 states, including the entry) agrees with Lemma 0: the minimum of v is 0 and its maximum 255/256. Negative currents outside the permitted-value table are not in 𝒪; Lemma 0 and the universal argument for K1 both presuppose 𝒪.

Lemma 1  On the valid domain of 3.1, K1 holds if and only if firing occurs at the actual values and vt ≥ θ when each free quantity takes its infimum.  
Proof: vt = λ·v + wA·sA + wB·sB, and λ, sA, sB are nonnegative, so vt is monotonically nondecreasing in each of v, wA, wB; κ does not enter vt. The infimum of each component of the valid domain is attained (v = 0, w = 0), so the least favorable values are the infima. ∎  
Corollary  In the context (A arrives, I = 0), an R containing syn\_A satisfies K1 if and only if wA ≥ θ; an R not containing syn\_A does not satisfy K1 (the free wA can take 0 and v can take 0, giving vt = 0). Hence the minimal solution of K1′, if it exists, is unique, namely {syn\_A}.  
Immediate drive cannot bear in its stead: three consecutive AB beats (κ = 0, I = 0) give v = 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, all within the valid domain; after teaching beat 2, wA = 3/4, and with v = 7/8 an arriving A fires, yet {syn\_A} does not satisfy K1. That cross-section does not have Cand.

3.3  Negative Determination at the Starting Point

At the entry cross-section wA = 1/4: when the free quantities take their infima, vt = 1/4 \< θ, so no candidate containing syn\_A satisfies K1, and candidates not containing syn\_A fail all the more. None of the 7 candidates has Cand. This condition blocks the whole candidate bearing domain, and the starting-point negative determination takes the value 0.

### 3.4  First Acquisition

Keep arm and retraction arm: after the direct write and after teaching beats 1 and 2, Cand has no solution; after teaching beat 3, wA = 1, and Cand({syn\_A}) holds for the first time—K1′ by the Corollary; K2: syn\_A belongs to the host level, and in the frozen context vt = 1 ≥ θ; K3: the current version of wA was written by the sedimentation of beat 3, its source contains input events of the teaching segment, and K1 does not hold at the entry cross-section. At every earlier cross-section of the same trajectory, Cand is empty.  
Control arm: Cand has no solution at any cross-section.  
rule\_κ is not in the minimal solution: κ is not on the generation chain of z; its bearing lies in the formation history (2.5), not in the current duty.

## Section 4  Three Items after Withdrawal

### 4.1  Withdrawal Declaration

At the withdrawal point, teaching input and all direct writes stop; the keep arm retains κ = 1/4; the retraction arm writes κ := 0 at the withdrawal point, which is counted in the withdrawal declaration.

### 4.2  Continued Running

After withdrawal, neither arm has direct-write events or inputs with I = 1; the field rules of N run as usual: in the keep arm every A beat fires and sedimentation continues (wA rises from 5/4 to 2, and on the AB beat wB rises from 1/4 to 1/2); in the retraction arm firing proceeds as usual with no sedimentation writes (wA stays at 1).

### 4.3  Preservation of Bearing (K4)

At the 8 cross-sections after withdrawal, the solution of Cand in both arms is constantly {syn\_A}; in actual running, every A beat without exception fires.

### 4.4  Re-traversal (K5)

(D) Diagnostic protocol: in each arm, the frozen state of each of the 9 cross-sections (the withdrawal point and the ends of the 8 beats after withdrawal) is run with A, rest, A, and all A beats fire.  
(C) Real-continuation protocol: in each arm, each of the same 9 cross-sections executes the 256 four-beat sequences, 2304 in all, and every A beat without exception fires. The real continuation of the keep arm includes continued sedimentation with κ retained, and is reported separately from the diagnostic protocol.  
The retraction arm shows that bearing and re-traversal after withdrawal do not depend on the retained plasticity parameter. After κ := 0, wA stays at 1, and K4 and K5 hold all the same.

## Section 5  Reachability Theorem and Unreachability Boundaries

### 5.1  Reachability Theorem

Theorem  Under N, the frozen χ\_L, the criterion Cand, and the permitted set 𝒪 (the permitted-value table of 1.4: four letters, I ∈ {0, 1}, κ writable in {0, 1/4, 1/2}), from the initial state of 1.3 (in which no member of the candidate bearing domain has Cand) there exists a finite legitimate path—1 direct write of κ and 3 teaching beats—that first acquires Cand (carried by {syn\_A}) after teaching beat 3 of the same run; after withdrawal, Cand is preserved cross-section by cross-section in the real continuation of N, A beats all fire, and re-traversal holds under both the diagnostic and the real-continuation protocols. Through the version chain of 2.5, the effect of the direct write bears the tendency change and the generation of the target Law; the same teaching without the direct write does not form them.  
Proof: The path and its beat-by-beat values are in 2.1 and 2.2, and all operations used are within the permitted-value table; the starting-point negative determination is in 3.3; first acquisition in 3.4; the items after withdrawal in Section 4, with the adjudication of K1 borne by Lemma 0, Lemma 1, and its Corollary. ∎

### 5.2  Unreachability

Proposition 1 (Plasticity frozen)  When κ may not be written and stays at 0, with letters from all four and I ∈ {0, 1}, wA stays at 1/4 and Cand is unreachable.  
Proof: When κ = 0 the Hebbian write always writes the same value. ∎  
The argument that the weight does not change holds for any value of the teaching current; the adjudication of Cand still takes the permitted-value table of 1.4 as its domain; the two are reported separately.

Proposition 2 (Insufficient family of teaching inputs)  When κ is writable (taking 0, 1/4, or 1/2) and letters are from all four, but I is constantly 0, z never fires and Cand is unreachable.  
Proof: When wA = wB = 1/4, if v \< 1 then v′ ≤ v/2 + 1/2 \< 1; at the entry v = 0. By induction on the number of beats, vt \< 1, z does not fire, the Hebbian write writes the same value, and wA stays at 1/4. ∎

Finite exhaustive enumeration from the entry agrees with both propositions (counts include the entry state): the protocol of Proposition 1 (κ constantly 0, four letters, I ∈ {0, 1}) has, for up to 8 beats, 511 reachable states; the protocol of Proposition 2 (κ ∈ {0, 1/4, 1/2}, four letters, I = 0) has, for up to 8 beats, 1533 reachable states; neither has any state with wA ≥ θ, and the maximum of v is 255/256. The fully permitted sub-protocol (κ ∈ {0, 1/4}, letters A and rest, I ∈ {0, 1}) has, for up to 4 beats, 57 reachable states (the entry and end-of-beat states), among which there are states with wA ≥ θ. The finite counts serve only as checks and do not replace the inductions of the two propositions. The two are unreachability results on specific restricted protocols and do not exhaust all causes of unreachability over the full permitted domain.

## Section 6  Declaration of Strength

\- Supported reachability: a finite legitimate path exists; this is not upgraded to controllability, universality, or probability one.  
\- The target qualification is the Law-candidate tier, under the explicitly stated criterion Cand. The formal Law tier is not signed; a formal Law is not signed.  
\- The three places at which the formal Law tier and Cand are not closed are kept as unanswered:  
　(i) stable re-traversal must read the stable re-traversal capacity Ω\_t over the required continuation segment (*The Mathematical Form of Law*, Definition 3.29); the firing protocol of K5 does not replace it;  
　(ii) Bind must be witnessed by one and the same re-entry realization that also satisfies the Ω witness and the realization-family condition (Definitions 3.44, 3.45);  
　(iii) the bridging of restricted evaluation, anchoring, chain closure, and the bearing set (Definitions 3.18–3.23).  
　All three lie within the formal solution and cannot be supplied by read-back antecedents or by SC\_Hold.  
\- The direct write lands on the physical layer of N, and the bearing of the tendency starts from this direct write; this Form does not sign the event qualification of host adjustment (the goal condition among the six duty groups is not modeled).  
\- No biological neural mechanism is signed.  
H24-1  The declaration that the mechanism routes of N are exhaustive is truthful.  
H24-2  The reading Φ := wA is given by declaration.
