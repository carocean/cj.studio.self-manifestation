# The Construction Form of Layer-by-Layer Manifestation of Overlapping Histories

## Section 1  Declaration of the Condition Group

### 1.1  Question

*The General Form of Tuning* states that tuning opens and closes existing pathways by suppression and disinhibition and adjusts their degree of permeability, and that the dynamic logical structure so formed is a subset of the current Trace-Response-set structure; bearing originates in the gating change, and the route after it is unrestricted. The phenomena of the Field Chapters go a step further: a single learning history overlaps in the field into gradations of depth, new learning lies on top, and the deeper receives the flow first; to retrieve content that arrived earlier, tuning must converge the route the flow passes through, and the earlier the content arrived, the more tuning it costs. The question is this: on a spiking sedimentation field that contains only generic mechanisms and in which no phenomenon rule is written, can one explicitly construct an overlapping history whose final state, under the same input, outputs the new learning by default and, under tuning convergence, retrieves the earlier-arrived content layer by layer; and is what is retrieved really borne by that history?

1.2  Host 𝔐\_S (Spiking Sedimentation Field)

Structural Positions: 24 channels c = 0, …, 23, each with one Sensory-Device R\_c and one integrate-and-fire field element F\_c.  
Trace-Responses: entry pathways w\_c (R\_c → F\_c, initial value w0 = 0.7, upper bound 1.6); inter-channel Gully-Lines W\_{ij} (F\_j → F\_i, initial value 0.02, upper bound 0.5, W\_{ii} = 0).  
Constants: η = 0.001; decay λ = 0.0001; zero-sum inhibition coefficient 0.1; membrane retention 0.7.  
No phenomenon rule of any kind is written in — Permeation and Sedimentary Saturation, depth thresholds, layers, default routes, rigidification, and so on; tuning is given no plasticity.

1.2.1  Discrete Recursion (presentations with plasticity on, during learning and use; 30 beats per presentation)  
At the start of each presentation, the phase accumulator a, the membrane potential v, the activity trace y, and the previous-beat firing s are all reset to 0. For t = 1, …, 30:  
(1) a ← a + r; Sensory-Device firing σ = \[a ≥ 1\]; a ← a − σ.  
(2) v ← 0.7·v + w ⊙ σ + W·s − 0.1·Σs − g; v ← max(v, 0). (g is the suppression vector of this presentation, and is 0 when no suppression is applied.)  
(3) Field-element firing s′ = \[v ≥ 1\]; for those that fire, v := 0; y ← 0.8·y + s′.  
(4) Potentiating plasticity: w ← w + η·σ ⊙ y ⊙ (1.6 − w)/1.6; then decay w ← w − λ·(w − w0).  
(5) W ← W + η·(y s^T) ⊙ (0.5 − W)/0.5 (diagonal set to 0); then decay W ← W − λ·(W − 0.02) (diagonal set to 0).  
(6) s ← s′; record the firing counts and the first-firing beat.  
With plasticity off, (4) and (5) are omitted.

1.2.2  Continuous Read-Out Recursion (plasticity off; 30 beats, 10 substeps per beat)  
At the start of a read-out, v and s are reset to 0. The input rate is 1.2·r (continuous drive), and the membrane retention per substep is 0.7^{1/10}. For substeps n = 0, …, 299:  
v ← 0.7^{1/10}·v + (w ⊙ 1.2r − g\_n)/10 + W·s − 0.1·Σs; v ← max(v, 0); s′ = \[v ≥ 1\], and for those that fire, v := 0; s ← s′; the first-firing time is recorded as beat (n + 1)/10.  
W feedback and zero-sum inhibition are computed at every substep with the original coefficients; entry drive and suppression are both divided by 10. This recursion contains no write term for w or W; read-out does not change the Trace-Responses.

1.3  Signal Source, Learning, and Read-Out

Signal source: one sound source. Content A: the fundamental {6}; B: the fundamental plus overtones {6, 12, 18}; C: a syllable with formants added {6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21}.  
Stray input r(X, k): first, for each channel c of the content channel set X, place stray components beside c according to pattern k mod 4 — pattern 0: left 1, right 1, left 2, right 2; pattern 1: left 1, left 2; pattern 2: right 1, right 2; pattern 3: left 1, right 1; rate 0.5 on the channels at ±1 and 0.3 on those at ±2; channels outside 0–23 are clipped; where stray components overlap, the maximum is taken. Then every content channel is set to 0.5 (overriding stray components).  
Learning: the j-th presentation (j = 0, …, 19) for learning content X uses r(X, j) and the suppression vector g = 1.0 (on the channels outside X) and 0 (on the channels inside X), with plasticity on per 1.2.1.  
Use-time tuning law: simulated by an external program; given a target zone Z (zone B {6, 12, 18} or zone A {6}), g\_n = g (on the channels outside Z) and 0 (on the channels inside Z); the in-field training of the tuning law is outside this Form.  
Default read-out: Z is all channels (no suppression), per 1.2.2, 30 beats.  
Scan read-out: g ranges over the grid {0, 0.05, …, 2.0}; each point restarts from the initial read-out state, per 1.2.2.  
Continuous suppression: within a single read-out, g\_n = 0.8·n/300 (n = 0, …, 299; the final value is less than 0.8); the output is recorded every 20 substeps (2 beats).

### 1.4  Criteria

Significant layers: take the entry depths w\_c strictly greater than w0 + 0.02, round each value to three decimal places, remove duplicates, and sort in descending order; starting from the deepest, proceed in turn — if the previous retained representative value minus the current value is strictly greater than 0.02, retain the current value as a new layer, otherwise merge it into the previous layer; this gives representative values d\_1 \> d\_2 \> …. Level sets Λ\_k = {c: w\_c ≥ d\_k − 0.02} (membership is decided on the unrounded w), numbered from the inside out.  
Output: with no firing, record ⊥; with firing, take the smallest level set containing all firing channels; if no level set contains them, record “beyond all level sets.” The criteria read only the entry-depth profile and the read-out record, not layer labels.  
Actual sedimentation set: the set of channels whose w at the end of a learning segment exceeds its value at the start of the segment by more than 0.01 (segment-level net write-back, not version-by-version dependence).  
Level set ↔ formation segment: when a level set equals “the intersection of the actual sedimentation sets of all segments from that segment onward,” the level set is recorded as corresponding to that segment; where there is no unique match, the actual correspondence is reported; this does not enter the output criterion.

### 1.5  Condition Group

C1 The host is as declared in 1.2, with not a single phenomenon rule written in.  
C2 Formation history: learn A, B, C in turn, 20 times each, 30 beats each time, with learning-time tuning as in 1.3.  
C3 Use: the stray C input r(C, 0) is the common input for the layer-by-layer causal controls; broadband input (rate 0.3 on every channel) serves as an independent test.  
C4 Tuning law: simulated by an external program; the target zone and the suppression strength are given by the program.  
C5 Separate signing: formation carry-over; default output; layer-by-layer output under tuning convergence; order of firing; Trace-Responses unchanged within the read-out window; the net write-back of the two protocols, learning and use, and the default output after them.

## Section 2  Construction Procedure

Step 1 (host): set 𝔐\_S to its initial state per 1.2.  
Step 2 (formation): learn A, B, C in turn (C2, per 1.3 and 1.2.1); for each segment, record the presentation count of positive input rate on each channel, the field-element firing counts, w at the start and end of the segment, and ΔW (the maximal elementwise net increase at the end of the segment relative to its start), and compute the actual sedimentation set.  
Step 3 (layers and correspondence): per 1.4, compute the significant layers and level sets from the final-state entry depths, and compute level set ↔ formation segment from the cumulative overlap of the actual sedimentation sets.  
Step 4 (default read-out): per the default read-out of 1.3, read out once with each of the two inputs of C3.  
Step 5 (tuning-law scan): per the scan read-out of 1.3, with target zones B and A and input r(C, 0); record how the output varies with g and the first grid point that hits the target zone.  
Step 6 (single continuous suppression): per the continuous suppression of 1.3, with target zones A and B and input r(C, 0).  
Step 7 (read-out window check): the read-out recursion of 1.2.2 contains no write term for w or W, so no read-out (default, each scan point, continuous suppression) changes the Trace-Responses; in addition, w and W are actually checked to be elementwise equal before and after the default read-out with stray C input.  
Step 8 (the two protocols, learning and use): starting from two copies of the same final state, with plasticity on per 1.2.1 — use: input r(C, 0), suppression 0.5 outside zone A, one presentation of 30 beats; learning: sound A, r(A, j) (j = 0, …, 19), suppression 1.0 outside zone A, 20 presentations × 30 beats; for each, record the absolute endpoint net change of w and W (maximum and element sum) and the subsequent default output.  
Step 9 (controls): delete learning A (learn only B and C); learn only C, three rounds; learning without tuning; frozen plasticity; matched entry direct write (the final-state w and W written directly into the initial state, with no formation history). Read each out per Steps 3–5.

### 3.2  Layers and Correspondence

Significant layers d = 1.204, 1.067, 0.884; Λ\_1 = {6}, Λ\_2 = {6, 12, 18}, Λ\_3 = the six C channels. By the cumulative overlap of the actual sedimentation sets: Λ\_1 ↔ A, Λ\_2 ↔ B, Λ\_3 ↔ C.

### 3.3  Default Output

Stray C input: the output is the six C channels, Λ\_3; first firing: fundamental at beat 1.9, overtones at beat 2.0, formants at beat 2.1.  
Broadband input (independent test; equal strength on every channel, containing none of the learned content): the output is the six C channels, Λ\_3; first firing: fundamental at beat 4.7, overtones at beat 4.8, formants at beat 4.9.  
Under both inputs, the first-firing order in this example is core, overtones, outer rim, consistent with the order of depth; this Form does not separately isolate the causal contribution of w relative to the input or W.

3.4  Layer-by-Layer Output under Tuning Convergence (Scan)  
Zone B: at g = 0 the output is Λ\_3; the first hitting grid point is 0.45, with output Λ\_2.  
Zone A: at g = 0 the output is Λ\_3; at g = 0.35 the output is Λ\_2; the first hitting grid point is 0.50, with output Λ\_1.  
In scan order the three layers are read out in turn (C, B, A); the first hitting point for retrieving A is higher than that for B. This is the read-out under these parameters, inputs, and grid; continuous minimal suppression and energy cost are not signed.

### 3.5  Single Continuous Suppression

Zone A: the outputs of the successive 2-beat time windows are Λ\_2, Λ\_3, Λ\_3, Λ\_3, Λ\_1 (g ≈ 0.26), Λ\_3, Λ\_2 (g ≈ 0.37), and Λ\_1 from g ≈ 0.42 until the end of this read-out.  
Zone B: Λ\_2, Λ\_3, Λ\_3, Λ\_3, Λ\_3, Λ\_2, Λ\_3, and Λ\_2 from g ≈ 0.42 until the end.  
In the transitional time windows the output moves back and forth between layers; in the later part of this run it holds the target layer. Monotone step-by-step ascent is not signed, nor is indefinite stability.

### 3.6  Read-Out Window Check

Before and after every read-out, w and W are elementwise equal.

## Section 4  Controls and Failure Branches

\| Control \| Level set ↔ formation segment \| Output aimed at zone A \| First hitting point, zone A/zone B \|  
\| Delete learning A \| Λ\_1 = {6, 12, 18} ↔ B; Λ\_2 ↔ C \| {6}, assigned to Λ\_1 ↔ B \| 0.45/0.45 \|  
\| Matched entry direct write \| three layers, none with a corresponding formation segment \| Λ\_1 \| 0.50/0.45 \|  
\| Learn only C, three rounds \| one layer ↔ C \| {6}, assigned to Λ\_1 ↔ C \| 0.80/zone B not retrievable within the scan range \|  
\| Learning without tuning \| layers do not correspond to formation segments (stray channels scour-deepened) \| — \| 0.65/zone B not retrievable \|  
\| Frozen plasticity \| no significant layer \| output beyond all level sets \| 0.10/0.10 \|  
Reading: with learning A deleted, the inner layer goes to B, so the independent A layer depends on the formation of segment A; the matched direct write reproduces only the readings, not the formation carry-over; with only C learned there can be a single layer, but no multiple layers to separate; without learning-time tuning the stray channels still actually deepen, and the B formation layer cannot be separated under these criteria; with plasticity frozen there can be firing but no significant sedimentation layer, and the output is not assigned to a layer under these criteria.

Failure branches: learning without tuning — the B formation layer cannot be separated under these criteria, the stray channels deepen, and layers do not correspond one-to-one to formation segments; an overlapping history of only one segment — there can be a single layer, but no multiple layers to separate; frozen plasticity — no significant sedimentation layer, and the output is not assigned to a layer.

## Section 5  Construction Theorem

Theorem (Explicit construction of layer-by-layer manifestation of overlapping histories)  Under 𝔐\_S and the signal source, learning, read-out, and tuning law declared in Section 1, the construction procedure of Section 2 gives an overlapping history whose final state satisfies:  
(1) Layers are borne by the overlapping history: the level sets of the three significant layers of the final state equal, in turn, the cumulative overlaps of the actual sedimentation sets from segments A, B, C onward; with segment A deleted, the independent innermost layer disappears;  
(2) New learning on top: under stray C input and broadband input, the default output is in both cases Λ\_3, which corresponds to segment C; in this example the first-firing order is core, overtones, outer rim (stray C: beats 1.9, 2.0, 2.1; broadband: beats 4.7, 4.8, 4.9), and the causal contribution of w is not separately isolated;  
(3) Layer-by-layer manifestation: with the tuning law aimed at zone B and zone A, the scan read-out retrieves Λ\_2 and Λ\_1, corresponding to segments B and A respectively, and the first hitting grid point for retrieving A is higher than that for B;  
(4) Read-out does not change the Trace-Responses: w and W are unchanged within the read-out window;  
(5) Controls: the matched direct write reproduces the readings without formation carry-over; learning only C gives no multiple layers to separate; without learning-time tuning the B formation layer cannot be separated; frozen plasticity gives no significant sedimentation layer.  
Proof: Section 3 gives, item by item, the formation record, the layers and correspondence, and the default and scan read-outs of the main instance; (4) follows from the absence of write terms in 1.2.2 and is actually checked before and after the default read-out with stray C input; Section 4 gives the controls. Each item is the result of deterministic computation under the declared host, parameters, and inputs, and the scope of the theorem is limited to this fixed instance. ∎

## Section 6  Declaration of Strength

\- Construction strength; the scope of the theorem is limited to the declared fixed instance: host 𝔐\_S, a single sound source, a one-dimensional channel arrangement, and the recursions, parameters, and inputs given in Section 1; the parameters are given in advance and are not fitted.  
\- The tuning law is simulated by an external program (with preset channel target zones); the in-field training of the tuning law and formal tuning-event qualification (six duty groups, Gate, Bear\_G, GoalCond) are not signed.  
\- Layer-by-layer manifestation is signed as a scan read-out; for single continuous suppression, only the final stable output within a finite time window is signed.  
\- Formation carry-over is segment-level net write-back with counterfactual controls, not a beat-by-beat event log or version-by-version dependence.  
\- The two protocols, learning and use, report only net write-back and the subsequent default output, with no attribution to a single factor.  
H27-1  The declaration that the mechanism routes of 𝔐\_S are exhaustive is truthful (Sensory-Devices, field elements, entry Trace-Responses, Gully-Lines, zero-sum inhibition, tuning suppression).  
H27-2  The output and level-set criteria are given by declaration.
