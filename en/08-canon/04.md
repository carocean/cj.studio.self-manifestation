# General Form of the Auxiliary Forms

I. General Proposition

This chapter attempts a general proposition:

Every form system that has actually executed a principal-form task and thereby produced attached tasks must minimally possess a formal auxiliary layer standing alongside the principal layer.

Attached tasks are tasks whose propositions depend for their standing on attachment to another Form's established structure (Canon Chapter 3, Section III). The working domain of this chapter is restricted to: at least one principal-form task has actually been executed and has produced one or more admitted attached tasks. This condition is written ActiveDep(T). This chapter proves:

form system(T) ∧ ActiveDep(T) ⇒ auxiliary layer(T).

ActiveDep(T) is the working-domain condition of this chapter's general proposition, not an H awaiting verification.

II. Version Positioning and Declaration of the Degree of Answer

The general proposition of this chapter decomposes into six sub-problems, each proven to the following degree:

(1) Within the working domain ActiveDep(T), do attached tasks exist and require a bearer — proven;  
(2) Do attached tasks pass the seven admissions — proven, with corpus attestation;  
(3) Can attached tasks be borne by the principal layer or by form-components — proven impossible;  
(4) Do four groups suffice to bear all current formal auxiliary tasks — proven not to; the stability task cannot be naturally assigned to the four groups;  
(5) What minimal group division does the current auxiliary layer require — proven; the current minimal division is five groups;  
(6) Do the five groups exhaust all attached tasks — not proven here. The existence of the layer is necessary within the working domain ActiveDep(T); the membership and grouping of the layer are empirical; the number five is handled continuously by the candidate testing and back-testing institutions of Canon Chapter 1, Part Two, Chapter Four.

III. Objective

The minimal paradigm this chapter seeks: the layer structure that the institution bearing attached tasks cannot lack in order to stand, together with the minimal group division required by the current formal auxiliary tasks.

IV. Advance Notice of the Conclusion

The general formula to be proven is announced:

form system(T) ∧ ActiveDep(T) ⇒ auxiliary layer(T).

The grouping formula to be proven is announced:

DepRel(τ) ∈ {Boundary, SourceTransition, CompletionDepth, InverseIdentification, Persistence}.

One sentence: once a principal-form system in actual operation produces an admitted attached task, a formal layer must bear it; the current formal auxiliary forms, by the dominant relation of attachment between each and the upstream established result, divide into at least five groups.

V. Abstract

Once principal-form tasks have actually been executed and have produced admitted attached tasks, those tasks cannot lawfully be borne by the principal layer or by form-components, and the system must therefore establish a formal auxiliary layer. Within the auxiliary layer, attached tasks are distinguished by the dominant relation each adds relative to the upstream established result: the four relations of boundary, source-and-transformation, completion-depth, and inverse identification are mutually irreducible; the stability task, with all four relations kept intact, still cannot be naturally placed, and thereby forces out a fifth relation, persistence. The five groups cover the current formal auxiliary-form corpus and, under the precondition of single-task normalization, satisfy coverage, non-emptiness, and mutual exclusion; whether the five groups exhaust all future attached tasks is listed as Canon-4-H1 for continuing back-testing.

VI. Constraint Recursion

The irreducible task: the form system must give every admitted task a bearer. Working-domain condition: ActiveDep(T) holds, that is, at least one principal-form task has actually been executed and has already produced one or more admitted attached tasks.

Step One. With the principal layer and the admission system alone, is the institution of tasks already closed? ActiveDep(T) already guarantees at least one attached task; an attached task takes an earlier Form's established result as its premise and, by Canon Chapter 3, Section III, does not belong to the basic task set 𝒯. If only the principal layer is permitted to bear it, there appears a task that has passed admission yet has no lawful bearer. The gap is explicit: attached tasks exist, and their bearer is nowhere yet.

Step Two. Can a new layer be avoided by letting the principal text bear them in passing? If the attached task is pushed back into the principal text it attaches to, the two tasks each hold an independent proposition to be proven, completion conditions, and breach method, and one text, one form is violated; if it is demoted to a form-component, the admission rule that "a complete theoretical task may not be demoted" is violated. What is forced out is therefore not some particular text but a formal layer standing alongside the principal layer and dedicated to bearing attached tasks — the auxiliary layer.

Step Three. The layer stands; does one auxiliary kind suffice? Grouping cannot be grounded on "different form-kinds have different completion conditions," since a single group already admits several form-kinds with different completion conditions. What the group level genuinely needs to distinguish is: what is the dominant relation of attachment between an attached task and the established result it attaches to?

Define DepRel(τ): for an attached task τ that has completed single-task normalization, DepRel(τ) denotes the dominant relation that τ's proposition to be proven adds relative to its upstream established result. Four relations appear in the current corpus:

Boundary — asking the limits of standing of an established structure and its degeneration beyond them;  
SourceTransition — asking the source, occurrence, or structural transformation of an established structure;  
CompletionDepth — asking how an established result advances to a higher strength of establishment;  
InverseIdentification — asking what can be determined, distinguished, or located from an established forward structure or result.

The four relations cannot be reduced to one another. Giving the boundary does not answer the source or the transformation; giving the source or the transformation does not answer the advance in strength of establishment; completing compatibility, construction, reachability, or realization does not answer what can be discerned from the result; and inverse identification cannot in turn supply the first three relations. Remove any one relation type and at least one current formal auxiliary task is left with no group to go to. The four groups thus receive their names by their dominant relation of attachment: the boundary-discernment group, the source-and-transformation group, the establishment-deepening group, the inversion-discernment group.

Step Four. Is there residue beyond the four groups? The stability task has passed the six-point candidate test of Canon Chapter 1 and become a formal auxiliary form. With the old four groups all kept intact, test group by group: stability does not ask only "where the boundary lies," so it cannot be absorbed by Boundary; stability can stand independently with the source unchanged, without transition and without phase transition, so it cannot be absorbed by SourceTransition; stability is not a completion strength of compatibility–construction–reachability–realization, so it cannot be absorbed by CompletionDepth; stability can stand even without any observational inversion, so it cannot be absorbed by InverseIdentification. The old four groups therefore leave one formal member with no group to go to.

Step Five. What difference should be added? Continue along DepRel(τ) and extract the dominant relation of the stability task. Its common premise is an already-established qualification, structure, or object; the new relation it seeks to prove is which relations of standing continue to be preserved, when they exit, whether they recover, and to which quantifier level they are preserved, under declared variation, perturbation, substitution of realization, or successor trajectories. Define Persistence as this dominant relation of attachment. It is neither Boundary, nor SourceTransition, nor CompletionDepth, nor InverseIdentification. Hence a fifth group is forced out: the persistence-and-stability group.

Step Six. Can the five groups be merged, and do they constitute a genuine division? The basis of classification of the five groups is uniformly the type of dominant relation DepRel(τ), not the names of form-kinds or their completion conditions. The five relations Boundary, SourceTransition, CompletionDepth, InverseIdentification, and Persistence are pairwise irreducible: for any one relation there exists at least one current formal auxiliary task that the remaining relations cannot close; therefore removing any group leaves a formal auxiliary form with no group to go to.

Mutual exclusion between groups is given by single-task normalization. A formal auxiliary-form text can bear only one principal task; if a candidate proposition contains two dominant relations of attachment at once, it must be split at the problem-posing stage into two tasks to be proven, or carried separately as a Combined Form, and may not bear load across two groups in one auxiliary text. Hence for a formal auxiliary form τ that has completed single-task normalization, DepRel(τ) takes exactly one dominant value. The current five groups thereby satisfy coverage, non-emptiness, and mutual exclusion.

Re-ask the irreducible task: within the working domain ActiveDep(T), admitted basic tasks go to the principal layer and attached tasks to the auxiliary layer; the current fifteen auxiliary forms all obtain a unique group position among the five DepRel types. The current institution of tasks is closed. Whether the five groups exhaust all future attached tasks is listed separately as Canon-4-H1.

VII. The Minimal-Structure Proof

Load-bearing provenance: the necessity of the auxiliary layer is forced by Steps One and Two of this chapter; the four types Boundary, SourceTransition, CompletionDepth, and InverseIdentification are obtained as group-level relations in Step Three on the existing auxiliary-form corpus; Persistence is the new relation type forced in Steps Four and Five after the stability task overflowed the old four groups; the definition of attached tasks is inherited from Canon Chapter 3, and admission and single-task normalization from Canon Chapter 1; the mutual exclusion between groups in the Grouping Theorem is given directly by the inherited precondition of single-task normalization and is not an item forced by this chapter — if single-task normalization is withdrawn, the exclusion conclusion becomes pending; "minimal division" means minimal on the current formal auxiliary-form corpus, not absolutely minimal for all attached tasks (the latter is Canon-4-H1). The sole conditional item is Canon-4-H1 (the exhaustiveness of the five groups).

Necessity. ActiveDep(T) already guarantees the existence of one or more admitted attached tasks. Without an auxiliary layer, such a task can only be pushed into a principal text or demoted to a form-component, the two routes violating respectively one text, one form and the rule of non-demotion. Within this chapter's working domain, therefore, the auxiliary layer is indispensable.

Independent degeneration. Remove the auxiliary layer while keeping the other constituents: attached tasks lose their lawful bearer, and the reviewability of the theory degenerates. Remove any one of the current five groups: at least one current formal auxiliary form loses its unique group position.

Non-mergeability. The five groups are distinguished by the five types of dominant relation of DepRel(τ): Boundary, SourceTransition, CompletionDepth, InverseIdentification, Persistence. The five relations are pairwise irreducible; for a formal auxiliary form that has completed single-task normalization, the dominant relation is unique. Hence the five groups cannot be merged into one another by renaming or by mixing the completion conditions of form-kinds.

Integral closure. All fifteen current auxiliary forms have a unique group position: Limit, Grading, and Dissolution go to Boundary; Source, Transition, and Phase-Transition go to SourceTransition; Compatibility, Construction, Reachability, and Realization go to CompletionDepth; Determination, Distinction, Indistinguishability, and Diagnosis go to InverseIdentification; Stability goes to Persistence. The current five groups therefore satisfy coverage, non-emptiness, and mutual exclusion, and constitute the minimal division of the current formal auxiliary layer.

VIII. General Formula and General Theorem

General formula:

form system(T) ∧ ActiveDep(T) ⇒ auxiliary layer(T).

Grouping Theorem: for the fifteen auxiliary forms that have currently passed candidate testing, there exists a unique mapping of dominant relation of attachment:

DepRel(τ) ∈ {Boundary, SourceTransition, CompletionDepth, InverseIdentification, Persistence}.

Under the precondition of single-task normalization, the current fifteen auxiliary forms are covered by the five groups, each group is non-empty, and the groups are mutually exclusive; removing any group leaves at least one formal auxiliary form with no group to go to. The five groups therefore constitute the minimal division of the current formal auxiliary layer.

General Theorem: every form system satisfying ActiveDep(T) — having actually executed a principal-form task and produced one or more admitted attached tasks — in standing, necessarily possesses a formal auxiliary layer standing alongside the principal layer, bearing the attached tasks, each member having its six-field specification.

The existence of the layer is necessary within the working domain ActiveDep(T); the membership and grouping of the layer are empirical. The current fifteen auxiliary forms and five groups are the current member table and the current minimal division; additions and removals of members are handled by the six-point candidate test of Canon Chapter 1, and the exhaustiveness of the grouping is continuously back-tested under Canon-4-H1.

IX. Gap Declaration

Canon-4-H1: the exhaustiveness of the five-group division — the current five dominant relations of attachment are Boundary, SourceTransition, CompletionDepth, InverseIdentification, and Persistence. The five groups cover the current fifteen auxiliary forms and, under the precondition of single-task normalization, constitute the current minimal division; whether they exhaust the group-relation types of all future attached tasks awaits verification. If future back-testing finds a new attached task that cannot be naturally assigned to the five DepRel types and passes the six-point candidate test, the five groups are upgraded further. Status: awaiting verification. The responsibility for verification lies with the upgrade of this chapter, in parallel with the continuing back-testing of the candidate watch list of Canon Chapter 1.

Working-domain note: the former Canon-4-H2, "the dynamic precondition of the necessity of residue," has been raised to the working-domain condition ActiveDep(T) of the general proposition and is no longer an H entry. The case of a system idling without producing attached tasks lies outside the working domain of this chapter's general theorem.

Cited gaps: Canon-3-H1 through H3, cited under their original numbers.

X. Appendix

## Appendix 1: Record of Residue Instances

Proposition: within the working domain ActiveDep(T), the actual completion of a principal-form task produces attached tasks that take its established result as premise; all five current principal forms already have such residue instances.

Explanation. A completed General Form delivers N and can go on to produce boundary or preservation tasks about N; a completed Mathematical Form delivers M and can go on to produce tasks of coupling, applicability limits, or preservation of formal qualification; a completed Necessity Form delivers C ⇒ R and can go on to produce tasks of failure boundaries or preservation of conditions; a completed Possibility Form delivers existence and can go on to produce establishment-deepening tasks of compatibility, construction, reachability, and realization; a completed Inversion Form delivers a bound of knowability and can go on to produce concrete tasks of determination, distinction, or diagnosis.

This appendix serves to show that ActiveDep(T) is not an empty condition in the current form system; it does not bear the unconditional proposition that "any idling form system also necessarily produces residue."

XI. Interfaces

Upward: the premises of this chapter come from Canon Chapter 1 (the seven admissions, the six fields of auxiliary forms, the member table of the fifteen auxiliary forms, the candidate-testing institution) and Canon Chapter 3 (the definition of attached tasks, the theorem of one text, one form); the propagation of a breach of this chapter — if the attached/basic dichotomy of Canon Chapter 3 is withdrawn, the non-mergeability proof of this chapter becomes pending with it.

Canon Chapter 1 supplies the membership fact that the Stability Form has passed candidate testing and become a formal auxiliary form; the second item of the candidate-test record of Canon Chapter 1 ("cannot be naturally absorbed by existing form-kinds") takes Steps Four through Six of this chapter as its responsible chapter, and a breach of this chapter voids that item with it; the necessity of the persistence-and-stability group is not supplied in reverse by the grouping table of Chapter 1, but is proven independently by the failure of absorption, in Steps Four through Six of this chapter, after each of the old four groups is kept intact in turn.

Downward: Canon Chapter 1, Part Two, Chapter Four, "the closure of the auxiliary layer is weaker than that of the principal layer," cites this chapter as its theoretical ground — within the working domain ActiveDep(T) the layer's existence is necessary, its membership and grouping empirical; the legitimacy of the fifteen chapters of Part Four rests on this chapter's general theorem; the order of generation of group forms in Part Six enters by the five residue groups.

Outward: every attached task found in back-testing to overflow the five groups is written back into Canon-4-H1; the breach method for the layer is as the General Form specification prescribes — with ActiveDep(T) holding, exhibit a form system without an auxiliary layer in which all admitted attached tasks nonetheless have lawful bearers. The breach method for the five-group division is: exhibit a formal attached task that has passed the six-point candidate test and cannot be naturally assigned to any of the five groups.
