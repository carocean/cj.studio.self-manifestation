# General Form of the Strata

I. General Proposition

This chapter attempts a general proposition:

Every part of a form system capable of bearing the book's non-proof roles must minimally possess three strata: the form-component layer, the comparative meta-form, and the integrative forms.

Outside the proof layer (principal and auxiliary forms), the operation of a theory necessarily produces three classes of roles: the components internal to proofs, the comparison of established Forms, and the organization of many established Forms. This chapter proves that these three roles necessarily arise, that each must have a formal stratum, and that no stratum can substitute for another.

II. Version Positioning and Declaration of the Degree of Answer

First version. The general proposition decomposes into three sub-problems:

(1) Do the three roles necessarily arise — proven (Constraint Recursion);  
(2) Are the three strata mutually non-substitutable — proven (the Minimal-Structure Proof);  
(3) Is there necessarily no fourth stratum beyond the three — conditional: closure rests on the fivefold division of roles (proof, component, comparison, organization, non-form), whose exhaustiveness enters the H-group awaiting verification.

III. Objective

The minimal paradigm this chapter seeks: the stratum structure that the institution bearing non-proof roles cannot lack in order to stand.

IV. Advance Notice of the Conclusion

The general formula to be proven is announced (it is only a result awaiting proof and cannot serve as a premise of the derivation that follows):

auxiliary institution ⇒ {form-component layer, comparative meta-form, integrative forms}

One sentence: proving produces components, upgrading produces comparison, growth produces organization — three necessities, each demanding a stratum.

V. Abstract

The conduct of proof necessarily produces internal components that fail admission; the institution of upgrading and citation necessarily produces comparison tasks over pairs of established Forms; the growth of group forms necessarily produces organization tasks beyond the visibility of cross-references. Components bear no complete tasks and cannot be Forms; comparison acts on pairs of Forms rather than on objects and cannot be assigned to principal or auxiliary forms; organization may produce no new proofs and cannot be assigned to the proof layer. Each of the three roles requires a formal stratum: the form-component layer, the comparative meta-form, the integrative forms.

VI. Constraint Recursion

The irreducible task: the form system must give every necessarily arising role a formal station, or numbering, citation, and review lose their objects. Non-degeneracy condition: the proof layer is already running (the corpus of Canon Chapter 1, Part Ten).

Step One. In a world of the proof layer alone, is role-assignment already closed? Imagine the world of Forms only: every formal station is an admitted form-text. Does that world hold — test what a form-text's own completion conditions produce. Completion conditions must be operationally verifiable (Canon Chapter 1, Part Eight, Chapter One), and verification must have something on file to check: the derivation necessarily produces at least one of a theorem, an equation, or a criterion, together with the definitions and symbols that support it (the Component-Necessity Lemma, Appendix 1 of this chapter). Yet these components each fail admission — no independent proposition to be proven, or compulsory demotion under the sixth admission — so in the imagined world they have no station at all; and cross-Form citation of a theorem must be able to locate it (the citation rules of Canon Chapter 1, Part Five, Chapter Three take this as their premise). The world breaks: what necessarily arises has nowhere to belong. The formal station of components is forced out: the form-component layer. Unclosed point one.

Step Two. With components housed, cross-Form citation can locate its objects and the citation institution runs; once citation runs, upgrading follows. After a cited Form upgrades, the citing side must check whether the cited conclusion survives in the new version (Canon Chapter 1, Part Six, Chapter Three) — and checking is comparing: at which level the two versions are equivalent, at which not. The comparison task takes the standing of both sides as premise — it is therefore a doubly attached meta-level task (attached to both Forms compared), and by the non-attached definition of Canon Chapter 3 it lies outside the basic task set 𝒯; its output is a determination of equivalence levels, not a new proof in any of the five classes of basic output; it acts on a pair of Forms, not on an object of study — it cannot be assigned to a principal form, nor is it an auxiliary task attached to a single principal form. The formal stratum of comparison is forced out: the comparative meta-form. Unclosed point two.

Step Three. With citation and comparison running, form-texts around one object keep multiplying; attachment, combination, and citation interweave until cross-references alone can no longer be seen through (the entry conditions of the integrative forms, Canon Chapter 1, Part Five, Chapter Two, were extracted from exactly this) — the task of organization necessarily appears. Can it simply be a Form? The dilemma defeats it either way: an organizing document with no independent proposition to be proven fails the first admission and cannot stand as a Form; if it truly carries a new proposition, then by the routing of relations that proposition must be established in a form-text of its own (the breach-method clause of Canon Chapter 1, Part Five, Chapter Two), and what remains in the organizing document is display without proposition. Organization cannot be a Form, yet at scale it is indispensable — a formal, non-load-bearing stratum is forced out: the integrative forms, every load-bearing conclusion annotated with its source Form.

Step Four. Re-ask the same task: does every necessarily arising role now have its station? All roles in the book divide five ways by nature: what produces the five classes of basic output belongs to the proof layer (principal and auxiliary forms, Canon Chapters 3 and 4); components failing admission, to the form-component layer; comparison acting on pairs of Forms, to the comparative meta-form; the organizing of established Forms, to the integrative forms; what changes no theoretical state, to the non-form. Every role has its station. The task closes.

VII. The Minimal-Structure Proof

Load-bearing provenance: the three strata are items forced by this chapter's constraint recursion, the Component-Necessity Lemma an item forced in Appendix 1; the citation rules and integrative clauses are inherited from Canon Chapter 1, the non-attached definition from Canon Chapter 3; the sole conditional item is Canon-5-H1 (the exhaustiveness of the five-way division).

Necessity. Steps One through Three: each of the three roles necessarily arises; without formal strata, components cannot be cited, upgrade checks have no institutional bearer, and complex group forms cannot be reviewed — each failure defeats reviewability.

Independent degeneration. Remove the form-component layer: components must pose as Forms (failing admission) or lose their numbering (theorems become uncitable) — Step Twelve of review and the citation institution fail independently. Remove the comparative meta-form: the standing check after upgrades has no bearer, and the version-citation institution (Canon Chapter 1, Part Six, Chapter Three) runs empty — that institution fails independently. Remove the integrative forms: Step Three of review (the layer review) loses its basis for complex group forms — Canon Chapter 1, Part Nine, Step Three fails independently. The three absences strike different checkpoints and do not mask one another.

Non-mergeability. Form-components bear no complete theoretical tasks and cannot rise into the proof layer (blocked by the seven admissions); the comparative meta-form acts on pairs of Forms, its domain disjoint from that of principal and auxiliary forms (objects and established structures); the integrative forms may not substitute for local proofs and have no independently breachable proposition, incompatible with the four-item definition of the proof layer; and the three functions are pairwise disjoint — components bear local work, comparison establishes equivalence levels, organization establishes master diagrams: products of different type, mutually non-substitutable.

Closure of the whole. Under the fivefold division of Step Four, with the strata in place every role has its home; lacking any one stratum, the corresponding role hangs unborne.

VIII. General Formula and General Theorem

The derivation complete, the general formula stands as a formal conclusion:

auxiliary institution ⇒ {form-component layer, comparative meta-form, integrative forms}

General Theorem: every part of a form system capable of bearing the book's non-proof roles, in standing, necessarily possesses the three strata of the form-component layer, the comparative meta-form, and the integrative forms; the three strata are mutually non-substitutable and, together with the proof layer, compose a complete covering of the book's roles.

IX. Gap Declaration

Canon-5-H1: exhaustiveness of the fivefold division of roles — the closure check rests on the division proof / component / comparison / organization / non-form. The division covers all current roles of the book; whether it exhausts all possible roles awaits verification. The discovery of a sixth role class upgrades the division; the necessity of the three strata is unaffected. Status: awaiting verification; responsibility with this chapter's upgrades, in parallel with back-testing.

Cited gaps: Canon-3-H1 through H3 (the fivefold division of basic outputs via the decision tree, indirect); Canon-4-H1 (the exhaustiveness of the five-group division of auxiliary forms, concerning the internal division of the proof layer; Canon Chapter 4 v1.2).

X. Appendix

## Appendix 1: the Component-Necessity Lemma

Proposition: the derivation of any well-formed form-text necessarily produces at least one component that fails admission.

Proof. A form-text's completion conditions must be operationally verifiable (Canon Chapter 1, Part Eight, Chapter One): verification must have something on file to check — so the text produces at least one of a theorem, an equation, or a criterion, together with the definitions and symbols that support it. Theorem, equation, criterion, proof, and definition each lack an independent proposition to be proven (they serve the proposition of the whole text), and so do not stand as Forms under the first admission; and under the sixth admission whatever can be demoted must be demoted. So components necessarily arise and necessarily remain in the form-component layer.

XI. Interfaces

Upward: the premises of this chapter come from Canon Chapter 1 (admission, citation rules, the clauses of the integrative forms, the five classes of form-components) and Canon Chapter 3 (the fivefold division of basic outputs); Canon Chapter 4 is the sister chapter — Chapter 4 proves, within the working domain ActiveDep(T) (at least one principal-form task actually executed and producing an admitted attached task), the necessity of the auxiliary layer within the proof layer, this chapter proves the necessity of the three strata outside it, and the two compose the full derivation of the layer structure; wherever this chapter cites Chapter 4's conclusion it inherits the precondition ActiveDep(T) together with it, which the present book satisfies. The derivation of this chapter's three strata does not take the auxiliary layer as a premise; the narrowing of Chapter 4's working domain does not alter the strength of this chapter's general theorem.

Downward: Canon Chapter 1, Part One, Chapter Five already cites the joint derivation of this chapter and Canon Chapter 6 (that chapter is sealed); the stratum legitimacy of the chapters of Part Five (the comparative meta-form, the integrative forms, the five classes of form-components) rests on this chapter, including the legitimacy of the integrative forms as the third layer of the four-layer system.

Outward: any role found in back-testing to overflow the fivefold division is written back into Canon-5-H1; the breach method of this chapter is as the General Form specification prescribes — exhibit a form system lacking one of the strata in which the book's non-proof roles nonetheless all have formal homes.
