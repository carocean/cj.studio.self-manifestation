# General Form of the Form-Text

I. General Proposition

This chapter attempts a general proposition:

Every reviewable formal theory, in order to stand, must minimally possess three layers of bearing: the form-text, the group form, and the whole book; and the form-text is the minimal unit of bearing.

Canon Chapter 1, Part One, Chapter Five established the four-layer system as a norm; this chapter gives the theoretical derivation of the three bearing layers (form-text, group form, whole book). The integrative forms, as the organizing form at the third layer of that system, have their stratum's necessity proven by Canon Chapter 5; they are not a bearing unit, and this chapter does not re-prove them.

II. Version Positioning and Declaration of the Degree of Answer

First version. The general proposition decomposes into three sub-problems:

(1) Why a minimal bearing unit must exist, and why that unit is precisely the form-text — proven (Constraint Recursion, Steps One and Two);  
(2) Why a group form layer necessarily appears above the form-text — proven (Step Three);  
(3) Why a whole-book layer necessarily appears above the group form, and why no further bearing layers necessarily appear between — the first half proven (Step Four), the second half conditional (H1).

III. Objective

The minimal paradigm this chapter seeks: the layered structure that the bearing of theory cannot lack in order to stand.

IV. Advance Notice of the Conclusion

The general formula to be proven is announced (it is only a result awaiting proof and cannot serve as a premise of the derivation that follows):

reviewable theory ⇒ (form-text ⊂ group form ⊂ whole book)

One sentence: the closure of the four items sets the lower bound, one text, one form sets the upper bound, and the minimal bearing unit is squeezed out between them — the form-text; attachment gathers texts into group forms, and freezing gathers groups into the book.

V. Abstract

Reviewability requires that the proposition to be proven, the proof objective, the completion condition, and the breach method close within one and the same bearer — otherwise a breach has no referent and accountability has no object. Texts smaller than a form-text (form-components) lack the four items and cannot be bearing units; texts larger than a single task violate one text, one form and must be split. So the minimal bearing unit exists and is unique: the form-text. Attachment and combination necessarily gather form-texts into group forms; terminology freezing, unique numbering, the interface master table, and sealing are book-wide institutions that no group form can bear on its own, necessarily demanding the whole-book layer. The three bearing layers are thus forced out.

VI. Constraint Recursion

The irreducible task: a reviewable theory must give every breach a definite referent and every result a definite home. Non-degeneracy condition: more than one task is actually in progress, and at least one admitted attached task or one Combined Form exists (the book's corpus; the latter is the working-domain condition ActiveDep(T) of Canon Chapter 4 v1.2, declared here explicitly as part of this chapter's non-degeneracy condition). Check point by point:

Step One. How small can the bearing unit be? Imagine the world in which components bear conclusions directly: a theorem drifts alone and is cited everywhere. Who declares it complete, and who receives its breach? The four items must close within one bearer: the completion condition announces "when it counts as done," the breach method announces "how it counts as defeated" — both must refer to one and the same text, or there arises the mismatch of "text A breached, text B declared defeated," and accountability fails. Form-components lack the four items (Canon Chapter 5, Appendix 1: components have no independent proposition) and cannot be the closing body. So the bearing unit cannot be smaller than "a single text with one task and all four items present." Unclosed point one.

Step Two. The lower bound stands; how large can the bearing unit be? Suppose one text bears two tasks, each with its four items complete. By Canon Chapter 3, Theorem Two, second case, they are two independent tasks, their completion conditions and breach standards mutually independent — one text then carries two sets of "when done, how defeated" at once, fails Step One of review (whether the four items are each independent), and must be split. So the bearing unit cannot be larger than a single task. Lower and upper bound squeeze out the unique solution: the minimal bearing unit is the form-text (a single text, one task, four items closed). Unclosed point one closes.

Step Three. The unit is fixed; who bears the relations between form-texts? This chapter's non-degeneracy condition explicitly declares ActiveDep(T); within that working domain attached tasks necessarily stand as texts of their own (the general theorem of Canon Chapter 4 v1.2, inherited together with the precondition ActiveDep(T)); an attached text declares the principal text it attaches to, and when the principal is breached the failure propagates to the attached text's conclusions (the attachment clauses of Canon Chapter 1, Part Six, Chapter Two; the definition of attached tasks in Canon Chapter 3). Imagine the world without a group-form layer: inter-text attachment lives scattered in the declarations of individual chapters, and when a principal is breached, the range of propagation can only be pieced together by searching every chapter in the book — the affected range cannot be found at a glance, upgrade and breach propagation have no executing layer, and the checking duty of Part Six, Chapter Three cannot be discharged. Meanwhile the independent shape of the Combined Form already constitutes a real organization centered on principal texts (Canon Chapter 1, Part Six, Chapter Two). The layer bearing inter-text attachment and combination is forced out: the group-form layer. Unclosed point two closes.

Step Four. Group forms stand; who bears the institutions of freezing and adjudication? Terminology freezing demands one book-wide glossary; numbering freezing demands book-wide uniqueness of H-group and chapter numbers; the interface master table demands one table for the book; the sealing declaration binds every change in the book to the upgrade procedure (Canon Chapter 1, Part Ten, Chapter Three). No single group form can bear these institutions on its own: if two group forms each froze their terminology, a conflict would demand adjudication one layer higher — and the adjudicating layer is the book. The whole-book layer is forced out. Unclosed point three closes. The task closes.

VII. The Minimal-Structure Proof

Load-bearing provenance: the three bearing layers are items squeezed and forced by this chapter's constraint recursion; one text, one form and the attachment clauses are inherited (Canon Chapter 3; Canon Chapter 1, Part Six); the standing of attached tasks in texts of their own is a conditional inherited item (Canon Chapter 4 v1.2, precondition ActiveDep(T), satisfied by this chapter's non-degeneracy condition); the sole conditional item is Canon-6-H1 (intermediate bearing layers).

Necessity. Step One: without the form-text layer the four items have no common closing body and breaches misfire; Step Three: without the group form layer the propagation of attachment has no home, and the failure of attached texts upon a principal breach cannot be executed; Step Four: without the whole-book layer conflicts of terminology and numbering have no adjudicator. Each layer is indispensable.

Independent degeneration. Remove the form-text layer (taking form-components as units): Step One of review fails at once — the object under review lacks the four items. Remove the group form layer (texts hanging directly from the book): the attachment-propagation clauses of Canon Chapter 1, Part Six, Chapter Two lose their executing layer, and affected texts cannot be located at upgrades. Remove the whole-book layer (group forms each on their own): parallel conflicting versions of the frozen glossary appear, and the sealing procedure of Part Ten becomes inexecutable. The three absences strike three different checkpoints.

Non-mergeability. The form-text bears the four-item closure of a single task; the group form bears the relations of attachment, combination, and citation between texts; the book bears the global institutions of freezing, numbering, the master table, and sealing. The three layers bear things of different type — task, relation, institution: a group form cannot be internalized as one enormous form-text (violating one text, one form), and the global institutions cannot be delegated to any one group form (adjudication must stand above both parties to a conflict).

Closure of the whole. With the three layers standing, tasks have their texts, relations their groups, institutions their book; the referent of every breach, the home of every result, and the adjudication of every term each have a unique bearing layer.

VIII. General Formula and General Theorem

The derivation complete, the general formula stands as a formal conclusion:

reviewable theory ⇒ (form-text ⊂ group form ⊂ whole book)

The group form in this chapter's bearing chain is the bearing group form: every form-text belongs to exactly one bearing group form (Canon Chapter 1, Part Six, Chapter Four); view group forms may overlap without bearing load and stand outside the bearing chain.

General Theorem: every reviewable formal theory, in standing, necessarily possesses the three bearing layers of form-text, group form, and whole book; the form-text is the minimal bearing unit, squeezed out uniquely between the lower bound of four-item closure and the upper bound of one text, one form.

Working-domain note: the necessity of the group-form layer (Step Three) takes ActiveDep(T) in the non-degeneracy condition as its working domain: where a theory has a single form-text and no inter-text attachment or combination relation, the group-form layer degenerates to coincide with the whole-book layer and Step Three does not apply; the necessity of the form-text layer and of the whole-book layer is not so limited. This is a working-domain condition, not an H awaiting verification.

IX. Gap Declaration

Canon-6-H1: the non-necessity of intermediate bearing layers — this chapter proves three layers necessary, not "necessarily only three": whether a theory of very large scale necessarily develops intermediate bearing layers between group form and book (volumes, parts) awaits verification. For the present book the three layers suffice. The discovery of a necessary intermediate layer upgrades the layering; the form-text's standing as minimal unit is unaffected. Status: partial back-test — the view-group-form mechanism shows that cross-group organization can be borne by non-bearing views without adding bearing layers (Canon Chapter 1 v1.3, the Specification of the Group Form); the question of scale-driven intermediate layers still awaits verification, responsibility with this chapter's upgrades.

Cited gaps: Canon-3-H1 through H3 (premises of the one-text-one-form theorem); Canon-4-H1 (the exhaustiveness of the five-group division of attached tasks; Canon Chapter 4 v1.2).

X. Appendix

## Appendix 1: the Squeeze Lemma

Proposition: a textual unit satisfying both "four-item closure" and "single task" exists and is unique in the sense of bearing.

Proof. Existence: any sealed form-text of the book is an instance. Uniqueness (in the sense of bearing): suppose another unit U satisfies both conditions. If U is smaller than a single-task text with the four items present, the four items are incomplete within U, violating closure; if U is larger, U contains several tasks, violating the single-task condition. So U coincides with the form-text at the boundary of bearing — the unit is unique.
