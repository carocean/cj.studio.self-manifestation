# Necessity Form of Sealing

I. Purpose

Canon Chapter 1, Part Ten established sealing as a procedure; this chapter proves that sealing is not an editorial preference but a dynamical necessity of reviewable theory: a theoretical process without a sealing institution necessarily reaches no settling, no sedimentation, no familiar route. This chapter is at the same time the junction of the Canon Chapters with the book's Field and Law Chapters — the revision process of theory itself falls within the sedimentation dynamics the book has already established.

II. Core Proposition

This chapter argues a central proposition:

A theoretical process whose dynamics of revision remain open without end necessarily brings no conclusion to stable standing; therefore every reviewable theory that requires citation to be completable and results to sediment necessarily possesses an institution by which revision can be closed under control — completion conditions, breach methods, sealing, and the upgrade procedure.

Shape: C ⇒ R. Condition group C: the dynamics of revision open; the institution of citation running; sedimentation premised on repetition. Result R: no settling, no sedimentation, no familiar route. The full argument in the contrapositive direction is given in Theorem Three.

III. Operator Mapping

The objects of this chapter are mapped one by one onto quantities the book has already established (the mapping is at the conceptual level; precise coupling awaits verification, see H1):

a finalized text ↔ current settling (Law Chapters: current settling is given by the settling cost synthesized from current fit and historical accumulated depth; the lower-cost path settles first);  
version history ↔ sedimentation history (Field Chapters: the sedimentation-field records repeatedly traversed trajectories);  
a sealed text repeatedly cited ↔ repeated hits and the generation of familiar routes (Field Chapters: familiar routes are pressed out by repeated traversal; Form Chapter 1, Appendix 1: the Repeated-Hit Theorem);  
revision disturbance ↔ field perturbation (Field Chapters: field perturbation dynamics);  
breach ↔ structural breakdown conditions triggering reconstitution (the Social Chapters' common practice);  
the stable standing of citations ↔ the growth of accumulated depth (Law Chapters: the depth W is updated by repeated settling).

IV. Dynamic Laws

(1) The law of citation: citation takes the Form as its unit; after a cited Form is upgraded, the citing text must check the standing of the cited conclusions (Canon Chapter 1, Part Six, Chapter Three). If the cited text keeps changing, the check keeps restarting and never terminates.

(2) The law of sedimentation: sedimentation is premised on repetition — a familiar route is generated by the same path being traversed again and again (dynamics already established in the Field and Law Chapters). If a text never stops changing, there is no "same" to be hit repeatedly: every citation hits a different version.

(3) The law of declaration: a form-text without gap declaration cannot be sealed (Canon Chapter 1, Part Seven, Chapter Three); gaps cleared or all explicit is the precondition of sealing — sealing is controlled closure, not unconditional closure.

V. The Theorem Group

Theorem One: the No-Settling Theorem

If the dynamics of revision remain open without end, no conclusion attains stable standing.

Proof. Let a conclusion p belong to a text T whose revision is open without end. At any moment t, T remains unconditionally alterable after t, and the standing of p cannot be warranted beyond t. The law of citation requires the citing side to check the standing of p; completing the check requires the standing to be warranted unchanged over some interval — contradicting the above. So the citation check on p cannot be completed: p does not settle.

Theorem Two: the No-Sedimentation Theorem

Without stable text, version history cannot sediment into familiar routes.

Proof. By the law of sedimentation, the premise of familiar-route generation is repeated traversal of the same path; by Theorem One, under unending revision no conclusion-text is the same across moments, and the object of repeated hits does not exist. The premise fails; no familiar route is generated: version history remains a record of changes, and sedimentation does not take. (This theorem holds through the mapping of Section III and is conditional on H1.)

Theorem Three: the Sealing-Necessity Theorem

Every reviewable theory that requires citation to be completable and results to sediment necessarily possesses a sealing institution.

Proof. A reviewable theory requires citation to be completable (otherwise the cross-Form citation institution runs empty and Canon Chapter 1, Part Six, Chapter Three fails) and results to sediment (otherwise every version starts from zero, the system's results cannot be output, and the fifth admission fails at the scale of the book). By the contrapositive of Theorem One: completable citation requires an institution that holds the text unchanged over a warranted interval; by the contrapositive of Theorem Two: sedimentation requires a same text that can be hit repeatedly. Together they require that revision be closable under control — that is, completion conditions announcing the point of closure, breach methods keeping the channel of reversal open, sealing freezing the text, and the upgrade procedure permitting recorded reopening. The sealing institution is necessary.

VI. Boundary Cases and Structural Breakdown Conditions

The conditions under which this chapter's conclusions fail, given explicitly:

(1) Sealing is not termination: the upgrade procedure preserves controlled revision. This chapter proves "revision must be closable," not "must be closed forever" — reading it as a prohibition of revision is a misplacement, to be handled by dissolution;  
(2) The mapping condition: Theorem Two holds through the conceptual-level mapping of Section III; if the mapping, once made precise, is refuted (H1), Theorem Two retreats to analogy, while Theorem One and the institutional half of Theorem Three are unaffected (the citation branch of Theorem Three's proof does not depend on the mapping);  
(3) The declaration precondition: sealing presupposes gaps cleared or all explicit (Dynamic Law 3); sealing a text with hidden gaps seals the error in — this chapter does not underwrite sealing without declaration;  
(4) The breach method: exhibit a theoretical practice whose revision remains open without end yet which long maintains completable citation and sedimenting results — that breaches Theorem One or Theorem Two.

VII. Summary Formula

The whole chapter's conclusion gathers into the general formula:

reviewable theory ⇒ sealing institution (completion conditions + breach methods + sealing + upgrade procedure)
