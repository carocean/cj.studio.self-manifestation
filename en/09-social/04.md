# Stratum Familiar Routes

This chapter argues a central proposition:

To acknowledge that a social field has been established and possesses coupling asymmetry is necessarily to acknowledge that systematic differences exist in the coupling intensity between individual fields and the social field; these differences solidify through the accumulation of ΔFamiliarity to form strata — strata are not externally imposed classifications, but continuation-bias structures generated by the differences in Familiar Route depth that different individual fields form within the social field.

We seek to identify the minimal dynamic conditions for stratum Familiar Routes and the structural reasons for stratum solidification.

We first state the result.

The core equations of stratum Familiar Routes:

SFR(f\_i, F\_S) = ∑\_{q ∈ HighFam(f\_i, F\_S)} Familiarity(f\_i → q, F\_S) · FlowShare(f\_i → q, F\_S) / (∑\_{q} FlowShare(f\_i → q, F\_S) + ε)

ΔStratum(f\_i, f\_j) = SFR(f\_i, F\_S) − SFR(f\_j, F\_S)

ΔStratum(f\_i, f\_j) ≫ 0 ⇒ ContinuationBias(f\_i \> f\_j) ∝ ΔStratum(f\_i, f\_j)

In a single sentence

Strata are not descriptions of social stratification,  
but continuation-bias structures formed by the solidification of Familiar Route depth differentials among individual fields within the social field;  
the larger ΔStratum, the harder it is to cross strata.

## Section 1. Purpose

Social Chapter 3 demonstrated institutional lock-in: institutions actively suppress the generation of alternative structures through high Familiarity within the social field. But institutional lock-in addresses the solidification of institutional paths themselves, without addressing a more fundamental question: why do different individual fields have systematically different capacities to acquire continuation flow within the social field? Why does this difference self-reinforce rather than tend toward equalization?

The answer to this question is stratum Familiar Routes.

Strata are not static classifications in social stratification theory (Marx’s classes, Weber’s strata, the functionalist notion of status), but the dynamic structure of coupling asymmetry between individual fields and the social field — different individual fields have systematically different FlowShare in the social field’s continuation flow, and this difference forms stable continuation bias through the accumulation of ΔFamiliarity, making high-stratum paths increasingly easy to continue and low-stratum paths increasingly difficult to acquire continuation resources.

This chapter provides the precise dynamic mechanism of stratum Familiar Routes, explaining why stratum solidification can be maintained without subjective discrimination; why stratum mobility is systematically difficult in high-K\_{F\_S} social fields; and why individuals with equal capability will have different continuation trajectories depending on their stratum starting point.

## Section 2. Core Proposition

Sociology typically attributes strata to: unequal distribution of economic capital (Marx), competition across multiple dimensions of capital (Bourdieu), and unequal educational opportunity (functionalism). These explanations correctly point to unequal resource distribution, but offer no dynamic equations: why does unequal resource distribution self-reinforce? Why do strata tend to reconstruct even after resources are equalized?

The position of On Self-Manifestation is:

Stratum Familiar Routes are the solidification of coupling asymmetry between individual fields and the social field at the level of ΔFamiliarity — it is not unequal resource distribution that creates strata, but the ΔFamiliarity differential among individual fields in the social field’s continuation flow that creates systematic differences in resource acquisition capacity, thereby forming strata.

This definition carries three key implications:

First, the root of strata is ΔFamiliarity, not resource quantity. Two individuals with identical economic resources, if their individual fields differ in the degree to which they connect with the social field’s high-Familiarity paths (different FamOverlap), will still differ in their capacity to acquire continuation flow in the social field — stratum differences still exist.

Second, stratum solidification does not require discrimination mechanisms. Even in the complete absence of subjective discrimination within the social field, high-ΔFamiliarity path differentials will still reinforce automatically through ContinuationBias — high-stratum paths receive more continuation flow, Familiar Routes deepen further, ΔFamiliarity expands further, forming positive feedback.

Third, the real obstacle to stratum mobility is the cost of reversing ΔFamiliarity. Stratum mobility is not a resource transfer problem but requires the individual field to establish new high-Familiarity paths within the social field while breaking the ContinuationBias of the original low-Familiarity paths — this is a structural reorganization requiring sustained high-intensity input to complete.

## Section 3. Operator Mapping

This chapter introduces the following new definitions on the basis of Social Chapters 1 through 3 operators:

Stratum Familiar Route: denoted SFR(f\_i, F\_S), representing the normalized weighted Familiar Route strength of individual field f\_i’s high-Familiarity paths within social field F\_S.

SFR(f\_i, F\_S) = ∑\_{q ∈ HighFam(f\_i, F\_S)} Familiarity(f\_i → q, F\_S) · FlowShare(f\_i → q, F\_S) / (∑\_{q} FlowShare(f\_i → q, F\_S) + ε)

Where HighFam(f\_i, F\_S) is the set of paths f\_i has established with high Familiarity in F\_S; FlowShare weighting with normalization avoids path-count contamination of stratum measurement — an individual field with many shallow paths should not receive the same SFR as one with few but extremely deep paths. ε is a normalization lower bound to prevent divergence. The higher SFR, the higher the continuation priority of f\_i within the social field.

Stratum Familiar Route differential: ΔStratum(f\_i, f\_j, F\_S) = SFR(f\_i, F\_S) − SFR(f\_j, F\_S), representing the difference in Familiar Route level between two individual fields within the social field.

Stratum continuation bias: StratumBias(f\_i, f\_j, F\_S), representing the systematic bias toward different stratum individual fields when the social field distributes continuation flow.

StratumBias(f\_i, f\_j) ∝ ΔStratum(f\_i, f\_j)

Citing Field Chapter 11 Theorem Four (Familiarity Differential Determines Biased Flow Theorem): the larger ΔFamiliarity, the more continuation flow tends toward the high-Familiarity path, and the less continuation flow the low-Familiarity path receives.

Stratum coupling intensity: StrCpl(f\_i → F\_S), representing the coupling intensity between individual field f\_i and the high-Familiarity paths of social field F\_S.

StrCpl(f\_i → F\_S) ∝ FamOverlap(f\_i, F\_S) · Cpl(f\_i → F\_S)

Where FamOverlap(f\_i, F\_S) represents the structural similarity between the internal Familiar Routes of individual field f\_i and the high-Familiarity paths of the social field (the degree of path-pattern matching), and Cpl(f\_i → F\_S) represents actual interaction frequency (the intensity of coupling that genuinely occurs). The two can vary independently: a highly educated rural individual field may have high FamOverlap but low Cpl; a CEO’s child may have both high FamOverlap and high Cpl. Note: resources (Resource) influence StrCpl through two paths — Path One: Resource → education/culture → FamOverlap; Path Two: Resource → network access/time → Cpl; therefore resources simultaneously affect structural match and actual interaction frequency, and the two paths cannot be merged. The higher FamOverlap, the more easily the individual field acquires continuation flow from the social field, and the higher SFR.

Minimal component structure of FamOverlap(f\_i, F\_S):

FamOverlap(f\_i, F\_S) = Φ(LanguageOverlap, NormOverlap, NetworkOverlap, InstitutionOverlap, PathPersistence)

Where LanguageOverlap is the overlap of language and discourse habits; NormOverlap is the overlap of behavioral norms; NetworkOverlap is the overlap of social network paths; InstitutionOverlap is the overlap of institutional participation paths; PathPersistence is path temporal stability — distinguishing short-term educational boosts from long-term structurally sedimented FamOverlap; a short-term educational shock yields low PathPersistence, while long-term sedimentation yields high PathPersistence. Note: resources influence FamOverlap through two paths — Resource → education → LanguageOverlap + NormOverlap; Resource → network access/time → Cpl (not directly FamOverlap). Pure resource transfer that does not change FamOverlap components has limited effect on StrCpl. The complete FamOverlap dynamic equation is developed in Social Chapter 5.

Stratum solidification intensity: StratLock(F\_S), representing the overall degree of solidification of the social field’s current stratum structure.

StratLock(F\_S) ∝ mean\_{i≠j} \|ΔStratum(f\_i, f\_j)\| · K\_{F\_S}

That is, the larger the mean stratum Familiar Route differential among individual fields and the higher the social field’s compression strength, the stronger the stratum solidification.

Stratum mobility cost: MobCost(f\_i, f\_j, F\_S), representing the minimum sustained input intensity required for an individual field to migrate from a low-stratum Familiar Route to a high-stratum Familiar Route.

MobCost(f\_i, f\_j) ∝ ΔStratum(f\_j, f\_i) · K\_{F\_S} / (φ\_{F\_S} + ε)

Note: this is a directional proportionality approximation; the physical meaning of the three terms is as follows — the larger ΔStratum, the greater the Familiar Route gap to be bridged and the higher the required input intensity; the higher K\_{F\_S}, the harder new differentials are to stabilize and the greater the migration resistance; the lower φ\_{F\_S}, the less structural space available to generate new paths and the harder migration becomes; ε ensures MobCost tends toward a maximum rather than mathematical infinity. Complete derivation is developed in Social Chapter 5. When φ\_{F\_S} → 0, MobCost tends toward its maximum value and stratum mobility enters a near-impossible state — corresponding to the pre-compressive-death state of Field Chapter 18, at which point replacement-level disturbance (Shock \> T\_replace) is the only possible structural reorganization path.

The larger the stratum Familiar Route differential and the more solidified the social field (high K\_{F\_S}, low φ\_{F\_S}), the higher the stratum mobility cost.

## Section 4. Dynamic Equations

Stratum Familiar Route deepening equation (with decay term):

dSFR(f\_i)/dt ∝ StrCpl(f\_i → F\_S) · φ\_{F\_S} − Decay\_SFR(f\_i)

Expanded as: ∝ FamOverlap(f\_i, F\_S) · Cpl(f\_i → F\_S) · φ\_{F\_S} − Decay\_SFR(f\_i)

Where Decay\_SFR(f\_i) represents the Stratum Familiar Route decay term caused by path aging, skill obsolescence, and institutional change — high-stratum paths can also shallow due to institutional transformation or technological rupture; complete decay dynamics are developed in Chapter 9. Under stable operating conditions (without large-scale institutional change), Decay\_SFR is small and the positive term of dSFR/dt dominates.

That is, the higher the coupling intensity between the individual field and the social field’s high-Familiarity paths, the higher the overlap degree, and the higher the social field’s Residual Capacity Ratio, the faster the stratum Familiar Route deepens; when path aging or institutional change occurs, the decay term rises and the deepening rate slows.

Stratum Familiar Route differential expansion equation:

dΔStratum(f\_i, f\_j)/dt = dSFR(f\_i)/dt − dSFR(f\_j)/dt

∝ \[StrCpl(f\_i) − StrCpl(f\_j)\] · φ\_{F\_S} − PolicyCorrection(F\_S)

Expanded as: ∝ \[FamOverlap(f\_i) · Cpl(f\_i) − FamOverlap(f\_j) · Cpl(f\_j)\] · φ\_{F\_S} − PolicyCorrection(F\_S)

Where PolicyCorrection(F\_S) represents the offsetting strength of active corrective mechanisms within the social field against stratum differentials, with minimal component structure:

PolicyCorrection(F\_S) = Π(Edu, Redis, Institution)

Where Edu is the intensity of educational resource equalization (primarily influencing FamOverlap components); Redis is the intensity of economic redistribution (primarily influencing Cpl components); Institution is the intensity of institutional stratum intervention (influencing FlowShare allocation structure). Minimal linear approximation: PolicyCorrection ≈ α·Edu + β·Redis + γ·Institution, where α, β, γ are the offsetting efficiency weights of each component, to be calibrated in Social Chapter 5. When PolicyCorrection \> \[StrCpl(f\_i) − StrCpl(f\_j)\] · φ\_{F\_S}, dΔStratum/dt \< 0 and stratum differentials tend to contract. The improvement in stratum mobility in Nordic countries historically corresponds to periods in which PolicyCorrection persistently exceeded the compression advantage.

If StrCpl(f\_i) \> StrCpl(f\_j) and \[StrCpl(f\_i) − StrCpl(f\_j)\] · φ\_{F\_S} \> PolicyCorrection, then ΔStratum tends to expand over time — the Familiar Route deepening speed of high-stratum individual fields is systematically faster than that of low-stratum fields, and stratum differentials naturally widen.

Stratum solidification self-reinforcing loop:

SFR(f\_i) ↑ → more social field continuation flow enters f\_i → FlowShare(f\_i) ↑ → Cpl(f\_i → F\_S) ↑ (continuation flow strengthens actual interaction frequency) → StrCpl(f\_i → F\_S) ↑ → dSFR(f\_i)/dt accelerates further

Simultaneously: SFR(f\_j) relatively stagnates (f\_j receives less continuation flow) → dΔStratum/dt \> 0

This is a positive feedback loop: the deeper the high-stratum Familiar Routes, the more easily social field continuation flow is acquired, and actual interaction frequency rises further, deepening the Familiar Routes further; the shallower the low-stratum Familiar Routes, the harder it becomes to acquire continuation flow, and the Familiar Routes may shallow further. Note: SFR rising directly strengthens Cpl (actual interaction frequency), not FamOverlap (path structural similarity) — the latter is determined by cultural background and historical sedimentation, and does not automatically rise with increased continuation flow.

Linkage between stratum Familiar Routes and social field compression:

K\_{F\_S} ↑ → old Co-Nuclei covering new differentials ↑ → priority of existing high-Familiarity paths further elevated → ΔStratum expansion accelerates

That is, the higher the social field’s compression strength, the faster stratum Familiar Route differentials expand. High-K\_{F\_S} social fields systematically tend toward stratum solidification.

## Section 5. Three Forms of Stratum Familiar Route Solidification

Stratum Familiar Routes manifest in three observable forms of solidification within the social field:

Form One: Cultural capital accumulation solidification. High-stratum individual fields obtain sustained continuation flow advantage through high FamOverlap with the social field’s high-Familiarity cultural paths (language, norms, aesthetics, knowledge structures). Low-stratum individual fields have low overlap between their cultural paths and the social field’s high-Familiarity paths — even when economic resources are acquired, FamOverlap cannot be rapidly established. Observable criterion: the intergenerational transmission rate of cultural capital is higher than that of economic capital.

Form Two: Network coupling solidification. High-stratum individual fields form strong-coupling networks among themselves, jointly maintaining high-FlowShare paths and making it difficult for individual fields outside the network to enter. This corresponds to the institutional lock-in of Social Chapter 3: high-stratum networks are institutional Familiar Routes based on interpersonal coupling. Observable criterion: intra-stratum coupling intensity (Cpl\_intra) is systematically higher than inter-stratum coupling intensity (Cpl\_inter).

Form Three: Capacity occupation solidification. High-stratum individual fields continuously occupy the social field’s Residual Capacity Ratio (φ\_{F\_S}) through high SFR, compressing the margin available for low-stratum individual fields to generate new differentials. When φ\_{F\_S} is very low, low-stratum individual fields lack the structural space within the field to generate new paths even when motivated to do so. Observable criterion: the lower the social field’s φ\_{F\_S}, the lower the stratum mobility rate — the two are negatively correlated.

## Section 6. Theorems

Theorem One: Stratum Solidification Tendency Theorem

In a social field satisfying SocialGenesis = 1 with initial differences in StrCpl(f\_i → F\_S) among individual fields (∃ f\_i, f\_j: StrCpl(f\_i) ≠ StrCpl(f\_j)), under conditions where PolicyCorrection is insufficient to offset the compression advantage (i.e., \[StrCpl(f\_i) − StrCpl(f\_j)\] · φ\_{F\_S} \> PolicyCorrection), ΔStratum(f\_i, f\_j) tends to expand over time and stratum solidification tends to appear as a structural tendency.

Proof outline: by dSFR/dt ∝ StrCpl · φ\_{F\_S}, if StrCpl(f\_i) \> StrCpl(f\_j), then dSFR(f\_i)/dt \> dSFR(f\_j)/dt; when PolicyCorrection \< \[StrCpl(f\_i) − StrCpl(f\_j)\] · φ\_{F\_S}, dΔStratum/dt \> 0 and ΔStratum tends to expand; by Social Chapter 1’s coupling asymmetry property, F\_S’s influence on f\_i is stronger than f\_i’s influence on F\_S, making the StrCpl differential difficult to self-eliminate; therefore under conditions of insufficient PolicyCorrection, stratum solidification is a structural tendency. ∎

Theorem Two: Stratum Mobility Resistance Theorem

In a high-K\_{F\_S} social field, the sustained input intensity MobCost required for an individual field to migrate from a low-stratum Familiar Route to a high-stratum Familiar Route rises monotonically with K\_{F\_S} and rises monotonically as φ\_{F\_S} declines.

Proof outline: MobCost ∝ ΔStratum · K\_{F\_S} / (φ\_{F\_S} + ε); by Social Chapter 2’s Compression Inevitability, K\_{F\_S} trends upward and φ\_{F\_S} trends downward; therefore MobCost rises systematically as the social field develops — stratum mobility is necessarily more difficult in historically older social fields than in nascent ones. ∎

Theorem Three: Pure Resource Equalization Insufficient to Eliminate Stratum Familiar Routes

Pure economic resource equalization is insufficient to eliminate ΔStratum — as long as differences in FamOverlap(f\_i, F\_S) persist, ΔStratum(f\_i, f\_j) will not disappear and stratum Familiar Route solidification will be maintained. Note: if resource equalization simultaneously brings changes to FamOverlap components (e.g., educational resource equalization changing LanguageOverlap and NormOverlap), ΔStratum can be partially compressed; the constraint of this theorem is “pure resource transfer,” not a denial of all effects of resources.

Proof outline: SFR is determined by StrCpl = FamOverlap · Cpl; economic resource equalization directly changes the magnitude of Cpl but does not directly change the five components of FamOverlap (LanguageOverlap, NormOverlap, NetworkOverlap, InstitutionOverlap, PathPersistence); if FamOverlap differentials persist, StrCpl differentials persist, and ΔStratum continues to tend toward expansion; therefore pure resource equalization (without simultaneously changing FamOverlap structure) is insufficient to eliminate stratum solidification. ∎

## Section 7. Boundary Cases and Structural Breakdown Conditions

Boundary Case One (Strong disturbance restructuring): If Shock \> T\_replace(F\_S), the social field undergoes structural reorganization, old high-Familiarity paths are fractured, and ΔStratum may be temporarily compressed. Revolutions in history have sometimes compressed stratum Familiar Route differentials in the short term. However, by Social Chapter 3’s Theorem Three (Deep Lock-in Directional Constraint), new stratum structures are still constrained by δ\_res^C and tend to re-differentiate within a few generations. This case delineates the applicable boundary of Theorem One (the condition of insufficient PolicyCorrection) and does not overturn it.

Boundary Case Two (High-φ\_{F\_S} nascent social field): If the social field is in its early formation period (φ\_{F\_S} high, K\_{F\_S} low), MobCost is very low, stratum mobility cost approaches zero, and stratum Familiar Route differentials have not yet solidified. Certain nascent societies in history (immigrant societies, early periods of new post-revolutionary regimes) have exhibited higher stratum mobility. This is a boundary condition and does not overturn Theorem Two — Theorem Two describes the tendency after temporal evolution, not the initial state.

Boundary Case Three (Cross-field coupling breakthrough): If a low-stratum individual field rapidly establishes overlap with the social field’s high-Familiarity paths through strong coupling with an external field with high FamOverlap (e.g., the stratum-crossing function of the education system), the FamOverlap differential can be partially bridged and ΔStratum can be compressed. This is the genuine mechanism of stratum mobility — not resource transfer but reconstruction of FamOverlap. This case supports the corollary of Theorem Three: genuinely effective stratum mobility policy must target FamOverlap, not only economic resources.

## Section 8. Summary Formula

Stratum Familiar Route dynamic general formula:

SocialGenesis = 1 ∧ ∃ f\_i, f\_j: StrCpl(f\_i) ≠ StrCpl(f\_j) ∧ \[StrCpl(f\_i) − StrCpl(f\_j)\] · φ\_{F\_S} \> PolicyCorrection ⇒ dΔStratum/dt \> 0 (stratum differential tends to expand)

SFR(f\_i) ↑ → FlowShare(f\_i) ↑ → Cpl(f\_i → F\_S) ↑ → StrCpl(f\_i → F\_S) ↑ → SFR(f\_i) deepens further (stratum solidification self-reinforcement)

K\_{F\_S} ↑ ∧ φ\_{F\_S} ↓ ⇒ MobCost(f\_i, f\_j) ↑ (stratum mobility cost rises with social field solidification)

Resource equalization ∧ FamOverlap differential not eliminated ⇒ ΔStratum does not disappear (pure resource equalization does not eliminate strata)

Shock \> T\_replace ⇒ ΔStratum may be temporarily compressed, but δ\_res^C constraint causes stratum differentials to tend toward reconstruction

Pure resource redistribution is insufficient to eliminate stratum solidification; the fundamental path is to change the FamOverlap structure between different individual fields and the social field’s high-Familiarity paths — and the sustained input required to change FamOverlap structure is systematically absorbed by old coupling structures in high-K\_{F\_S} social fields.
