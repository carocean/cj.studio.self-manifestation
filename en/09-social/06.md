# Opinion Flow Concentration

This chapter argues a central proposition:

To acknowledge that multiple individual fields’ continuation flows exist within a social field is necessarily to acknowledge that these continuation flows are not uniformly distributed across topic paths, but under certain conditions tend toward concentration — when the OpinionFlow of a topic path exceeds the critical threshold θ\_opinion, positive feedback is triggered, and continuation flows accelerate their convergence on that path; public opinion is not the democratic aggregation of views, but the non-uniform concentration structure of continuation flows within the social field.

We seek to identify the dynamic equations of opinion flow concentration, and the conditions for determining its formation, maintenance, and collapse.

We first state the result.

The core equation of opinion flow concentration:

OpinionFlow(T, F\_S) = ∑\_{f\_i ∈ F\_S} Cpl(f\_i → T) · FlowShare(f\_i → T, F\_S)

OpinionFlow(T) \> θ\_opinion ⇒ positive feedback triggered, opinion flow concentration forms

In a single sentence

Public opinion is not a reflection of the majority’s views,  
but a self-organizing concentration of social field continuation flows on a topic path after exceeding the critical threshold;  
once concentration forms, it actively absorbs and suppresses alternative discourse paths.

## Section 1. Purpose

Social Chapter 5 demonstrated the coupling tension between individual fields and the social field: the tension-equilibrium band is the source of social innovation, and assimilation dominance causes individual fields to lose differential-generative capacity. But Social Chapter 5 primarily addresses coupling dynamics at the individual field level, without addressing a more macroscopic question: when large numbers of individual fields simultaneously output continuation flows to the social field, how are these flows distributed across the topic space? Why do some topics suddenly “ignite” while others, even with good reasoning, cannot obtain social response?

The answer to this question is opinion flow concentration.

Opinion flow concentration is not the “agenda-setting” or “spiral of silence” of communication studies, but a phase-transition phenomenon of continuation flows on topic paths within the social field — when a topic path’s OpinionFlow exceeds the critical threshold θ\_opinion, the concentration effect triggers positive feedback, the continuation flow density on that path rapidly rises, forming a self-reinforcing opinion structure.

This chapter provides the complete dynamic mechanism of opinion flow concentration, explaining why public opinion has nonlinear eruption characteristics; why it is difficult for minority rational arguments to counter an established concentration; why opinion suppression often backfires; and why public opinion collapses suddenly.

## Section 2. Core Proposition

Communication scholarship typically explains public opinion through: agenda-setting — media determines what people think about; the spiral of silence — minority views are silenced under pressure; information cascades — individuals rely on others’ signals rather than independent judgment. These explanations correctly point to certain phenomena of public opinion, but offer no dynamic equations: why does concentration erupt, what is the eruption threshold, what are the collapse conditions?

The position of On Self-Manifestation is:

Opinion flow concentration is a phase transition of social field continuation flows on topic paths — when the OpinionFlow of a topic path exceeds the critical threshold θ\_opinion, the positive feedback mechanism (more flows entering → path Familiarity deepens → more flows preferentially select this path) is triggered, continuation flows accelerate their convergence, forming a self-organizing opinion structure.

This definition carries three key implications:

First, opinion flow concentration is a phase transition, not linear accumulation. When OpinionFlow(T) \< θ\_opinion, the topic path competes with other paths and continuation flows have not yet entered a single-path self-reinforcing concentration state; once OpinionFlow(T) exceeds θ\_opinion, positive feedback triggers and concentration accelerates at nonlinear speed, manifesting as opinion concentration “eruption” rather than “growth.”

Second, concentration structures actively suppress alternative paths. Already-formed high-OpinionFlow / high-FlowShare topic paths systematically suppress competing topics’ capacity to acquire continuation flows by occupying the social field’s Residual Capacity Ratio (φ\_{F\_S}) and raising compression strength against alternative paths (K\_{F\_S} locally rising) — this is why heterogeneous voices are suppressed when opinion erupts: not necessarily because of censorship, but because they cannot acquire continuation flow support in a field whose capacity is occupied.

Third, concentration has inherent instability. Concentration structures depend on sustained continuation flow input to maintain — when input weakens (topic exhausted, new event appears) or the Shock of an alternative path exceeds the replacement threshold, concentration quickly dissipates, manifesting as the sudden silence of public opinion.

## Section 3. Operator Mapping

This chapter introduces the following new definitions on the basis of Social Chapters 1 through 5 operators:

Topic path: denoted T, representing the set of stable continuation paths formed around a topic within the social field. Topic paths do not exist independently, but are a subset of the social field’s Familiar Route structure — the formation of T depends on at least some individual fields’ historical coupling with the topic path.

Opinion flow concentration intensity: OpinionFlow(T, F\_S, t), representing the total intensity of continuation flow directed toward topic path T within social field F\_S at time t.

OpinionFlow(T, F\_S) = ∑\_{f\_i ∈ F\_S} Cpl(f\_i → T, t) · FlowShare(f\_i → T, F\_S, t)

Where: Cpl(f\_i → T) is the coupling intensity between individual field f\_i and topic path T (determining coupling direction and intensity); FlowShare(f\_i → T) is the proportion of f\_i’s total output flowing toward T, with 0 ≤ FlowShare ≤ 1, and ∑\_T FlowShare(f\_i → T) = 1 (the sum of an individual field’s continuation flow distribution proportions across all topic paths equals 1). Note: FlowShare here uses a Type-B definition — a pure flow distribution proportion independent of coupling intensity, representing the individual field’s continuation flow allocation structure in multi-path competition; Cpl determines coupling direction and whether stable paths form, FlowShare determines flow allocation on already-formed paths, and the two do not overlap.

Concentration critical threshold: θ\_opinion, representing the value of OpinionFlow beyond which the positive feedback mechanism is triggered and concentration enters the self-reinforcing stage. θ\_opinion is jointly determined by the social field’s overall K\_{F\_S} and φ\_{F\_S}:

θ\_opinion ∝ φ\_{F\_S} / K\_{F\_S}

That is, the more solidified the social field (high K\_{F\_S}, low φ\_{F\_S}), the lower the concentration threshold θ\_opinion, and the more easily topic paths exceed the threshold to form concentration — in solidified social fields public opinion more easily erupts, but the range of topics that can erupt is narrower (topics covered by Familiar Routes).

Topic path Familiar Route depth: Familiarity(T, F\_S, t), representing the overall Familiar Route depth of topic path T within the social field — the degree to which T is embedded in the social field’s Co-Nucleus structure.

dFamiliarity(T)/dt ∝ OpinionFlow(T) · φ\_{F\_S} − Decay\_T · Familiarity(T)

Where Decay\_T is the natural dissipation rate of the topic path — after a topic loses continuation flow input, its Familiar Route depth dissipates over time.

Concentration seed: Seed(T, t\_0), representing the perturbation bias of topic path T at the initial moment — the initial continuation flow injection provided by an external event or accumulated historical Familiar Routes that triggers concentration. Seed(T) \> 0 is a necessary condition for the concentration positive feedback loop to start; when Seed(T) = 0, the positive feedback loop does not self-start. Note: ∑\_T OpinionFlow(T, F\_S) ≤ Capacity(F\_S) (the total concentration intensity in the social field is bounded by field capacity); when FlowShare(T) → 1, the saturation constraint causes dOpinionFlow(T)/dt → 0, and concentration does not grow without bound.

Concentration suppression effect: SuppressEffect(T, T’, F\_S), representing the degree to which topic path T’s high FlowShare suppresses the continuation flow of competing topic path T’.

SuppressEffect(T, T’) ∝ max(0, FlowShare(T, F\_S) − θ\_sup) · K\_{F\_S}

Where θ\_sup is the suppression activation threshold — when FlowShare(T) is below θ\_sup, the suppression effect is almost nonexistent; beyond this, the suppression effect rises nonlinearly (corresponding to attention capture, trending topic monopolization, and recommendation system lock-in).

## Section 4. Dynamic Equations

Concentration formation equation:

When OpinionFlow(T) \< θ\_opinion (pre-concentration phase):

dOpinionFlow(T)/dt ∝ OutFlow\_mean · Compat(T, F\_S) − Decay\_T · OpinionFlow(T)

Continuation flows compete among multiple topic paths, and OpinionFlow(T) is at a low-level dynamic equilibrium.

When OpinionFlow(T) \> θ\_opinion (post-concentration trigger):

dOpinionFlow(T)/dt ∝ OpinionFlow(T) · \[Familiarity(T, F\_S) · φ\_{F\_S} − Decay\_T\]

Note: in this equation φ\_{F\_S} appears as the social field’s write-in margin — the higher φ\_{F\_S}, the more easily new topic concentrations obtain space and deepen within the social field; the lower φ\_{F\_S} (the more solidified the field), the harder it is for formed concentrations to be replaced, but the harder it is for new concentrations to gain a foothold. As with φ\_{f\_i} in Social Chapter 5’s FamOverlap dynamic equation, this is plasticity margin, not resistance capacity.

Positive feedback trigger: Familiarity(T) ↑ → more individual fields preferentially select topic path T (Familiar Route Priority Law) → FlowShare(T) ↑ → OpinionFlow(T) ↑ → Familiarity(T) deepens further

Concentration self-reinforcing loop:

OpinionFlow(T) ↑ → Familiarity(T, F\_S) ↑ → θ\_sep declines (topic path more easily enters individual fields) → Cpl(f\_i → T) ↑ → OpinionFlow(T) rises further

Simultaneously: SuppressEffect(T, T’) ↑ → competing topic path T’ FlowShare ↓ → T’ continuation flows exhausted

Concentration collapse conditions:

OpinionFlow(T) rapidly declines ⟺

(a) A new topic path T’‘s Shock \> T\_replace(T), triggering topic replacement; or  
(b) Topic path T’s OutFlow input is exhausted (topic depleted, no new events to supply); or  
(c) Familiarity(T) dissipates below θ\_opinion after sustained input ceases (natural concentration dissipation)

## Section 5. Three Forms of Opinion Flow Concentration

Opinion flow concentration manifests in three observable forms within the social field, depending on the trigger mechanism and maintenance mode:

Form One: Event-driven concentration (OutFlow-driven)

Triggered by an external event (high OutFlow input), the topic path receives a sudden large injection of continuation flows, rapidly exceeding θ\_opinion. Characteristics: fast eruption, fast dissipation (as event heat declines, OutFlow weakens and concentration quickly dissipates). Observable criterion: opinion erupts within hours to days of the event, and dissipates within one to two weeks of the event fading.

Form Two: Accumulative concentration (Familiarity-driven)

The topic path has long existed in the social field’s Familiar Route structure (high Familiarity), gradually approaching θ\_opinion under sustained low-intensity continuation flow input, erupting upon any small trigger (low-intensity event). Characteristics: eruption appears sudden but is the product of long accumulation; dissipation is slow (deep topic Familiar Routes, dissipation requires extended time). Observable criterion: opinion erupts after an “unremarkable” event and persists longer than the event’s own novelty.

Form Three: Suppression-induced concentration (anti-rejection trigger)

When a topic path is subjected to forced suppression (Shock injection forcing FlowShare down), the individual fields participating in the topic path continuously accumulate P\_Δ, which after exceeding their respective B\_{f\_i} triggers rejection (citing Social Chapter 3’s Reform Rebound Theorem: AltPath fails ∧ FlowReturn \> 0 ⇒ Familiarity(T, t+τ) \> Familiarity(T, t)), the topic path’s FlowShare rebounds and concentration re-erupts in stronger form. Observable criterion: discussion intensity of the topic rises in the short term after suppression (the dynamical version of the “Streisand Effect”).

## Section 6. Theorems

Theorem One: Opinion Concentration Formation Tendency Theorem

In any social field satisfying SocialGenesis = 1, under conditions of sustained multi-topic continuation flow competition, if there is initial asymmetry in topic path Familiarity (∃ T\_i, T\_j: Familiarity(T\_i, t\_0) ≠ Familiarity(T\_j, t\_0)) and non-zero Seed input (∃ T: Seed(T) \> 0), the system systematically tends toward the appearance of at least one topic path T whose OpinionFlow exceeds θ\_opinion, forming concentration.

Proof outline: by Social Chapter 2’s Compression Inevitability, K\_{F\_S} trends upward; by θ\_opinion ∝ φ\_{F\_S} / K\_{F\_S}, K\_{F\_S} ↑ causes θ\_opinion to decline (concentration threshold lowers, more easily exceeded); by Field Chapter 11’s Familiar Route Priority Law, in multi-path competition, high-Familiarity paths systematically acquire higher FlowShare; under conditions where average Cpl does not tend to zero and participating individual field scale does not decline, FlowShare(T) ↑ will push OpinionFlow(T) ↑; therefore over sufficient time, the system systematically tends toward the appearance of some topic path T whose OpinionFlow exceeds the continually declining θ\_opinion, and concentration forms. ∎

Theorem Two: Concentration Argument-Resistance Theorem

In a social field where concentration has already formed (OpinionFlow(T) \> θ\_opinion), an individual field f\_i’s independent differential generation (P\_Δ(f\_i) \> 0, supporting alternative topic T’) cannot break the concentration through logical argument alone — alternative topic T’ requires Shock \> T\_replace(T) to trigger genuine topic replacement. Note: this theorem addresses the structural allocation of continuation flows and does not involve truth-value judgment about topic content — the maintenance of opinion flow concentration does not equal the truthfulness of topic content; the influence of truth-value on concentration operates indirectly through real-world feedback (real events injecting Seed and Shock), which belongs to the discussion of Social Chapter 8 (Consensus Formation).

Proof outline: in the concentration state FlowShare(T) \> θ\_sup, so SuppressEffect(T, T’) ∝ max(0, FlowShare(T) − θ\_sup) · K\_{F\_S} ≫ 0, systematically suppressing T’ continuation flows; individual f\_i’s OutFlow is far smaller than the entire concentration structure’s OpinionFlow(T); by Social Chapter 3’s Reform Rebound Theorem, inputs below the replacement threshold are absorbed and reinforce the existing structure; therefore T’ cannot break T’s concentration through a single f\_i’s logical output unless Shock(T’) \> T\_replace(T). ∎

Theorem Three: Suppression Rebound Theorem

When forced suppression is applied to an already-formed concentration topic path T (forcibly lowering FlowShare(T)), if the suppression intensity does not exceed T\_replace(T), the rejection mechanism is triggered and concentration returns in stronger form after suppression dissipates; if the suppression intensity exceeds T\_replace(T), the concentration is broken, but the replacement topic T’’ may appear in a harder-to-control form.

Proof outline: suppression below the replacement threshold corresponds to Social Chapter 3’s Reform Rebound Theorem, AltPath fails ∧ FlowReturn \> 0 ⇒ Familiarity(T, t+τ) \> Familiarity(T, t); suppression exceeding the replacement threshold triggers Field Chapter 22’s fracture-type disturbance, the old topic path collapses, but when continuation flows are redistributed they preferentially enter the next-highest Familiarity path, potentially forming a new concentration. ∎

## Section 7. Boundary Cases and Structural Breakdown Conditions

Boundary Case One (Multi-polar concentration coexistence): If Frag(F\_S) within the social field is high (high internal fragmentation), multiple topic paths may simultaneously exceed their respective local θ\_opinion, forming multi-polar opinion concentrations that mutually suppress but do not mutually collapse. This state corresponds to the opinion polarization of social fracture periods — not a single concentration but multiple parallel concentrations coexisting in the same social field. This is a boundary condition that extends rather than overturns Theorem One.

Boundary Case Two (Dispersed state in high-φ\_{F\_S} social field): If the social field is in a high-φ\_{F\_S} state (low K\_{F\_S}, typically a nascent social field or reconstruction period after disturbance fracture), θ\_opinion is systematically high, and continuation flows are difficult to accumulate on a single topic path to the concentration threshold — topics disperse and opinions are pluralistic. This is a boundary condition supporting Theorem One (K\_{F\_S} trending upward causes θ\_opinion to decline) and does not overturn it.

Boundary Case Three (External forced injection breaking concentration): If an external field (such as authoritative media or state apparatus) forcibly injects Shock exceeding T\_replace into the social field, the existing concentration can be broken and continuation flows redirected in the short term. However, by Field Chapter 18’s residual constraint, the broken concentration topic path leaves behind δ\_res^C, and after the forced pressure dissipates, continuation flows may flow back. This is a structural breakdown condition corresponding to the supra-threshold case of Theorem Three and does not overturn the theorem.

## Section 8. Summary Formula

Opinion flow concentration dynamic general formula:

SocialGenesis = 1 ∧ multi-topic competition ∧ ∃ T\_i, T\_j: Familiarity(T\_i, t\_0) ≠ Familiarity(T\_j, t\_0) ∧ ∃ T: Seed(T, t\_0) \> 0 ∧ θ\_opinion declines as K\_{F\_S} ↑ ⇒ system systematically tends toward ∃ T: OpinionFlow(T) \> θ\_opinion (Opinion Concentration Formation Tendency Theorem)

OpinionFlow(T) \> θ\_opinion ⇒ Familiarity(T) ↑ → FlowShare(T) ↑ → OpinionFlow(T) accelerates (concentration positive feedback)

OpinionFlow(T) ↑ → SuppressEffect(T, T’) ↑ → alternative topic path continuation flows exhausted (minority views suppressed)

Shock(T’) \< T\_replace(T) ⇒ alternative topic cannot break concentration (Concentration Argument-Resistance Theorem)

Forced suppression Shock\_suppress \< T\_replace(T) ⇒ concentration stronger after suppression (Suppression Rebound Theorem)

OpinionFlow(T) collapses ⟺ OutFlow exhausted ∨ Shock(T’’) \> T\_replace(T) ∨ Familiarity(T) dissipates below θ\_opinion

Public opinion is not directly controlled by rational input, but is jointly determined by the social field’s K\_{F\_S}, φ\_{F\_S}, the historical Familiar Route depth of topic paths, and continuation flow input intensity — the fundamental path to changing public opinion is not providing better arguments, but changing the social field’s structural parameters or providing a new topic event that exceeds the replacement threshold.
