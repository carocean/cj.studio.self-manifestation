# Consensus Collapse and Social Fracture

This chapter argues a central proposition:

Social Chapter 8 demonstrated consensus deepening: under conditions where CrossCpl persists and FamOverlap does not decline, δ(Core\_S) systematically tends to rise and the Dominant Core stabilizes. This chapter addresses its mirror image — consensus collapse: when the social field’s internal fragmentation (Frag) continuously rises and FamOverlap among Sub-Cores continuously declines, the Dominant Core loses its integrative advantage over the social field, or enters the process of death through depth decay (Death(Core\_S)), and multiple Sub-Cores begin competing for continuation flow dominance, causing structural fracture of the social field.

We seek to identify the dynamic equations of consensus collapse, and the conditions for determining Death(Core\_S), DominanceLoss(Core\_S), Sub-Core competition, and social fracture.

We first state the result.

The core equation of consensus collapse:

dδ(Core\_S)/dt ∝ CrossCpl\_mean · FamOverlap\_mean · φ\_{F\_S} − Decay(Core\_S)

When Decay(Core\_S) \> CrossCpl\_mean · FamOverlap\_mean · φ\_{F\_S}, dδ/dt \< 0, δ(Core\_S) trends toward decay, and consensus enters the collapse process.

In a single sentence

Consensus collapse is not caused by someone “attacking” consensus,  
but by rising internal fragmentation causing Decay to exceed the deepening rate —  
the Dominant Core loses integrative capacity, multiple Sub-Cores begin competing  
for continuation flow dominance, and this is the dynamic root of social fracture.

## Section 1. Purpose

The Summary Formula of Social Chapter 8 gave the necessary conditions for consensus deepening: SocialGenesis = 1 ∧ CrossCpl\_mean \> 0 ∧ FamOverlap\_mean not declining ∧ CrossCpl · FamOverlap · φ \> Decay(Core\_S). This chapter addresses what happens when these conditions are not satisfied.

Historical consensus collapses — the Reformation, ideological splits, cultural fractures, political polarization — share a common pattern: not that a single event toppled old consensus, but that the decay rate of the old Co-Nucleus persistently exceeded its deepening rate, the Dominant Core gradually lost its capacity to integrate individual fields in the social field, and multiple competitive Sub-Cores began independently deepening in different segments of the social field, ultimately forming a multi-core competitive configuration of mutual suppression without mutual absorption.

This process is social fracture, not social revolution. Social fracture does not necessarily lead to revolution — many societies sustain themselves in multi-core competitive states for extended periods without collapse (the United States over the past two decades, Lebanon, Belgium, Northern Ireland). Revolution requires additional conditions (power field destabilization), to be developed in Social Chapters 10 through 12.

This chapter addresses only the complete dynamic chain: from consensus deepening to Dominant Core death, through to multi-Sub-Core competition.

## Section 2. Core Proposition

The position of On Self-Manifestation is:

Consensus collapse (Consensus Collapse) is the dynamic process in which, after the social field’s internal fragmentation structure (Frag) exceeds a critical value, the Dominant Core’s decay rate persistently exceeds its deepening rate, causing δ(Core\_S) to irreversibly trend downward and ultimately triggering Death(Core\_S) or DominanceLoss(Core\_S) — not external attack that destroys consensus, but internal fragmentation that prevents the Co-Nucleus from maintaining its own structural depth or competitive advantage.

This definition carries three key implications:

First, the direct dynamic condition for consensus collapse is Decay(Core\_S) persistently exceeding the deepening term, not heterogeneous information itself. Heterogeneous information (the differential output of high-P\_Δ individual fields) can trigger Shock, but as long as Shock \< T\_replace(Core\_S), heterogeneous information is absorbed by the Co-Nucleus without changing δ. What actually causes δ to persistently decline is Decay(Core\_S) \> CrossCpl\_mean · FamOverlap\_mean · φ\_{F\_S} — and Frag(F\_S) ↑ is the primary source driving Decay ↑: rising fragmentation accelerates Co-Nucleus decay, while new cross-field coupling (CrossCpl\_mean) is insufficient to compensate, and δ gradually declines. Note: if external forced integration (high PolicyCorrection\_S) or external common threat (high NaturalDecohesion) is sufficient to suppress Frag from rising, then Decay does not necessarily exceed the deepening term — Frag ↑ is one sufficient triggering condition, not the only one.

Second, Death(Core\_S) does not equal the disappearance of the social field. After the Dominant Core dies, the social field still exists, but is no longer integrated by a single Dominant Core — multiple Sub-Cores begin independently deepening in their respective segments, forming a multi-core competitive configuration. The social field transitions from “single-core integrated state” to “multi-core fractured state”: this is the structural definition of social fracture.

Third, Sub-Core competition does not necessarily converge. Under certain conditions (restorative coupling with high CrossCpl, strong external Shock injecting new integrating force), multi-core competition may converge toward the formation of a new Dominant Core; under other conditions, multi-core competition persists for extended periods, with each Sub-Core using flow competition (FlowShare contestation) and SubCoreSuppress mechanisms to mutually weaken competitors, but none reaching Dominant Core status, and the social field maintaining a fractured state.

## Section 3. Operator Mapping

This chapter introduces the following new definitions on the basis of Social Chapter 8 operators:

Sub-Core: denoted Core\_k (k = 1, 2, …, n), representing competitive Co-Nucleus structures that independently deepen in segments of the social field after Dominant Core death or dominance loss. Each Sub-Core satisfies δ(Core\_k) \> θ\_sub (minimum sedimentation depth threshold for Sub-Core establishment), but none satisfies δ(Core\_k) \> θ\_dominance (integration depth required to become Dominant Core).

CoreSet(F\_S): the Co-Nucleus set of the social field, defined as:

CoreSet(F\_S) = {Core\_1, Core\_2, …, Core\_n}

Dominant Core = argmax\_{Core\_k ∈ CoreSet} \[δ(Core\_k) · Ω(Core\_k)\]

When CoreSet has only one member satisfying integration conditions, that member is the Dominant Core (corresponding to Core\_S in Social Chapter 8); when multiple members compete, the social field enters the multi-core fractured state.

Fragmentation: Frag(F\_S, t), citing Field Chapters, representing the structural fragmentation degree within the social field. Frag ↑ directly causes Decay(Core\_S) ↑.

Sub-Core inter-FamOverlap: FamOverlap(Core\_i, Core\_j), representing the Familiar Route overlap degree between two Sub-Cores. FamOverlap(Core\_i, Core\_j) ↓ means the structural divide between Sub-Cores deepens, the possibility of mutual absorption declines, and the fractured state tends toward stability.

Sub-Core flow-share: FlowShare(Core\_k, F\_S), representing Sub-Core k’s proportion of continuation flows within the social field, satisfying ∑\_k FlowShare(Core\_k, F\_S) ≤ 1.

Sub-Core suppression effect: SubCoreSuppress(Core\_i, Core\_j), representing the degree to which Sub-Core i suppresses the continuation flows of Sub-Core j.

SubCoreSuppress(Core\_i, Core\_j) ∝ max(0, FlowShare(Core\_i) − θ\_sub\_sup) · K\_i

Where θ\_sub\_sup is the suppression activation threshold and K\_i = δ(Core\_i) · Ω(Core\_i) is the strength of Sub-Core i.

Death(Core\_S): the death of the Dominant Core, defined as δ(Core\_S) falling below θ\_dominance, losing the social field’s minimum integration depth:

Death(Core\_S) ⟺ δ(Core\_S) \< θ\_dominance

DominanceLoss(Core\_S): the loss of the Dominant Core’s integration advantage, defined as the existence of a Sub-Core whose structural strength has reached or exceeded the Dominant Core:

DominanceLoss(Core\_S) ⟺ ∃ Core\_k: K\_{Core\_k} ≥ K\_{Core\_S}, i.e., δ(Core\_k) · Ω(Core\_k) ≥ δ(Core\_S) · Ω(Core\_S)

Note: Death(Core\_S) and DominanceLoss(Core\_S) are two independent conditions, each capable of triggering on its own. Death is absolute depth decay; DominanceLoss is relative competitive failure. Both can cause the social field to enter the multi-core fractured state, but through different paths: Death is triggered by old Co-Nucleus decay, DominanceLoss by new nucleus ascent. Death(Core\_S) does not equal δ\_res^C = 0 — the old Dominant Core leaves Co-Nucleus residuals after death or fracture, constraining the growth direction of all subsequent Sub-Cores.

## Section 4. Dynamic Equations

Co-Nucleus decay equation:

dδ(Core\_S)/dt ∝ CrossCpl\_mean · FamOverlap\_mean · φ\_{F\_S} − Decay(Core\_S)

When Frag(F\_S) ↑: Decay(Core\_S) ∝ Frag · (1 − Recompression) ↑

When Decay(Core\_S) \> CrossCpl\_mean · FamOverlap\_mean · φ\_{F\_S}: dδ/dt \< 0, δ trends downward

Consensus collapse thresholds:

Death(Core\_S) ⟺ δ(Core\_S) \< θ\_dominance (absolute depth decay triggers death)

DominanceLoss(Core\_S) ⟺ ∃ Core\_k: K\_{Core\_k} ≥ K\_{Core\_S} (relative competitive failure triggers dominance loss)

Both can cause the social field to enter the multi-core fractured state, but through different paths.

Fragmentation dynamic equation:

dFrag(F\_S)/dt ∝ ΔCoreCompetition · (1 − PolicyCorrection\_S) − NaturalDecohesion

Where ΔCoreCompetition is the multi-Sub-Core competition intensity, defined as:

ΔCoreCompetition ∝ CoreParity · \[1 − FamOverlap\_mean(Core\_i, Core\_j)\] · SubSuppress\_mean

CoreParity represents the degree of competitive parity among multiple Sub-Cores (the closer the Sub-Core strengths, the higher CoreParity); FamOverlap\_mean(Core\_i, Core\_j) is the mean Familiar Route overlap among Sub-Cores (the lower it is, the stronger the mutual repulsion); SubSuppress\_mean is the mean suppression intensity. Note: when one nucleus is extremely strong and others extremely weak, CoreParity is low, ΔCoreCompetition is reduced, and the system may trend toward re-integration; when multiple nuclei are evenly matched, CoreParity is high and fragmentation accelerates. PolicyCorrection\_S is the social integration policy correction intensity; NaturalDecohesion is the natural de-fracturing term, such as common external threat or economic integration.

Sub-Core deepening competition equation:

dδ(Core\_k)/dt ∝ CrossCpl\_k · FamOverlap\_k · φ\_{F\_S,k} − Decay(Core\_k) − SubCoreSuppress\_mean(Core\_k)

Where φ\_{F\_S,k} is the local Residual Capacity Ratio of the division where Sub-Core k is located; SubCoreSuppress\_mean is the mean suppression intensity from other Sub-Cores.

Three outcomes of Sub-Core competition:

Outcome One (New Dominant Core formation): If a Sub-Core k sustains high CrossCpl\_k and low competitive suppression, δ(Core\_k) exceeds θ\_dominance while other Sub-Cores relatively weaken, then Core\_k becomes the new Dominant Core and the social field re-enters the single-core integrated state.

Outcome Two (Long-term multi-core fracture): If multiple Sub-Cores maintain δ between θ\_sub and θ\_dominance with SubCoreSuppress mutually balanced, the social field enters a long-term fractured state — no Dominant Core, multiple Sub-Cores coexisting in competition.

Outcome Three (Co-Nucleus structure collapse state): If all Sub-Cores’ δ falls below θ\_sub (total decay) and no new continuation flow injection triggers new nucleus formation, the social field enters a no-stable-nucleus state — no stable Co-Nucleus structure exists. Note: the absence of a Co-Nucleus does not equal the disappearance of the social field (the condition for SocialGenesis is the existence of CrossCpl, not the existence of a Co-Nucleus); the social field can continue to operate without a Dominant Core, but loses structural integrative capacity, corresponding to the highly unstable pre-state of civilization (Social Chapters 13–14).

## Section 5. Three Paths of Consensus Collapse

Path One: External shock path (Shock \> T\_replace). A single supra-threshold disturbance (military defeat, technological rupture, economic collapse) directly fractures the Dominant Core, δ\_res^C forms, but new integrating forces have not yet emerged, Frag rapidly rises, and multiple Sub-Cores compete for dominance in the fracture aftermath. Characteristic: fast collapse, but rapid reconsolidation may follow (a new Dominant Core quickly deepens under high-φ\_{F\_S} conditions). Historical empire collapses and the initial stage following religious schisms fit this pattern.

Path Two: Internal erosion path (cumulative Frag ↑). Without a single supra-threshold shock, Frag slowly rises over a long period (demographic pluralization, urban-rural fracture, solidification of economic inequality, media ecosystem bifurcation), Decay(Core\_S) gradually exceeds the deepening term, δ slowly declines, and the Dominant Core gradually dies without anyone noticing. Characteristic: slow collapse, often misidentified as “normal social pluralization,” until a critical point suddenly manifests as severe fracture. The polarization phenomena in contemporary democratic nations fit this pattern.

Path Three: Sub-Core competition acceleration path (multi-core competition triggering Frag positive feedback). Once multiple Sub-Cores form, each Sub-Core exclusively reinforces its own δ through opinion suppression (OpinionFlow capture) and economic flow bias (EconFlow concentration), while simultaneously weakening cross-core FamOverlap, causing Frag to rise further — this is a positive feedback loop: multi-core competition → Frag ↑ → harder to integrate → more intense competition → multi-core competition. Characteristic: once this loop is entered, natural convergence is extremely difficult without strong external Shock or policy correction.

## Section 6. Theorems

Theorem One: Consensus Collapse Tendency Theorem

In a social field satisfying SocialGenesis = 1, if Frag(F\_S) continuously rises and PolicyCorrection\_S is insufficient to offset it (dFrag/dt \> 0 sustained), the system systematically tends toward Decay(Core\_S) \> CrossCpl\_mean · FamOverlap\_mean · φ\_{F\_S}, δ(Core\_S) trends downward, and consensus systematically tends toward collapse.

Proof outline: by Decay(Core\_S) ∝ Frag · (1 − Recompression), Frag ↑ causes Decay ↑; if CrossCpl\_mean and FamOverlap\_mean do not rise synchronously (Frag ↑ typically accompanies CrossCpl\_mean ↓ and FamOverlap\_mean ↓), the deepening term persistently falls below the decay term; by dδ/dt \< 0, δ monotonically trends downward; when δ \< θ\_dominance, Death(Core\_S) triggers. Therefore under conditions of sustained fragmentation and insufficient correction, consensus collapse is a structural tendency. ∎

Theorem Two: Sub-Core Fracture Stability Theorem

After Death(Core\_S) or DominanceLoss(Core\_S) occurs, if FamOverlap(Core\_i, Core\_j) continues to decline (the structural divide among Sub-Cores deepens), the multi-core fractured state systematically tends toward stability and the formation of a new Dominant Core becomes increasingly difficult.

Proof outline: new Dominant Core formation requires some Sub-Core k’s δ(Core\_k) to exceed θ\_dominance, and the growth of δ(Core\_k) depends on FamOverlap(Core\_k, F\_S) ↑ (more individual fields joining Core\_k); FamOverlap(Core\_i, Core\_j) ↓ means members from other Sub-Cores cannot be absorbed by Core\_k (structural incompatibility); the SubCoreSuppress mechanism further suppresses competitors’ FlowShare; therefore FamOverlap persistently declining causes multi-core competition to trend toward solidification, and the difficulty of new integration systematically rises. ∎

Theorem Three: Old Nucleus Residual Constraint Theorem

After Death(Core\_S) occurs, δ\_res^C \> 0 constrains the growth direction of all Sub-Cores — no newly formed Co-Nucleus (whether a new Dominant Core or a competitive Sub-Core) can completely escape the structural residuals of the old Dominant Core, and new consensus systematically tends to partially overlap with old consensus.

Proof outline: by Field Chapter 18, Death\_C produces δ\_res^C, constraining the initial FamOverlap of new nuclei; all Sub-Cores grow within the same social field and share the same δ\_res^C constraint; therefore the growth direction of new consensus is biased by old nucleus residuals, and no consensus replacement begins from a structural zero point. ∎

## Section 7. Boundary Cases and Structural Breakdown Conditions

Boundary Case One (External threat triggering integration): A strong external threat (war, external competitive pressure) can provide high-intensity common Shock input in a short time, rapidly raising FamOverlap(Core\_i, Core\_j) among multiple Sub-Cores and triggering integration rather than fracture. This is the dynamical version of the “rally round the flag” effect. This is a structural breakdown condition demonstrating that Frag ↑ is not a sufficient condition for consensus collapse (the de-fracturing effect of external threats must not be strong enough to offset Frag ↑), and does not overturn Theorem One.

Boundary Case Two (High-intensity correction policy maintaining integration): If PolicyCorrection\_S is extremely high (forced educational homogenization, monopolized media narrative, mandatory shared rituals), CrossCpl\_mean and FamOverlap\_mean can be artificially maintained even as Frag rises, delaying δ decline. But this artificial maintenance has a cost: the forced assimilation rejection mechanism of Social Chapter 5 will simultaneously activate, and long-term high-pressure maintenance accumulates rebound tension. This is a boundary condition and does not overturn Theorem One (Theorem One’s conditions explicitly include insufficient PolicyCorrection\_S).

Boundary Case Three (Rapid reconsolidation after collapse): If external Shock \> T\_replace(Core\_S) and φ\_{F\_S} is very high (Residual Capacity Ratio rapidly recovers after fracture), a new Co-Nucleus may quickly deepen, bypassing the long-term multi-core fracture phase and directly forming a new Dominant Core. Rapid ideological unification following revolutions in history fits this pattern. This is a boundary condition supporting Theorem One (collapse can occur) and does not overturn Theorem Two (in this case FamOverlap did not persistently decline but was rapidly rebuilt by high φ\_{F\_S}).

## Section 8. Summary Formula

Consensus collapse and social fracture dynamic general formula:

Frag(F\_S) ↑ ∧ FamOverlap(Sub\_i, Sub\_j) ↓ ∧ PolicyCorrection\_S insufficient ⇒ Decay(Core\_S) \> CrossCpl · FamOverlap · φ ⇒ dδ(Core\_S)/dt \< 0 (consensus tends toward collapse)

dδ(Core\_S)/dt \< 0 sustained ∧ δ(Core\_S) \< θ\_dominance ⇒ Death(Core\_S) (Dominant Core death)

\[Death(Core\_S) ∨ DominanceLoss(Core\_S)\] ∧ FamOverlap(Core\_i, Core\_j) ↓ ⇒ multi-Sub-Core competition tends toward stability (social fracture solidifies)

Sub-Core competition outcomes:

\- δ(Core\_k) \> θ\_dominance (a Sub-Core breaks through the integration threshold) ⇒ new Dominant Core forms  
\- Multiple Sub-Core δ maintained in \[θ\_sub, θ\_dominance\] (competitive balance) ⇒ long-term fractured state  
\- All Sub-Core δ \< θ\_sub (total decay) ⇒ no-stable-nucleus state (social field loses structural integrative capacity)

δ\_res^C constrains the growth direction of all new consensus — old nucleus residuals mean no consensus replacement begins from a structural zero point

After the Dominant Core dies, the social field does not immediately disintegrate. Multiple Sub-Cores begin competing for continuation flow dominance within the social field. Under certain conditions this competition forms a new Dominant Core; under other conditions it further acts upon the power field and ultimately triggers revolution. This is what the subsequent chapters address.
