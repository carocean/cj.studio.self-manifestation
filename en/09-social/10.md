# The Field Dynamics of Power

This chapter argues a central proposition:

To acknowledge that multiple individual fields simultaneously output continuation flows within a social field is necessarily to acknowledge that these continuation flows cannot jointly occupy the same slot when competing for the finite continuation slots of the social field — when multiple candidate continuations compete for the same slot simultaneously, some fields are systematically more likely than others to occupy the subsequent continuation position; this systematic continuation priority, this structural positional advantage in continuation slot competition, is power. Power is not a resource or position that someone possesses, but the structural positional advantage of a field in continuation slot competition.

We seek to identify the dynamic equations of power structure formation, and the conditions for determining power deepening, power-Co-Nucleus co-evolution, and the power replacement threshold.

We first state the result.

The core equations of the power field:

PowerPos(f\_i, F\_S) ∝ Cpl(f\_i → F\_S) · FamOverlap(f\_i, F\_S) · K\_{f\_i} / (K\_{F\_S} + ε)

dδ(Power)/dt ∝ PowerPos\_mean · FlowShare(Power, F\_S) · φ\_{F\_S} − Decay(Power)

In a single sentence

Power is not something anyone possesses,  
but the structural position in which a field systematically prevails  
in continuation slot competition;  
a continuation is selected not because someone permitted it,  
but because that field’s PowerPos exceeds its competitors.

## Section 1. Purpose

Political science typically defines power as: the capacity to make others act according to one’s will (Weber), control over scarce resources (Dahl), or structural domination (Foucault). These definitions all correctly point to certain manifestations of power, but offer no generative equation: why can some fields influence others’ actions while other fields cannot? Why does power recur across different institutional forms? Why is a power structure, once formed, extremely difficult to change?

From field theory, these questions have a unified answer:

Individual fields within the social field continuously output continuation flows, and these continuation flows cannot jointly occupy the same slot when competing for the finite continuation slots of the social field — the finitude of continuation slots is the structural precondition for power generation. When multiple candidate continuations compete for the same slot simultaneously, the social field’s biased continuation mechanisms (K\_{F\_S}, FamOverlap, Cpl, etc.) cause certain candidate continuations to be systematically more likely to be selected than others. This systematic selection advantage is the dynamic source of power.

Power does not depend on consensus to exist — highly consensus-based societies (Chapter 8) and consensus-collapsed societies (Chapter 9) both have power fields, only with different power field configurations. Power also does not equal the state — the state is the solidified manifestation of the power field in a particular institutional form; the power field is more fundamental.

This chapter provides the complete dynamic mechanism of power structure formation, explaining why power appears in any social formation; why the power field, once formed, has a self-reinforcing deepening mechanism; and why a systemic co-evolutionary relationship exists between power and consensus.

## Section 2. Core Proposition

The position of On Self-Manifestation is:

Power is the structural positional advantage of a field in social field continuation slot competition — when multiple candidate continuations compete for the same finite slot simultaneously, the field with higher PowerPos is systematically more likely to occupy subsequent continuation; power is not the product of subjective will, but the objective result of the social field’s biased continuation structure (K\_{F\_S}, FamOverlap, Cpl) in continuation slot competition.

This definition carries three key implications:

First, the precondition for power generation is continuation slot finitude, not some institutional arrangement. Continuation slot finitude (non-joint-occupation) is a structural characteristic of the social field — any social field contains the dynamic configuration of multiple candidate continuations competing for finite slots, and power structures therefore systematically tend to form regardless of institutional form.

Second, PowerPos is a structural position, not a personal attribute. PowerPos(f\_i, F\_S) is jointly determined by the coupling intensity, path overlap, and individual field compression strength between the individual field and the social field — it is not that someone “possesses” power, but that a field occupies a high-PowerPos position in the current social field structure; when the social field structure changes, PowerPos changes accordingly.

Third, power and continuation flow distribution are mutually causal. Fields with high PowerPos receive more continuation flow support (FlowShare ↑), and more continuation flows cause that field’s FamOverlap and K\_{f\_i} to rise further, raising PowerPos further — this is the self-reinforcing mechanism of power, structurally consistent with the positive feedback mechanisms of economic flow bias (Social Chapter 7) and opinion flow concentration (Social Chapter 6).

## Section 3. Operator Mapping

This chapter introduces the following new definitions on the basis of the preceding nine chapters’ operators:

Continuation Slot: the upper limit on the number of effective continuations the social field can sustain per unit time. Continuation slot finitude is the structural precondition for power generation — when the number of candidate continuations exceeds slot capacity, the biased continuation mechanism determines which candidate continuations are selected.

Power field position: PowerPos(f\_i, F\_S, t), representing the structural continuation positional advantage of individual field f\_i within social field F\_S.

PowerPos(f\_i, F\_S) ∝ Cpl(f\_i → F\_S) · FamOverlap(f\_i, F\_S) · K\_{f\_i} / (K\_{F\_S} + ε)

That is, the higher the coupling intensity, path overlap, and individual field compression strength between the individual field and the social field, and the lower the social field’s overall compression strength (K\_{F\_S}), the higher the field’s power position. ε is the anti-divergence lower bound. Note: the denominator K\_{F\_S} represents the relative compression advantage threshold — the higher K\_{F\_S}, the more a field needs higher K\_{f\_i} to form advantage in continuation slot competition; high K\_{F\_S} does not suppress power formation but raises the structural threshold for entering PowerCore. Historically, highly solidified societies (Qin, Rome, modern great powers) simultaneously possessed high K\_{F\_S} and highly concentrated power precisely because PowerCore members’ K\_{f\_i} was also systematically far above average, maintaining a high K\_{f\_i}/K\_{F\_S} ratio. Note: PowerPos is the structural positional advantage at the current moment; its value is permitted to incorporate sedimentation results formed by historical FlowShare (both Cpl and FamOverlap are influenced by historical FlowShare) — PowerPos is a current state variable, not a pure causal variable, and the closure of the self-reinforcing loop is legitimate under this semantic definition.

Power field depth: δ(Power, F\_S, t), representing the historical sedimentation depth of power structure in the social field — corresponding to the accumulated depth of high-PowerPos fields’ long-term occupation of continuation slots. The higher δ(Power), the more stable the power structure and the harder it is to replace.

Power Core: PowerCore(F\_S), representing the field or field group with the highest PowerPos in the social field, corresponding to the dominant structure of the power field.

PowerCore(F\_S) = argmax\_{f\_i ∈ F\_S} \[PowerPos(f\_i, F\_S)\] (single core)  
or PowerCore(F\_S) = {f\_i: PowerPos(f\_i) \> θ\_power} (multi-core, θ\_power is the Power Core threshold)

When multiple f\_i have similar PowerPos and all exceed θ\_power, PowerCore is a multi-core set; when one core significantly exceeds the others, PowerCore is a single core.

Note: PowerCore in this chapter is treated as a unified concept. In actual social fields, PowerCore may differentiate into two structurally relatively independent sub-nuclei: the Legitimation Core (continuation priority derived from high FamOverlap(Power, Core\_S) embedded in consensus) and the Enforcement Core (continuation priority derived from high K\_{f\_i} coercive suppression). The two typically co-exist, but may separate and lose integration under conditions of social fracture or revolution — this is the developmental precondition for Social Chapter 12 (Revolutionary Disturbance Conditions), not developed further here.

Power replacement threshold: T\_replace(Power), representing the minimum disturbance intensity required to trigger genuine replacement of PowerCore. T\_replace(Power) ∝ δ(Power) · Ω(Power).

Power-Co-Nucleus coupling intensity: PowerCoreCpl(Power, Core\_S), representing the structural coupling degree between the power field and the Social Co-Nucleus. The higher PowerCoreCpl, the stronger the mutual reinforcement between the power field and consensus. Note: PowerCore and Core\_S are independent structures; the two are coupled through PowerCoreCpl without any necessary containment relationship — they can coincide (democratic legitimacy, traditional dynasties), partially overlap, or be completely separate (colonial rule, military occupation, late-stage authoritarian states). PowerCore describes continuation-slot-dominance structure; Core\_S describes consensus sedimentation structure; they are independent dimensions of field dynamics, linked through PowerCoreCpl rather than merged.

## Section 4. Dynamic Equations

Power field deepening equation:

dδ(Power)/dt ∝ PowerPos\_mean · FlowShare(Power, F\_S) · φ\_{F\_S} − Decay(Power)

Where PowerPos\_mean is the mean power field position of PowerCore members within the social field; FlowShare(Power, F\_S) is the continuation flow proportion occupied by PowerCore; φ\_{F\_S} is the social field’s Residual Capacity Ratio (writable capacity); Decay(Power) is the power field decay term, rising with Challenge intensity (challenge strength from competing fields) and declining with Consolidation strength (power field self-consolidation capacity).

Power self-reinforcing loop:

PowerPos(f\_i) ↑ → FlowShare(f\_i → F\_S) ↑ → Cpl(f\_i → F\_S) ↑ → FamOverlap(f\_i, F\_S) ↑ → PowerPos(f\_i) rises further

Simultaneously: high-PowerPos fields systematically suppress low-PowerPos fields’ capacity to acquire continuation flows through slot occupation — this suppression mechanism is structurally analogous to SuppressEffect in Social Chapter 6, proportional to max(0, FlowShare(Power) − θ\_power\_sup) · K\_{Power}, where θ\_power\_sup is the power suppression activation threshold.

Power-Co-Nucleus co-evolution equation:

When PowerCoreCpl is high, dδ(Power)/dt and dδ(Core\_S)/dt form positive coupling:

dδ(Power)/dt ∝ PowerCoreCpl · δ(Core\_S) · φ\_{F\_S} − Decay(Power)  
dδ(Core\_S)/dt ∝ PowerCoreCpl · δ(Power) · φ\_{F\_S} − Decay(Core\_S)

That is, power field deepening is driven by Co-Nucleus depth, and Co-Nucleus deepening is driven by power field depth — the deeper the power field, the more stable the Co-Nucleus; the deeper the Co-Nucleus, the more easily the power field acquires continuation flow support from consensus. PowerCoreCpl is the coupling intensity between the two, not unidirectional causality, but a bidirectional positive feedback structural linkage. Note: these equations represent coupling enhancement terms and do not replace the respective complete deepening equations (the complete forms of dδ(Power)/dt and dδ(Core\_S)/dt still require combining their respective PowerPos, FlowShare, and Decay terms).

Power replacement dynamics:

Shock \> T\_replace(Power) ⇒ PowerCore replacement ⇒ δ\_res^P forms (power field residual)

The new PowerCore redeepens under δ\_res^P constraint, and new power structure is biased by old power field residuals.

## Section 5. Three Forms of the Power Field

The power field manifests in three observable forms within the social field, depending on the combination of PowerCoreCpl and Frag(F\_S):

Form One: Consensus-integrated power (high PowerCoreCpl, low Frag). The power field is highly coupled with the Social Co-Nucleus — PowerCore’s continuation priority derives from its deep embedding in the Social Co-Nucleus structure (high FamOverlap(Power, Core\_S)). Observable phenomena: the legitimacy of power derives from consensus recognition; the existence of the power field reinforces consensus; challenging power is automatically equated with challenging consensus. Examples: traditional divine right, modern democratic legitimacy, scientific authority systems.

Form Two: Coercive-suppression power (low PowerCoreCpl, persistently high K\_{Power}/K\_{F\_S}). The power field is weakly coupled with the Social Co-Nucleus, but PowerCore’s relative compression advantage remains persistently high (high K\_{Power}/K\_{F\_S}) with strong coercive continuation suppression capacity. Observable phenomena: power maintains slot occupation through coercive means without depending on consensus; high Shock maintenance costs accelerate power field Decay; brittle collapse when PolicyCorrection fails. Examples: colonial rule, authoritarian suppression, military occupation.

Form Three: Multi-core competitive power (multiple PowerCores, high Frag). Multiple fields with similar PowerPos compete for continuation slots within the social field without any achieving integration. Observable phenomena: dispersed power, multiple power centers in mutual check; high decision costs; slow response to external Shock. Examples: feudal fragmentation, multiparty democracy, multipolar configurations in international relations.

## Section 6. Theorems

Theorem One: Power Formation Tendency Theorem

In any social field satisfying SocialGenesis = 1, with finite continuation slots (CandidateFlowCount \> SlotCount) and differential candidate continuations (∃ f\_i, f\_j: Output(f\_i) ≠ Output(f\_j)), under conditions where no sustained equilibrating mechanism completely offsets PowerPos differentials, the social field’s biased continuation mechanism systematically tends to cause some fields’ PowerPos to exceed others’, and power structure systematically tends to form.

Proof outline: by Field Chapter 11’s Familiar Route Priority Law, in multi-path competition, high-Familiarity paths systematically acquire higher FlowShare; slot finitude ensures not all candidate continuations can be selected; by PowerPos ∝ Cpl · FamOverlap · K\_{f\_i} / K\_{F\_S}, any initial differential in Cpl and FamOverlap is amplified through positive feedback; therefore under conditions of multiple candidate continuations competing for finite slots, PowerPos differentials systematically tend to expand and power structure tends to form. ∎

Theorem Two: Power Deepening Theorem

In a social field where power structure has already formed (∃ f\_i: PowerPos(f\_i) \> θ\_power), under conditions where PowerPos\_mean · FlowShare(Power) · φ\_{F\_S} \> Decay(Power), δ(Power) tends to rise over time, power structure systematically tends toward solidification, and T\_replace(Power) systematically tends to rise.

Proof outline: by dδ(Power)/dt ∝ PowerPos\_mean · FlowShare · φ − Decay, when the positive term exceeds the decay term, δ trends upward; by the power self-reinforcing loop, FlowShare(Power) ↑ causes Cpl ↑ causes FamOverlap ↑ causes PowerPos ↑, forming positive feedback; by T\_replace ∝ δ · Ω, δ ↑ causes T\_replace ↑; therefore once a power field forms, it systematically tends toward self-deepening and becomes increasingly difficult to replace. ∎

Theorem Three: Power-Co-Nucleus Co-Evolution Theorem

In a social field with high PowerCoreCpl, δ(Power) and δ(Core\_S) form positive feedback co-evolution — power field deepening reinforces the Co-Nucleus, Co-Nucleus deepening reinforces the power field; the two mutually constrain each other, causing power structure and consensus structure to systematically tend toward joint solidification.

Proof outline: by the Power-Co-Nucleus co-evolution equation, high PowerCoreCpl causes dδ(Power)/dt and dδ(Core\_S)/dt to move in the same direction; high δ(Core\_S) provides consensus foundation for the power field, further elevating PowerPos through high FamOverlap(Power, Core\_S); high δ(Power) provides structural protection for the Co-Nucleus, suppressing competitive differentials from entering the nucleus; therefore under high-PowerCoreCpl conditions, the two systematically tend toward joint deepening, with power and consensus mutually reinforcing. ∎

## Section 7. Boundary Cases and Structural Breakdown Conditions

Boundary Case One (Power dispersion in high-Frag society): If Frag(F\_S) is extremely high (the multi-core fractured state of Social Chapter 9), multiple competitive Sub-Cores each support different PowerCore candidates, PowerPos differentials are offset by multipolar competition, and power is difficult to deepen on a single PowerCore. Historical feudal fragmentation, the Warring States period, and post-revolutionary power vacuums fit this pattern. This is a boundary condition and does not overturn Theorem Two (Theorem Two holds under the condition PowerPos\_mean · FlowShare \> Decay, which high Frag makes difficult to satisfy).

Boundary Case Two (External strong Shock breaking power deepening): If external Shock \> T\_replace(Power), the old PowerCore is replaced, δ\_res^P forms, and new power structure redeepens under residual constraint. Historical revolutions, coups, and external conquest fit this pattern. This is a structural breakdown condition supporting Theorem Two (T\_replace can be exceeded by sufficiently strong Shock) and does not overturn it.

Boundary Case Three (Consensus collapse exposing the power field): If δ(Core\_S) rapidly declines (consensus collapse of Chapter 9), the high-PowerCoreCpl power field loses its consensus foundation, the contribution of FamOverlap(Power, Core\_S) to PowerPos weakens, and the power field is forced to shift toward coercive-suppression power (Form Two) or enter multi-core competition (Form Three). This is a boundary condition supporting Theorem Three (the co-evolution relationship breaks down when the high-PowerCoreCpl condition is no longer satisfied) and does not overturn it.

## Section 8. Summary Formula

Power field dynamics general formula:

SocialGenesis = 1 ∧ CandidateFlowCount \> SlotCount ∧ ∃ f\_i, f\_j: Output(f\_i) ≠ Output(f\_j) ⇒ PowerPos differentials systematically tend to expand ∧ power structure tends to form (Power Formation Tendency Theorem)

PowerPos(f\_i) ↑ → FlowShare ↑ → Cpl ↑ → FamOverlap ↑ → PowerPos rises further (power self-reinforcing loop)

PowerPos\_mean · FlowShare · φ \> Decay(Power) ⇒ dδ(Power)/dt \> 0 ∧ T\_replace(Power) ↑ (Power Deepening Theorem)

High PowerCoreCpl ⇒ dδ(Power)/dt ↑ ↔ dδ(Core\_S)/dt ↑ (Power-Co-Nucleus Co-Evolution Theorem)

Shock \> T\_replace(Power) ⇒ PowerCore replacement ∧ δ\_res^P constrains new power structure direction

The fundamental path to changing power structure is not opposing a particular power holder, but changing the social field’s biased continuation structure — changing the relative relationships of FamOverlap, Cpl, K\_{f\_i}/K\_{F\_S}; or providing disturbance exceeding T\_replace(Power) to fracture the old PowerCore. The former is incremental structural reform; the latter is revolution. This is what the subsequent chapters address.
