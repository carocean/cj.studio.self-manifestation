# Non-Social Conditions

This chapter argues a central proposition:

Outer Chapter 11 (Non-Social) provides a core proof: if group = compensation for insufficient capacity, and capacity can be sustainably enhanced, then group dependence tends to zero — the Self-Manifesting Being necessarily tends toward the non-social. But this proof requires two steps to be formalized in the Social Chapters: first, “at what threshold does individual capacity begin to allow withdrawal from group dependence”; and second, “in which key functional dimensions does individual capacity enhancement make group functions substitutable.” This chapter addresses the first question: non-social conditions.

The non-social is not anti-social, not the active rejection of groups, but the dissolution of the necessity of group dependence — the individual’s effective capacity in all key functional dimensions has already exceeded the functional gains that groups can provide, and the social group is no longer a prerequisite for that individual’s continuation and self-manifestation, but becomes optional.

We seek to identify the source structure of group dependence, the compositional dimensions of individual effective capacity, and the complete criteria for non-social conditions.

We first state the result.

Non-social condition:

∀ j ∈ KeyFunctions: IndividualCapacity\_j(f) ≥ GroupSupport\_j(f)

⇒ SocialDependency(f) → 0

⇒ Non-social condition holds

In a single sentence

Groups are not the ultimate destination of the Self-Manifesting Being,  
but a compensatory structure for the stage of insufficient capacity —  
when the individual’s effective capacity in all key functional dimensions  
exceeds the functional gains groups can provide,  
the group is no longer a prerequisite but becomes optional;  
at that point, the non-social condition holds.

## Section 1. Purpose

Social Chapters 1 through 18 addressed the complete dynamics of social field genesis, operation, and evolution, but all these mechanisms were built on an implicit premise: the individual field (f\_i) must be embedded in the social field (F\_S) to maintain sufficient continuation capacity and self-manifestation capacity. This premise was never explicitly examined — it was treated as self-evident.

Outer Chapter 11 fundamentally questions this premise: group dependence is not an essential feature of the Self-Manifesting Being, but a historical-stage feature of capacity structure. Under primordial biological conditions (humans have no gills, fish have no feet, perception limited, action restricted, cognition isolated), every individual must depend on groups to complete functions that cannot be accomplished alone — communication, division of labor, competition, tool production, knowledge accumulation. But as the Self-Manifesting Being’s capacities expand, these functions that only groups could originally provide can be realized by individuals through other means.

This chapter provides the complete formalization of “non-social conditions”: when individual effective capacity exceeds group support in all key dimensions, the social group is no longer a prerequisite for survival and self-manifestation.

Note: the holding of non-social conditions does not presuppose that the non-social necessarily occurs — it only means that at this point, group dependence is no longer dynamically compulsory. The necessity of the non-social belongs to Social Chapter 20.

## Section 2. Core Proposition

The position of On Self-Manifestation is:

Group dependence (SocialDependency) is essentially a compensatory structure for the gap between individual effective capacity and required functions — as long as an individual cannot independently complete some key function (perception, action, construction, communication, cognition, continuation), it will systematically tend toward coupling with other individuals to fill this gap, thereby forming a social group. When individual effective capacity exceeds the gains groups can provide in all key functional dimensions, the dynamic source of group dependence disappears and the individual enters non-social conditions.

This proposition carries three key implications:

First, group dependence is functional, not purposive. Humans aggregate not because aggregation itself has value, but because aggregation can fill individual capacity gaps. The moral value, emotional meaning, and cultural meaning of social groups are all secondarily formed on the basis of functional aggregation — this chapter exclusively addresses the functional dimension and makes no judgment about secondary dimensions.

Second, effective capacity is multidimensional, and non-social conditions must simultaneously hold in all key dimensions. An individual with extremely strong capacity may have already surpassed the group in the cognitive dimension while still depending on tools or group cooperation in the physical action dimension — in this case non-social conditions do not fully hold. Only full-dimensional transcendence makes group dependence truly tend to zero.

Third, “capacity surpassing the group” does not require infinite expansion of a single field. Through body substitution, body augmentation and tool extension, sensory extension, external action connection, cognitive parallelism, memory externalization, and sustainable self-repair, the upper bound of individual effective capacity tends toward openness relative to primordial group functional requirements — not infinite single field capacity, but multi-level extension mechanisms allowing effective capacity to persistently exceed the group dependence threshold.

Note: non-social conditions implicitly require that GroupSupport\_j has a finite effective upper bound — that is, for the individual’s actual functional requirements, the support groups can provide has a surpassable ceiling. If the group itself also continuously upgrades and its growth rate exceeds individual capacity enhancement rate, non-social conditions may not be reachable. This chapter assumes that on the individual capacity-expansion pathway, the growth rate of IndividualCapacity can sustainably exceed that of GroupSupport.

Note: SocialGain → 0 is not a sufficient condition for non-social conditions — group functional degradation (GroupSupport → 0) also causes SocialGain → 0, but in that case individual capacity has not necessarily surpassed the group, and non-social conditions do not necessarily hold (both parties may be declining simultaneously). The correct criterion for non-social conditions is ∀ j: IndividualCapacity\_j ≥ GroupSupport\_j, not SocialGain → 0.

## Section 3. Operator Mapping

This chapter introduces the following new definitions on the basis of Social Chapter 18 operators:

Group dependence: SocialDependency(f\_i), representing the functional dependence intensity of individual field f\_i on social group F\_S:

SocialDependency(f\_i) = Σ\_j w\_j · max(0, GroupSupport\_j(f\_i) − IndividualCapacity\_j(f\_i)) / Σ\_j w\_j · GroupSupport\_j(f\_i)

That is, group dependence is the weighted proportion across key functional dimensions of “group support exceeding individual capacity”; if individual capacity exceeds group support in all dimensions, SocialDependency → 0.

Key functional dimension set: KeyFunctions = {Sensory Extension, Action Execution, Construction and Manufacturing, Communication and Information Acquisition, Cognition and Computation, Continuation and Repair}, being the set of core functions that social groups under primordial conditions can provide but individuals alone cannot fully provide.

Capacity gap: CapacityGap\_j(f\_i) := max(0, GroupSupport\_j(f\_i) − IndividualCapacity\_j(f\_i)), representing the amount of group support not yet covered by individual capacity in the j-th dimension; CapacityGap\_j = 0 indicates non-social conditions hold for that dimension.

Group functional support: GroupSupport\_j(f\_i), representing the maximum functional gain that the social group can provide for individual f\_i in the j-th key functional dimension — including specialized division of labor, knowledge accumulation, collective action, tool sharing, etc.

Individual effective capacity: IndividualCapacity\_j(f\_i), representing individual f\_i’s actually available capacity in the j-th key functional dimension, comprising:

IndividualCapacity\_j = NativeCapacity\_j + ExtendedCapacity\_j

Where NativeCapacity\_j is the native capacity of the individual field itself; ExtendedCapacity\_j is the effective capacity increment obtained through body substitution (BodySubstitution), external tools (ExternalTool), sensory extension (SensoryExtension), cognitive parallelism (CognitiveParallel), memory externalization (MemoryExternalization), and self-repair mechanisms (SelfRepair).

Note: ExtendedCapacity\_j is not directly constrained by the single-field internal capacity ceiling (Cap\_f), because it primarily depends on the expansion of external resources and tool structures rather than the internal capacity of a single sedimentation field — this is the structural reason why the upper bound of the Self-Manifesting Being’s effective capacity tends toward openness. Note: externally extended capacity must ultimately enter the individual field through observation circuits, so it is not completely decoupled from field structural constraints; but its growth is not capped by single field capacity.

Social coupling cost: SocialCost(f\_i), representing the sustained resource consumption required for individual field f\_i to maintain social coupling: including Core\_S constraint costs (Social Chapters 1 through 12), intergenerational transmission costs (Social Chapter 17), historical residual bias costs (Social Chapter 16), etc.

Social functional gain: SocialGain(f\_i) = Σ\_j w\_j · max(0, GroupSupport\_j(f\_i) − IndividualCapacity\_j(f\_i)), i.e., the net functional gain actually provided by the social group (counting only dimensions where individual capacity has not yet been covered).

Non-social condition: NonSocialCondition(f\_i), requiring the following four conditions to simultaneously hold:

Condition One (Full-dimensional transcendence): ∀ j ∈ KeyFunctions: IndividualCapacity\_j(f\_i) ≥ GroupSupport\_j(f\_i)

Condition Two (Extended capacity robustness): ExtendedCapacity\_j(f\_i) can be independently maintained by the individual — that is, extended capacity does not depend on the continuous operation of social group infrastructure, but comes from tool structures the individual can autonomously control and repair; extended capacity dependent on social intermediaries is not counted in IndividualCapacity but only in GroupSupport.

Condition Three (Demand finiteness): the individual’s actual functional requirements have a finite upper bound — non-social conditions address the functional set actually required by the individual, not hypothetically infinitely expanding requirements.

Condition Four (Functional criterion): non-social conditions describe only the dissolution of functional dependence, not emotional attachment, habitual sedimentation, or other secondary dependence — functional non-social ≠ emotional non-social; the latter belongs to a different level of analysis.

That is, individual effective capacity in all key functional dimensions meets or exceeds group support levels, and this capacity has independent maintainability.

## Section 4. Dynamic Equations

Social dependence equation:

dSocialDependency(f\_i)/dt ∝ −dIndividualCapacity/dt · CapacityGap(f\_i) / GroupSupport(f\_i)

That is, when individual effective capacity persistently increases and functional gaps exist, social dependence systematically declines; when functional gaps tend to zero, dependence decline tends to stop (non-social conditions reached).

Effective capacity expansion equation:

dIndividualCapacity\_j/dt ∝ dNativeCapacity\_j/dt + dExtendedCapacity\_j/dt

Where dExtendedCapacity\_j/dt depends on tool self-manufacturing capacity (ToolSelfMake), body extensibility (BodyExtensibility), and external resource accessibility (ExternalResourceAccess); these three factors are not constrained by single field capacity, and can remain persistently positive on the Self-Manifesting Being’s active upgrade pathway.

Non-social condition dynamic equation:

NonSocialCondition holds ⇒ SocialGain(f\_i) → 0; the reverse does not hold: SocialGain → 0 does not imply NonSocialCondition, because group functional degradation can also cause SocialGain to trend toward zero without involving individual capacity transcendence.

That is, when non-social conditions hold, the net functional gain the social group provides for the individual tends to zero; but net functional gain tending to zero is not itself a non-social condition — group functional degradation can equally cause SocialGain to tend toward zero, without involving individual capacity transcendence.

Note: non-social conditions holding does not mean social relationships immediately sever — a transition zone exists (SocialGain \> 0 but trending toward 0) within which social relationships become optional rather than obligatory, and individuals can autonomously decide whether to maintain coupling.

## Section 5. Six Dimensions of Non-Social Conditions

Non-social conditions must simultaneously hold in six key functional dimensions:

Dimension One: Sensory Extension. Under primordial conditions, groups provide extended perception (sentinels, reconnaissance, group information networks); non-social condition: the individual independently achieves equivalent or superior perceptual coverage through sensory extension devices and external perceptual systems. Criterion: IndividualSensory ≥ GroupSensory\_network.

Dimension Two: Action Execution. Under primordial conditions, groups provide collective action capacity (construction, transport, combat); non-social condition: the individual independently completes action tasks originally requiring group cooperation through body augmentation and tool extension, mechanical external connection, and self-manufactured tools. Criterion: IndividualAction ≥ GroupAction\_collective.

Dimension Three: Construction and Manufacturing. Under primordial conditions, groups provide specialized manufacturing (technology accumulation, tool chains, production cooperation); non-social condition: the individual independently completes construction and manufacturing requirements through self-manufacturing capacity (ToolSelfMake) and integrated design-manufacture. Criterion: IndividualConstruction ≥ GroupManufacturing.

Dimension Four: Communication and Information Acquisition. Under primordial conditions, groups provide information networks (language, writing, education, media); non-social condition: the individual no longer needs the group as information intermediary through cognitive parallelism, direct access to external knowledge bases, and autonomous information processing. Criterion: IndividualInformation ≥ GroupInformation\_network.

Dimension Five: Cognition and Computation. Under primordial conditions, groups provide collective intelligence (specialized cognition, cooperative decision-making, specialized knowledge); non-social condition: the individual independently reaches or surpasses collective group cognitive effectiveness through cognitive parallelism (CognitiveParallel) and external cognitive field connection. Criterion: IndividualCognition ≥ GroupCognition\_collective.

Dimension Six: Continuation and Repair. Under primordial conditions, groups provide continuation guarantees (medical care, mutual aid, defense, food production); non-social condition: the individual independently maintains continuation and repair requirements through self-repair mechanisms (SelfRepair) and survival self-sufficiency (SurvivalSelfSufficiency). Criterion: IndividualSurvival ≥ GroupSurvival\_support.

Full-dimensional non-social condition: when all six criteria simultaneously hold, SocialDependency → 0 and the non-social condition is fully established.

## Section 6. Theorems

Theorem One: Group Dependence Origin Theorem

The fundamental source of group dependence is capacity gaps (CapacityGap) in key functional dimensions — as long as any dimension j exists such that IndividualCapacity\_j \< GroupSupport\_j, the individual has functional dependence on the social group and SocialDependency \> 0.

Proof outline: by the SocialDependency definition, SocialDependency \> 0 ⟺ ∃ j such that max(0, GroupSupport\_j − IndividualCapacity\_j) \> 0; that is, at least one dimension has individual capacity insufficient relative to group support; under primordial biological conditions, significant gaps exist in all six dimensions, so SocialDependency is high; as gaps narrow, SocialDependency trends downward. ∎

Theorem Two: Effective Capacity Expansion Theorem

Through ExtendedCapacity (body substitution, external tools, sensory extension, cognitive parallelism, memory externalization, self-repair), the upper bound of individual effective capacity is not directly constrained by single field capacity, and can in each key functional dimension persistently exceed the primordial group functional requirements level.

Proof outline: ExtendedCapacity\_j depends on external resources and tool structures rather than the internal capacity (Cap\_f) of single field f\_i; as long as external resources can be accessed and tools can be self-manufactured, dExtendedCapacity\_j/dt \> 0 can remain sustainably positive; when ExtendedCapacity\_j is sufficiently large, IndividualCapacity\_j can surpass any fixed GroupSupport\_j level; therefore the upper bound of effective capacity tends toward openness relative to primordial group functional requirements, not sealed by field capacity ceilings. ∎

Theorem Three: Non-Social Stable State Theorem

If non-social conditions hold in all dimensions (SocialGain → 0) and individual effective capacity is sustainably maintained or enhanced, the non-social state is systematically stable — dimensions already surpassed will not spontaneously revert to requiring group support. Note: if effective capacity undergoes structural decline (external connection capacity lost, tool chain fractured, self-repair failed), non-social conditions can be reversed; therefore this theorem represents conditional stability, not absolute irreversibility.

Proof outline: after non-social conditions hold, SocialDependency → 0, and the dynamic source motivating maintenance of social coupling disappears (SocialGain → 0); under these conditions, group coupling intensity trends downward (Social Chapter 18 Evolution Laws: low CurrentDynamics pressure → trajectory tends toward StableCoreRegion, and at this point the individual field’s StableCoreRegion is a low-coupling state); therefore as long as effective capacity is maintained, non-social conditions constitute a stable state that does not spontaneously reverse — but structural decline in effective capacity can break this stability. ∎

## Section 7. Boundary Cases and Structural Breakdown Conditions

Boundary Case One (Residual secondary dependence): Some individuals, even having reached non-social conditions in functional dimensions, still maintain social coupling due to emotional attachment, habitual sedimentation, and cultural identification (deep sedimentation of ValueLayer and NarrativeLayer, see Social Chapter 15). This shows that the functional holding of non-social conditions does not mean social relationships are immediately severed — secondary dependence (non-functional dependence) can persist and even strengthen after functional dependence dissolves. This is a boundary condition, does not overturn Theorem Three (Theorem Three addresses functional conditions and does not handle secondary dependence).

Boundary Case Two (Vulnerability from capacity concentration): If an individual reaches non-social conditions through highly specialized external connection systems, but those systems depend on other individuals or social institutions to maintain (such as technical capacity highly dependent on infrastructure), then once social infrastructure fails, effective capacity will sharply decline and non-social conditions will reverse. This shows that the robustness of effective capacity (robustness of ExtendedCapacity) is a necessary condition for non-social conditions to hold — extended capacity dependent on social groups to maintain does not constitute genuine IndividualCapacity enhancement. This is a boundary condition supporting Theorem Two (effective capacity expansion requires external resources to be independently accessible, not dependent on social intermediaries).

Boundary Case Three (Imbalanced-dimension transition state): In actual evolution, individual capacity enhancement speeds differ greatly across dimensions — some Self-Manifesting Beings may have far surpassed the group in cognitive and information dimensions while still depending on social infrastructure in action and continuation dimensions. At this point non-social conditions partially hold, producing a “semi-non-social” transition state: the individual’s relationship with the social group transitions from comprehensive dependence to selective dependence — maintaining coupling only in dimensions where gaps still exist, progressively decoupling in dimensions already surpassed. This is the normal transition pathway for non-social conditions, and does not overturn Theorem One (any dimension with remaining gap implies SocialDependency \> 0).

## Section 8. Summary Formula

Non-social conditions dynamics general formula:

SocialDependency(f\_i) = Σ\_j w\_j · max(0, GroupSupport\_j − IndividualCapacity\_j) / Σ\_j w\_j · GroupSupport\_j (group dependence definition)

IndividualCapacity\_j = NativeCapacity\_j + ExtendedCapacity\_j (effective capacity expansion; ExtendedCapacity not directly constrained by single field internal capacity)

∀ j ∈ KeyFunctions: IndividualCapacity\_j ≥ GroupSupport\_j ⟺ SocialDependency → 0 (non-social condition)

NonSocialCondition holds ⇒ SocialGain → 0 ∧ SocialCost \> 0 (when non-social conditions hold, net gain disappears and social coupling becomes net cost; SocialGain → 0 does not reversely imply non-social conditions — group functional degradation can equally cause SocialGain to tend toward zero without individual capacity transcendence)

The dynamic source of group dependence is capacity gaps in key functional dimensions — gap disappears, dependence disappears; groups are not the ultimate destination of the Self-Manifesting Being but a compensatory structure for the stage of insufficient capacity; the non-social condition is a reachable state after the Self-Manifesting Being’s effective capacity in all dimensions surpasses group support, arising without opposing groups — only requiring that capacity conditions be met.
