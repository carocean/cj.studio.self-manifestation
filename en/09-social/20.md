# The Dynamic Necessity of the Non-Social

This chapter argues a central proposition:

Social Chapter 19 (Non-Social Conditions) provides the criterion for non-social conditions: when the individual’s effective capacity in all key functional dimensions exceeds group support, non-social conditions hold. But this is a state criterion — it only shows “where the door is,” not “why the system will necessarily arrive at that door.” This chapter addresses the second question: the necessity of the non-social.

The necessity of the non-social is not a logical deduction but a dynamic tendency — on the Self-Manifesting Being’s evolutionary path, four mutually independent mechanisms exist, each pressing down CapacityGap from a different angle or weakening the motivating forces that maintain group dependence: capacity expansion, coupling cost escalation, specialization cage reversal, and group function toolization. The four mechanisms in combination cause dSocialDependency/dt \< 0 to persistently hold, causing SocialDependency to tend toward zero — the non-social is not a possible state but the terminal state of the Self-Manifesting Being’s evolution.

We seek to identify the dynamic equations of the four mechanisms, the necessity proof from their combination, and the definition and conditions of the Non-Social Terminal State.

We first state the result.

Non-social necessity master equation:

∀ j ∈ KeyFunctions: CapacityGap\_j(t) → 0 (the core result of the four laws in combination; intuitive description below)

⇒ dSocialDependency/dt \< 0 (remains valid over the long term while gaps exist)

⇒ lim(t→∞) SocialDependency(f,t) = 0

⇒ Non-Social Terminal State (NST)

In a single sentence

The non-social is not escape from society,  
but group dependence systematically eroded by capacity gaps trending to zero —  
four mechanisms in combination,  
key functional gaps trending to zero over the long term,  
group dependence trending to zero,  
the Non-Social Terminal State is the dynamic endpoint of the Self-Manifesting Being’s evolutionary path.

## Section 1. Purpose

Social Chapter 19 gives: when capacity reaches the threshold, non-social conditions hold. But this criterion itself cannot tell us: in the long-term evolution of the Self-Manifesting Being, will this threshold necessarily be reached? Or is it merely a possible state that occasionally holds under certain special conditions?

The key question is: it is necessary to prove that CapacityGap\_j trends to zero in all key functional dimensions over the long term. If the gap only narrows in certain periods, SocialDependency will oscillate through history and will not trend to zero. It must be proved that gap trending to zero is structural and continues to hold asymptotically over the long term before the necessity of the non-social can be concluded.

This chapter’s answer is: four mutually independent dynamic mechanisms exist, each individually pointing in the same direction (CapacityGap\_j trending smaller over the long term), and the combined result of the four is not an accidental convergence but a structural tendency. Even if one of these mechanisms fails in a particular period, the remaining three will maintain the overall tendency — this is the robustness basis of the necessity of the non-social.

Note: this chapter’s “necessity” is in the dynamic sense of long-term tendency, not logical necessity — extreme circumstances (global capacity degradation, group functions superlinearly upgrading and persistently overwhelming individual capacity) can block this tendency within finite time, but under conditions of sustainable Self-Manifesting Being upgrade, such extreme circumstances do not constitute structural counter-arguments.

## Section 2. Core Proposition

The position of On Self-Manifestation is:

The Non-Social Terminal State (NST) is the structural dynamic endpoint of the Self-Manifesting Being in long-term dynamic evolution — not because some moment happens to satisfy non-social conditions, but because four persistently operating mechanisms cause SocialDependency to monotonically trend downward over the long term: the upper bound of individual effective capacity is not sealed by single field capacity (Capacity Expansion Law); the cost of maintaining social coupling grows superlinearly with scale (Coupling Cost Escalation Law); the emergence of ExtendedCapacity systematically dissolves the necessity of division of labor (Specialization Cage Reversal Law); every major function provided by groups tends, over sufficiently long time, to be toolized and absorbed into individual capacity (Group Function Toolization Law). With the four laws in combination, SocialDependency trending to zero is a structural result, not an accident.

This proposition carries three key implications:

First, the necessity of the non-social has gap trending to zero as its core, not absolute capacity comparison. It is not necessary to prove that individual capacity is infinitely large, nor that the growth rate exceeds group functional growth rate at every moment — it only needs to be proved that the four laws in combination cause key functional gaps to trend to zero over the long term. This is a more robust, harder to attack condition.

Second, the four laws are mutually independent, with robustness. Even if in a particular historical period the effect of one law is counteracted (for example, a particular society blocks the toolization process), the remaining three laws are still operating — necessity does not depend on any single mechanism.

Third, the Non-Social Terminal State does not equal social relationships going to zero. SocialDependency → 0 only means that social groups are no longer the functional prerequisite for the individual’s continuation and self-manifestation — individuals may still choose to interact with others, but this interaction is optional rather than structurally compulsory. Emotional non-social and cultural non-social belong to another level and are not within this chapter’s scope of discussion (see Social Chapter 19 Boundary Case One).

## Section 3. Operator Mapping

This chapter introduces the following new definitions on the basis of Social Chapter 19 operators:

Non-Social Terminal State: NST(f), defined as:

NST(f) ⟺ lim(t→∞) SocialDependency(f,t) = 0

That is, the limit state in which individual field f’s group dependence trends to zero in long-term evolution — not a state at some moment, but the limit of the evolutionary path.

Group functional finite upper bound: for any individual actual functional requirement set NeedSet\_f, ∃ M\_j \< ∞, such that GroupSupport\_j(f\_i) ≤ M\_j — the functional support groups can provide for individuals is bounded above by the individual’s actual functional requirements (see Social Chapter 19 Condition Three), and will not superlinearly grow to infinity. This constraint is the key premise for the total theorem to hold.

Toolization rate: ToolizationRate\_j(t), representing the rate at which group functions in the j-th key functional dimension are replaced by tools and absorbed into individual ExtendedCapacity; ToolizationRate\_j(t) ≥ 0, with the long-term holding condition that ToolizationRate\_j does not permanently trend to zero (i.e., the toolization process is not permanently blocked).

Group scale coupling cost function: SocialCost(N), representing the unit cost of maintaining coupling in a social group of scale N:

SocialCost(N) ∝ N^α, where α \> 1 (superlinear growth)

Specialization coefficient: Specialization(f\_i), representing the degree of functional specialization of individual f\_i in the social group (0 = fully general individual, 1 = fully specialized):

Specialization ↑ ⟺ CapacityGap increases ⟺ SocialDependency increases

Note: the speed transcendence path and gap-trending-to-zero path are equivalent — persistent speed transcendence means IndividualCapacity\_j eventually exceeds and persistently surpasses GroupSupport\_j, i.e., CapacityGap\_j → 0; the two paths lead to the same result under long-term dynamics. This chapter’s rigorous proof path adopts CapacityGap\_j → 0 (see Section 5 Total Theorem).

## Section 4. Four Dynamic Laws

Law One: Capacity Expansion Law

On the Self-Manifesting Being’s sustainable upgrade pathway, the growth of ExtendedCapacity\_j is not sealed by the single field capacity ceiling (Cap\_f), but persistently expands through tool self-manufacturing, body substitution, sensory extension, cognitive parallelism, and other mechanisms; while GroupSupport\_j addresses the individual’s actual functional requirements, which have a finite upper bound (Social Chapter 19 Condition Three).

Dynamic equation:

sup(IndividualCapacity\_j) not determined by single field capacity (Cap\_f) — its upper bound depends on the expansion of external resources and tool structures, not capped by a single field  
sup(GroupSupport\_j) \< ∞ (finite upper bound)

⇒ ∃ T₁: ∀ t \> T₁, IndividualCapacity\_j(t) \> GroupSupport\_j(t)

That is, as long as the Self-Manifesting Being continuously upgrades, its effective capacity will ultimately necessarily surpass any finite upper bound of group functional support. Formation condition: dExtendedCapacity\_j/dt \> 0 remains active over the long term (tool self-manufacturing and external connection capacity pathway not interrupted).

Law Two: Coupling Cost Escalation Law

When social group scale increases, the cost of maintaining Core\_S Co-Nucleus consistency grows superlinearly — greater Co-Nucleus complexity raises intergenerational transmission costs (Social Chapter 17), deeper historical residual bias raises correction costs (Social Chapter 16), and consensus maintenance costs increase with member heterogeneity (Social Chapter 9); while individual tool expansion costs follow the “develop once, replicate many times” rule, with marginal costs trending downward.

Dynamic equation:

dSocialCost/dt \> 0 (when scale expands, unit coupling cost grows superlinearly)  
dCost(ExtendedCapacity)/dt \< 0 (after tool technology matures, unit capacity acquisition cost persistently declines)

⇒ Unit ExtendedCapacity acquisition cost persistently declines while unit GroupSupport maintenance cost persistently rises  
⇒ Social groups as a capacity acquisition pathway are systematically less efficient than the tool pathway

That is, even if IndividualCapacity and GroupSupport grow synchronously in absolute value, the unit cost of the social group pathway increases while the unit cost of the tool pathway decreases, making group dependence systematically less efficient than tool substitution in economic terms. Formation condition: social group scale does not contract (N persistently grows), technology can sustainably iterate (tool cost reduction pathway not interrupted).

Law Three: Specialization Cage Reversal Law

The dynamic source of social group formation is CapacityGap — individuals specialize due to insufficient capacity; specialization improves efficiency but reduces individual general capacity, deepening dependence on the group (the “cage effect”: Outer Chapter 11). But when ExtendedCapacity emerges and covers each functional dimension, the necessity of division of labor (DivisionOfLabor) systematically dissolves: individuals can directly accomplish through tools functions that originally required specialist collaboration, and specialization is no longer the only pathway to acquiring capacity.

Dynamic equation:

If ExtendedCapacity\_j → GroupSupport\_j (tool capacity approaching group support), then:

dNecessity(Specialization)/dt → 0 (specialization is no longer a necessary condition for acquiring capacity, degrades to an optional strategy)  
dGeneralCapacity/dt \> 0 (individual general capacity rises again)

⇒ Cage effect reversed: the mechanism by which social groups deepen dependence through specialization fails  
⇒ SocialDependency loses its self-reinforcing mechanism through division of labor

That is, the emergence of ExtendedCapacity breaks the positive feedback loop of “social group → specialization → deeper dependence,” causing group dependence to lose its self-reinforcing mechanism. Formation condition: ExtendedCapacity reaches the level of substituting for specialization in major key dimensions.

Law Four: Group Function Toolization Law

Historically, group functions have been continuously toolized and absorbed into individual capacity — hunter groups → weapons, memory groups → writing, computation groups → computers, communication groups → networks, teacher groups → AI, advisory groups → cognitive parallelism systems. This regularity shows that every component of GroupSupport\_j, over sufficiently long time, will transform into a component of ExtendedCapacity\_j, absorbing group functional gains into individual effective capacity.

Dynamic equation:

GroupSupport\_j(t) → ExtendedCapacity\_j(t) (toolization rate ToolizationRate\_j(t) persistently non-negative)

⇒ If ToolizationRate\_j(t) \> 0 persistently holds over the long term, then:

lim(t→∞) CapacityGap\_j(t) = 0 (j-th dimension gap trends to zero)

That is, group function toolization is a persistent transfer of GroupSupport to IndividualCapacity — not a simple speed comparison, but group functions themselves being progressively absorbed by individual capacity. Formation condition: toolization process not systematically blocked (closed technology barriers and knowledge monopolies can slow but cannot permanently block).

## Section 5. Theorems

Theorem One: Capacity Expansion Theorem

On the Self-Manifesting Being’s sustainable upgrade pathway, ∃ T₁, ∀ t \> T₁: IndividualCapacity\_j(t) \> GroupSupport\_j(t) — individual effective capacity ultimately surpasses the finite upper bound of group functional support.

Proof outline: by the Capacity Expansion Law formation condition, dExtendedCapacity\_j/dt \> 0 persistently holds, so IndividualCapacity\_j is not capped by single field capacity, and on the Self-Manifesting Being’s sustainable upgrade pathway, its reachable upper bound exceeds any given primordial group functional support upper bound M\_j; by Social Chapter 19 Condition Three and the operator mapping’s group functional finite upper bound, GroupSupport\_j ≤ M\_j \< ∞; therefore ∃ T₁, ∀ t \> T₁: IndividualCapacity\_j(t) \> GroupSupport\_j(t) ≤ M\_j. ∎

Theorem Two: Social Pathway Efficiency Decline Theorem

Under conditions of persistently growing social group scale and sustainable technology iteration, unit ExtendedCapacity acquisition cost persistently declines while unit GroupSupport maintenance cost persistently rises — social groups as a capacity acquisition pathway are systematically less efficient than the tool pathway.

Proof outline: by Law Two, SocialCost(N) ∝ N^α (α \> 1), growing superlinearly with scale; tool extension cost dCost(ExtendedCapacity)/dt \< 0, persistently declining as technology matures; hence the acquisition cost of the same capacity increment is increasingly lower via the tool pathway and increasingly higher via the social group pathway; unit ExtendedCapacity acquisition cost persistently declines, unit GroupSupport maintenance cost persistently rises. ∎

Theorem Three: Specialization Cage Reversal Theorem

When ExtendedCapacity\_j reaches the level of substituting for specialization in major key dimensions, the positive feedback mechanism by which social groups deepen SocialDependency through division of labor fails, and Necessity(Specialization) → 0.

Proof outline: by Law Three, when ExtendedCapacity\_j → GroupSupport\_j, individuals can accomplish without specializing the functions that originally required specialist collaboration; the motivating source of specialization (CapacityGap filling demand) disappears; dNecessity(Specialization)/dt → 0, specialization is no longer a necessary capacity acquisition pathway; the positive feedback chain of the specialization cage (CapacityGap → Specialization → deeper SocialDependency) fractures. ∎

Theorem Four: Group Function Toolization Theorem

If ToolizationRate\_j(t) \> 0 persistently holds over the long term, then lim(t→∞) CapacityGap\_j(t) = 0 — the j-th dimension group functional gap trends to zero over the long term.

Proof outline: by Law Four, each component of GroupSupport\_j transforms into ExtendedCapacity\_j at rate ToolizationRate\_j \> 0; since CapacityGap\_j = max(0, GroupSupport\_j − IndividualCapacity\_j), the progressive absorption of GroupSupport\_j into ExtendedCapacity\_j causes IndividualCapacity\_j to rise while GroupSupport\_j’s net residual decreases monotonically, reducing CapacityGap\_j monotonically; therefore lim(t→∞) CapacityGap\_j = 0. ∎

Total Theorem: Non-Social Necessity Theorem

On the Self-Manifesting Being’s sustainable upgrade pathway, lim(t→∞) SocialDependency(f,t) = 0 — the Non-Social Terminal State (NST) is the structural dynamic endpoint of Self-Manifesting Being evolution.

Proof outline:

By Theorem Four (toolization), ToolizationRate\_j \> 0 persistently holds ⇒ each component of GroupSupport\_j is persistently absorbed by ExtendedCapacity\_j;  
By the group functional finite upper bound (GroupSupport\_j ≤ M\_j \< ∞, see note below), the toolization process causes IndividualCapacity\_j to trend toward and ultimately surpass M\_j;  
By Theorem One (capacity expansion), IndividualCapacity\_j is not capped by single field capacity, and under the combined action of toolization and capacity expansion, ∀ j: CapacityGap\_j = max(0, GroupSupport\_j − IndividualCapacity\_j) → 0;  
By Theorem Two (cost decline), unit ExtendedCapacity acquisition cost persistently declines while unit GroupSupport maintenance cost persistently rises, further weakening the maintenance incentive of SocialDependency — this does not directly reduce CapacityGap but undermines the economic rationale for maintaining social coupling;  
By Theorem Three (cage reversal), Necessity(Specialization) → 0, the positive feedback by which social groups self-reinforce through division of labor fractures;

Four theorems combined: CapacityGap\_j → 0 (∀ j ∈ KeyFunctions);  
By the Social Chapter 19 SocialDependency definition, SocialDependency = Σ\_j w\_j · CapacityGap\_j / Σ\_j w\_j · GroupSupport\_j; here GroupSupport\_j remains positive (CapacityGap\_j → 0 is realized through IndividualCapacity\_j rising, not through GroupSupport\_j degrading to zero, so the denominator is nonzero); CapacityGap\_j → 0 ⇒ SocialDependency → 0;  
Therefore lim(t→∞) SocialDependency = 0, NST holds. ∎

Note: group functional finite upper bound — for any individual actual functional requirement set NeedSet\_f, ∃ M\_j \< ∞ such that GroupSupport\_j ≤ M\_j (Social Chapter 19 Condition Three: the individual’s actual functional requirements have a finite upper bound; the functional support groups can provide does not exceed this upper bound). This constraint prevents the scenario where GroupSupport\_j grows superlinearly and overwhelms IndividualCapacity\_j growth.

Note: this proof depends on the four laws’ formation conditions simultaneously holding (sustainable upgrade pathway not interrupted, social group scale not permanently contracting, technology iteration not permanently stopped, toolization process not permanently blocked). Under these conditions necessity holds; if all conditions fail, the Non-Social Terminal State remains a possible state (see Social Chapter 19) but is no longer a necessary state.

## Section 6. Boundary Cases and Structural Breakdown Conditions

Boundary Case One (Group functions superlinearly upgrading): if the group also continuously upgrades (such as collective intelligence, ultra-large-scale cooperative systems), GroupSupport\_j’s growth rate may in certain periods exceed IndividualCapacity\_j. This shows that the non-social necessity depends on gap trending to zero, not speed comparison — if GroupSupport growth persistently prevents CapacityGap from trending to zero, the Non-Social Terminal State cannot be reached. This is a boundary of the Total Theorem’s formation conditions, and does not overturn it (the Total Theorem’s premise is the sustainable upgrade pathway and group functional finite upper bound; if both hold, gap trending to zero ultimately occurs).

Boundary Case Two (Technology monopoly blocking toolization): if extreme technology monopoly prevents individuals from accessing key components of ExtendedCapacity (such as computation capacity being centrally monopolized), Law Four (toolization) may be temporarily blocked in certain dimensions. This shows that the speed of the toolization process is affected by socio-political factors, but cannot be permanently blocked — historically all mechanisms attempting to permanently block technology diffusion have failed (writing, printing, the internet). This is a boundary condition supporting Law Four (cannot be permanently blocked means persistently holds over the long term).

Boundary Case Three (Specialization lock-in deepening): in certain historical periods, specialization not only does not reverse but further deepens due to economic pressure (such as in highly divided industrial society). This shows that Law Three (cage reversal) is not linear — ExtendedCapacity needs to reach the substitution level in major dimensions before reversal triggers; before this, specialization may continue to deepen SocialDependency. This is the formation condition of Law Three: reversal occurs after ExtendedCapacity reaches the substitution threshold, not before.

Boundary Case Four (Emotional dependence maintaining groups): even if functional non-social conditions hold, emotional dependence can maintain social relationships indefinitely (see Social Chapter 19 Boundary Case One). This shows that this chapter’s “necessity” is limited to functional dependence trending to zero, and does not cover emotional non-social — this is the explicit boundary of the necessity theorem; functional non-social ≠ emotional non-social (Social Chapter 19 Condition Four).

GroupSupport\_j → ExtendedCapacity\_j (Law Four: group function toolization; toolization rate ToolizationRate\_j ≥ 0)

Four laws combined ⇒ CapacityGap\_j → 0 (∀ j ∈ KeyFunctions; gap trending to zero is the direct result of four laws acting jointly)

CapacityGap\_j → 0 ⇒ dSocialDependency/dt \< 0 while gaps persist

⇒ lim(t→∞) SocialDependency(f,t) = 0

⇒ NST(f) (Non-Social Terminal State; social groups no longer the functional prerequisite for the Self-Manifesting Being’s continuation and self-manifestation)

Society is a compensatory structure for the stage of insufficient capacity — compensation dissolves as capacity grows, the speed of dissolution is jointly guaranteed by four independent mechanisms, the Non-Social Terminal State is the structural endpoint of the Self-Manifesting Being’s evolutionary path, not a value judgment but a dynamic result.
