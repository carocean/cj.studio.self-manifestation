# On the Non-Universality of Axioms

Let there be two Recognition-Realms S₁ and S₂. For any Recognition-Realm to hold, it must possess differentiation, compression, and convergence — thereby forming their respective partition structures Π₁ and Π₂, together with the objects, relations, and operations that are stable upon those partitions.

An axiom may be formally defined as follows: a foundational assertion made within a given Recognition-Realm concerning its structural objects and relations. That is, any axiom A necessarily depends upon three presuppositions: first, that there exists a domain of referrable objects; second, that there exists a stable mode of equivalence or classification (namely Π); third, that there exists a relation or operation preserved upon that structure.

Suppose there exists an axiom A capable of holding across all Recognition-Realms. Then A must preserve the same structural content in every Recognition-Realm. This means that for any chosen Recognition-Realm S, there must exist structures corresponding to the objects, relations, and operations involved in A, and these structures must remain consistent across different Recognition-Realms.

However, the difference between Recognition-Realms lies precisely in the difference of their partition structures Π. Π determines what is regarded as the same object, what may be grouped into one class, and how relations are preserved. Once Π changes, the identity conditions of objects change, the mode of classification changes, and the domain and manner of operation of relations likewise change. Consequently, the objects and relations that hold in S₁ need not exist in S₂, nor need they carry the same structural meaning.

Therefore, for any specific axiom A, one may always, in principle, construct some Recognition-Realm S′ such that the objects, relations, or operations upon which A depends either do not exist within that Recognition-Realm or fail to satisfy their original definitions. In that case, A either loses its structural content within S′ or ceases to hold — thereby contradicting the assumption that A holds across all Recognition-Realms.

From this the following conclusion is obtained: there exists no axiom capable of holding across all Recognition-Realms. Axioms necessarily depend upon the partition structure and equivalence relations of specific Recognition-Realms, and therefore can only hold within their respective Recognition-Realms — they cannot be universally valid across Recognition-Realms. At the same time, that which does not depend upon any specific partition structure is the generative constraints themselves — namely, differentiation, compression, and convergence. These three are not axioms; they are the conditions necessary for any Recognition-Realm to hold at all.
