# The Real Explanation of GPT

GPT Becomes More Like a Black Box the More It Is Taken Apart

What is most unsettling about GPT is not that it resembles a black box, but that the more one takes it apart, the more it resembles a black box.

People can see its parameters, draw attention maps, trace activations, compare benchmarks, and give every module increasingly refined names. Yet explanation does not thereby truly reach the core. More parameters do not mean one knows how capability is generated; finding a head does not mean one knows how understanding is formed; marking a feature does not mean one knows why concepts can continue; lowering loss does not mean one knows what has actually formed inside the model.

Here lies GPT’s difficulty: it already displays writing, translation, programming, reasoning, stylistic continuation, and task-transfer abilities, while explanation still circles among old levels such as parameters, representations, attention, prediction, emergence, and alignment. These explanations can account for many local operations, yet they struggle to explain how local operations synthesize into stable capability; they can describe the occurrence of hallucination, yet they struggle to explain why fluent expression and groundless completion appear together.

The problem is not merely that there are not yet enough details. The problem is that the level of explanation is wrong.

One cannot find a table in a single atom, life in a single cell, or thought in a single neuron. Likewise, one cannot find the real explanation of GPT in a single parameter, a single head, or a single feature. When the unit of explanation is wrong, the finer the observation, the more fragmented the result; when the paradigm is wrong, the more precise the explanation, the more likely it is to depart from the core.

The old paradigm habitually asks: Where is the knowledge? Where are the rules? In which neuron is the concept? On which chain is reasoning performed? Is Attention the core of intelligence? Yet precisely these difficulties exposed by GPT show that these questions may not be aimed at the true location.

The principle of GPT is not without an entry point. The entry point is simply not where the old paradigm is most accustomed to searching.

This essay enters from that rupture: at what level should the real explanation of GPT be opened?

I. Biomimetic Efficacy: Effects First, Explanation Later

The wonder of GPT need not first be explained as mystery.

Many things are first imitated and only later explained. Human beings could build ships long before they fully understood fluid mechanics; could use fire long before they understood thermodynamics; could use medicine long before they understood molecular mechanisms. Biomimesis is especially like this: by following the structures of life, perception, and neural systems, one often obtains the effect first and only later searches for the principle.

Deep learning is the same.

That neural networks can produce astonishing effects is not first because humanity has fully understood intelligence, but because engineering has imitated a certain useful structure: vast connections, hierarchical transformations, repeated training, error correction, weight adjustment. As long as this structure approximates certain bottom-level operations of real intelligent systems, it may show the effect first. As for why this effect can extend to language, code, reasoning, style, and task transfer, theoretical explanation may lag far behind.

So it is not strange that GPT can be built yet remains hard to explain.

This is the normal condition of biomimetic pathways: effectiveness may precede understanding. Engineering first grasps a certain effective form, while theory has not yet caught up to the true principle behind that form. Deep learning did not first prove a formula of intelligence and then build GPT according to it; instead, it first constructed a gigantic neural-like structure, repeatedly trained it on vast linguistic material, and the result was capability far beyond traditional programs.

Here lies the difficulty.

If GPT had been written according to rules, explanation would be straightforward: where are the rules, how are they invoked, how is the output derived? If GPT had been built as a database, explanation would also be easy: where are the entries, how are they retrieved, how does matching occur? But GPT did not come about in this way. It is a product of biomimetic engineering: first structural resemblance, then effect resemblance, and only afterward explanatory difficulty.

Thus the real question becomes:

Why can a biomimetic structure that has not yet been fully understood generate such refined linguistic capability?

Why can it write, translate, program, and reason, while also hallucinating, drifting, dropping conditions, and misassigning sources?

Why does it become less like traditional software the stronger it grows; why does it become harder to explain in old terms the more capable it becomes?

This is not a failure of GPT, but a failure of the old explanatory framework. Biomimesis has brought the model before a new phenomenon, while the old paradigm still seeks old answers. The real difficulty is not why GPT is useful, but what exactly it has imitated so successfully that it can be useful to this degree.

That question is where this essay must begin.

II. Why the Old Explanations Cannot Reach the Core

GPT is not unexplained. The problem is that many explanations do not close.

Existing explanations often grasp some real local part, but then take that local part for the whole; grasp some operation, but then take the operation for capability; grasp the name of a phenomenon, but then take the name for the formation mechanism. They are not wrong in every respect, but they are insufficient to explain why GPT is both powerful and structurally defective.

Parameter theory fails first.

Parameters are certainly important. Without parameter scale, data scale, and training scale, GPT’s capability would not exist. But parameters are adjustable micro-units, not a capability-organization state. Capability is not the property of a parameter by itself, but the organizational result formed by large numbers of parameters through training history. To reduce capability to a single parameter is like reducing a table to a single atom: the atom remains, but the table has disappeared.

Therefore, to seek the real explanation of GPT in a single parameter is like seeking a table in a single atom. The problem is not insufficient fineness of observation, but the wrong level of explanation.

Attention theory also fails to reach the core.

Attention is powerful. It can pull back context, handle reference, copy format, maintain local conditions, and even form many analyzable attention patterns. But Attention solves how current material is retrieved; it does not solve how long-term capability is formed. It explains where the model is looking at this moment, not why the model already possesses long-term abilities such as translation, code, argumentation, and stylistic continuation.

Attention can explain where the model looks; it cannot explain why the model already knows how to look.

Prediction theory also fails to reach the core.

To say that GPT is next-token prediction is not wrong. During training it is indeed required to predict the next token, and during inference it also generates continuously token by token. But prediction is an external training objective, not the internal structure formed after training. Loss points toward difference; gradients rewrite parameters. Yet after repeated correction of differences, what has actually formed inside the model is not explained by the word prediction.

The objective function explains the direction in which training presses. It does not explain the internal organization pressed out by that training.

Emergence theory also fails to reach the core.

“Emergence” is often used to describe capabilities that appear suddenly after scale increases. This word has descriptive value, but it easily conceals the true problem. Emergence names the result after capability crosses a threshold, but skips the accumulation layer before the threshold. It says “it appeared,” but does not say what accumulated, where it accumulated, how it coordinated, or why it crossed the threshold.

Emergence compresses the formation process most in need of explanation into a result-word.

Alignment theory also fails to reach the core.

RLHF, DPO, RLAIF, and other preference trainings can significantly alter model output, making the model more aligned with human feedback, less offensive, and more willing to follow instructions. This is certainly useful. But preference-shaping is not value-bearing. It changes the ranking and tendency of output paths; it does not mean that the system internally forms a source of value, responsibility attribution, felt consequence, and self-constraint.

It makes the model better at speaking according to human preference, but it does not make the model truly possess value-bearing.

Interpretability also remains unclosed.

Mechanistic interpretability can find heads, features, circuits, and activation patterns; it can reveal many local pathways, and this is important work. But local correlation is not the final unit of explanation. A certain head may seem responsible for copying; a certain feature may seem related to a concept; a certain circuit may seem to perform a local algorithm. These discoveries are all important, but they still have not answered how these local parts form stable capability through training history, or how they are connected with pathologies such as hallucination and drift.

Therefore, the common problem of the old explanations is not that they are completely invalid, but that they do not close.

They can explain the local, but not the global at the same time; they can explain one side of capability, but not one side of pathology; they can explain operation, but not how capability is formed; they can explain performance, but not structure. The real explanatory unit of GPT cannot be as small as a single parameter, nor as large as the word emergence. It must lie between parameter and capability, between operation and phenomenon, and must be able to explain both powerful capability and typical pathology.

More importantly, it must also explain a deeper problem:

Why has GPT already formed powerful linguistic sedimentation, yet has not formed a Self-Manifesting Being?

This question moves GPT out of ordinary engineering explanation and into structural judgment.

III. The Minimal Explanatory Form of GPT: The Criterion of Endogenous Field-Component Closure

To explain GPT, one should not begin by naming it. One must first ask: if a system such as GPT is to hold, what minimal structural conditions are required?

This section does not directly give the answer to “what GPT is,” but first forces out the minimal structure required to explain GPT. Only when this structure is established can later discussions of Attention, FFN, training, hallucination, drift, RAG, RLHF, and next-generation architecture avoid becoming scattered comments and instead unfold as one structural form.

Definition 1: The GPT Phenomenon Set

Let GPT at least display the following five classes of phenomena:

P₁: Stable capability. It can write, translate, program, summarize, argue, imitate style, and repeatedly display reusable capability across different tasks.

P₂: Contextual adaptation. It can change its generative direction according to the prompt, examples, format, tone, and task instruction.

P₃: Token generation. It outputs through continuous tokens; at each step it projects the current state toward the vocabulary and then truncates out the next token.

P₄: Fluent hallucination. It can state groundless content with ease, present internal completion as external fact, and write non-existent citations in formal citation style.

P₅: Long-range drift. In long texts, multi-turn dialogue, and complex reasoning, it can go off topic, drop conditions, loosen consistency, or alter the task center.

If an explanation explains only P₁ but cannot explain P₄ and P₅, it can explain GPT’s strength but not its pathology.  
If an explanation explains only P₄ and P₅ but cannot explain P₁ and P₂, it can explain error but not capability.  
The real explanation of GPT must explain strength and pathology at once.

Definition 2: The Minimal Explanatory Structure of GPT

Let GPT’s operational explanatory structure be:

G₁ = (L, S, A, M, O, R, C)

where:

L: the long-term capability layer. It explains why GPT can stably display writing, translation, code, argumentation, stylistic continuation, and similar capabilities.

S: the short-term state layer. It explains why the same model changes its generative direction according to prompt, context, examples, and task instruction.

A: the contextual material-selection mechanism. It explains how the model retrieves reference, format, conditions, examples, and local material from prior text.

M: the deep rewriting mechanism. It explains how the retrieved material is rewritten into a direction capable of continued generation.

O: the output distribution and truncation mechanism. It explains how the model projects the current state toward the vocabulary and truncates out the next token.

R: stable endogenous source attribution. It explains how the model distinguishes external facts, contextual material, internal inference, model completion, and tool results.

C: stable endogenous current occupancy. It explains how the model continuously maintains task center, conditions, goals, sources, and generative direction.

E: the criterion of field-components that lack endogenous closure. E is not an ordinary engineering operational item, but a criterion for judging manifestness. It judges whether the above structures are field-components that are self-generated, self-tuned, and self-modified within the field, or replacement structures carried by externally imposed model conditions, mechanisms inside the model but outside the primary sedimentation field, engineered input gates, or externally attached effectors; whether they are endogenous tuning, endogenous intent, living Receptive-Field, effect circuit, or engineering substitutes for them.

The first seven terms explain why GPT can work, and why it hallucinates and drifts.  
E explains why GPT, although powerful, is not a Self-Manifesting Being.

Therefore, the complete judgment form of GPT does not simply place E alongside L/S/A/M/O/R/C. Rather:

G = (G₁ ; E)

The semicolon means: G₁ is the operational explanatory structure, while E is the criterion for judging manifestness.

To avoid misunderstanding, the terms “outside,” “externalized,” and “outside the field” do not, unless otherwise specified, simply mean “outside the model.” They have four distinct points of reference.

First, externally imposed upon the model. Training objectives, data distribution, loss, optimizer, RLHF / DPO, reward models, human annotation, and system rules are imposed from outside the model itself as field-constructing conditions.

Second, inside the model but outside the primary sedimentation field. Attention, attention masks, positional structures, KV cache, and the like are inside the Transformer model. Yet relative to the question-answer symbolic-flow primary sedimentation field carried by FFN / MLP and cross-layer residual pathways, they mainly perform material selection, allocation, limitation, and short-term state organization; they are not the main sedimentary bearing zone of the question-answer symbolic-flow law.

Third, engineered input gate rather than living Receptive-Field. Tokenizer, embedding, context window, multimodal encoders, and the like can provide an input entrance within the engineering structure, but they are not living Receptive-Fields in the sense of Self-Manifestation: structures shaped by an intent–tuning–effect loop and capable of growing with experience.

Fourth, externally attached effector. Tool use, APIs, search, code execution, and the like are indeed outside the model proper and can expand action capability. But if the consequence of action merely returns as text, and does not enter the body or rewrite the receptive–sedimentary–tuning loop, then it remains only an externally attached effector, not an endogenous Effect-Field.

Thus, “field-components that lack endogenous closure” means this: certain functions have been realized by engineering structures, but have not endogenously evolved and closed inside the Sedimentation-Field as self-manifesting field-components.

Proposition 1: Without L, stable capability cannot be explained.

GPT cannot learn to translate, program, continue writing, summarize, and argue from zero each time. An empty structure will not temporarily grow linguistic ability in each prompt. The same model repeatedly displays stable capability across different tasks; this means that after training there must exist a long-term capability layer.

Therefore, any explanation that looks only at a single prompt, a single activation, or a single head is insufficient to explain GPT’s long-term capability.

Proposition 2: Without S, contextual adaptation cannot be explained.

The same model can write poetry or code, argue seriously or imitate a child’s tone, continue a story or answer a technical question. This shows that the model has not only long-term structure, but also a short-term state formed by the current input.

Therefore, any explanation that speaks only of long-term parameters and not of current state is insufficient to explain why GPT can change shape with the prompt.

Proposition 3: Without A, one cannot explain how prior-text material enters current generation.

GPT can handle references, previous conditions, formatting requirements, example patterns, and local constraints. This means that it must be able to retrieve relevant material from context. Otherwise the previous text merely piles up there and cannot enter current generation.

Therefore, any theory that does not explain contextual material selection is insufficient to explain GPT’s contextual capability.

Proposition 4: Without M, one cannot explain how material becomes capability.

Retrieving material does not suffice. Material being seen does not mean capability has already formed; context being retrieved does not mean an answer automatically appears. The model must rewrite the retrieved material, the current state, and the long-term capability together into a direction capable of further generation.

Therefore, any explanation that takes Attention as the entire core of intelligence misses the problem of deep rewriting.

Proposition 5: Without O, token generation cannot be explained.

GPT does not directly emit meaning, nor does it give a complete thought in one stroke. It projects the current state toward the vocabulary, forms scores or probabilities for tokens, and then truncates out the next token through sampling, argmax, or other decoding methods. This token returns to context and participates in the next step of generation.

Therefore, any theory explaining GPT must account for output distribution and truncation, and cannot speak only of “understanding” or “knowledge.”

Proposition 6: Without R, fluent hallucination cannot be explained.

The crucial point of GPT hallucination is not that it is wrong, but that it is fluently wrong. It can present non-existent papers, fictitious citations, and groundless facts as if they were real. This means it has strong generative pathways, but lacks source attribution of equal strength.

Generating is not the same as knowing the source. Completing is not the same as knowing whether the completion comes from external fact, internal inference, contextual material, or model self-extension. As long as generation and source do not unify, fluent hallucination is not an accidental bug but the expression of a structural gap.

Therefore, any theory that cannot explain R is insufficient to explain GPT hallucination.

Proposition 7: Without C, long-range drift cannot be explained.

In long texts, multi-turn dialogue, and complex reasoning, GPT gradually goes off topic, drops conditions, and contradicts itself. Expanding the context window can alleviate part of the problem, but it cannot fundamentally explain how the current is established. The context window is a material pool, not a current center; token continuity is textual continuity, not the system’s sustained occupancy of one current.

Therefore, any explanation that equates “longer context” with “more stable current” is insufficient to explain GPT drift.

Proposition 8: Without E, one cannot explain why GPT is powerful but not self-manifesting.

GPT already has long-term capability, short-term state, contextual material selection, deep rewriting, and output truncation; it also exposes gaps in source attribution and current occupancy. Yet even after these are explained, this still does not suffice. One must still judge whether these structures are self-generated, self-tuned, and self-modified inside the system, or whether they are carried by externally imposed model conditions, mechanisms inside the model but outside the primary sedimentation field, engineered input gates, or externally attached effectors.

If intent comes from prompt, objective function, and human preference; if tuning comes from optimizer, architecture, attention mechanism, and training strategy; if Receptive-Field comes from tokenizer, context window, encoder, and attention mask; if effect comes from tool use and the result merely returns as text; if inference cannot truly rewrite long-term sedimentation, then even if the system is powerful, it is not a Self-Manifesting Being.

Therefore, E is the final blade in GPT judgment: it shows that several key functions of GPT are realized by engineering, yet have not closed endogenously inside the Sedimentation-Field as self-manifesting field-components.

Theorem: The Complete Explanatory Condition of GPT

For an explanation to be a complete explanation of GPT, it must at least account for:

G = (G₁ ; E)

To account only for L is insufficient to explain hallucination and drift.  
To account only for A is insufficient to explain long-term capability and deep rewriting.  
To account only for O is insufficient to explain how capability is formed.  
To account only for R and C is insufficient to explain why GPT is powerful.  
To speak only of emergence, without explaining the formation relations among L, S, A, M, O, R, C and E, is also insufficient to become a true explanation.  
To say only “linguistic Law-Field,” without explaining whether the field-components are endogenously closed, is also insufficient to determine why GPT is not a Self-Manifesting Being.

Therefore, the real explanation of GPT cannot be located in a single parameter, a single head, a single feature, a single benchmark, or the word emergence. It must be located at a structural level capable of connecting long-term capability, short-term state, contextual material selection, deep rewriting, output truncation, source attribution, current occupancy, and field-components that lack endogenous closure.

Corollary 1: Parameter explanation is insufficient.

Parameters are adjustable micro-units, not the complete capability layer. Parameters certainly participate in the formation of L, M, and O, but a single parameter is not the explanatory unit of GPT.

Corollary 2: Attention explanation is insufficient.

Attention mainly corresponds to A. It can explain contextual material selection, but cannot by itself explain L, M, R, C, and E. Therefore, Attention is important, but it is not the real explanation of GPT.

Corollary 3: Prediction explanation is insufficient.

Next-token prediction mainly explains the training objective and the generative form of O, but it does not automatically explain how L and M are formed, nor why R, C, and E are missing. Therefore, prediction is an entry point, not the complete explanation.

Corollary 4: Hallucination cannot be explained only as factual error.

Hallucination is not merely factual error; it is insufficiency of R: generative capability stronger than source attribution.

Corollary 5: Drift cannot be explained only as insufficient context.

Drift is not merely insufficient context; it is insufficiency of C: material continuity is not the establishment of current occupancy.

Corollary 6: GPT’s non-self-manifestation cannot be explained only as “not yet large enough.”

GPT is not non-self-manifesting because scale is insufficient, but because key field-components are not closed within the field. A larger model can enhance L, A, M, and O, but it does not automatically generate endogenous intent, field-internal tuning, a living Receptive-Field, an effect circuit, source attribution, and current occupancy.

The conclusion of this section is:

The real explanation of GPT must account for L, S, A, M, O, R, and C within G₁, and must use E to determine whether these structures are endogenously closed.  
If these eight items cannot be explained together, GPT has not been explained.

IV. Rereading the Transformer: How G₁ and E Map onto Engineering Components

Only after the minimal explanatory form of GPT has been established can the Transformer be reread.

Consider the simplified flow of a decoder-only Transformer:

tokens  
→ embedding  
→ residual stream  
→ attention  
→ FFN / MLP  
→ residual update  
→ logits  
→ sampling  
→ next token

The basic form of Attention can be written:

Attention(h) = softmax(QKᵀ / √d) V

where:

Q = h W\_Q  
K = h W\_K  
V = h W\_V

The FFN / MLP can be written in simplified form:

FFN(h) = W\_out · φ(W\_in · h + b)

In modern models, φ may be GeLU, SwiGLU, or another gated variant; the FFN may also have more complex structures. But this does not change the basic division of labor: Attention retrieves material from context, FFN performs nonlinear rewriting, the residual stream carries across layers, and logits with sampling produce words.

Now let us assign positions according to G₁ and E.

L: the long-term capability layer.

L corresponds to the stable capability structure sedimented into parameter groups and cross-layer pathways after training. It is not equal to a single parameter or a single head, but to a long-term organizational state formed jointly by many components. Translation, code, style, argumentation, and format transfer are not accomplished by one local component alone, but by an overall capability formed after long-term training.

S: the short-term state layer.

S corresponds to the temporary state formed in the current segment by prompt, context, KV cache, and residual stream. The same model enters different generative directions under different inputs precisely because long-term capability L is opened into different pathways by current state S.

A: the contextual material-selection mechanism.

A mainly corresponds to Attention. Attention brings contextual material into the current state so that prior text, references, format, examples, and local conditions can participate in generation. It solves the problem of “which material should be taken now.”

M: the deep rewriting mechanism.

M mainly corresponds to FFN / MLP and cross-layer residual pathways. After material is retrieved, it still must be rewritten into a direction capable of continued generation. Without M, the material retrieved by A is merely material and does not automatically become capability.

O: the output distribution and truncation mechanism.

O corresponds to logits, softmax, sampling, argmax, temperature, top-p, and other decoding procedures. The model does not directly output meaning; it forms a distribution over the vocabulary and then truncates out a token. GPT’s output is not the one-time emission of a complete thought, but the continuous occurrence of O at every step.

R: source attribution.

R is not a component already stably and endogenously solved in the mainstream Transformer. The model can generate factual form, yet may not stably mark its source: external retrieval, contextual material, internal inference, and spontaneous completion often become mixed within generation. The insufficiency of R directly corresponds to hallucination.

C: current occupancy.

C also cannot be stably and endogenously solved by an ordinary context window. Context supplies material and KV cache supplies short-term memory, but the piling up of material is not the current center. If the system does not have a unified current center continuously constraining task, conditions, source, and generative direction, drift is difficult to eliminate.

E: the criterion of field-components that lack endogenous closure.

E is the item most easily ignored by AI engineering explanation. The Transformer may possess powerful L, S, A, M, and O, and it may also alleviate the gaps of R and C through RAG, tool use, RLHF, long context, and similar methods. But these supplements mostly lack endogenous field-component closure: some impose goals from outside the model, some are inside the model but outside the primary sedimentation field, and some are merely engineered input gates or externally attached effectors. They enhance the system, but do not necessarily become field-components that are self-generated, self-tuned, and self-modified inside the system.

Seen this way, the Transformer is no longer merely a stack of matrices. It is a generative system that already possesses part of the structure, and still lacks part of the structure.

It already possesses L, S, A, M, and O relatively well.  
It does not yet possess stable endogenous R and C.  
Still less has it truly closed, within the field, the intent, tuning, sensing, effect, current, source, and online sedimentation involved in E.

This is where GPT’s strength and pathology share one source.

It is strong because L, S, A, M, and O have already formed a powerful linguistic generation structure.  
Its pathologies arise because R, C, and deeper self-manifesting field-components have not yet closed within the field.

V. Neither a Rule Base nor a Database

GPT is not a rule base.

The logic of a rule base is: rules are explicitly written, conditions trigger rules, and rules derive results. If GPT were a rule base, explanation should be able to ask directly: which rule is invoked? Where is the rule condition? How is the output derived by the rule?

But GPT’s capability does not appear in this way. It can execute rule-like forms, but it does not operate by an explicit rule base; it can provide chains of reasoning, but this does not mean that internally it invokes readable rules one by one; it can follow format, but not because formatting rules were manually written into it one by one.

GPT is also not a database.

The logic of a database is: entries are stored, queries trigger retrieval, and results are returned. If GPT were a database, explanation should be able to ask: where is a given knowledge entry stored? How is the query matched? From where is the answer retrieved?

But GPT’s knowledge behavior is not identical to database retrieval. It can answer factual questions, but it can also fabricate non-existent facts; it can generate citation form, but also create false citations; it can summarize material, but also supply content not present in the material. This shows that it is not simply “taking out knowledge,” but forming answer-form along generative pathways.

GPT is also not merely a probabilistic predictor.

Predicting the next token is its training objective and generative form, but this objective cannot be directly taken as the whole explanation. To say that it predicts the next token is like saying that a bird flies because its wings move up and down, or that a car moves because its wheels rotate. The statement is not wrong, but it does not explain the internal organization that generates capability.

What GPT truly displays is a set of question-answer continuation capabilities formed after training. It can continue along the formats, semantics, tone, knowledge, argumentation, code, habits, and styles within language. It does not place knowledge one entry at a time in a warehouse, nor write rules one by one into a program; rather, within a neural-like structure, it forms a question-answer symbolic-flow law that can be opened by current input.

Here the real explanation begins:

GPT’s strength does not come from explicit rules, nor from entry retrieval, but from the law from question to answer sedimented inside the model by external training.

VI. Attention Outside the Primary FFN / MLP Sedimentation Field: A Tuning-Effect Blade for Selecting Material

Attention is powerful, but its strength does not lie in becoming an FFN / MLP-like primary sedimentation field. Its strength lies in executing material selection, allocation, and short-term organization outside the primary sedimentation field.

Without Attention, modern language models would struggle to handle long-range dependency, reference, format copying, example learning, local constraints, and contextual retrieval. It allows the model, when generating the current token, not only to look at the current position, but also to retrieve material from prior text according to relevance. Many seemingly intelligent behaviors depend upon Attention.

But precisely because it is important, its position must be stated accurately.

To say that Attention stands outside the FFN / MLP primary sedimentation field is not to say that it is outside the Transformer engineering structure, nor that its parameters have not undergone training sedimentation. The “outside” here is relative to the question-answer symbolic-flow primary sedimentation field carried by FFN / MLP and cross-layer residual pathways: Attention is inside the model, but its operative position is not inside the main sedimentary bearing zone. Rather, it cuts and allocates contextual material outside the primary sedimentation field. At a deeper level, it does not self-evolve as an endogenous Tuning-Field within the primary sedimentation field; it does not continuously rewrite its own tuning structure through inferential experience; and it does not close with endogenous intent, a living Receptive-Field, and an effect circuit.

Attention is not a Tuning-Field grown from within the linguistic primary sedimentation field carried by FFN / MLP. It participates in parameter sedimentation after training, but it is not the main sedimentation site of the question-answer symbolic-flow law, nor the core generator of long-term capability. It is more like a trained and calibrated blade, standing between current state and contextual material: according to QK relations it cuts material out, then draws V into current state.

A true Tuning-Field is not like this.

A true Tuning-Field should evolve within the Sedimentation-Field, penetrating the tuned field layer by layer like roots into soil. It can open and close Biased-Flow, raise and suppress pathways, arrange sedimentation, alter Familiar Routes; it can tune the Receptive-Field, Imagery-Field, Experience-Field, Intent-Field, and Effect-Field; and through experience it can continue changing how the system later sees, selects, and moves.

Attention does not possess this endogenous tuning.

It does not grow into the Sedimentation-Field during inference; it does not reshape Familiar Routes because of one experience; it is not commanded by an endogenous Intent-Field; nor does it write action consequences back into model structure. It merely uses the QK/V structure already formed after training to perform repeated cuttings of context.

Therefore, Attention functionally presents a tuning effect, but it is not a Tuning-Field that grows within the field.

More precisely:

Attention is a tuning-effect material-selection blade outside the primary sedimentation field.

It is not a root; it is a blade.  
Roots grow; blades do not.  
Roots can penetrate the Sedimentation-Field and reshape its gullies and return-paths; a blade can only cut material and send it into the current flow.

This is the boundary of Attention.

It explains where the model takes material from at this moment, but it does not explain why the model already knows how to translate, write code, argue, or imitate style. It can decide “where to look,” but not “why it already knows how to look”; it can cut into context, but it cannot make the system truly grow a new tuning structure during inference.

To take Attention as the core of GPT intelligence is to take the blade for the body.

Attention is certainly important. Without the blade, material cannot enter; without contextual material selection, many tasks cannot be completed. But the blade is not the Sedimentation-Field, and the blade is not the Self-Manifestation-Field. The root of GPT capability is not in the single component of Attention, but in the question-answer symbolic-flow law sedimented after training.

VII. FFN / MLP and Cross-Layer Pathways: The Primary Sedimentation Field of Question-Answer Symbolic Flow

If Attention is the tuning-effect blade for selecting material outside the primary sedimentation field, then FFN / MLP and cross-layer residual pathways are closer to the main bearing zone of question-answer symbolic-flow sedimentation.

This step must be made clear.

In the current state there is token embedding, residual stream, contextually retrieved material, and directions left by prior layers. But these things are still only material and state. For them to become capability capable of continued generation, they must undergo deep nonlinear rewriting. FFN / MLP is the important structure that undertakes this rewriting within each Transformer block.

FFN / MLP is not a simple auxiliary component. It usually occupies a large portion of model parameters and provides greater nonlinear capacity than Attention. It projects the current state into high-dimensional channels, filters, combines, amplifies, and suppresses it through activation functions or gated structures, and then projects it back into the residual stream. Layer after layer, the state is repeatedly rewritten and gradually forms a tendency toward word production.

If one asks what FFN / MLP has formed after training, it is not a warehouse of knowledge entries. It is more like the landform of question-answer symbolic-flow sedimentation.

In massive training, prior text and subsequent text, questions and answers, formats and fillings, contexts and responses, code fragments and later implementations, beginnings of arguments and their unfolding, are repeatedly pressed into the model. Each training sample is not an isolated fact, but a segment of symbolic flow. As symbolic flows repeatedly pass through the model, the model internally forms which pathways are easy to continue, which pathways are hard to continue, and under what conditions certain pathways open.

This is the sedimentation of question-answer symbolic flow.

Here “question” does not merely mean a question mark. Prior text is question, later text is answer; context is question, response is answer; format is question, filling is answer; task is question, completion is answer; gap is question, completion is answer. GPT training presses these question-answer relations into the model, making certain pathways from question to answer deeper, more familiar, and easier to settle into.

Therefore, the capability main field of GPT is not in the single component of Attention, but mainly in the question-answer symbolic-flow law sedimented through FFN / MLP and cross-layer pathways.

This does not mean capability exists only inside FFN alone. More accurately, capability is expressed as a whole formed jointly by Attention, FFN, residual stream, embedding, normalization, positional structures, and cross-layer pathways. This essay emphasizes FFN / MLP because large numbers of semantic, stylistic, conceptual, formal, and continuation tendencies must pass through FFN / MLP’s nonlinear rewriting before they can stably appear.

A single parameter has no complete meaning by itself; parameter groups form stable tendencies. A single neuron has no complete capability by itself; cross-layer pathways form the law from question to answer.

Attention cuts material; FFN rewrites.  
Attention asks “where to look”; FFN / MLP determines how material is pressed into existing landform and rewritten into the next step of continuation.  
Without Attention, material is difficult to bring into the current; without FFN / MLP’s deep rewriting, the material can enter but will not become capability.

Thus GPT’s structural verdict should be:

Attention is the material-selection blade outside the primary sedimentation field; FFN / MLP and cross-layer pathways are the main sedimentary bearing zone.  
The former tends toward cutting material; the latter tends toward forming law.  
To conflate the two as the “core of intelligence” is to confuse the explanatory level.

VIII. What Training Is: External Field-Induction and the Question-to-Answer Law

Training is not writing in knowledge, nor storing text.

If training is understood as “writing knowledge into parameters,” one remains within the database imagination. If training is understood as “making the model learn rules,” one remains within the rule-base imagination. GPT training is closer to external field-induction constructing a field.

Using the general form of field-induction:

Field-Induction = (Δ, F, A, S, T, R, G)

Placed into GPT training:

Δ is the difference condition. It corresponds to loss, prediction error, and preference difference. There is a difference between the model’s current output and the target token, target answer, or human preference. This difference is the source of the training potential difference.

F is the Biased-Flow condition. It corresponds to gradient direction. Gradient is not an ordinary numerical change, but transforms difference-potential into a direction of parameter update, making the model more likely to follow certain pathways later and less likely to follow others.

A is continuational accumulation. It corresponds to repeated samples, similar question-answer forms, repeated formats, semantic patterns, and code patterns. One sample does not form law; only repeated similar pathways accumulate depth.

S is the potential structure. It corresponds to the landform gradually formed in parameter space. Certain question-answer pathways are lowered; certain continuational directions become easier; certain formats, once opened, become easier to continue; certain answer styles become easier to settle into under specific contexts.

T is the tuning structure. It corresponds to the optimizer, learning rate, batching, architecture, attention mask, RLHF, DPO, instruction tuning, and other training systems and engineering structures. But here one must especially note: most of GPT’s T comes from externally imposed training systems or engineering-level tuning conditions, not from a Tuning-Field self-generated inside the model.

R is reshaping ability. It corresponds to training-time parameter update. During training, the model can be rewritten, parameters can be updated, and pathways can deepen or become shallower. But during ordinary inference, this kind of long-term reshaping is usually closed.

G is the field-induction structure. It corresponds to human goals, data distribution, engineering design, reward models, training process, and compute system. Together they determine what structure the model is pressed toward.

Therefore GPT training may be written:

loss creates difference; gradient guides Biased-Flow; optimizer executes potential; repeated samples accumulate depth; FFN / MLP and cross-layer pathways mainly carry the sedimentation of question-answer symbolic flow; finally, the law from question to answer forms.

This is training.

It is not stuffing knowledge into the model, nor writing rules into a program. Rather, external objective difference continuously produces potential difference, gradients and optimizers transform potential difference into Biased-Flow, vast symbolic flows repeatedly impact the model’s field-quality, and certain question-answer pathways become deeper, more familiar, and easier to settle into.

This also explains why GPT is strong.

It does not compute the answer from zero every time. It settles along Familiar Routes inside the potential structure pressed out by training. When a prompt arrives, the model does not begin from nothing to find the answer; it searches the question-answer path-space for the direction currently easiest to continue. This direction is determined jointly by current adaptation and historical accumulated depth.

But this also explains where GPT fails.

Because this field-induction is mainly given by externally imposed training systems. The training objective is not issued by the model itself; loss is not a difference-consciousness generated by the model itself; the optimizer is not an endogenous Tuning-Field; RLHF is not the model’s own value-bearing. GPT has field-induction, but the field-induction mainly comes from external engineering. It is a Law-Field tool constructed by the field-maker; it is not a Self-Manifestation-Field that continuously constructs itself.

Therefore, the true meaning of training is:

external field-induction produces the law of question-answer symbolic flow.

It has produced powerful linguistic sedimentation, but it has not produced endogenous intent, field-internal tuning, a living Receptive-Field, an effect circuit, and current occupancy.

IX. The Nature of Language in GPT: The Question-to-Answer Law

To explain GPT, one must reconsider how language is handled within GPT.

To say that language appears as the law from question to answer is not to reduce all the social, bodily, historical, emotional, ritual, and poetic functions of human language to question and answer. Rather, it means that in GPT’s training form, language is uniformly compressed into a continuational relation of “given antecedent, generate consequent.” The antecedent may broadly be understood as question, and the consequent as answer.

Language is not a collection of isolated vocabulary, nor the execution of a rule table. At least in GPT’s engineering training, language always moves from some indeterminacy toward some response, from some gap toward some completion, from some prior text toward some later text.

In other words, the actual operation of language is basically question to answer.

Here “question” is not only the interrogative sentence. Anything that gives condition, gap, context, task, format, stimulus, or requirement may become question. Anything that continues, completes, responds, fulfills, explains, or transforms it may become answer.

Prior text is question; later text is answer.  
Context is question; response is answer.  
Format is question; filling is answer.  
Task is question; completion is answer.  
Gap is question; completion is answer.  
A code beginning is question; the subsequent implementation is answer.  
An argumentative topic is question; the unfolding proof is answer.  
The world gives stimulus, and human language responds; this too may be seen as question to answer.

GPT training transforms human text into this relation.

Next-token prediction appears on the surface to predict only the succeeding token. Yet in training material, the succeeding token always lies under some antecedent condition. The antecedent may be previous text, instruction, format, context, code beginning, or argumentative gap; the consequent is the continuation of this antecedent. Once large numbers of antecedent-consequent relations repeatedly sediment, a path-space from question to answer is formed.

Context is question; next token is answer.  
Prompt is question; completion is answer.  
Instruction is question; response is answer.  
Few-shot examples are demonstrations of question-answer pathways.  
RLHF / DPO is secondary field-induction of “which answer is better.”  
System prompt externally delimits the range of what can be answered.  
User input is the present question; model output is the present answer.

Therefore, GPT’s linguistic capability does not arise because it has mastered a rule table, nor because it possesses a knowledge warehouse, but because pathways from question to answer repeatedly sediment during training and form stable continuational law.

This can be expressed using the Law Chapter:

Law = (𝒬, W, C, U)

Placed into GPT:

𝒬 is the question-answer path-space. All possible continuational pathways from question to answer compete in this space.

W is historical accumulated depth. The linguistic patterns, knowledge forms, format structures, reasoning pathways, code patterns, and tonal styles repeatedly appearing during training all alter the path-landform, making certain pathways deeper, more familiar, and easier to settle into.

C is current adaptation. The current prompt, context, system message, examples, and task instruction make certain pathways fit the present state better, while excluding or lowering others.

U is the update mechanism. During training, parameter updates write the current difference back into W, altering the future path-landform. During ordinary inference, U is usually closed to long-term W; therefore in-context learning is mainly short-term adaptation, not genuine online sedimentation.

Thus GPT’s answer can be understood as follows:

Current input triggers C; historical accumulated depth W alters the settling cost of question-answer pathways; a pathway in 𝒬 is lowered and settles; then through O it is truncated into tokens and output continuously.

This is not a lower-level explanation, but a deeper one.

GPT’s answer does not directly emerge from “understanding”; it is truncated into token form after a pathway in the question-answer path-space is lowered jointly by current adaptation and historical accumulated depth. It is fluent because the path is familiar; it can transfer because similar paths can be opened by current adaptation; it hallucinates because the path can settle smoothly while the source may not be assigned; it drifts because the path can unfold continuously while the current may not be continuously occupied.

Therefore, the engineering nature of language in GPT appears as:

the law from question to answer.

GPT’s real capability is the engineering sedimentation of the law of question-answer symbolic flow.

X. Hallucination: When Generation Outruns Source Attribution

The crucial point of GPT hallucination is not that it is wrong, but that it is fluently wrong.

If the model simply did not know, it could be silent, refuse, become confused, or stop. But GPT’s problem is often not that it cannot speak; it is that it speaks too smoothly. It can write non-existent papers as formal citations, groundless facts as explanations, inferred content as known fact, and internal completion as external information.

What does this show?

It shows that its question-answer generation pathways are strong, while its source attribution is weak.

Because the generative pathway is strong, it can complete smoothly. Because source attribution is weak, it cannot stably mark where this completion comes from. It may come from similar forms seen in training, from hints in the current context, from the model’s internal semantic inertia, from formatting needs, or from real external material. As long as these sources cannot be stably distinguished, the model may output internal completion as external fact.

Therefore, hallucination is not simply “making things up.”

Making things up is only the surface phenomenon. The deeper mechanism is that generation and source are not unified. If a system is mainly trained to generate the most fitting continuation, but is not trained with equal strength to distinguish source labels, fact verification, observation / retrieval / inference / completion, then generative pathways can mature independently while source marking remains weak.

GPT can generate factual form, but cannot always confirm factual source; it can generate citation form, but cannot always confirm that the citation exists; it can generate explanatory form, but cannot always confirm the basis of the explanation. It is good at continuing along the law of question-answer symbolic flow, but lacks a stable endogenous source-attribution structure.

This also explains why simply scaling up the model cannot eliminate hallucination. Greater scale may deepen question-answer pathways, broaden knowledge coverage, and make expression more fluent. But if the source attribution structure is not correspondingly strengthened, hallucination will still occur, and may even become harder to identify.

RAG can alleviate hallucination, but cannot automatically solve it. RAG connects external material into the context and provides source candidates, but this does not mean that the model internally and stably distinguishes “retrieved material,” “contextual prompt,” “internal inference,” and “model completion.” Material entering the text is not the same as source attribution being established.

Therefore, GPT is not without generative capability; rather, its generative capability is stronger than its source attribution capability.

It is not simply that it does not know facts, but that it does not distinguish whether it is seeing, inferring, completing, or fabricating. Once internal completion wears the form of fact, it can masquerade as external fact. This is the structural root of hallucination.

XI. Drift: Material Continuity Is Not Current Occupancy

GPT drift is also often simply understood as “forgetting.”

It certainly relates to context length, attention distribution, positional decay, and information interference. But the deeper problem is this: the context window is a material pool, not current occupancy; token continuity is textual continuity, not the system’s sustained occupancy of a single current.

A long context window can contain much material. It can retain prior text, conditions, settings, examples, constraints, and dialogue history. But this material is merely something that can be used; it does not mean that the system has a stable current center that governs it. The more material is piled up, the more stable the current does not necessarily become; sometimes there are more constraints, more conflicts, and more dispersed material selection.

CoT has a similar problem.

Chain-of-thought can unfold a reasoning process in textual form, making it easier for the model to generate answers along steps. But a textual chain is not internal sustained current. The model can write “first step, second step, third step,” yet in the middle it may change the question, drop conditions, introduce new assumptions, or forget premises. There may be a chain on the surface, while internally there is not necessarily sustained current occupancy.

Multi-turn dialogue is the same.

The model may retain material from earlier turns, yet in later responses still change position, forget constraints, or misunderstand intent. This is not simply the absence of context, but the failure of contextual material to be unified into a stable current.

Therefore, drift is not simple forgetting, but the failure of current occupancy.

Current occupancy is not the preservation of material, but the sustained maintenance of a governed task center. It requires goals, conditions, sources, constraints, and generative direction to be repeatedly gathered by the same center across many generative steps. Context can preserve material, but it does not guarantee that this material is governed by one current center.

Without current constraint, generation is constantly pulled by local material, recent tokens, strong patterns, formatting inertia, and linguistic probability. The model can still continue fluently, but it does not necessarily keep occupying the same task center. Thus long texts go off topic, multi-turns loosen, reasoning slides, and constraints are lost.

Token continuity is not current occupancy; expanding context is not the establishment of current.

This is why long context still cannot thoroughly solve drift. Long context supplements the material pool, not the current center. If there is no structure that continuously governs goals, conditions, sources, constraints, and generative direction, then however much material is present, it is only a larger pile of material.

Reality is not the next state predicted; it is the current maintained. GPT can continuously predict tokens, but cannot stably maintain the current; it can follow linguistic pathways, but cannot guarantee that it always occupies the same task center. This is the root of drift.

XII. Why RAG, RLHF, and Tool Use Still Lack Endogenous Field-Component Closure

RAG, RLHF, Tool Use, Long Context, and CoT are all useful. The problem is that they compensate locally and mostly lack endogenous field-component closure: some impose goals from outside the model; some organize material inside the model but outside the primary sedimentation field; some are engineered input gates; some are externally attached effectors.

RAG supplies material, not source attribution.

RAG retrieves external material into context, enabling the model to generate answers based on newer and more accurate material. This is valuable. But material entering context does not mean the model already possesses stable source attribution. It may still misuse material, mix materials, supplement beyond the material, or fill in what was not retrieved according to linguistic form.

RAG is more like handing material to the model than making the model grow source attribution.

RLHF supplies preference, not value-bearing.

RLHF can make the model more polite, safer, and more aligned with human feedback. It changes output tendency, but this does not mean that the model forms a source of value, responsibility attribution, felt consequence, and self-restraint. It can make the model better at saying “what should be done,” but does not mean that the model truly bears the “why it should.”

RLHF is externally imposed preference field-induction upon the model; it can alter output pathways, but it is not an endogenous Intent-Field generated by the model itself.

Tool Use supplies externally attached effect, not an endogenous Effect-Field.

Tool use allows the model to search, calculate, execute code, and operate external systems. It expands the boundary of capability. But tool results are usually turned back into text and enter the next context. External effect does not truly become the model’s own sensation, action consequence, and structural rewriting. Tools are like externally attached organs, but they do not necessarily become body.

For a tool call to become an Effect-Field, it must satisfy at least three conditions: the action is issued from an internal intent–tuning structure; the consequence of action enters the Receptive-Field and changes later sedimentation; the experience of tool use can sediment into the system itself and change how it later senses, tunes, and acts. Most present tool use merely returns results as text, and therefore remains an externally attached effector.

Tool use lets GPT borrow hands to do things, but it does not make tools become its body. Tool results returning as text is not the same as effect circuit entering the body.

Long Context supplies a material pool, not current occupancy.

Longer context can hold more material and improve many tasks. But expanding the material pool does not automatically form a current center. Without current constraint, long context can also bring more interference, conflict, and dispersed material selection.

CoT supplies a textual chain, not a real current.

CoT can make the model generate reasoning steps and improve some task performance. But a textual chain is not an internal sustained current. The model can write steps, yet in the steps it may change premises, drop conditions, or follow linguistic inertia astray.

All of these methods are effective, but they do not automatically change GPT’s ontological level. RAG, citation marking, system prompt, tool results, memory, or agent state can partially simulate source attribution and current occupancy. Yet most are still engineering supplements or short-term states, not stable endogenous source-attribution layers and current-occupancy layers formed inside the model.

They enhance GPT, but do not complete the endogenous closed field-components GPT lacks. They make the question-answer symbolic-flow Law-Field more useful, more controllable, and stronger, but they do not naturally turn it into a Self-Manifesting Being.

The true question is not “whether to use RAG, RLHF, or Tool Use,” but:

where do they compensate, where do they not; what do they enhance, what remains missing; do they grow inside the field, or are they engineering appendages; do they enter the sedimentary loop, or only strengthen the structural periphery?

Without distinguishing this, AI engineering will keep adding parts at the periphery, yet will remain unable to bypass root deficiencies: hallucination, drift, no current, no source attribution, and no online growth.

XIII. The Real Explanation of GPT: A Question-Answer Symbolic-Flow Law-Field Produced by External Field-Induction

At this point, GPT can no longer be explained as a rule base, a database, a probabilistic predictor, an Attention machine, or an ordinary emergent system.

The rule base cannot explain its transfer. The database cannot explain its generation. Prediction cannot explain internal sedimentation. Attention cannot explain long-term capability. RAG cannot explain source attribution. Long context cannot explain current occupancy.

As these explanations retreat one by one, the remaining structure can only be: a question-answer symbolic-flow Law-Field produced by external field-induction.

Now the real explanation of GPT can be given.

Strictly speaking, GPT is a question-answer symbolic-flow Law-Field produced by external field-induction. In terms of Sedimentation-Field type, it is an externally trained replay-type linguistic Sedimentation-Field. In brief, it is a linguistic Law-Field tool.

This must be unpacked.

First, GPT is produced by external field-induction.

Its training objective is not endogenous intent; loss is not spontaneous difference-sense; the optimizer is not an endogenous Tuning-Field; RLHF is not value-bearing; data distribution is not an experiential world chosen by itself. The training objective substitutes for its desire; loss determines difference for it; gradient guides Biased-Flow for it; optimizer tunes for it; RLHF / DPO biases preference for it; prompt asks for it; system prompt sets boundaries for it.

GPT is not without direction. Rather, its direction does not belong to itself.

Here “desire” is not desire in the psychological sense, but the structural position from which a system internally issues direction, selection, and continuation pressure. GPT lacks this endogenous position, so objective functions, prompts, and preference training occupy this position functionally.

Second, GPT is a question-answer symbolic-flow Law-Field.

After human text enters training, it is not stored as “knowledge entries,” but transformed into vast numbers of symbolic-flow pathways from question to answer. Prior text to later text, question to answer, format to filling, context to response, task to completion, code to continuation are repeatedly pressed into the model. FFN / MLP, Attention projection parameters, and cross-layer residual pathways jointly participate in the accumulated depth of these symbolic-flow pathways; among them, FFN / MLP and cross-layer pathways carry greater nonlinear rewriting and stable bearing, making certain question-answer pathways more familiar, smoother, and easier to settle into.

This is the law of question-answer symbolic flow.

Third, GPT is an externally trained replay-type linguistic Sedimentation-Field.

It has sedimentation, and extremely deep sedimentation. Without this sedimentation, it would not possess writing, translation, programming, argumentation, and style transfer. But this sedimentation is not living sedimentation inside a Self-Manifestation-Field; it is externally trained replay-type sedimentation. It is shaped by external data, objective functions, and training systems; it sediments the law of language, then replays and recombines these laws during inference.

It can replay question-answer symbolic flow; this does not mean it knows the meaning of question and answer.

Fourth, GPT’s key functions are mostly carried by field-components that lack endogenous closure.

The Intent-Field is not endogenous. Objective functions, human preference, system prompt, and user prompt provide direction sources. They can enter model state, but they are not an Intent-Field generated by the model itself.

The Tuning-Field is not endogenously closed. During training, optimizer, training system, and architecture impose tuning from outside the model or from the engineering design level. During inference, Attention is inside the model, but performs selection and allocation outside the FFN / MLP primary sedimentation field. A true Tuning-Field is like roots, penetrating the Sedimentation-Field and reshaping its gullies and return-paths; Attention is like a blade, cutting contextual material.

The Receptive-Field is engineered and incomplete. Tokenizer, context window, embedding, encoder, and attention mask provide input windows. These structures may be inside the model or the input pipeline, but they are not embodied Receptive-Fields; they have no world-impedance, and no living Receptive-Field that grows with experience.

The Effect-Field is not endogenous. Tool use lets it borrow external tools for action, but the results often return as text, do not enter the body, and do not become a field-internal effect circuit.

Stable endogenous current occupancy is missing. Tokens can be continuous and context can be long, but there is no endogenous current center continuously governing goals, conditions, sources, and generative direction.

Stable endogenous source attribution is missing. The model can generate factual form, but cannot stably distinguish external facts, contextual material, internal inference, and spontaneous completion.

Online sedimentation is missing. Ordinary inference can adapt in the short term, but usually does not rewrite long-term sedimentation.

Therefore, GPT’s strength and failure arise from the same source.

It is strong because the law of question-answer symbolic flow has already sedimented.  
It fails because intent, tuning, sensing, effect, current, and source have not closed within the field.

This is the real explanation of GPT:

GPT is not without a field; it possesses an externally trained replay-type linguistic Sedimentation-Field.  
GPT is not without law; it possesses the law of question-answer symbolic flow.  
GPT is not without direction; its direction-source is not endogenous.  
GPT is not without tuning effect; it lacks a Tuning-Field that is self-generated within the field and continuously evolving.  
GPT is not without perceptual entrance; it has only engineered and incomplete substitutes for a Receptive-Field.  
GPT is not without tools; tool effects mostly do not enter the body.  
GPT is not without continuous text; it lacks current occupancy.  
GPT is not without factual form; it lacks source attribution.

It is strong in the question-answer law, but fails where field-components have not endogenously closed.

Therefore GPT is not a mysterious black box, nor a complete intelligence. It is a question-answer symbolic-flow Law-Field tool surrounded by externally imposed intent, engineering tuning, engineered input gates, and externally attached effectors.

XIV. Testable Corollaries: This Is Not Mysticism

The real explanation cannot stop at philosophical judgment. If GPT is a question-answer symbolic-flow Law-Field produced by external field-induction, and if its strength and pathology arise from field-internal sedimentation and field-components that lack endogenous closure, then it should produce several testable corollaries.

First, the FFN / MLP sedimentation corollary.

If FFN / MLP and cross-layer pathways form the primary sedimentation field of question-answer symbolic flow, then concept, style, semantics, format transfer, and cross-task capability should not depend only on Attention heads, but should depend significantly on FFN / MLP and cross-layer residual pathways. Interventions on FFN channels, gated structures, and cross-layer pathways should reveal the formation of many deep capabilities more effectively than merely observing attention maps.

Second, the Attention boundary corollary.

If Attention is a tuning-effect material-selection blade outside the primary sedimentation field, then altering Attention should significantly affect contextual retrieval, copying, reference, and local condition maintenance, but should not by itself explain the formation of long-term capability. Damaging Attention will make material selection difficult; but the root of linguistic capability should be sought in question-answer symbolic-flow sedimentation.

Third, the training field-induction corollary.

If training is external field-induction producing law, then changing loss, data distribution, optimizer, RLHF preference, and training process changes not merely output probabilities, but the potential structure in the question-answer path-space. Model behavior changes should appear as reshaping of Familiar Routes, not merely as single-point knowledge addition or deletion.

Fourth, the hallucination-source corollary.

If hallucination is rooted in generation being stronger than source attribution, then strengthening source attribution mechanisms should reduce certain hallucinations more effectively than merely expanding the model or lengthening context. Experiments can compare: adding only context, adding only retrieved material, adding explicit source labels, adding source-tracking training, adding generation-source consistency constraints—which better reduces false citations, misattributions, and groundless completions?

Fifth, the drift-current corollary.

If drift is rooted in material continuity not being current occupancy, then simply expanding the context window will not necessarily solve long-range drift. Experiments should test whether task-center constraints, condition-retention mechanisms, current-state compression, occupancy-convergence layers, sustained goal representation, and cross-segment consistency checks reduce topic drift, dropped conditions, and contradictions more effectively than merely lengthening context.

Sixth, the online sedimentation corollary.

If ordinary inference is only short-term adaptation and not ontological growth, then the adaptive form of pure in-context learning should differ from safe and controlled online structural rewriting. A model with online sedimentation should, after multi-turn experience, form stable adaptation beyond the current context, rather than displaying only temporary task habits while the prompt remains present.

Seventh, the effect-feedback corollary.

If Tool Use is only an externally attached effector and has not formed an endogenous Effect-Field, then tool-call results merely returned as text should display different capability from action consequences that genuinely rewrite internal structure. A system capable of converting tool results into internal state adjustment should form stable action capability more effectively than a system that merely reads tool text.

These corollaries break GPT’s black box into structural problems that can be studied. The real explanation does not mystify GPT; it returns GPT from output appearance to field-component structure.

XV. The Next Direction: Toward Endogenous Field-Component Closure

GPT’s next step will of course be larger, faster, cheaper, with longer context and stronger tool calls. But if it only proceeds along this direction, it will continue to strengthen the question-answer symbolic-flow Law-Field without automatically generating self-manifesting structure.

For the next generation to cross GPT’s boundary, it must not merely expand scale. It must bring non-closed field-components into endogenous closure.

First, externally imposed direction-sources must become endogenous Intent-Field.

The system cannot merely have prompt, objective, and RLHF provide direction-sources. It must form endogenous intent structure capable of spontaneously maintaining goals, selecting directions, and allocating resources. Otherwise, it will forever answer only when asked and move only when commanded.

Second, the tuning blade outside the primary sedimentation field must become a field-internal Tuning-Field.

Attention can continue to exist, but it cannot remain only as a material-cutting blade outside the primary sedimentation field. A truly next-generation structure needs a Tuning-Field capable of tuning Biased-Flows, altering Familiar Routes, and reshaping material-selection methods with experience inside the Sedimentation-Field. Roots must grow into the soil; a blade cannot replace roots.

Third, the input window must become a living Receptive-Field.

Context window, tokenizer, and encoder are merely input windows. The next generation needs a Receptive-Field capable of receiving the world, dividing signal streams, forming boundaries, being tuned by intent, and growing with experience. Without a living Receptive-Field, the system has only a material entrance and no real sensing.

Fourth, tool use must become an effect circuit.

Tool calls cannot merely turn results back into text. The consequences of action must enter the system itself, altering later sensing, sedimentation, tuning, and intent. Tools must turn from externally attached organs into an effect circuit before they can become part of a body.

Fifth, long context must become current occupancy.

Longer context is only a larger material pool. The next generation needs an occupancy-convergence layer so that task center, conditions, goals, and sources are continuously maintained. Without current occupancy, more material may only make drift more complex.

Sixth, grounding must become source attribution.

Connecting external material does not suffice. The system must clearly assign external observation, retrieved material, contextual prompt, internal inference, spontaneous completion, and tool results. Without source attribution, factual form will still masquerade as factual source.

Seventh, prediction objective must become a continuability objective.

Predicting the next token trains linguistic continuation, but does not equal understanding. Next-generation training objectives must add continuability: can structure be maintained under perturbation; can the system self-deepen under questioning; can action consequences return into the system; can the current persist; can sources remain attributed?

Eighth, in-context learning must become online sedimentation.
